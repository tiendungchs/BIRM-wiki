### Wave 25 — the associative store itself: what the brain's attractor does that Hopfield does not

From the user's framing: *current associative-memory models are nothing compared to the brain; a
human-level reasoning model may need a re-invented associative memory.* The wiki's own state says
the same thing twice over, and both halves are acquisition problems rather than writing problems.

**Half one — the mechanism page has no primary source.** [[wiki/concepts/attractor-dynamics.md]] is
the wiki's most heavily used mechanism: attractors appear on **39 of 73** concept and entity pages
and were load-bearing on eight of them before the page existed. It holds no survey and no tissue
measurement of its own. Every claim about what an attractor *is in a brain* is currently inherited
from modelling pages. [[wiki/entities/hopfield-network.md]] — the page the whole vocabulary rests on
— is sourced from **a Towards Data Science tutorial**, with the 1982 and 1985 primaries cited inside
its own provenance note as textbook, and every claim unique to it marked `(tentative)`.

**Half two — the rows that would price a new design are open on one side only.** `T49` holds
Sammons 2023 (dense, random) and cites Guzman 2016 (sparse, non-random) second-hand. `T56` runs on
Jezek 2011 through a model that re-describes it. [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]] turns on the
Wills 2005 / Colgin 2010 replication reversal and holds neither paper. `T62` ("should a fast
associative store hold instances, or model a distribution?") has no biological write rule in hand
at all. And `G42` — *no machine fast store has a capacity model* — is the longest cell in the
registry, assembled entirely from papers about **other** stores; the dense-associative-memory line
that raised the ceiling (Krotov & Hopfield) is cited on three pages and read on none.

Block A buys **attractor dynamics as measured in tissue**. Block B buys **the associative-memory
rewrite** — the machine-side primaries the wiki cites second-hand, and the three papers that
propose a different store rather than a bigger one.

**Probe results, all 2026-09-19.** `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org` renders `1606.01164`, `2008.02217`,
`2008.06996`, `2110.13976` and `2311.08135` in full with equations. It **307-redirects to
`arxiv.org/abs`** for `2112.03978` (Khona & Fiete) — no ar5iv build exists, so that row takes the
Nature Reviews HTML instead, which is the skill's preferred venue anyway. `arxiv.org/html/2501.02950v2`
renders in full. `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3904133` renders full body text including methods.
`pnas.org` answers `403` and `journals.physiology.org` answers `403` — bot-blocks, not paywall
verdicts, and neither venue is excluded. `nature.com`, `cell.com` and `elifesciences.org` are `clip`
on wave 22–24 precedent. **Two rows sit on `science.org`, which is excluded**: Wills 2005 (row A7)
and Guzman 2016 (row A9) take the author/repository PDF route per the skill's do-instead column.
No target duplicates a `_work/manifest.tsv` row.
