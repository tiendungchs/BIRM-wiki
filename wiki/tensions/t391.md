# T391 — Should a fast store's write gate be uncoordinated across units, sequenced, or rate-regulated?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The three gate policies are run on the *same* store at matched hardware and matched trace width, with capacity, retrograde interference and behaviour-at-saturation all measured — showing either that sequencing's capacity advantage survives a distributed trace, or that independent gating's uniform load survives slot-based storage, and whether rate regulation recovers either without a per-unit coordination signal.

## Position A

**Uncoordinated — each unit decides independently, and that is the feature.** BTSP's plateau gate is an independent per-cell event with probability `f_q`, generated at ≈0.0005 s⁻¹ and converted to `f_q ≈ 0.005` by the seconds-long window. Because the gate is *independent of the input*, memory load spreads uniformly over the population instead of concentrating on the easily-driven cells, and reconstruction error for the first 100 of 10,000 sequentially learned items is nearly identical to the last 100 — no retrograde interference, where a postsynaptically-gated one-shot rule degrades early traces badly ([[wiki/entities/btsp-cam.md]], Wu & Maass 2025). No cross-unit signal is required, and none is observed.

**Qualified by the primary BTSP source, which observes one** (Milstein et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]] rule 6): in a 500-cell CA1 network model constrained by the plateau-regulation literature, plateau probability is *raised* by long-range entorhinal feedback and *lowered* by local feedback inhibition, so the gate is stochastic but its rate is a function of population activity. The consequence is load-bearing in that model — fields tile the track and then stop changing because rising population activity closes the gate — and it is a cross-unit signal by construction. So `f_q` is an average over a regulated process, not a primitive

## Position B

**Sequenced — least-recently-used allocation, and it is worth 6×.** In a slot-based key-value store at `d = N = 40`, cycling the local third factor (`[γ_t]_i = 1` iff `t ≡ i mod N`) gives capacity `C ≈ 1.0N` — one pattern per slot, the analytic maximum — while independent `Bernoulli(p)` gating gives `C ≈ 0.16N`, barely above Hebbian Hopfield's `0.14N`, because random selection collides. The sequential version is also the one that learns the copy-paste task and generalizes past trained sequence lengths; the random version does not ([[wiki/entities/three-factor-key-value-memory.md]], Tyulmankov et al. 2021)

## Where it bites

Whether a write gate needs a coordination signal at all — an `L2` question about what the gating circuit is allowed to see. Position B's mechanism is unsupplied: the offered candidates are a per-neuron timer firing every `N` steps, a priming chain `i → i+1`, or an external coordinating circuit, and the paper demonstrates none, so the 6× is a bound on what coordination would buy rather than a measurement of biology. Position A's uniformity argument is measured but on a *distributed* trace, where a collision degrades many cells slightly; under slot storage a collision destroys one memory outright, so the two results may not be in conflict at all — the confound is trace width, not gate policy.

That reading makes the design question sharp rather than empirical: **a store that writes one item to one unit needs allocation to be coordinated; a store that writes one item to many units does not.** If true, the cost of per-item retention policy (only slot storage supports the metaplasticity latch that makes flashbulb memories cheap) is a coordination signal that biology has not been shown to have — and the wiki's two feedforward one-shot stores each pay one of the two prices.

## Position C

**Rate-regulated — uncoordinated *per write*, but the aggregate write rate is closed-loop.** Neither A's independence nor B's sequencing: which cell writes is still a private stochastic event, but `f_q` itself is driven up by a long-range instructive pathway (entorhinal drive, raised at a goal) and down by local feedback inhibition proportional to how much of the population is already active (Milstein et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]). This buys two things A cannot state and B does not need: **coverage control** — writing stops when the population is committed, and resumes when the environment changes — and **content bias without per-cell coordination**, since raising drive at behaviourally relevant locations both recruits silent cells and translocates existing fields there. The coordination signal is one scalar per population, not a permutation over units, so it costs what A costs and delivers part of what B delivers

## Status

`LIVE` — neither store has been run under the other's gate. The decisive experiment is small: sweep trace width from 1 to `f_q N` in one architecture under both gates, and read off where the sequencing advantage disappears. Position C changes what that sweep should measure: it is a third gate policy on the same axis, cheap enough to add to the sweep, and it predicts that A's flat recall curve over 10,000 sequential items is an artefact of a *fixed* `f_q` — under a rate-regulated gate the store should decline writes as it fills, which is exactly the behaviour `G42` says no store has
