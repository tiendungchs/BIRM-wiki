# T327 — Is a world model's payoff the rollout, or the state it carries?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One world model is scored on one task under two read-outs — decision-time rollout against a reactive policy on the model's own recurrent/predictive state — with model, data and controller capacity held fixed, and the gap is attributed to search rather than to features.

## Position A

**The rollout.** The whole justification for holding a transition model separately from a policy is that it can be *searched*: re-goaling without retraining, counterfactual queries, contingency inside the horizon ([[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]]). Every planner in the wiki spends decision-time compute on rollouts — cross-entropy-method search in [[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]] and [[wiki/entities/adaworld.md]], gradient descent on a summed cost in [[wiki/entities/h-jepa.md]], expectimax in [[wiki/entities/aixi.md]] — and the model-based/model-free table's flexibility column is a claim about what search buys. Where the policy is amortised instead ([[wiki/entities/continual-dreamer.md]]), the rollout is still doing the work, just offline: policy gradients are taken entirely inside imagination.

## Position B

**The state.** In the origin paper of the whole family, the measured payoff is the predictive hidden state used **reactively**, with no rollout at decision time at all: concatenating the MDN-RNN's `h_t` to a *linear* controller's input moves CarRacing from 632 ± 251 to 906 ± 21, while adding a hidden layer to the `z`-only policy reaches only 788 ± 141 — so the recurrent predictive state beats extra policy capacity, and the authors' own reading is that the agent "does not need to *plan ahead*", it queries the RNN instinctively ([[wiki/entities/world-models-vmc.md]], Ha & Schmidhuber 2018). The same shape appears where a latent predictive loss is bolted onto a model-free agent: 4 of 5 DeepMind Lab tasks improve and the exception is the one requiring no memory, i.e. the benefit is state-carrying ([[wiki/entities/cpc.md]]). And [[wiki/entities/othellogpt.md]] holds a causally verified world model and still answers many inputs through a cheaper circuit that bypasses it (`T158`).

## Where it bites

Gap `G15` (no control policy over simulation) and the whole latency argument in `G62`. Under **A**, decision-time compute is where a world model earns its keep and the missing rollout controller is urgent. Under **B**, the search is optional for reactive control and the model's job is to supply a sufficient statistic — which would mean the wiki's world-model pages are measuring the wrong quantity, and that the cases where rollout is genuinely necessary (re-goaling, sparse reward, novel counterfactuals) have to be separated out and tested on their own rather than assumed to be what the benchmark scores.

The two are not yet arguing about the same experiment. **B**'s evidence comes from dense-reward, short-horizon reactive control (driving, dodging, navigation) where a one-step predictive statistic is plausibly sufficient; **A**'s claims are about flexibility under a *changed goal*, which none of **B**'s tasks vary. Neither side has run the ablation the `Closes when` names, and in the one system where it would be cheapest — an agent that already computes `h_t` and already has a simulator — nobody has reported the reactive baseline alongside the planned one.

## Status

`LIVE` — B has the larger measured effect on the tasks it covers and A has the argument for the tasks nobody has run. **(brainstorm)** The likely resolution is that they are answers about different reward structures rather than about world models, and the discriminating variable is whether the task's optimal action is a function of the current predictive state — which is exactly what a re-goaling manipulation destroys (`G28`).
