# T194 — Is approaching a memory's capacity a failure mode or the mechanism that produces generalisation?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The class-learning effect is measured against a store trained on the family directly, with an independent measure of what was abstracted, at more than `N = 200` and one 2-parameter family.

## Position A

**Failure.** Every capacity account in the wiki treats the load limit as the point where the store stops working: Hopfield-class recall collapses catastrophically past `p_max ≈ k·C/(a ln(1/a))` ([[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]]), scaffold stores degrade resolution ([[wiki/entities/vector-hash.md]]), and the design goal in each case is to stay below the number ([[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] G42)

## Position B

**The mechanism.** Jaeger's **class learning effect**: a 200-unit reservoir loaded with `k` patterns drawn from one 2-parameter family and then cued with patterns *never loaded* from the same family recalls the novel ones worse than the loaded ones only while `k` is small; **above a threshold the two accuracies become equal**, which the author reads as the network abandoning rote storage of instances and representing the parametric class instead. Loading pressure is what forces the abstraction ([[wiki/entities/conceptor.md]])

## Where it bites

Sets whether a fast store should be sized *below* the item count it must hold or deliberately *above* it. If B holds, a store operated near saturation is a schema learner and a store operated well below it is a lookup table — which makes capacity a curriculum parameter (G32) rather than a safety margin, and makes the quota a control signal rather than an alarm

## Status

`LIVE` — B is one demonstration, on one 2-parameter synthetic family, at `N = 200`, with no independent measure of what was abstracted and no comparison against a store trained on the family directly. But the two positions are not incompatible in the way they look: A measures recall of *stored items* and B measures recall of *unstored members of the same family*, so the same overload can be both a loss of item fidelity and a gain in class coverage, and no experiment here reports both curves for one store
