# T129 — Is the direct/indirect relation a scalar balance, or two concurrently active ensembles?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A model computes the difference over co-active ensembles and reproduces the timing result, which lies outside both current formulations.

## Position A

**A balance.** The classical model reads movement as a shift in the net drive on a single output interface, and the wiki's model of it computes a literal difference, `net_j = [Go_j − NoGo_j]⁺` ([[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]). Dopamine depletion moving the two pathways in opposite directions, optogenetic activation of each pathway producing opposite motor effects (Kravitz et al. 2010; Hikida et al. 2010), and the opponent plasticity mechanism all fit it

## Position B

**Two ensembles, both up.** Mink's elaboration predicts that during ongoing behaviour activity *increases* in ensembles of **both** pathways — select this program, suppress the competing ones — and primate limb-reaching recordings confirm it (Turner & Anderson 1997). On this reading the informative variable is *which* ensembles are co-active, and in Parkinson's models it is the **timing** of the two pathways' activity rather than the level that predicts movement choice and initiation (Bevan et al. 2002); Gerfen & Surmeier 2011 add that whether the two pathways even receive the same cortical information is unresolved

## Where it bites

Decides whether an opponent gate can be collapsed to one scalar per channel. If B holds, a difference discards the identity of the co-active ensembles and the relative timing between them, and no architecture in the wiki represents either — PBWM's `kWTA` over `[Go − NoGo]⁺` cannot express "both channels active, different sets" at all

## Status

`LIVE` — the two are not strictly incompatible (a difference can be computed over co-active populations), but no wiki model computes one, and the timing result is outside both formulations
