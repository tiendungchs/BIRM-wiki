# T68 — Does strong activity raise or lower a neuron's intrinsic excitability?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The composition is stated and tested — what happens to the CREB excitability tag when the homeostatic term is blocked, which no source measures.

## Position A

**It lowers it — homeostatic intrinsic plasticity.** The standard account of intrinsic and synaptic conductance regulation is negative feedback: activity above a set point reduces excitability, which is what keeps a recurrent network with Hebbian potentiation from running away ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]], where every rule needs an external stabiliser)

## Position B

**It raises it — and non-homeostatically.** Learning phosphorylates CREB for hours, which reduces K⁺ conductance, shrinks the post-burst after-hyperpolarization and lowers spike-frequency adaptation, so the cells that just encoded are *more* excitable and preferentially recruited into the next memory. This is stated by Lisman et al. 2018 as an explicit contrast with homeostatic regulation, and it is causally sufficient (KCNQ2/KCNQ3 dominant negatives increase allocation, Kir2.1 cuts activation probability ~5×)

## Where it bites

Decides the **sign** of any intrinsic-excitability term a builder adds, and whether the fast store needs its own brake. Under A the term is a stabiliser and can be added freely; under B it is positive feedback whose only named brake is *saturation of synaptic strength* — a ceiling most machine implementations do not have, so importing the mechanism without a weight bound reproduces the runaway the biology avoids by accident ([[wiki/concepts/memory-allocation-excitability.md]])

## Status

`LIVE` — the two may be one system at different timescales (transient anti-homeostatic tag over a slow homeostatic set point), but no source in the wiki states the composition, and nothing measures what happens to the tag when the homeostatic term is blocked
