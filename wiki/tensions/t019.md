# T19 — Is scalar reward a sufficient training signal, or a marginal one?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Reward-only and predictive-cost training are compared at matched sample budget on the same task, separating "sufficient in the limit" from "marginal in practice" — which no side currently disputes.

## Position A

**Sufficient in principle** — "reward is enough" (Silver et al. 2021), and the wiki's own ceiling agrees at the ideal level: AIXI maximises expected reward over a universal mixture and needs nothing else ([[wiki/entities/aixi.md]])

## Position B

**Marginal in practice, and structurally so.** Scalar reward is low-information feedback, and in standard RL the cost function *is the environment* — an unknown, non-differentiable oracle whose gradient must be estimated by trials. Make the cost a known differentiable function of predicted states instead and the design becomes optimal control, not reinforcement learning: almost all parameters are trained by predicting observations, and reward "plays a relatively minor role" (LeCun 2022). Hutter's own accounting agrees quantitatively — demonstrations beat reward by `≈K(R)` bits ([[wiki/concepts/universal-induction.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides where the wiki's learning signal comes from. If B, the design problem is *specifying a differentiable cost* rather than shaping a reward, which relocates the difficulty from credit assignment to objective design (gap G30) and makes the immutable Intrinsic Cost a first-class architectural decision rather than an environment property

**A measured lower bound on how little a scalar carries, from the aversive side.** The one ascending line that a mouse's forebrain reads for "something is wrong" pools cutaneous pain of four kinds, visceral malaise, itch, meal-termination satiety, food and object novelty and a shock-paired tone onto the same cells, keeping only intensity and sign ([[wiki/concepts/general-danger-channel.md]], Campos et al. 2018). That is B's "low-information feedback" charge made concrete on a real signal — and it is also B's problem, since the animal nevertheless produces stimulus-appropriate responses, so either the parallel modality pathway those authors assume does the work (`T357`) or scalar feedback is doing more than B's accounting allows.

## Status

`LEANING` — toward B on sample efficiency, which no side disputes; A survives only as a statement about what is *representable* in the limit, which is the same regime split as T16
