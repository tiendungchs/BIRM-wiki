# T216 — Does a human adversary paid to fool one model find that model's idiosyncratic blind spots, or the model class's?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Adversary-model identity is varied while holding the annotator pool fixed, separating class-general vulnerabilities from one seed's.

## Position A

**The model class's.** In rounds 2 and 3 of ANLI the adversary was an *ensemble* of differently-seeded models, and the base model still scores **19.3 and 20.4 against a 33.3 chance level** on the round it authored — below chance, not at it, so the annotators found a systematic wrong belief shared across seeds rather than one parameter vector's noise. The exclusive-annotator test subset tracks the main test set within ~4 points, which also rules out the items being artefacts of particular writers' style. And the discovered weakness class transfers to a differently-authored probe: adding the data lifts an independent numerical-reasoning stress test by **+18.5** points (Nie et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/anli.md]])

## Position B

**Partly that one model's.** In the same results table, BERT and XLNet — trained on identical data, including all three ANLI rounds — **beat RoBERTa on A2 and A3, the two rounds RoBERTa itself seeded** (50.7 / 48.3 vs 48.9 / 44.4). If the vulnerabilities were purely class-general, an architecture that never served as the adversary would not find them easier. The authors' own reading is that the three models have different weaknesses, plausibly inherited from their different pretraining corpora

## Where it bites

Whether an adversarially-collected benchmark is a *benchmark* or a *report on one system*. If A, the loop is the wiki's cheapest o.o.d. instrument and its scores are comparable across systems; if B, each round measures its own adversary and cross-system comparison is invalid — which also decides whether the +27.5-point matched-budget data-efficiency result (G32) is a general curriculum finding or a fact about RoBERTa.
