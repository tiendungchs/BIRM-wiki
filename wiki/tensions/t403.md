# T403 — Does a self-generated goal curriculum teach through the goals, or through the solutions shipped with them?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One architecture, one task suite, the *goal distribution held fixed* and the demonstration channel switched on and off — plus the converse arm, where a proposer's difficulty is regulated toward the solver's frontier and the demonstration is then removed. Position A predicts the demonstration is redundant whenever proposed goals lie near the frontier; position B predicts the demonstration remains necessary at any difficulty the proposer is rewarded for raising, because the goal's *reachability by the solver* and its *value as training signal* are the same quantity.

## Position A

**The goals carry it.** [[wiki/concepts/hindsight-goal-relabelling.md]] (Andrychowicz et al. 2017) supplies a goal distribution and nothing else — no demonstration, no shaping, no schedule — and takes three Fetch manipulation tasks (push, slide, pick-and-place) from a flat `0` to solved, and bit-flipping from `n ≤ 13` to `n = 50`. The mechanism is entirely label-side: the same collected transitions, rewritten under goals the episode happened to achieve. Its own strategy table isolates what does the work — `future` (goals from the trajectory's near future) beats `episode`, which beats `final`, and `random` (goals from the global history) is the one arm that fails — so the value of a goal generator is the *reachability profile of the distribution it emits*, and a generator that hits that profile needs no further channel into the learner. The pick-and-place caveat points the same way: the fix for the one task that stalled was **widening the goal sampler** (sometimes on the table, sometimes in the air), not adding demonstrations.

## Position B

**The solutions carry it.** [[wiki/entities/asymmetric-self-play.md]] (OpenAI et al. 2021) generates goals adversarially from a second policy in the same body, and the ablation is unambiguous: with Alice Behavioural Cloning removed, Bob "completely fails to solve any holdout task" — **zero**, on push, flip, pick-and-place and stack, against a full system that solves all four and generalises zero-shot to table setting, tangram and dominoes. The goal generator is unchanged in that arm; only the relabelled trajectory `τ_BC = {(s_t, s_T, a_t, r_t)}` is withdrawn. The supporting ablations sharpen it rather than soften it: the *filter* (demonstrate only the goals Bob failed) and the *clip* (PPO trust region on the BC loss, `Â = 1`, `ε = 0.2`) are both worth measurable stability, i.e. the demonstration channel is not a bulk data subsidy but a precisely targeted one. Under B, "generate your own goals" without a witness trajectory is an empty prescription for any proposer strong enough to be worth having.

## What separates the two, and why it is not yet decided

The regimes differ on one axis neither paper isolates: **how far the proposed goal is from what the solver can already do.**

| | Goal source | Distance from the solver's competence | Demonstration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hindsight relabelling | The solver's *own* realised future | Zero by construction — it was just traversed | None, and none needed |
| Asymmetric self-play | A separate policy **paid `+5` when the solver fails** | Maximised on purpose | Required, or the whole system scores `0` |

So the two results are compatible under a single rule — *a goal needs a witness in proportion to how far outside the solver's current reach it was pushed* — and incompatible under either side's own account, since A claims the distribution is the whole content and B's generator would be worthless by A's `random`-strategy logic (an adversarially-hard goal carries no more gradient than an arbitrary one) if the witness were not there to convert it.

**(brainstorm)** If that rule holds, the demonstration channel is not a second mechanism at all but the **price of decoupling the proposer from the solver**. Self-sampled goals are free and bounded by the solver's own coverage; proposed goals can exceed that coverage and must then pay a route back. This predicts a measurable exchange rate: regulate Alice's difficulty (reward Bob's failure *rate* toward a set-point rather than each failure) and the no-ABC arm should recover continuously as the set-point drops toward the frontier — which is also the point at which the system stops being adversarial and becomes [[wiki/concepts/learning-progress.md]]'s scheme with a second body.

## Where it bites

**Whether a goal generator has to be embodied.** Under A a goal is a *sample*, so any generative model over goal space will do — a GAN over achieved states, a setter network, a latent sampler — and the design problem is calibrating its output distribution. Under B a goal is a *sample plus a route*, which no generative model can emit, and the proposer must therefore share the solver's action space and initial state. That forecloses an entire design family, and it is the reason asymmetric self-play needs a resettable simulator at all: the witness is only valid if the solver starts where the proposer started.

The two also differ in what is **architecturally denied**. A cannot express a goal the solver has never come near — the distribution can only walk outward at the speed of the solver's own coverage, which is exactly the failure mode the four hand-designed ADR curricula display (three of them acquire no pick-up or stacking skill at all, because the annealed distribution never reaches a regime the policy can enter). B cannot express a goal that *ought* to be wanted but has no known route — an unsolved problem is inadmissible as a training goal, so the scheme can never set itself a target it has not already accidentally hit.
