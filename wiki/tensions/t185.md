# T185 — Does structural similarity influence memory *access*, or only evaluation after access?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Access is surface-driven.** Gentner & Forbus 1991 ([[wiki/entities/macfac.md]], now in the wiki as a primary source) and Gentner, Rattermann & Forbus 1993 find little effect of analogical similarity on reminding — the 1991 numbers are LS .56, SF .53, AN .12, FOR .09 with rated soundness running the *other* way (AN, LS > SF, FOR) and the dissociation holding **within subject**, i.e. people judge the matches that came to mind most easily to be unsound; the 1991 paper is careful that the finding is not that higher-order relations fail to contribute (one of three studies found a small significant benefit) but that their effect on retrieval is far smaller than on mapping; this is the premise of the two-stage MAC/FAC architecture, whose first stage inspects only object features and relations and is *designed* to be structure-blind so it scales to a whole memory

## Position B

**Access is partly structure-sensitive.** Wharton et al. 1994 find an effect; Thagard et al. 1990 and the 1993 study agree the effect exists but appears only, or is greater, in the presence of surface similarity. Plate's contribution is that the debate need not be settled architecturally — a structure-sensitive first stage costs exactly one dot product, so structure-blindness at access is not forced by tractability

## Where it bites

Decides whether a reasoning model's retrieval stage should pay for structure at all. If access is genuinely surface-driven in humans, the wiki's schema-retrieval mechanisms ([[wiki/concepts/subgraph-matching.md]], G37) are modelling the *second* stage and have no biological first stage to match; if it is structure-sensitive, the cheap-first-stage assumption that motivates order embeddings and every amortised matcher here is a tractability artefact rather than a design constraint

## Status

`LIVE` — the psychological evidence is mixed and all of it predates the mechanism that makes the question cheap to test. **Two 1995 additions narrow the question without settling it** (Forbus, Gentner & Law, [[wiki/entities/macfac.md]]). (i) *Both* kinds of content are required in the cheap statistic: sweeping the entire design space with relations dropped from the content vector leaves no viable setting anywhere, so structure-blindness at access cannot mean structure-*free* — the argument is about relational **arrangement**, not relational vocabulary. (ii) Structure-sensitivity at access is real but is a property of the *encoding*, not of the matcher: relational retrieval rises with expertise and intensive encoding (T199), which position A absorbs without amendment. What A concedes is the direction of the remaining disagreement — Wharton et al. 1994's competition effect, where competition between memory items heightens structural similarity's influence, is an inter-item effect this architecture cannot express at all, because each item is scored independently
