# T257 — In the default/control switch, who drives whom?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** An intervention rather than fitted dynamics: the salience nodes and the default hubs are perturbed in turn and the switch measured, which no human study has done.

## Position A

**The salience network drives.** The triple-network model: the anterior insula and dorsal anterior cingulate detect a behaviourally relevant event and, by one common mechanism, disengage the default mode network while engaging lateral frontoparietal cortex. The anterior insula is the causal *outflow* hub across a range of tasks under dynamic causal modelling; rodent optogenetic stimulation with fibre photometry reproduces the switching signature; and the behavioural signature of the mechanism failing — weak switching → reduced default deactivation, reduced stimulus-evoked sensory activity, frontoparietal hyperactivity on slow trials — is replicated (Menon 2023, [[wiki/entities/salience-network.md]])

## Position B

**The default network drives.** From the same review: dynamic causal modelling of *resting* fMRI finds posterior cingulate and medial prefrontal cortex exerting greater causal influence on lateral frontoparietal networks than the reverse, and information-theoretic analysis of distributed intracranial depth electrodes finds the default network's net causal outflow to all other networks — including sensory and motor — significantly exceeding its inflow, both at rest and during episodic memory formation, with interaction strength *higher* during memory formation. On this reading the default network is the broadcaster and the control networks are among its readers

## Where it bites

Whether an architecture's mode variable is *set by* a detector upstream of both modes, or *emitted by* the internal-mode system itself as part of its broadcast. The first is a two-line addition to any controller — one salience scalar, two opposed gains ([[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] `G90`); the second makes the internal simulator the system's top-level sequencer and demotes the control networks to consumers, which is a different machine

## Status

`LIVE` — and both sides come from the *same author in the same review*, which does not adjudicate them beyond suggesting they are different regimes (event-driven switching vs sustained broadcast). Neither is an intervention in humans: dynamic causal modelling and directed information both infer direction from fitted dynamics on observational data ([[wiki/concepts/effective-connectivity.md]]), and the estimates disagree while sharing a dataset type. The one causal manipulation, rodent optogenetics, supports side A in a species whose default-mode homology the review itself calls incompletely understood. **The cheap discriminator**: the two sides predict opposite lead–lag at the *onset* of an unexpected salient event versus at a spontaneous, internally generated shift of mental state — the second of which the review's own event-boundary results say is detectable in posterior cingulate patterns
