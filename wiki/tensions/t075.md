# T75 — Does expanding a code's dimensionality improve linear separability?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The reversal point is predicted from dataset statistics rather than found by sweep, so expansion recoding can be sized rather than tuned.

## Position A

**Yes — that is the point of expansion recoding.** Cover's theorem is the stated justification for reservoir computing, for the cerebellar granule-layer expansion in [[wiki/entities/sparse-distributed-memory.md]], and for [[wiki/concepts/sparse-distributed-representations.md]]'s recognition-by-subsampling, whose error rates fall superexponentially in dimension: lift the input into a higher-dimensional nonlinear space and the probability that the classes are linearly separable rises. Under this reading, more effective dimensions are strictly better and the only cost is units.

**A second, behaviour-linked datum for A, in a brain rather than a model.** Monkey lateral prefrontal populations reach the *maximum* dimensionality their task design allows (`d = 24` of 24 conditions, versus `d < 8` for simulated pure-selectivity cells at matched noise), and on error trials the dimensionality collapses while the task variables stay fully decodable — so here more dimensions track *correct behaviour*, not just separability in principle (Rigotti et al. 2013, [[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]).

## Position B

**Only up to a dataset-dependent point, after which it reverses.** Cazalets et al. 2025 measure effective dimensionality (fewest principal components reaching 90% variance) against cluster quality across ~500-unit reservoirs: the best-performing networks sit at *moderate* dimensionality with near-optimal within-class compactness, and architectures pushing dimensionality higher do not convert it into accuracy — "beyond a dataset-dependent tipping point, extra dimensions mainly inject noise and erode class structure". The operative split is **task-agnostic expressivity** vs **task-relevant effective dimensionality**, which are measured to come apart.

**The same source supplies the mechanism B's reversal has been missing.** A strongly non-linear mixed-selectivity unit integrates the sources *not* under task control along with those that are, so it is more sensitive to task-irrelevant variability than a pure-selectivity unit — "most useful but most fragile". Extra dimensions are therefore bought with variance sensitivity, which is exactly what "beyond the tipping point, extra dimensions mainly inject noise" would look like from the inside. This makes A and B compatible under one condition: expansion pays only where the non-linear terms are **trial-consistent**, i.e. where the nuisance sources are suppressed rather than merely mixed in — which is a constraint on the *variance* of the expansion, not on its width.

## Where it bites

Every design choice justified by "expand, then separate linearly": reservoir size, granule-layer expansion ratios, the width of a random-feature basis, and any regulariser that rewards decorrelation for its own sake. It also bounds the reading of [[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]'s shattering-dimensionality results in the other direction from T50 — high dimensionality is no more a proxy for a useful code than low dimensionality is a proxy for abstraction. **Not yet a real contradiction:** Cover's theorem is about *random* lifts of a fixed input, while the reversal is measured over architectures whose lifts differ in kind, so the two may be measuring different things. The discriminating experiment — hold the growth rule fixed and sweep `n` alone — is not run. **A third property splits the difference and is monotone in dimension:** *addressability*. `d ≥ log C(n,k)/log(1+1/γ)` is a necessary condition for a `d`-dimensional similarity store to be able to return every `k`-subset of `n` items, and the measured critical-`n` of freely optimised embeddings rises monotonically (cubically) in `d` with no reversal ([[wiki/concepts/retrieval-capacity.md]], Weller et al. 2026). So dimension is strictly good for *which questions can be asked* and possibly bad for *how well classes separate*, which suggests A and B are arguing about two different quantities that a single architecture must trade off.
