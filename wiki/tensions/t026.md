# T26 — Do newborn dentate granule cells perform pattern separation, or block it?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Environmental similarity is varied parametrically with mature and immature granule cell populations distinguished in the same animal.

## Position A

**They perform it.** Loss and gain of function agree and are parametric: ablating adult neurogenesis by x-irradiation impairs delayed-non-match-to-place at 2 intervening radial-maze arms but not at 3–4, exercise-driven neurogenesis improves discrimination of adjacent identical stimuli, and genetically enhancing newborn-neuron survival improves contextual fear discrimination (Yassa & Stark 2011)

## Position B

**They oppose it while immature.** A computational account has immature granule cells *increasing* similarity among temporally correlated inputs — "pattern integration" — because they are far more excitable and respond too broadly to separate, negatively regulating separation until they mature. A reactivation/Arc study goes further: the number of reactivated granule cells matches the newly-generated proportion, suggesting mature cells are functionally "retired" and calling their long-hypothesised orthogonalizing role into question

## Where it bites

Decides what the machine analogue of neurogenesis is. Under A it is **capacity added where separation demand is high** — grow the sparse layer when discrimination fails. Under B it is a **temporary integration bias**, i.e. the opposite sign, and the store's separating population is the *young* one with the mature population doing something else entirely. The two prescribe opposite growth policies for a fast **M** ([[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]], [[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]])

## Status

`LIVE` — the review flags this as under active debate and the direct data on mature vs. immature contributions as scant; what would move it is parametric manipulation of environmental similarity with the two populations distinguished
