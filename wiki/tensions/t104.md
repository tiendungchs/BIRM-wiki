# T104 — Is the working-memory capacity limit a resource ceiling or a safety margin the system imposes on itself?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The brakes are removed and capacity measured against the resource bound: if performance rises to a new ceiling the margin account holds, if it does not the ceiling account does.

## Position A

**A ceiling.** Every account in the wiki locates the bound in something scarce: distinguishable noisy transitions in an attractor chain give `7 ± 2` ([[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]]), superposition interference among concurrent control states degrades both ([[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]), and softmax competition at the read spreads one unit of mass over every candidate ([[wiki/concepts/attention.md]]). On all three the store is already doing the best its substrate allows

## Position B

**A chosen margin.** Recurrent excitation is unsafe, so prefrontal microcircuits run a Ca²⁺→K⁺ shunt whose *purpose* is to prevent runaway activity, and the store therefore operates below its achievable capacity: blocking the SK channel (apamin) or IP₃-mediated Ca²⁺ release (xestospongin C) in rat prefrontal cortex **improves** working memory, while removing the same brakes genetically lowers cortical seizure threshold and, for KCNQ2/3, causes childhood epilepsy. The ~10–30 s point at which hippocampal connections become necessary is offered as the same feedback's doing (Arnsten et al. 2010, [[wiki/concepts/dynamic-network-connectivity.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a machine store's capacity is a fact about its design or a hyperparameter: if B holds, every recurrent store with a stability regulariser is running an unmeasured margin, and the sweep that measures it (lower the regulariser until instability, record capacity) has never been run. It also decides whether "enlarge the store" or "loosen the brake" is the right intervention (gap G42)
