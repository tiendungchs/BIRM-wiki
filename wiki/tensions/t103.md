# T103 — What is the common component of control?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The assumed measurement is made: whether inhibition-specific machinery carries low between-subject variance, which B assumes and nobody has tested.

## Position A

**Inhibition.** Once a higher-order factor is fitted over inhibition, updating and shifting, it predicts the inhibition factor almost perfectly, and a bifactor reparameterization yields updating-specific and shifting-specific factors but **no inhibition-specific factor** — the variance shared across stopping tasks *is* the variance shared across all control tasks, and every control operation can be described as suppressing something (an intruding item, a stale item, an abandoned task set)

## Position B

**Goal maintenance and biasing.** The same statistics follow from a controller whose only primitive is an actively held goal that biases processing: inhibition tasks load hardest because a lapsed goal lets the prepotent response win by default, so *individual differences* in stopping are differences in goal activation rather than in a stop process. The inhibition reading also assumes that processes sharing the word share a mechanism, which is false — the several "inhibitions" dissociate and some are not neural inhibition at all (Friedman & Robbins 2021, [[wiki/concepts/control-unity-and-diversity.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a machine controller needs a dedicated suppression channel with its own parameters ([[wiki/concepts/inhibitory-control-of-coding.md]]) or gets suppression for free from bias into a competitive substrate ([[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]) — and the two designs predict different factor structures over a population of trained instances

## Status

`LEANING B` on parsimony, not on data: B derives A's statistics while A cannot derive B's, and B is what the wiki's own mechanism page already implements. The discriminating measurement — whether inhibition-specific machinery (global motor suppression via the right inferior frontal gyrus→subthalamic pathway) carries low between-subject variance — is assumed by B and has never been made. A third possibility neither side states: stopping is a *hub with a fixed spoke*, so the shared hub varies and the spoke does not
