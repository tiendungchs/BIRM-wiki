# T402 — On sparse-reward long-horizon tasks, is the missing component a task decomposition or a persistent frontier store?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One paper runs an archive-and-return baseline and a subgoal-discovery method on the same environment at the same interaction budget with the same state abstraction. The abstraction clamp is the whole experiment: every existing comparison confounds the discovery mechanism with the representation it was given. Position A predicts the decomposition still wins once the archive is denied simulator restoration; position B predicts the archive wins at every budget and that the decomposition's advantage disappears entirely once the flat learner is allowed to store its frontier.

## Position A

**The task must be decomposed.** The wiki's canonical statement is [[wiki/entities/dqn.md]]'s: a learner with deep features, off-policy value learning and a replay buffer reaches human parity wherever dense reward makes value backup sufficient and scores exactly `0` on Montezuma's Revenge, and the paper names temporally extended planning as the missing capability. `G33` collects eleven families built on that diagnosis — bottleneck partitioning ([[wiki/entities/cscg.md]]), eigenoptions ([[wiki/concepts/eigenoption-discovery.md]]), grammar induction over demonstrations ([[wiki/entities/hisd.md]]), return-differentiated terminations ([[wiki/entities/option-critic.md]]), latent directions ([[wiki/entities/feudal-networks.md]]), discriminator regions ([[wiki/entities/diayn.md]]) — and [[wiki/concepts/optimal-hierarchy-criterion.md]] supplies a normative argument that a decomposition provably reduces the expected number of trial-and-error attempts on a drawn task. Humans parse the same tasks into the same bottlenecks with no prompting (Solway et al. 2014).

## Position B

**The task must be *remembered*.** [[wiki/entities/go-explore.md]] (Ecoffet et al. 2021) decomposes nothing — no options, no levels, no intrinsic reward term, no subgoal ever selected as a target — and takes Montezuma's Revenge to a mean above 1,700,000 (150× the state of the art, above the human world record) and Pitfall to 102,571 against a field-wide `0`, with random actions as its entire explore step. The diagnosis it substitutes is that a parametric agent loses the *address* of its own frontier (detachment) and cannot reproduce a long correct prefix while exploring (derailment); both are properties of storing the frontier in weights, and both vanish when it is stored in a list. On the games FuN and option-critic report, the gap is two to three orders of magnitude.

## The control that makes this a tension rather than a confound

Go-Explore's own baseline is the load-bearing one: **count-based intrinsic motivation given the identical domain-knowledge cell representation scores 12,240 on Montezuma's Revenge against Go-Explore's 1.7M**, and finds *zero* reward on the robotics task where Go-Explore succeeds reliably. Representation and novelty statistic are held fixed; only the consumer differs — a gradient into a policy versus a sampling weight over an external archive. So position B is not "a better state abstraction wins"; it is specifically that the frontier must be *stored rather than learned*.

## Where it bites

**Whether the next architecture buys a hierarchy or a memory.** Under A, a builder adds levels, an inter-level signalling convention and a granularity parameter, and inherits `T367`'s channel question and `G33`'s unsolved discovery problem. Under B, a builder adds a non-parametric store, a state→cell map and a re-entry mechanism, and inherits a different bill: unbounded memory, a designer-authored abstraction worth 150× in score, and the requirement that a stored state can be *re-entered* — trivially true in a simulator, the central difficulty in a body.

The two also differ in what is **architecturally denied**. A decomposition is reusable across tasks and transfers by construction; an archive is per-environment, per-run, and nothing is ever abstracted out of it, so B forfeits transfer entirely. Conversely, A cannot express "go back to exactly where I was" — a policy-carried frontier has no address — which is precisely the operation B's evidence says the benchmark rewards.

**The fair-fight problem is real and cuts both ways.** Go-Explore's headline numbers assume a restorable simulator, which no hierarchical method is given. Removing it (policy-based Go-Explore) costs an order of magnitude, 1.7M → 97,728 — still ~40× FuN on the same game, but now the archive must *learn* to return, which reintroduces a goal-conditioned policy and a path of intermediate targets. At that point B's architecture contains a subgoal sequence after all: the soft-trajectory window `N_w = 10` over archived cells, with a per-leg reward of 1 and a terminal reward of 3. **(brainstorm)** The likely resolution is that the two positions converge on the policy-based variant and the real variable is *where the subgoal sequence comes from* — computed by a discovery objective (A) or read out of a stored trajectory (B) — which is a much narrower question than either literature currently asks, and is testable by replacing Go-Explore's archived path with a discovered option sequence in the same code.
