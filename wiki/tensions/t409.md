# T409 — Is a stream's structure measured by its statistical complexity `C_μ`, or only by the divergent part of its predictive information?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** For a process with a finite ε-machine, it is shown, by derivation or on a worked example, whether `C_μ` and `𝐄` are unchanged under an invertible, temporally local recoding of the observation stream (a finite-window sliding-block code). If they are unchanged, B's uniqueness argument fails for the finite class and A stands. If they change, `C_μ` is a property of the process *under a chosen observation code*, and every instrument that uses it as a target (`I48`, `G2`) must fix that code in advance.

## Position A

**`C_μ` measures structure, and it is a property of the process.** Shalizi & Crutchfield 2001 ([[wiki/concepts/causal-states.md]]). Among all partitions of the past that predict as well as the whole past, the causal-state partition is unique and has the least state entropy `C_μ = H[𝒮]`. "Without minimality, internal states could be elaborated without bound", so minimality is what makes `C_μ` belong to the process rather than to a model of it. The measure is finite and positive for finite-state processes (`C_μ = log₂p` for period `p`), zero for i.i.d. noise, and orthogonal to the entropy rate. The wiki uses it this way: as the `L0` target partition for de-aliasing (`G2`), as `G26`'s fifth selection criterion, and as instrument `I48`'s excess-memory reference `H[R] − C_μ`.

## Position B

**Only the divergent part of `I_pred` is a complexity measure; constant terms belong to the description.** Bialek, Nemenman & Tishby 2001 ([[wiki/concepts/predictive-information.md]]). Complexity must be invariant to instantaneous coordinate changes, which removes the extensive entropy. It must also be invariant to temporally local transformations, equivalently to any reference distribution built from local operators, and those change the *constant* subextensive terms. What survives is the class and coefficient (or exponent) of the divergence. The polymer case states it in one line: in `𝒩 ∼ A·N^γ·z^N`, only `γ` is lattice-independent. The paper names the excess-entropy programme directly. By restricting to finite excess entropy, it covers only the class where `I_pred` is constant, which on B's account has zero complexity. B admits that `C_μ` (Grassberger's true measure complexity) is "very similar" and is the entropy of the minimal compression keeping all of `I_pred`. It does not treat that as a measure of complexity.

## Where it bites

| | Under A | Under B |
|---|---|---|
| A known finite graph navigated passively | Has structure `C_μ > 0`, and a model can be scored on recovering it | Has complexity zero. Recovering it is synchronisation, not learning |
| `I48`'s target `H[R] − C_μ` | Well defined from `P(↔S)` | Defined only relative to the observation code. Two encodings of one environment can have two different target graphs |
| What a benchmark should report | `C_μ` of the generator | The growth class of `I_pred` over the task family, plus its coefficient |
| `G27` (where the discretisation comes from) | Independent of `G27` once the alphabet is given | Coupled to `G27`: the graph size is partly a fact about the chosen alphabet |

The two are not exclusive. A's object is one stationary process, and B's is an ensemble whose parameters are drawn per stream. Applied to B's ensemble, A's construction gives a continuum of causal states (the posterior over parameters), so `C_μ` is infinite and B's growth rate is the finer description. The dispute that remains is the **finite** class only: is its `C_μ` structure or description? The `Closes when` question decides that.

## Status

`LIVE`. Neither source computes `C_μ` under a local recoding. B's invariance claim is argued for constant subextensive terms in general, through the reference-distribution argument, not demonstrated for `C_μ` specifically. A's "property of the process" is argued from minimality at a fixed alphabet and does not address recodings.
