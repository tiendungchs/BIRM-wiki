# T9 — Are installed priors unitary modules or bundles of independently violable constraints?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A single core system is shown to lose one principle while retaining the others in the *same* subject and stimulus — the graded prediction — against an all-or-none control.

## Position A

**Unitary / all-or-none.** An entity that violates cohesion loses the continuity and persistence expectations too — infants stop expecting a non-cohesive entity to move continuously or survive occlusion (Huntley-Fenner et al. 2002). All-or-none operation is what distinguishes a core system from a graded composite (Spelke 2022)

## Position B

**Graded.** Principles dissociate: adults accept teleportation (continuity without cohesion) and remote controls (objecthood without action-on-contact); infants explore *selectively by violation type*, banging solidity-violating objects on the table (Stahl & Feigenson 2015), which a unitary system cannot produce (Revencu & Csibra 2023)

## Where it bites

Sets whether a prior is one gate or *n* gates. The graded form yields a **violation-typed prediction error** — an intrinsic-motivation signal pointing at the specific principle that broke — where the unitary form can only report "not an object"

## Status

`LEANING` — toward graded on the evidence cited, and the graded form is the better architecture either way
