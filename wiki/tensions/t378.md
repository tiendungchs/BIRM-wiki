# T378 — Does a hierarchical depth coordinate exist for association cortex, or only for the sensory streams it was defined on?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Closes when:** SLN (or any laminar depth estimator) is measured across repeated injections into two or more association areas and shown either to be consistent between injection sites — a ruler that travels — or inconsistent, at which point depth for those areas is an artefact of the sensory areas used to anchor it.

## Position A

**One hierarchy, estimable everywhere.** Markov et al. 2014 fit a beta-binomial probit model of SLN over 339 pathways and 11 macaque areas and report a hierarchy for *all* of them, correlating well with Felleman & Van Essen's, with the frontal eye field split into 8L (at the level of V4) and 8m (high). The distribution of SLN values is approximately uniform with no clustering, which is positive evidence for a continuum of levels rather than bins, and the estimated levels come with explicit ±1–2 level confidence intervals — an uncertain coordinate is still a coordinate ([[wiki/concepts/counterstream-organization.md]])

## Position B

**The ruler only travels inside the sensory streams.** In the same data, SLN values from common source areas correlate across injection pairs at a mean of 0.73 (probit) for V1, V2, V4, TEO, TEpd, MT and DP — and at or near zero for 8m, STPc and 7A. The two adjacent frontal-eye-field subdivisions 8L and 8m show **no correlation at all** in their inputs. Prefrontal areas had to be **excluded** from the weight-distance fit because they "overrun the distance and hierarchy rules". The authors' own closing position is that "the functional hierarchy is dynamic and task-dependent, with the structural hierarchy providing boundary values" — i.e. a range, not a coordinate. Independently, the highest cortical system is measured as selective-in / diffuse-out across all six laminar types ([[wiki/concepts/broadcast-hierarchy.md]]), which is what a node with no well-defined depth would look like

## Where it bites

Whether a builder gets a global depth index at all. Every architecture in the wiki that lays a scalar along cortical depth — the recurrent-gain gradient of [[wiki/concepts/timescale-hierarchy.md]], the `S_i` chain of [[wiki/concepts/predictive-coding-free-energy.md]], the level-indexed precision terms — assumes the coordinate is defined where reasoning happens. If depth is well-posed only near primary sensory cortex, those gradients are being extrapolated past their support exactly where abstraction is supposed to live, and the correct object for association cortex is an unordered set of level-matched channels rather than a stack. It also decides what "deeper" means as a design variable: a position, or only a distance to the nearest sensory anchor

## Status

`LIVE` — the disagreement is inside one dataset, which is unusual and makes it cheap to settle: the same laboratory's injections give a consistent ruler for seven posterior areas and an inconsistent one for four high-order areas, and nothing distinguishes the two groups except hierarchical position and the number of injections available. The cheap test is more injections into association cortex; the decisive one is the same measurement in a species or parcellation where association cortex is larger. Note the estimator is bounded on (0,1), so areas near the top are compressed into the tail where SLN differences become uninformative — the failure could be the instrument saturating rather than the hierarchy dissolving, which is the alternative Position A would need to demonstrate. Related but distinct from `T259`, which asks the *shape* of the hierarchy given that depth is defined; this asks whether it is defined


**A second method fails on the same areas, which is the strongest evidence yet for Position B** (Bastos et al. 2015, [[wiki/concepts/inter-areal-synchrony.md]]). A hierarchy built from interareal Granger-influence asymmetry alone — no anatomy in it — is as globally consistent as the anatomical one (86% of 28 pairs vs. 80% of 45 pathways, `p = 0.79`) and reproduces it at `R = 0.93`. Its positions for V1, V2, V4, TEO, DP and 7A are stable across three task epochs. The positions for **8L and 8m are not**: both fall to the bottom of the functional hierarchy before stimulus onset, and the anatomy–function correlation is not significant in that epoch.

So the two frontal-eye-field subdivisions whose *anatomical* depth cannot be estimated consistently between injections are also the ones whose *functional* depth moves with task. That is a reading neither position had: the ruler may be failing in frontal cortex not because the instrument saturates and not because depth is undefined, but because **there is nothing static there to measure** — a single-epoch anatomical study cannot distinguish an area with no depth from an area whose depth is a task variable. It weakens the "instrument saturating" defence of Position A, since a bounded-estimator artefact predicts nothing about task-dependence in an independent modality. It does not close the row: the cheap test named above (more injections into association cortex) still discriminates, and it now has a prediction attached — if depth is a task variable rather than absent, repeated injections should give a *reproducible* SLN spread rather than noise, since the tracing is done under one fixed (anesthetized) state.
