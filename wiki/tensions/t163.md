# T163 — Can retrieval be amortised into a geometry, or is it irreducibly a joint computation?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The cascade is built and measured: cheap index proposes, joint scorer disposes, with multi-vector late interaction placed in a theory that currently covers neither end.

## Position A

**Amortised.** Encode every stored structure once, offline, into a vector; answer a query by a coordinate comparison against the index. This is what makes schema retrieval tractable at all — median 95.5 AUROC at 0.03 s against 7.5–25.9 s for exact combinatorial search ([[wiki/concepts/subgraph-matching.md]]), and it is the shape of every content-addressed read in the wiki: attention over a cache, key–value memory, dense retrieval

## Position B

**Irreducibly joint.** Factorising the compatibility as `⟨f(q), g(x)⟩` makes the score matrix rank-`d`, so realizing all `k`-subsets of `n` items with margin `γ` requires `d ≥ log C(n,k)/log(1+1/γ)` — and the empirical requirement is ~4.5× that even for freely optimised vectors with no encoder and no generalisation demand. On a task consisting of *who likes apples?* over 46 documents, every single-vector model from 32 to 4096 dimensions fails (best R@2 54.3, none solved at R@20) while a cross-encoder scoring query and document *together* gets 100% in one forward pass (Weller et al. 2026, [[wiki/concepts/retrieval-capacity.md]])

## Where it bites

G37's whole answer set, and the wiki's default assumption that a memory read is cheap. If B holds, the cheap geometric read is a *first-stage filter* with a ceiling fixed at architecture time, and any query that composes — *X and Y*, *X but not Z* — climbs `C(n,k)` faster than any affordable `d`. It also relocates the cost question: amortisation does not remove the computation, it caps which computations are expressible

## Status

`LIVE` — the two are compatible as a cascade (cheap index proposes, joint scorer disposes), which is what deployed retrieval already does and what no memory architecture in the wiki has. What is unresolved is the middle, and it now has two occupants rather than one: multi-vector late interaction beats single vectors by 20–60 recall points on the same task with no theory covering it, and [[wiki/entities/learningmatch.md]] keeps the factorised encoders while making only the *score* un-factorised — a 5-layer head over two cached embeddings, 20 µs per pair — which escapes the rank bound at `O(n)` cheap head calls instead of `O(n)` model calls. Nothing says which queries the first stage will silently drop
