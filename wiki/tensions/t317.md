# T317 — Is gated-slot binding a different mechanism from algebraic binding, or the same operator with a one-hot role code?

**Status:** `BOTH`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A slot-gated store and a distributed-role binder are compared on the same task with the same nonlinearity and the same filler code, varying only `Ψ_R` from one-hot to distributed, and the reported differences (generalisation to unseen role–filler pairs, capacity, lesion profile) are attributed to the role code rather than to the architecture — settling whether "routing" and "binding" name one design axis or two.

## Position A

**They are distinct mechanisms and the wiki treats them as rivals.** [[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]] binds by *where* a filler is gated: a learned per-stripe write-enable routes a value to an address, and the paper's own limit is that stripes are **not** symbolic variables — each stripe must learn to encode the items it holds and every downstream layer must learn to decode *that* stripe, so an arbitrary value cannot go in an arbitrary slot. [[wiki/concepts/vector-symbolic-binding.md]] binds by *how* the filler is transformed, and its decoder is one fixed permutation shared by every reader. The wiki states the opposition directly on both pages, and it predicts a real difference: a routed slot generalises over fillers only within a learned vocabulary (10 phonemes, 5 letters), where an algebraic binder is indifferent to the filler's identity by construction.

## Position B

**They are one operator at two points on a locality spectrum** (Smolensky 1990, Defs 2.13–2.16, §2.4; [[wiki/concepts/tensor-product-representation.md]]). A tensor-product binding `Ψ_F(f) ⊗ Ψ_R(r)` with `Ψ_R` **local** is a copy of the filler pattern in a pool of units dedicated to that role — a register, i.e. a stripe. With `Ψ_R` distributed it is a vector-symbolic code. Nothing else changes: same product, same superposition, same unbinding by partial inner product. Under **linear dynamics the local, semi-local and fully distributed cases are isomorphic** — a change of basis maps them into each other. On this reading, PBWM's two acknowledged limits are not architectural facts but consequences of choosing a one-hot `Ψ_R`: readers must learn each stripe's code because the role basis is the unit basis and carries no shared structure, and a finite net admits only finitely many roles because each needs its own pool.

## Where it bites

Whether "add a gating controller" and "add a binding algebra" are alternative solutions to the same problem or complementary ones. Under **A**, a builder chooses: routing gives a learnable, credit-assignable write policy ([[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]'s per-stripe `δ_j = snr_j · δ`) and algebra gives filler-generality — and a system wanting both must run both. Under **B** the question collapses into a single design parameter, `Ψ_R`, and the interesting objects are the intermediate codes nobody builds: a *distributed* role code that is still gated, which would give a write policy over roles that are not units. It also decides how to read `G104`: if a stripe is a semi-local tensor product, then "PBWM stripes keep the constituents and require every reader to learn every stripe's code" and "tensor products keep the parts and grow exponentially" are the same objection stated twice, at opposite ends of the same axis, rather than two independent failures.

## Status

`BOTH` — B's isomorphism is real but conditional, and Smolensky states the three conditions under which it fails, all of which hold for every architecture the wiki cares about. (i) **Nonlinear units break it outright**, and PBWM's stripes are bistable by an explicit maintenance current, not linear. (ii) **Locality of damage is not preserved** by the change of basis, so lesion profiles differ. (iii) **What local learning rules can learn differs**, because the rules are written in the distinguished basis — which is precisely where PBWM's dopamine-gated per-stripe credit assignment lives, and it has no counterpart under a rotated basis. So B is right that the two are one *representational* family and wrong that they are interchangeable *systems*: the difference is not in what is represented but in what a local rule can be trained to do with it. The row stays open because the discriminating experiment — vary only `Ψ_R` at fixed everything else — is cheap and unrun, and because A's strongest claim (filler-generality) is a property of the role code's *shared structure*, which is testable without touching the gate at all.
