# T97 — Does the cortical controller hold a memory from the start, or only after consolidation?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The reviewers' own prediction is tested: experiments showing a selective remote role are re-examined for a weak recent one.

## Position A

**Only remote.** Medial prefrontal cortex is selectively engaged by memories weeks old and dispensable for the same task 1–2 days after learning: metabolic imaging at 5 vs. 25 days, greater remote dendritic spine growth, and lesion/inactivation deficits confined to remote recall across radial-arm maze, water maze, contextual fear, conditioned taste aversion, trace eyeblink and cocaine reinstatement — with the hippocampus showing the complementary profile (reviewed in Euston et al. 2012)

## Position B

**Both, with remote worse.** The same region is needed for recent navigational, object-place and fear memory learned 1–2 days earlier, and for the *consolidation* of tasks whose acquisition it is not needed for; the authors' reading is that at recent delays the controller represents the terms while the hippocampus holds the mapping, so its loss is compensable, and at remote delays it holds both (Euston et al. 2012, [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]])

**The re-examination the row asks for, run halfway** (Frankland & Bontempi 2005). Reviewing the same imaging experiments that produced the selective-remote claim: cortical regions *are* activated at recall of **recent** contextual and spatial memories — the reviewers read this as those regions participating in the early stages of consolidation — while the networks recruited at remote delays are more expansive. But inactivation at recent delays still leaves memory intact, so what the source delivers is **activation without necessity**, which is what B predicts (the controller represents the terms early and holds the mapping only later) and is not the weak *necessity* result the `Closes when` requires. Same review adds a second role that neither position accounts for: on a successful remote read the region appears to *inhibit* the fast store ([[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]])

**A second half-answer, from the consolidation channel rather than from recall** (Rothschild et al. 2017, [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]). During the sleep that follows learning — i.e. at *recent* delays, while the memory is still hippocampus-dependent — cortical ensemble patterns are not passive recipients: pre-ripple auditory-cortex activity predicts what CA1 does inside the ripple, the reverse prediction is null, and which cortical ensemble reactivates is set by cortical rather than hippocampal dynamics. Cortex is therefore **doing work at recent delays**, and instructive work rather than storage work. This is B's shape (the controller is co-active early) generalised from prefrontal cortex to a primary sensory area, and it sharpens A's problem: A must hold that a region which selects what the fast store replays is nonetheless dispensable for reading the memory it is selecting. It is still activation-without-necessity — no lesion, no clamp — so it does not meet the `Closes when` either.

## Where it bites

Whether the slow learner is written to *during* experience or only by the offline channel. Under A a machine controller can be trained purely from replay after the fact; under B it is co-active at encoding and the replay channel only completes an association it has already started, which also predicts different lesion timings. The confound neither side controls is compensation: a region silent at learning may have its function reallocated, so a null recent-memory result cannot distinguish "not involved" from "involved and substitutable"

## Status

`LEANING` (toward B, but the discriminating prediction — that experiments showing a selective remote role also show a weak recent one on closer examination — is the reviewers' own and is untested)
