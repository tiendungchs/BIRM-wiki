# T309 — Does a learned proposer beat blind enumeration, or only when the library is co-adapting?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A neurally-guided synthesiser and a matched brute-force enumerator are run at equal compute on one benchmark under two conditions — library frozen vs. library grown by the same system's compression phase — and the guidance advantage is shown to depend on which condition holds.

## Position A

**It buys a great deal.** [[wiki/entities/dreamcoder.md]] ablates the dreaming phase across eight domains and loses in every one; against 24 h/task type-directed enumeration testing up to `4·10⁸` programs per task, the full system solves more held-out tasks in mean 54.1 s. The recognition model also raises performance **at every library depth** and produces deeper libraries — so guidance is not merely a constant-factor speed-up. The mechanism is factored: library depth cuts search *depth*, the proposer cuts search *breadth*.

## Position B

**It buys nothing where it matters.** After five years on ARC-AGI-1, deep-learning-guided program synthesis and blind brute-force DSL search both land near **40%** at comparable compute budgets (Chollet et al. 2024, [[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]]); the report's own diagnosis is that the learned component is in the wrong place. [[wiki/entities/neo-neural-theorizer.md]] sharpens it: an amortised proposer holding a provably complete primitive set scores 0.019–0.038 on 6-step programs, and 1024 blind samples plus majority vote recover 0.696–0.707 at ~180× cost — the proposer is worse than sampling from itself.

## Where it bites

Whether cost 2 of [[wiki/concepts/program-induction.md]] is payable by amortisation at all, and therefore whether [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]] is a general remedy or one that requires a specific coupling. **The candidate reconciliation, and it is testable:** DreamCoder's proposer is trained on **fantasies sampled from a library it is simultaneously deepening**, so its training distribution tracks the hypothesis space it must search; every Position-B system trains a proposer against a **fixed authored DSL** and a task distribution it does not shape. If that is the whole difference, the rule is that amortisation pays only inside a generative/recognition bootstrap and never as a bolt-on ranker — which would relocate five years of ARC effort rather than merely explain it.

## Status

`LIVE`. The two sides differ on benchmark, on whether the library is learned, and on whether the proposer's training distribution is self-generated, so no direct comparison exists. Against the reconciliation: DreamCoder's domains are ones where *some* system reaches near-100%, while ARC is unsolved by everything, so guidance may simply be measurable only where the ceiling is not the bottleneck — the ablation and the null could both be reporting task difficulty
