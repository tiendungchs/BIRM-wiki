# T49 — How densely is CA3 recurrently connected — and does pattern completion need *structured* wiring or just enough of it?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** CA3 connectivity is measured in one species by both techniques, or by a method that does not truncate axons — both current numbers are lower bounds from different species.

## Position A

**Sparse and non-random.** Rat paired recordings give 0.9% pyramidal→pyramidal connectivity, with the few connections embedded in richly interconnected subnetworks showing an over-representation of disynaptic motifs; a full-scale binary model completes patterns *only because* of that non-randomness (Guzman et al. 2016). Rolls' capacity theory sits nearer this end, assuming dilution 0.04 and arguing dilution is functional ([[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]])

## Position B

**Dense and random.** Two independent techniques in mouse agree on roughly 10×: 3D electron microscopy gives 11.2 ± 2.7% within 50 µm (5.5 ± 1.9% by 250 µm), octuple patch gives 8.8% (103/1,172 pairs, 52 mice) with reciprocal pairs (6/586) and all disynaptic motifs at chance given that rate; a spiking model with log-normal weights replays sequences over a wide parameter range at `c ≈ 8.8%` with purely random connectivity (Sammons et al. 2023). Proposed causes of the discrepancy: species (rat vs mouse), and slice storage (beaker vs interface chamber)

## Where it bites

Decides whether an artificial fast store must *learn its own topology* (motif enrichment as a prerequisite for completion) or can be wired at random and rely on density — and by how much the wiki's CA3 capacity numbers are off, since `C^RC = c·N`

## Status

`LIVE` — both sides are direct measurements, neither is reproduced across species, and slicing truncates axons in both, so both are lower bounds. The EM arm is one animal; the structural distance-dependence it reports is absent functionally and is attributed to the thinner (62 µm vs 400 µm) section
