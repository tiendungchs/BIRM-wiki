# T332 — Which frontal region is the apex of the lateral-frontal control hierarchy, and does "apex" survive the choice of ordering criterion?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One ordering criterion is shown to predict the other — a lesion or stimulation result in which disrupting the region that is apical *by connectivity* impairs the abstract level that the region apical *by activation* is supposed to control, and not vice versa — or both criteria are conceded to name different objects, in which case the wiki's rostro-caudal ladder is a gradient of representational abstractness with no claim of control flow attached.

## Position A

**The frontal pole is the apex.** The Rule Abstraction model (Badre & D'Esposito) and the Information Cascade model (Koechlin) both order lateral frontal cortex caudal→rostral by the abstractness of what is controlled: premotor = stimulus–response, mid-DLPFC = abstract rule, FPl = remapping of the rules themselves and temporal control (integrating past information into future action). The lesion evidence is asymmetric in the direction the ordering predicts — premotor lesions impair concrete *and* abstract task demands, mid-DLPFC lesions impair only the abstract ones. The same gradient recurs outside rule learning: N-back sits in DLPFC (BA 9/46), prospective memory (a more abstract memorandum — an action not yet performed) sits in frontal pole; filtering actual choice options is DLPFC, decomposing high-dimensional option features is FPl ([[wiki/entities/lateral-frontal-pole.md]], [[wiki/concepts/policy-abstraction-hierarchy.md]])

## Position B

**Mid-DLPFC is the apex, and FPl is a service it calls.** If a region is higher in a control hierarchy it should impose more efferent than afferent influence. Dynamic causal modelling over fMRI in a series of studies (Nee et al.; Pitts & Nee) puts the greatest efferent-minus-afferent asymmetry at **mid-DLPFC**, not FPl — so the caudal-to-rostral gradient is not recoverable from the causal-influence data at all. Mid-DLPFC's connectivity profile is the diverse one, reaching both internally-oriented (default-mode) and externally-oriented (dorsal/ventral attention) networks, and the abstract functions attributed to FPl frequently co-activate mid-DLPFC. Badre & Nee's reading: mid-DLPFC is the domain-general controller and FPl a specialised operation it recruits — which Chau et al. 2025 then build into their FPl model as *mid-DLPFC shapes the filter weights of the decomposition*

## Where it bites

Decides the *direction of the arrow* between a machine's most abstract module and its general controller, and the two options are different architectures. Under A, abstraction and control are one axis: build a stack whose top level is both the most abstract representation and the source of top-down signal, which is what every hierarchical controller in the wiki does ([[wiki/concepts/temporal-abstraction-options.md]], and the shared-observation defect `G59` is a property of exactly this design). Under B they are orthogonal: the controller sits in the *middle* of the abstraction gradient and calls the most abstract module as a peripheral transform, which is a **service-call topology**, not a stack — the abstract module then has no authority, only a signature, and is invoked when the controller cannot decompose the input itself. The whole of [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontal-pole.md]]'s proposed model is Position B instantiated, and its central untested claim (DLPFC sets FPl's filter weights) is only coherent under B.

## Status

`LIVE`, and the two positions are **not measuring the same thing**, which is the row's real content. A's ordering is over *representational abstractness*, inferred from where activation appears as task demands are made more abstract; B's is over *effective influence*, inferred by fitting a generative model to covariance. Neither is a manipulation. A inherits the standing objection to activation-based hierarchy claims — a region that responds to abstract demands need not control anything — and B inherits dynamic causal modelling's dependence on the model space searched and on regions chosen in advance. Chau et al. 2025's own conclusion is that the disagreement is not to be resolved: multiple hierarchies coexist over one anatomy because cortex is wired as long- and short-range **loops** rather than as a layered feedforward stack, and the recovered ordering varies with the criterion and even with sensory modality (the macaque cortical hierarchy re-orders between visual and somatosensory processing). If that is right, the wiki should stop reading a control claim off a rostro-caudal activation gradient anywhere it currently does ([[wiki/concepts/broadcast-hierarchy.md]], [[wiki/concepts/effective-connectivity.md]]) — and `T259`'s question about chain-of-command versus broadcasting apex is the same question asked of the posterior predictive hierarchy, with the same criterion-dependence unaddressed.
