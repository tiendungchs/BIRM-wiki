# T263 — Does within-module connectivity move with brain state, or only between-module connectivity?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Only between.** Time-resolved graphs over 375 parcels, 92 + 92 + 152 subjects, rest and seven tasks: all 375 parcels show higher participation coefficient `B_T` in the integrated state and **zero** parcels differ in within-module degree `W_T`; no `W_T` value relates to any diffusion parameter of behaviour either (Shine et al. 2016, [[wiki/concepts/integration-segregation-balance.md]])

## Position B

**Within moves too.** Within one network, coupling *among* the regions that disengage under load falls with demand and coupling *among* the recruited outside regions rises, while only the stable core is flat (Gao et al. 2013, `N = 19`, [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether the reconfiguration surface of a modular architecture is exactly the cross-module bandwidth — in which case a single gain on inter-module edges reproduces the whole phenomenon and module internals never need touching — or whether modules also lose internal cohesion under load, which requires per-module gains and makes "module" a state-dependent object

## Status

`LIVE, probably not comparable` — the two use different objects: hard parcels with modules re-estimated per window versus three fixed functionally defined classes that are *subsets of one network*, so B's "within-class" edges may be A's between-module edges under a different partition. The cheap discriminator is to recompute A's `W_T`/`B_T` with the default network's disengaging subset forced into its own module
