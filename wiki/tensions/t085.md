# T85 — Is phasic dopamine a reward *prediction* or a reward *association*?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Prediction (temporal difference).** Phasic midbrain dopamine matches a reward prediction error qualitatively (Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997) and quantitatively (Bayer & Glimcher 2005), with the cue-onset burst produced by chaining value estimates backwards across adjacent time steps ([[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]])

## Position B

**Association (PVLV).** TD's chaining requires an unbroken sequence of predictable states; where the stimulus stream is near-unpredictable (the 1-2-AX task) the chain breaks and TD-based gating models degrade. PVLV reproduces the same firing record with two Rescorla–Wagner systems using only within-step terms: PV cancels the burst at expected reward, LV is *trained only when reward is present or expected* and is therefore free to fire at cue onset without ever being punished for it — so the anticipatory burst signals a learned association, not a prediction (O'Reilly & Frank 2006, [[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]])

## Where it bites

Any model that uses dopamine to train an internal control policy (what to store, what to attend, when to plan): TD is fine on tasks with predictable state sequences and fails exactly where the reasoning tasks live. The two accounts are behaviourally close and differ in the *unpredictable-sequence* regime, which is where the discriminating experiment is
