# T56 — What is theta-paced flickering between hippocampal maps?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A discriminator is found that separates sampling from synaptic rebound and is not confounded — the distance-travelled effect currently confounds distance with velocity at `c = 0.64`.

## Position A

**The inference algorithm.** The posterior over hidden states is represented by *sampling*: one state drawn per theta cycle, at a rate proportional to its probability, which is why maps alternate rapidly after ambiguous evidence and why switching should decline as the evidence ratio moves away from 0 (Sanders et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/hidden-state-inference-remapping.md]]; the same reading is offered for mental exploration among candidate contexts)

## Position B

**The discharge of a synaptic transient.** Two competing attractor maps with Tsodyks–Markram short-term plasticity: the abandoned map's recurrent synapses rebound (`τ_f = 1.9 s > τ_r = 0.6 s`), giving it a gain advantage that theta troughs periodically let win a cycle. Flickers decay because the rebound does, and re-arm each other, which is why the transient outlasts every time constant in the system (Mark et al. 2017, [[wiki/entities/stp-flickering-cann.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether observed map alternation is evidence that the brain represents a *distribution* over contexts, or only that attractor selection carries a recency term. The wiki's retrieval-and-allocation policy ([[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]]) leans on the first; the exportable machine design differs — sample from a posterior, versus add a decaying per-slot gain bias plus a periodic threshold dip

## Status

`LIVE`, and the primary source read directly (`raw/jezek-2011-theta-paced-flickering-between-place-cell-maps.md`, ingested after both models) moves it without settling it. **For A:** the suppression of mixed cycles *below* a per-unit shuffle null (1.25%, `p < 0.03`, tightening to `p < 0.001` late in the cycle) is the datum a sampling code predicts and a posterior-mean code forbids — though it equally fits any winner-take-all competition, so it argues for "sample, not blend" and not for "sample, not rebound". **Against B:** the original authors report flicker events **distributed across the entire recording box with no preference for running or heading direction**, having looked for spatial structure and found none — the variable B's sharpest discriminator lives on; and relapses are observed *occasionally tens of seconds* after the switch, which re-arming stretches the transient toward but does not bound. **Against A:** a 1–3% flicker rate persists before any cue switch, in a familiar environment with no ambiguity to sample over. The original argument stands underneath: the decaying switch rate is predicted by both, so it does not discriminate. Two asymmetric discriminators exist and neither is decisive: B predicts flicker rate falls with **distance travelled from the switch position** (partial `r = −0.157`, `p = 0.027`, but distance and velocity are confounded at `c = 0.64`) and A has no mechanism for that; B's own control shows a *noise-driven* network without short-term plasticity flickers at a **constant** rate with no dependence on teleportation, which rules out generic noise but not a sampler. A also has the wider brief — it explains flickering between maps that were never recently active, which a rebound cannot
