# T37 — Is grid-like coding a discovery or a detection artefact?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A single-unit grid representation is recorded in a conceptual space in human or monkey — which nobody has, and which is what both positions turn on.

## Position A

**A discovery, and near-universal.** Grid, place and head-direction cells recorded in rat primary somatosensory (S1 hindlimb) and secondary visual (V2M) cortex, surviving whisker trimming and darkness; grid-like coding of gaze in monkey entorhinal cortex without locomotion; hexadirectional BOLD in orbitofrontal, ventromedial prefrontal and cingulate cortex over concept, word and discrete social-hierarchy spaces (Chen et al. 2022, [[wiki/concepts/distributed-reference-frames.md]]). Qu et al. 2026 answer part of the detector objection without leaving fMRI: in 203 subjects, **three independent estimators** (hexagonal modulation, cross-run angle consistency, angular-difference RSA) converge on the same entorhinal cluster, the effect is six-fold only, it survives an entorhinal signal-to-noise control and a distance-per-angular-bin control, and the estimated orientation `φ` transfers to a *different* map learned afterwards — a detector returning noise should not do any of these. And the detector was not assumed into existence: Doeller et al. 2010 measured its three linking assumptions in rat entorhinal cortex in the same paper that introduced it — conjunctive grid cells' preferred firing directions are aligned to their own grid axes (mean 3.15°, `P` < 0.001) and gridness is higher during fast movement (113 cells, `P` = 2.2×10⁻¹¹) — then built a detector with adjacent-periodicity nulls (4-, 5-, 7-, 8-fold), a predicted speed gate, `φ` random across subjects, split-half estimation, and two independent readouts (activation and 60°-shifted adaptation) converging on the same voxels ([[wiki/concepts/hexadirectional-signal.md]])

## Position B

**The detector is not trustworthy, per the same paper.** Identifying grid patterns is "a relatively arbitrary threshold phenomenon"; the standard spatial-autocorrelation method generates false positives without spike-shuffle controls; fMRI BOLD does not measure spiking, so hexadirectional modulation cannot be read as a grid population; and **no single-unit grid representation in a conceptual space has ever been recorded** in any species. Patterns are also *missed* when grid scale mismatches enclosure size, so the false-negative rate is unknown too. **And the validation above does not travel.** Links 2 and 3 were measured over a foraging box with real self-motion; no conjunctive-alignment result and no speed-gating result exists for a conceptual space, where "direction" is an angle in a designed feature plane and "speed" has no referent. So the abstract-grid literature inherits a linking chain earned in a domain it does not occupy — which is the sharper form of the objection than "the detector is unvalidated"

## Where it bites

Every "grids outside space" claim the wiki carries ([[wiki/concepts/abstract-structural-codes.md]] T4, the beyond-space table of [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]]). If B, the case that `g` is periodic rests entirely on an aggregate signal with an unvalidated detector, and the biological warrant for importing a periodic code is weaker than the wiki has been treating it. Generalises: the same objection applies to any pattern-score threshold used as evidence that a code exists

## Status

`LIVE` — the two positions are the same author's, and what would settle it is human or monkey single-unit recording in a conceptual space, which nobody has. A cheaper partial test exists and nobody has run it: if the same population mechanism runs off the plane, a conceptual task with a manipulable traversal rate should reproduce the speed gating that the spatial instrument depends on
