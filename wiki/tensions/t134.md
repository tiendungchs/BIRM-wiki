# T134 — Does serotonin set the prediction horizon or report the average reward?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The stated experiment is run: serotonergic effect on dopamine activity where predicted future reward is positive versus negative — facilitation-then-inhibition against inhibition in both.

## Position A

**The discount factor `γ`.** Serotonergic depletion makes rats take the small immediate over the large delayed reward (Mobini et al. 2000), low serotonin tracks impulsivity, and depression is recovered as a short-horizon regime in which a cost-then-reward policy scores below doing nothing (Doya 2002, [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]]).

## Position B

**The predicted average reward** in average-reward reinforcement learning, which replaces `(1−γ)V(s)` in the temporal-difference error and therefore makes serotonin a straightforward *opponent* of dopamine (Daw, Kakade & Dayan 2002).

## Where it bites

The two predict opposite signs of the serotonin→dopamine interaction, and only under one of them is the horizon a settable parameter at all — under B the wiki has no candidate substrate for `γ`.

## Status

`LIVE` — with a stated discriminating experiment nobody in the wiki has run: measure the serotonergic effect on dopamine activity in conditions where predicted future reward `V(s)` is positive versus negative. A predicts facilitation then inhibition; B predicts inhibition in both
