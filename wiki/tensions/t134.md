# T134 — Does serotonin set the prediction horizon or report the average reward?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The stated experiment is run: serotonergic effect on dopamine activity where predicted future reward is positive versus negative — facilitation-then-inhibition against inhibition in both.

## Position A

**The discount factor `γ`.** Serotonergic depletion makes rats take the small immediate over the large delayed reward (Mobini et al. 2000), low serotonin tracks impulsivity, and depression is recovered as a short-horizon regime in which a cost-then-reward policy scores below doing nothing (Doya 2002, [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]]).

## Position B

**The predicted average reward** in average-reward reinforcement learning, which replaces `(1−γ)V(s)` in the temporal-difference error and therefore makes serotonin a straightforward *opponent* of dopamine (Daw, Kakade & Dayan 2002).

**Withdrawn as stated, by one of its own authors.** Boureau & Dayan 2010 ([[wiki/concepts/affective-opponency.md]]) retract the 2002 tonic assignment in both directions: tonic dopamine reports the average rate of reward *and of controllable punishment* as an opportunity **cost** for time (⇒ vigour), and tonic serotonin reports average **punishment** as an opportunity **benefit** for time (⇒ quiescence). So B's sign is flipped — serotonin is no longer the average-*reward* signal, which was the property that made it a clean opponent of dopamine. Three stated reasons: the 2002 model had no Pavlovian controller, it ignored the reward/punishment asymmetry (learning yields *repeated* reward but *avoided* punishment), and it is challenged by designs that orthogonalize valence against action. The algebra survives — the phasic error is still antagonized by the long-run rate — but the authors note that antagonism "could be realized in many other ways, for instance by a form of adaptation", so it no longer forces a chemical carrier at all.

## Where it bites

The two predict opposite signs of the serotonin→dopamine interaction, and only under one of them is the horizon a settable parameter at all — under B the wiki has no candidate substrate for `γ`.

## Status

`LIVE` — B's serotonin-as-average-*reward* form is withdrawn by its own author (see Position B), which removes B's main attraction without touching A; what remains of B is serotonin as average *punishment*, still an opponent but of the vigour signal rather than of the reward error. Boureau & Dayan also call A "hard to integrate" with their account, offering only "a different function of a separate group of 5-HT neurons" — an appeal to topography, which is the move that settled the analogous dopamine dispute and which nothing here has tested for the raphe. Two riders on any adjudication: the depletion and lesion evidence A rests on moves **tonic** levels while the claim is about the horizon term, and `G116` says none of the cited designs separates valence from required action. The discriminating experiment nobody in the wiki has run is unchanged: measure the serotonergic effect on dopamine activity in conditions where predicted future reward `V(s)` is positive versus negative. A predicts facilitation then inhibition; B predicts inhibition in both
