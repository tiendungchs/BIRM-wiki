# T61 — Are the patterns a fast store holds random, or orthogonalised?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Both write rules are run on one matrix and their stored patterns compared for mutual orthogonality at matched capacity.

## Position A

**Random.** Every capacity result the wiki carries assumes i.i.d. patterns written by a Hebbian rule — `p_max ≈ kC/(a ln(1/a))` ([[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]]), the `s`-context gating multipliers ([[wiki/entities/context-modular-memory-network.md]]), the sequence bounds ([[wiki/entities/dense-sequence-memory.md]]) — and randomness is defended as a feature, since it needs no statistics of the input (T55)

## Position B

**Orthogonalised.** Free-energy minimisation writes with `ΔJ_ij ∝ σ_iσ_j − L(·)σ_j`, whose anti-Hebbian term subtracts what the network already predicts, driving stored states toward mutual orthogonality — a Kanter–Sompolinsky projector network with maximal capacity and error-free recall. Human functional connectomes show the predicted signature: attractors aligned with the eigenvectors of their own coupling matrix ([[wiki/entities/fcann.md]])

## Where it bites

Every capacity number in the wiki. Random-pattern bounds are the *pessimistic* case; if a local rule can orthogonalise, the ceiling moves and the separation/completion knob (G38) is partly redundant — orthogonality is bought by the learning rule rather than by sparsifying the code. Caveats on B: the rule is never actually run in the empirical work (orthogonality is inferred from a signature in fitted weights), the demonstration is at 122 coarse-grained macroscopic units rather than at neurons, and Chaudhry et al. 2023 report that the batch pseudoinverse route to the same decorrelation is non-local and numerically unstable
