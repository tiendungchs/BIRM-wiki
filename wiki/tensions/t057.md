# T57 — How should a memory store a sequence — the successor state, or the increment that generates it?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A Polynomial or Exponential DenseNet is run on Vector-HaSH's benchmark, or Vector-HaSH on the dense-sequence benchmark — no published comparison exists.

## Position A

**Store the increment.** Chaining states costs `O(N)` bits of recurrent storage per step and empirically caps out at ≈50 steps for an asymmetric Hopfield network and ≈100 with a scaffold; storing the 2-D velocity on a prestructured manifold instead reaches ~1.5×10⁵ steps, with recall length degrading only as the *log* of the number of admissible shift vectors, because the manifold's own dynamics regenerate the state (Chandra et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/vector-hash.md]])

## Position B

**The ≈50-step ceiling is an artefact of the interaction function, not of state-chaining.** Keep the asymmetric weights and pass the state–pattern overlap through a steep nonlinearity before it drives the next state: capacity becomes polynomial in `N` at `f(x)=x^d` and exponential at `f(x)=e^{(N−1)(x−1)}`, with 200,000 correlated MovingMNIST frames recalled perfectly at `N=784` (Chaudhry et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/dense-sequence-memory.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a sequence memory needs an action algebra on its latent (hence a path-integrable code, [[wiki/concepts/path-integration.md]]) or only a sharper read-out. The two costs are not comparable as stated: A pays `log|actions|` bits/step but requires the latent to have a group structure and cannot store a step that is not an admissible shift; B pays `O(N)` bits/step but stores arbitrary successors, at the price of one hidden unit per stored pattern in its biologically plausible form — so B's exponential scaling is exponential in the *visible* count only.
