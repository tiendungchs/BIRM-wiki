# T410 — Does a coarse level that predicts its own future (Markovian macrostates) certify a self-contained level, or must the micro level also add nothing (information closure)?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A procedure is derived, or shown impossible, that certifies information closure `I(←X; →Z | ←Z) = 0` using only what an agent holding `Z` has: its own observation stream plus its own interventions on `Z`. If such a test exists, the wiki's stopping test is replaced by it. If none can exist, every stream-only level test in the wiki (`G27`'s stopping test, the event-segmentation scoring rule) is relabelled as certifying predictive completeness *relative to the observer*, not self-containment.

## Position A

**A level is finished, and self-contained, when its states are their own causal states.** Shalizi & Moore 2003 ([[wiki/concepts/emergent-macrostates.md]]). Refine the observation partition until the macrostates are Markovian: then `I[→S ; ←S | current node] = 0`, and the end point is "the minimal variable set that predicts the chosen observables optimally and is causally complete". The test is computed from the observed stream with no threshold. The wiki uses it as `G27`'s stopping test, and on [[wiki/concepts/independent-causal-mechanisms.md]] as "a stated sufficient condition for coarse variables to admit self-contained dynamics".

## Position B

**Markovianity of the coarse level is neither sufficient nor necessary; self-containment is information closure, which conditions on the micro past.** Rosas et al. 2024 ([[wiki/concepts/computational-closure.md]]). Counterexample 4 (after Pfante et al. 2014) has a 4-state chain whose coarse-graining is i.i.d., hence Markov and trivially computationally closed. Yet the micro state determines the next macro symbol: `I(←X; Z_{t+1} | ←Z) = 1` bit. Counterexample 6 has an informationally closed level that is second-order, not first-order, Markov. Self-containment is causal closure (`ε`-machine of `Z` = υ-machine of `Z`), equivalent to information closure (Thm 1), and for first-order Markov `X` it equals **strong** lumpability (Prop. 4). Markovianity of `Z` under the stationary distribution is only weak lumpability.

## Where it bites

| | Under A | Under B |
|---|---|---|
| `G27`'s stopping test | Certifies a finished, self-contained node set | Certifies only that no *macro* history is missing; a node set can pass and still be driven by unobserved micro detail |
| Denying the upper level access to the lower | Free once the upper level is Markov | Free only under information closure; the price otherwise is `I(←X; →Z \| ←Z)` |
| A learned abstraction verified on-policy | Valid if Markov on the data | May be weakly lumpable only, and fails when the behaviour policy moves the state distribution (`G63`) |
| What a stream-only agent can claim | Self-containment | Predictive completeness relative to its own observables, nothing more |

The two positions answer different questions. A's object is the best predictor of `Z` *from `Z`*. B's is whether anything *below* `Z` still matters to it. A's "causally complete" claims B's property, and B proves that the stream-only test cannot deliver it: in CE4 no statistic of `Z` distinguishes the closed i.i.d. case from the micro-determined one.

## Status

`LEANING` toward B. The counterexamples are exact, so as a certification of *self-containment* A's test fails. What stays open is the instrument question in `Closes when`. B's test needs the micro past, which a passive agent at level `Z` never has. Whether interventions on `Z` can stand in for it is not addressed by either source.
