# T174 — Does the recurrent connectivity of a fixed-dynamics sequence model carry expressive power?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The grown-topology advantage is shown to survive against a single-cycle reservoir with a tuned `λ`, which the universality construction says should match it within the fading-memory class.

## Position A

**Yes — a random fixed reservoir is "the antithesis of the optimal", and growing the right graph is what pays.** [[wiki/entities/hag-reservoir.md]] starts from `W = 0` and grows edges by long-window correlation; the grown reservoirs beat every static and locally-tuned baseline across five classification benchmarks, drive mean pairwise correlation from 0.994 to 0.476 on the hardest task, and lift effective dimensionality from 1.0 to 13.0. The stated premise of the whole plasticity-in-reservoirs literature (IP, Oja, Anti-Oja, BCM) is that *which units connect to which* is a lever worth pulling. **The oldest datum on side A is also the tightest controlled one, and it is fixed-`n`:** in the original liquid state machine the liquid is 135 integrate-and-fire neurons in every condition, and only the connection-probability kernel `C·exp(−(D/λ)²)` changes — `λ = 0` (no recurrence) classifies barely above chance, `λ ≈ 2` (mostly local plus a few long-range) is optimal, and large `λ` homogenises the circuit into quasi-chaos where state distance grows as fast for jittered copies of one input as for genuinely different ones. Two further within-class effects at fixed neuron count: replacing dynamic synapses with static ones at matched firing rate destroys the classification of every input segment except the last, and **four uninterconnected columns at `λ = 2` beat one column at `λ = 8`** across input jitter 0–16 ms, because replication raises between-class separation without raising noise sensitivity while density raises both (Maass, Natschläger & Markram 2002, [[wiki/entities/liquid-state-machine.md]]). B does not contradict any of this — its claim is about representable functions, not about performance at a given `n` — which is exactly why the row is decided by whichever quantity `λ`-sweeps are moving, and nothing measures it.

## Position B

**No — not within the fading-memory class.** Li, Fong & Tiňo 2024 prove constructively that a **single ring with one shared weight `λ` and `±1` (or `±1, ±i`) input signs** approximates any linear reservoir system, hence any time-invariant fading-memory filter over uniformly bounded inputs, to arbitrary `ε` ([[wiki/entities/simple-cycle-reservoir.md]], Thm 22). Three visibly different constrained families — C-SCR, SMCR, Twin SCR — are *all* universal with one tunable recurrent parameter each. Any topology reachable is reachable from the ring, paid for in state dimension `n' ≈ (N+1)·n·k`, `k > nm`.

## Where it bites

Every argument in the wiki of the form "this recurrent topology is the right one because it can express X". If B, that argument is empty for every `X` inside the fading-memory class, and architecture must be defended on conditioning, dimension-at-fixed-`ε`, sample efficiency or class membership instead — which are exactly the quantities neither paper measures. **The two are not yet in formal contradiction, for three reasons.** (i) A's dynamics are `tanh`-nonlinear, B's theorems are for linear state updates only. (ii) B is existential in `n'` with no lower bound and an admittedly loose `k > nm`, so "expressively equal" is compatible with "wildly different at the `n ≈ 500` A actually runs". (iii) A's best variant reaches `ρ(W) = 2.89`, i.e. **outside** the contractive class B's theorem covers — so A's largest gains may come from leaving the class rather than from rewiring within it. The discriminating experiment: construct B's C-SCR approximant of a fitted reservoir and compare it to A's grown network at matched `n` and matched `ρ`. Unrun. **Update (Gu, Goel & Ré 2022, [[wiki/entities/s4.md]]) — the missing quantity now has a name and a measurement, and it is neither topology nor width.** S4 is B's object exactly (linear contractive state update, all nonlinearity outside the recurrence) and it reports that *which* matrix, at fixed topology and fixed `n`, is worth **more than 15 points of validation accuracy** and the difference between 50% and 96.35% on a 16,384-step task — while every variant reaches 100% training accuracy. So B's "topology is free" and A's "connectivity is a lever" are both compatible with the decisive variable being the **spectrum of `A` and its conditioning**: the matrices that work (HiPPO) are maximally *non-normal*, with a diagonaliser whose entries reach `2^{4N/3}`, i.e. exactly the ones B's unitary-dilation route conjugates away. Neither A nor B measures conditioning; S4's Lemma 3.2 is the wiki's only number on it.
