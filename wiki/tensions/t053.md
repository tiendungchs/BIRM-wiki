# T53 — Is orthogonalization of aliased states a requirement, or a free choice the brain happens to make?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**A requirement, and the brain's own solution.** Mouse dorsal CA1 drives visually identical, latently distinct task states to near-orthogonality (population-vector angle → 90°) over weeks of learning, in a fixed order, and the wiki's whole capacity argument runs on sparse orthogonal codes being what memory and intelligence need ([[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]], Sun et al. 2025)

## Position B

**A free choice.** On the identical symbol stream, ReLU and sigmoid recurrent networks, long short-term memory networks and transformers reach full next-observation accuracy with their aliased states still strongly correlated — perfect performance only needs separability in the low-dimensional subspace the readout uses, and every other direction is unconstrained. Orthogonality appears only when it is *imposed*: soft winner-take-all activation, feedback inhibition, or an explicit correlation penalty in the loss (Sun et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/cscg.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether decorrelation goes into the objective slot (G30) as a term to be paid for, or is expected to fall out of prediction. If B, then behavioural success — including out-of-distribution success — is not evidence that a model split the nodes, which adds a class of false positive to G17

## Status

`LEANING B` on the mechanism, `A` on the normative question — B is a controlled comparison of six architectures on one task; what B does *not* explain is why biology pays for the extra property, and the candidate answers (memory capacity, interference, reusability of states across task variants) are untested here
