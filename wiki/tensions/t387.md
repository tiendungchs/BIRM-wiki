# T387 — Is a visual pointer assigned bottom-up by the stimulus, or bound top-down by the program that needs a variable?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The *same* display is presented under two different goals and the **set of individuated items** is measured to differ — not what is reported about them, but which items are tracked. The cheap form: multiple-object tracking in which the to-be-tracked set is left to the task rather than cued, with the goal switched mid-trial.

## Position A

**Bottom-up. The stimulus assigns the pointer; the program inherits it.** Ullman's **indexing** operation shifts processing to an *odd-man-out* — a location differing from its surround in one **pre-computed** property — so the anchor where a routine starts is chosen by local surprise, not by content or goal: constant-time pop-out for colour and simple shape up to ~30 distractors, with conjunctions not indexable at all (linear search; Treisman & Gelade 1980), and the indexable list fixed by early vision (orientation, colour, motion, disparity, terminators). Pylyshyn's FINSTs are stated by Ballard to be "a product of bottom-up processing" (Trick & Pylyshyn 1996) — 4–6 items individuated and tracked *before* any conceptual description, which is what makes them available to a program rather than produced by one. ([[wiki/concepts/visual-routines.md]]; primary Pylyshyn source queued at `raw/pylyshyn-2001-visual-indexes-preconceptual-objects.md`.)

## Position B

**Top-down. A pointer is a variable in a behavioural program and is bound when that program's current step needs one.** Ballard et al. 1997 state the disagreement in their own words — "deictic pointers are required for variables in a cognitive 'top-down' program", and "therein lies the crucial difference" from Pylyshyn's. Their evidence is that the *same* stimulus at the *same* fixated location yields **different bound values at different points in one task**: in block-copying, one model fixation acquires the next block's colour and a later fixation to the same area acquires its relative offset. The saccade-contingent colour change is the direct measurement — changing a block's colour costs **+50 ms** when the subject has not yet bound colour and **+129 ms** when they have, i.e. what is held across a saccade is set by task phase and not by the display, which was identical in both conditions. And the search region itself is a *stored task template* (`T_os` mapped through `T_sr`) rather than a property of the image. ([[wiki/concepts/active-vision.md]])

## Where it bites

**What a machine parser computes, and how often.** Under A, objectness is a property of the input: compute a saliency or segmentation map once per image and hand the result downstream — which is what [[wiki/entities/spelkenet.md]] does (a counterfactual probe run once) and what every fixed parse rule in the wiki assumes. Under B there is no such map to compute, because the individuated set is a function of the current query, and a parse computed before the goal is known is computing the wrong thing. This is exactly the **query-relativity** requirement `G75` records as never tested: the same input under two goals must yield two different retained structures. It also decides whether the wiki's saliency-map machinery ([[wiki/concepts/priority-map.md]]) is a *selector* the program reads or a *register* the program writes — the two are indistinguishable in every measurement currently on that page, because the goal is instructed and held constant throughout.

## Status

`LIVE`. Neither source runs the discriminating test, and each is measuring a different thing: A's evidence is about *where attention goes first* with no task in play (pop-out latency, tracking under a cued set), B's is about *what is retained* once a task is running. The obvious reconciliation — indexing proposes a small candidate set bottom-up, the program binds among them top-down, two stages rather than two theories — is stated by neither and is not free: it predicts that a task-relevant item which is *not* an odd-man-out cannot be bound without a serial scan, which is testable and unrun. Ballard's own response leans B further by nominating the hippocampus as the source of pointers to regions that are not currently foveated (`L4`, no direct evidence). Expect the Pylyshyn primary source to sharpen A rather than settle the row.
