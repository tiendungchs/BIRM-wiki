# T80 — Can a population run a stored sequence backwards?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Hippocampal reverse replay is attempted under volitional BCI control, or M1 is examined for spontaneous reverse sequences offline — neither has been run.

## Position A

**Yes — reverse traversal is a normal mode.** Hippocampal place-cell sequences replay both forward and reverse after experience, CA3's symmetric spike-timing kernel lets one weight matrix generate both directions, and the forward:reverse mixture is used as a knob that sets whether the consolidated map stores the policy or the geometry ([[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]])

## Position B

**No — the flow is directional and volition cannot flip it.** Macaques driving a BCI cursor from ~90 M1 units fail, across sessions and under a corridor constraint that makes it the only rewarded solution, to emit an existing neural trajectory in time-reversed order; the two directions of cursor movement turn out to use *distinct* neural trajectories rather than one path traversed both ways (`raw/talk-nd-brain-learning-limits.txt`, **(tentative)** — talk only, no paper read)

## Where it bites

Decides whether a model may treat a stored sequence as reversible for free. If B generalises, an asymmetric-recurrence sequence generator cannot serve backward credit propagation, and reverse traversal needs its own machinery — which is exactly what [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]] uses reverse replay *for*

## Status

`LIVE` — the two are not yet a contradiction: different areas (hippocampus vs M1), different regimes (spontaneous offline generation vs online volitional control), different timescales (compressed ripple vs behavioural). The discriminating test is whether *hippocampal* reverse replay is achievable under volitional BCI control, or whether M1 shows spontaneous reverse sequences offline — neither is run
