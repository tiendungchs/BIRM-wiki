# T204 — Does test-time compute buy generalization on ARC, or does it only buy the tail?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The compute curve is measured over more than one order of magnitude for a second method, establishing whether the +1.8-points-per-9.5× exponent is method-specific or general.

## Position A

**Compute is nearly irrelevant at the current frontier.** The two 2024 ARC Prize leaderboards differ by ~1,000× in budget per entry — Kaggle allows one P100 for 12 hours with no internet (~$10 of compute), ARC-AGI-Pub allows up to $10,000 in API credits — and their top scores landed 0.1 points apart (53.5% private vs. 53.6% semi-private). The report's own conclusion: *"algorithmic improvements towards AGI hold significant power and massive compute may not be necessary"*, reinforced by OmniARC reaching 40% with a 0.5B model inside the Kaggle budget ([[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]])

## Position B

**Compute is the whole remaining gap, at a brutal exponent.** The same report estimates that Greenblatt's LLM-program-generation method would reach the 85% target at ~10⁸ generated-and-evaluated programs per task — a multi-million-dollar bill for 100 tasks — and states that a score can no longer be assigned to an approach, only to an approach *plus* a budget. The December 2024 o3-preview result is the same shape: 75% at low compute, ~87% at high, at ~$30,000/task ([[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides which lever a builder pulls, and whether the wiki's benchmark rows need an inference-budget column or a compute-efficiency *score*. The two claims are formally compatible — the frontier is algorithm-limited while the tail is compute-limited — but only if the returns curve is flat then steep, which nobody has measured. If instead the 1,000× null reflects a *ceiling of the current approach class*, then both leaderboards are measuring the same missing mechanism and neither compute nor the next increment of engineering will move it

## Status

`LIVE`, but the curve now has its first segment. [[wiki/entities/poe-arc-solver.md]] sweeps one fixed method's inference budget over ~1 order of magnitude on ARC-AGI-1 public eval: DFS threshold `T` = 20% / 9% / 0.5% costs 14:12 / 20:50 / **134:43** hours and scores 70.0% / 71.6% / **71.8%** — 9.5× compute for **+1.8 points**, and the last 6.5× for +0.2, with candidate *coverage* still rising (73.5 → 76.0 → 83.5). So within one approach the returns are flat, and the reason is identified: the extra compute buys proposals a selector cannot use. That supports the left-hand column at the frontier and refines the right-hand one — the tail may be selector-limited rather than compute-limited. Still unrun: the same sweep over four orders of magnitude, and any sweep at all on ARC-AGI-2
