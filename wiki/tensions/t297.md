# T297 — Is ordinary supervised learning causal or anticausal?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** For a labelled benchmark, `P(Y|X)` is measured across environments constructed by intervention on the input-generating process, and either shown stable (causal) or shown to shift (anticausal) — with the annotation protocol's determinism reported alongside, since that is the variable the two positions disagree about.

## Position A

**Anticausal (Schölkopf et al. 2012).** `P(Y^e|X^e)` is invariant across environments when `X` *causes* `Y`, and varies when `X` is an *effect* of `Y`. MNIST classification is anticausal: the pixels are an effect of the mental concept that led the writer to draw the digit. On that reading most perceptual classification has an unstable conditional and ERM has no principled claim on it.

## Position B

**Causal (Arjovsky et al. 2019, concluding dialogue).** Most supervised learning predicts *human annotations* `Y^e` from pixels `X^e`, imitating a cognitive process that runs in the causal direction; annotation protocols deliberately use multiple annotators to make `P(Y^e|X^e)` near-deterministic. If the annotation process is deterministic and shared across environments, the conditional expectation is invariant by construction and ERM is expected to succeed.

## Where it bites

Directly on `G6` and `G16`, and on how much of this wiki's shortcut machinery is needed. Under Position B, ERM's empirical success on annotation-prediction is *explained* rather than lucky, and the failures reduce to three separately-nameable causes (disjoint `P(X^e)` supports, insufficient data, insufficient capacity) — invariance methods would then be a targeted fix for those three, not a replacement for ERM. Under Position A the conditional is unstable in the common case and every i.i.d.-trained perceptual classifier is on borrowed time.

The split also predicts *where* invariance methods should pay: both positions agree the conditional is unstable when the label is not created from the input — an X-ray image with a biopsy result as the target, self-supervised prediction of parts of the input from other parts, and unsupervised recovery of the causal factors behind `X^e` (which inverts the generative process outright). Those are the cases the wiki cares most about, so the tension may bound the *scope* of Position B rather than defeat it.

## A second, cheaper discriminator — and evidence on Position A's side

Schölkopf et al. 2021 (`raw/scholkopf-2021-toward-causal-representation-learning.md`) restate Position A and attach a consequence that is measurable **without constructing environments at all**: by the Independent Causal Mechanisms principle ([[wiki/concepts/independent-causal-mechanisms.md]]), if `X → Y` then `P(X)` carries no information about `P(Y|X)`, so **semi-supervised learning must be futile in the causal direction**; in the anticausal direction the dependence between `P(X)` and `P(Y|X)` is provably strictly positive (Daniušis et al. 2010), so it may help. The test is therefore: *does unlabelled data improve this task at all?* — one ablation on an existing pipeline, no environment partition, no intervention.

Three things this adds:

- **Empirical corroboration, on Position A's side.** Schölkopf et al. 2012 measured the predicted asymmetry across real cause–effect datasets and found it. That is evidence that the causal/anticausal split is a real property of learning problems, though not yet evidence about the annotation-prediction case Position B is specifically about.
- **The standard SSL justifications are anticausal assumptions in disguise.** The cluster assumption, low-density separation, and semi-supervised smoothness each assert that `P(X)` constrains `P(Y|X)`; the co-training theorem assumes predictors conditionally independent given the label, which holds when the label *causes* the predictors. So the field's own working assumptions presuppose Position A wherever SSL is used and works.
- **It sharpens the scope reading in the section above rather than settling it.** Position B's claim is narrow — annotation-prediction with a deterministic protocol — and that is precisely the regime where SSL should be useless. A finding that SSL helps on a benchmark is then evidence the benchmark is *not* the case Position B describes.

## Status

`LIVE — stated as a disagreement, never measured.` Both positions are asserted in the source dialogue with no experiment separating them, and each is consistent with the reported Colored MNIST result (there the shift is manufactured by the experimenter, so the annotation-determinism argument never engages). The discriminating measurement is cheap and is stated in `Closes when`; nothing in the wiki has run it. A second, cheaper proxy (does unlabelled data help?) is now on the row with corroborating evidence for Position A's asymmetry in general, but not for the annotation-prediction case the two positions actually dispute — so the row stays `LIVE`.
