# T322 — Does the ESBN's out-of-distribution generalisation come from its binding mechanism, or from normalising each problem's embeddings against itself?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The ESBN is run on same/different at `m = 98` with the two-stream binding intact and TCN replaced by a normalisation that carries no within-problem information (batch norm, layer norm, or none plus a matched learning-rate search), and either recovers near-ceiling accuracy — binding is sufficient — or does not — the two are jointly necessary and the architectural claim is over-attributed.

## Position A

**The binding mechanism does the work.** The paper's argument is architectural and its three analyses support it: the controller has no input line from the encoder, the keys it writes are entity-independent and overlap completely between train and test entities (PCA, `m = 95`), and the whole result survives replacing the convolutional encoder with an **untrained random projection** (`p = 0.13`) while the Transformer's does not (`t = −4.0`, `p = 0.001`). Encoder-invariance is the hard part of the claim and TCN cannot supply it: normalising a sequence of embeddings does not tell you which rule to apply. Every competing model also has TCN, and every one of them still has a cliff at `m ≥ 95`.

## Position B

**TCN is a precondition, not a detail, and the paper does not price it.** Table 9: **ESBN without TCN scores 50.0 ± 0.02 on same/different at every `m`, including `m = 0`** — chance, in the easiest regime, on the simplest task — while a plain LSTM without TCN scores 88.2 and the Transformer 100.0. The model whose headline is that binding is the necessary ingredient is the model that fails hardest when a normalisation layer is removed. On distribution-of-three the without-TCN ESBN falls from 99.7 to 62.0 at `m = 95`. TCN is not neutral machinery: it normalises over the *problem's own* window, so it injects exactly the relational information (each entity's embedding relative to the others in this problem) that the tasks ask about — it is a within-episode re-representation, which is why it is also the wiki's best partial answer to `G94`. On this reading the result is a conjunction, and the source states the mechanism half only.

## Where it bites

Whether a builder copying this design copies one thing or two. Under **A**, the transferable primitive is "cut the controller's access to the values" and any normalisation will do. Under **B**, the ESBN's controller is *starved* by construction — its only input is a retrieved key — so it has no way to recover a scale it was not handed, and the two-stream cut and the within-window normalisation are complementary halves of the same fix: the wall removes entity identity, TCN restores the relational content the wall would otherwise have removed with it. B also predicts a dependency that A does not: the more strictly the streams are separated, the more the model should depend on TCN — testable by the regularised-division variant the paper names as future work.

## Status

`LIVE` — both positions cite the same source's own tables, and the source's discussion never addresses the interaction, reporting TCN as a uniform improvement for all models while its own numbers show the improvement is 50 points for one model and 2 for others.
