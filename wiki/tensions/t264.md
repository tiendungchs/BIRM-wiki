# T264 — Is default-mode anticorrelation a neural phenomenon or a product of global signal regression?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Anticorrelation magnitude is reported with *and* without global signal regression on the same data and regions — no source currently does.

## Position A

**Substantially manufactured.** Global-signal regression mean-centres the correlation distribution and therefore *forces* negative values to exist; the anchor review states the caveat about its own anticorrelation result and leaves the magnitude without GSR unknown (Buckner et al. 2008); the connectome-methods review lists GSR among rejected practices because it removes task-related neural signal and "spuriously inflates anticorrelations" (Bijsterbosch et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]); and Gao et al. 2013's own check finds all values shift positive without it, with several rest-state core↔salience correlations losing their negative sign. Fox et al. 2009 supply A's strongest same-data fact against their own conclusion: **before any nuisance regression every seed correlates positively with essentially every voxel**, and at the a priori seeds no significant negative correlation exists without global correction

## Position B

**Neural, and GSR is what makes it detectable.** The global signal is a composite of neural and non-neural sources; removal increases anticorrelations by virtue of mean-centering rather than by fabricating structure, and effects that are not reproducible without GSR "may not be analysis artifacts in as much as they reflect neural features whose detection benefits from removal of shared trends" — with a TMS meta-analysis finding subgenual-ACC connectivity↔treatment-response associations carried specifically by patients with large global-signal fluctuations. Recommended practice: report both, especially for anticorrelation research (Tripathi et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]).

**Primary for B, with the same-data comparison this row asked for** (Fox, Zhang, Snyder & Raichle 2009, J Neurophysiol 101:3270, `n = 17`, 4 × 7 min fixation; [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]). The authors concede the algebra (any global correction makes the voxelwise β map sum to zero, so negatives are mandated per subject) and test what the algebra does *not* fix:

| What GSR does not mandate | Result |
|---|---|
| **Where** the negatives fall | After GSR: seed-specific, on the system with the opposite task response. After post hoc distribution centring (same zero mean): in white matter, the same for every seed — the artefact signature |
| **Cross-subject consistency** | PCC seed: consistent negative regions in the random-effects map. White-matter seed: β maps also sum to zero per subject, no consistent negatives |
| **Survival when the networks are outside the mask** | Global signal recomputed after removing 30–95% of voxels most (anti)correlated with MT+; anticorrelation persists at 80% and is still significant at 95%; a simulation confirms the test separates anticorrelation from independence |
| **Presence before GSR** | Not at the a priori seeds. Qualitatively present at a PCC seed re-centred on positive default-network correlations, at lowered threshold (post hoc) |
| **Lagged-correlation mechanism** (Murphy et al. 2009's route to artefactual negatives) | Cross-correlogram between the two networks before GSR shows no lag |

The global signal itself: significant in every grey-matter voxel, *least* in the task-negative system, most in visual cortex; its measured variance exceeds the average of five systems' variances (`2.51 vs 1.55`, `p < 2.94 × 10⁻⁴`), so it is not just the mean of system signals; removing it raises structure↔function correspondence (see Status)

## Where it bites

Whether `G90`'s two-mode competition has any measured basis. The internal/external anticorrelation is the wiki's main evidence that the brain runs two mutually suppressing modes at all; if the negative sign is a centering artefact, what remains is a graded reduction in coupling, and an architecture needs a gain rather than an opponent process. Note the positions are not symmetric: side B does not claim GSR is unbiased, only that the bias is a shift whose direction is known, which makes the dispute settleable by reporting both — and almost no source in this literature does

## Status

`LIVE` — the closing comparison now exists in one source, but **qualitatively**: Fox et al. 2009 map the same seeds on the same data at three preprocessing stages, and the anticorrelation *magnitudes* are only in Z-maps and histograms lost in conversion, so `Closes when` is not met as written. It favours B on the *spatial pattern* (seed-specific, cross-subject consistent, survives a 95%-restricted mask, no lag) and A on the *sign* (no significant negatives at a priori seeds without GSR). It does not settle the row, because every pro-B test except spatial distribution assumes **an independent global signal added on top of interacting systems**. The authors name the rival — interconnectedness itself produces the uniformly positive correlations — and cannot exclude it. So the dispute has moved from "report both" to **is the global component additive nuisance or part of the network dynamics?** Corollary for `G90`: if it is part of the dynamics, the resting relation is "less positive", not "opposed", and the architecture needs a gain rather than an opponent process (as in Where it bites). Two further results bear on the row: Gao et al. 2013 find that state *changes* survive without GSR while absolute signs do not; and on Honey et al. 2009's diffusion data (`n = 5`, preliminary), GSR raises structure↔function correspondence (all pairs `r = 0.36 vs 0.32`; anatomically connected pairs `0.53 vs 0.45`; path length `0.47 vs 0.35`)
