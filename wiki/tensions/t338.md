# T338 — Is the relational integrator one shared head with routed inputs, or a tiled family of heads each wired to its own content feed?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A graded ablation is run *across the extent of* a candidate integrator — cut one half of the component, hold the input encoders intact, and measure the two content domains separately. Position A predicts equal loss in both domains from any cut of equal size; Position B predicts a **domain-selective** loss that follows which half was cut. Equivalently in tissue: a within-region multivariate analysis showing whether dorsal and ventral rostrolateral voxels carry *separable* domain codes during second-order processing, or one code with a domain-varying input weighting.

## Position A

**One shared operator; domain lives entirely in the afferents.** The integrator is a single site that is not re-instantiated per content type; what changes across domains is which posterior/ventral store fans into it. Evidence: across 27 experiments the semantic and visuospatial analogy maps share exactly one cluster — left BA 10 at the rostral inferior frontal sulcus, MNI −48/44/−8, the only voxels surviving family-wise-error correction in the global map — and the domain differences fall in **flanking** regions, BA 47 ventral for semantic and anterior middle frontal gyrus plus parietal and fusiform dorsal for visuospatial (Hobeika et al. 2016). Converging: one 177-voxel left-RLPFC seed changes its *partners* with content (left middle temporal gyrus on semantic trials, right DLPFC on visuospatial) while the seed itself is defined as the domain **intersection** (Wendelken et al. 2012); and the region shows no hemisphere × domain interaction where its ventrolateral and dorsolateral inputs both do. [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]

## Position B

**A tiled family; the integrator's own cells are domain-sensitive by the provenance of their inputs.** Within left rostrolateral prefrontal cortex, the visuospatial integration peak sits **dorsal** to the semantic one, transverse to the anterior middle frontal gyrus, in almost every subject (`F₁,₂₁ = 9.1`, `P = 0.007`, `n = 23`) — and the offset is present for the second-order contrasts and **absent** for first-order processing of the same stimuli in the same voxels (`F < 1`), so it is a property of the integration operation rather than of the stimuli. It appears in left anterior middle frontal gyrus only, and not in right anterior or bilateral posterior middle frontal gyrus. The authors' reading is explicit: relational integration is one operation, but the cells running it are **not uniformly distributed** — ventral cells have privileged access to first-order semantic content in ventrolateral cortex, dorsal cells to visuospatial content in the superior frontal sulcus and adjacent dorsolateral cortex (Wendelken, Chung & Bunge 2012). [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]

## Where it bites

The two positions license different architectures for the same box, and the wiki has been writing the first while its primary source argues the second. Under A a machine analogy system is **one relational head over per-modality encoders** — the reading already exported to [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]] and to `I38` — and the head is the thing to scale, share and reuse; adding a modality costs an encoder. Under B the head is a **mixture of experts with a wired gate**: sub-populations specialised by input locality, no routing decision anywhere, and adding a modality costs a new sub-population plus the tract that reaches it ([[wiki/concepts/sparse-expert-routing.md]]). The transfer predictions diverge sharply — A predicts a new content domain inherits the integrator for free once its encoder is trained, B predicts it does not — and that is the claim the whole "domain-general relational operator" line of the wiki is trading on. It also changes what `G21`'s composer *is*: A's composer is switchable fan-in, B's is fan-in fixed by wiring, and only A's is a composer in the sense the gap asks for.

## Status

`LIVE`, and unusually cheap to move, because the disagreement is not between two datasets — the two positions are supported by the *same* laboratory, the same task family, and in the Wendelken case the same 23 subjects.

- **The observations do not conflict.** Large overlap plus offset peaks is one result, and both positions are readings of it. The measurement in dispute is what a **peak offset in a smoothed univariate map** licenses: two intermingled populations, one population with smoothly varying input weights, or neither (`L0-INSTR` — see the open-problems row on the entity page; a gyrus-aligned vascular or registration gradient produces the same picture).
- **A's strongest card is the null.** The whole-brain interaction `(SEM2−SEM1) > (VIS2−VIS1)` returns **no voxels**, at power equivalent to a main effect that is robust — so nothing in the brain integrates semantic relations specifically, which is a genuinely domain-general result at the region level. Position B lives entirely below that resolution.
- **B's strongest card is the second-order specificity.** A vascular, registration or stimulus-driven gradient should be present on first-order trials too. It is not.
- **The discriminating tissue experiment exists and is standard** — multivariate pattern analysis within the region, asking whether a classifier trained on semantic second-order trials transfers to visuospatial ones. This page's open problems already record that **no MVPA has been run on rostrolateral cortex at all**, which is why the row is open rather than decided.
- **The machine-side test is cheaper still and has no confound**: a shared relational head trained on two domains can be halved along any axis and scored per domain. No wiki architecture reports it, and every architecture that hard-codes one relational module has Position A assumed rather than tested.
