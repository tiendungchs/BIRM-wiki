# T354 — Does a growth schedule buy *competence*, or only *compute*?

**Status:** `LIVE`
**Level:** `L0`
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]
**Closes when:** A grown model and a full-size-from-step-0 twin are trained to **matched total updates and matched wall-clock** (not to matched score), at the same final size, and scored on a held-out task the pretraining objective does not reward. The row resolves for A if the grown model wins there, and for B if the only reproducible difference is the point on the loss curve at which the two meet.

## Position A

**Late capacity conditioned on early capacity is a better representation, not just a cheaper one.** This is the premise `G100` was opened on and the reason [[wiki/concepts/emergent-modularity.md]] gives developmental timing the greatest weight of any evolutionary lever: the human neonatal brain is ~27% of adult size against 70% in macaques, so most human wiring is laid down *after* the system is already interacting, and [[wiki/concepts/developmental-heterochrony.md]]'s delayed subset peaks at the synaptic-elimination window — i.e. what is prolonged is the interval before capacity is irreversibly spent. The machine evidence on this side is [[wiki/entities/progressive-neural-networks.md]]'s baseline 4: same parameter count, same lateral wiring, anterior column **random and frozen** rather than trained, and growth-with-content wins in all three domains (209 vs 134 Pong Soup, 132 vs 96 Atari, 491 vs 185 Labyrinth), with transfer still rising at four columns.

## Position B

**The schedule is an optimisation shortcut and nothing else.** [[wiki/entities/ligo.md]] is the only learned growth operator in the wiki run at pretraining scale across two modalities and four model families, and its result is uniform: 44.7–55.4% of the FLOPs to reach the from-scratch model's score, and then **no capability anywhere**. BERT-Base GLUE average 82.57 against scratch's 82.25, inside the spread of the hand-designed operators it beats on compute (StackBERT 82.36, bert2BERT 82.42, and the *most expensive* method, KI at −5.7% savings, scores 82.59). SQuAD is flat or slightly down (78.76/72.31 vs 78.79/72.19). DeiT-B gains +0.61 on ImageNet — the pretraining objective's own eval — and then **exactly zero** on all five transfer tasks: CIFAR10 99.12 vs 99.09, CIFAR100 90.74 vs 90.76, Flowers 97.77 vs 97.79, Cars 92.09 vs 92.06, ChestXRay8 55.82 vs 55.81. Adapter-based tuning reproduces the parity (82.88 vs 82.51). Five growth methods spanning −11.2% to +55.4% in compute land within 0.7 points of each other downstream.

## Where it bites

On `G100`'s `Closes when`, which demands that the grown model **beat** a full-size-from-step-0 twin at matched final size. If B holds, that criterion is unmeetable and the row's whole motivation is misstated: a growth schedule would be an efficiency technique filed with mixed precision and token dropping, not a structural prior, and [[wiki/concepts/developmental-heterochrony.md]]'s per-module `Δ` would be worth fitting for training economics rather than for what the resulting system can do. It also bites `P24`: "give one architecture a growth schedule" is a different task depending on which readout it is scored with, and only A licenses scoring it on abstract-reasoning transfer at all.

## Status

`LIVE`, and the two positions are not yet comparable — they were scored on different axes.

- **Neither measured final competence against a compute-matched control.** LiGO stops at the scratch model's score by construction (savings are *defined* as the FLOPs to reach it), so it cannot report what the grown model does with the remaining 45% of the budget. Progressive networks' transfer score is area under the learning curve, which is *also* a speed measure — so the wiki's Position-A evidence is arguably a compute result wearing a capability label.
- **The controls differ in what they hold fixed.** Baseline 4 varies stored content at fixed size and fixed schedule; LiGO varies schedule at fixed content and fixed final size. A's win therefore says "content transferred", which B does not dispute — LiGO's own donor-quality dial says the same (45.2% saving from BERT-Base vs 30.3% from BERT-Small; 35.2% from a donor trained 50k steps instead of 220k).
- **The one cheap discriminator is unrun and is stated in the `Closes when`:** train both to matched updates rather than to matched score. LiGO's step-count ablation is the nearest thing and points weakly toward B — fitting the growth map 100× longer moves the convergence point by 1k steps out of 215k.
