# T245 — How many connectivity parameters are identifiable from one resting-state fMRI dataset?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Essentially one.** The whole-brain biophysical-network-model tradition: the network is large and nonlinear, so estimating individual coupling strengths is intractable — models use structural connectivity as a proxy for synaptic weight and fit a *single global scaling coefficient* `G` for all inter-regional connections, or a small subset of parameters at group average (Deco et al. 2013b; Wang et al. 2019a estimate 138 parameters, and still only one inter-regional scaling). Fits are to summary statistics, not to raw time series.

## Position B

**All of them, per subject.** Regression DCM estimates >200 regions and >40,000 connections in minutes; MINDy estimates hundreds of nodes' connections in 1–3 min per subject with no anatomical prior; MOU-EC estimates whole-brain directed connectivity per subject by maximum likelihood (Frässle et al. 2017–2021; Singh et al. 2020; Gilson et al. 2016–2020).

## Where it bites

Whether an individual edge in a fitted brain graph can be read at all — every clinical biomarker, individual-difference and effective-connectivity-fingerprint claim rests on B, while every criticality and working-point claim rests on models of type A ([[wiki/concepts/effective-connectivity.md]], [[wiki/concepts/metastability.md]])

## Status

`LIVE` — **and the two sides are not fitting the same model class.** B's identifiability is purchased by linearisation (frequency domain, fixed haemodynamic response function, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck or linearised Wilson–Cowan dynamics), so the disagreement is about what the model may assume, not about what the data contain — and neither side has been run against a ground-truth directed connectome, since no invasive validation of a recovered edge exists in this literature (Li & Yap 2022). The sharp consequence: the regime the type-A models exist to study, proximity to a bifurcation, is outside the model class of every estimator that scales. **Updated (Hashemi et al. 2025):** there is a third position neither side occupies — keep the model *nonlinear* and shrink the parameter set instead. A Virtual Brain Twin frees only a global coupling `G` and a per-region excitability, and amortised simulation-based inference returns that posterior per subject in seconds (against 7–15 h of MCMC), with per-subject clinical validation behind it (epileptogenic-zone precision 0.972 in seizure-free patients; conduction velocity predicting multiple-sclerosis disability beyond lesion load). So scale and nonlinearity are not the trade — scale and *parameter count* are, and which parameters are identifiable is readable off the model's degeneracy structure before any data arrive ([[wiki/concepts/structured-flows-on-manifolds.md]], [[wiki/entities/virtual-brain-twin.md]]). Still LIVE, because side B's claim is that *individual edges* are recoverable, and the third position recovers none. **A fourth position, and it dissolves the question as posed** (Schirner, Deco & Ritter 2023, [[wiki/concepts/excitation-inhibition-balance.md]]): fit **two parameters per edge** — ~143,000 over 379 nodes — to individual resting functional connectivity at `r > 0.97`, then refit 1000 times from random initialisations and measure what is stable. The *parameters* are not (`CV_LRE = 0.5`, `CV_FFI = 0.72`); the *predictions* are (simulated time series correlate at `r = 0.9962` under matched noise; simulated functional connectomes at `r ≥ 0.9946`). So identifiability is a property of a functional of the parameters, not of the parameter count, and the audit that decides it — refit with new seeds, correlate the outputs — costs one extra run and is performed nowhere else in this wiki.
