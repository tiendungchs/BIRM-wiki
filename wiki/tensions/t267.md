# T267 — Which function do the medial prefrontal / posterior cingulate / precuneus hubs serve — broadcasting current content, or generating internal content?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The third position is tested: two overlapping populations sharing midline hubs, separable by whether a hub's activity tracks broadcast content or internally generated content.

## Position A

**Broadcasting.** Mashour et al. 2020 lists anterior and posterior cingulate cortex and precuneus alongside dorsolateral prefrontal and inferior parietal cortex as workspace nodes, i.e. part of the long-range population that amplifies and globally distributes whatever content is currently ignited, external or internal ([[wiki/entities/global-neuronal-workspace.md]])

## Position B

**Generating.** The default mode literature assigns the same hubs to internally generated processing — remembering, prospecting, mentalizing, self-reference — and defines the system by its *deactivation* under externally directed tasks, which is the opposite of a bus that must serve external content ([[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a broadcast architecture needs one shared bus or two systems. If the same hubs do both, the internal-mode generator and the global publication channel are one module and the anticorrelation is a scheduling fact, not an anatomical one — which would answer half of `G90` for free. If they are distinct, an architecture needs a bus *and* a generator, plus an arbitrator between them

## Status

`LIVE` — neither source addresses the other. The overlap is partial (workspace emphasises dorsolateral prefrontal and inferior parietal, which are not default-mode nodes; default mode emphasises medial temporal, which is not a workspace node), so a third position — two overlapping populations sharing midline hubs — is available and untested here

**The node-definition fix this row needed is now in** (Foster et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/posterior-cingulate-cortex.md]]), and it makes the third position *anatomical* rather than a population overlap. "Posterior cingulate cortex" is three regions in three different large-scale networks: **dorsal PCC** couples to frontoparietal/executive networks and carries decision variables, **ventral PCC** couples to the default network and carries autobiographical retrieval, **retrosplenial cortex** couples to the medial temporal lobe. Human intracranial recording separates the first two directly, at the single-unit level, on executive versus mnemonic tasks. So the hub both positions are claiming is not one hub — Position A's workspace nodes and Position B's internal generator can both be right about *different subregions of the same gyrus*, with no shared population required. Two things this does **not** settle: the precuneus, which Position A lists as a workspace node, is not cingulate cortex at all and retrosplenial cortex does not connect to it; and nothing here tests either subregion against the row's own criterion — whether its activity tracks broadcast content or internally generated content — so the `Closes when` is unchanged and the row stays `LIVE`.
