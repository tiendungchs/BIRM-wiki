# T42 — Is the conjunctive place cell `p = f(g̃ ⊙ x̃)` required, or does it block the structural code?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The spiking reimplementation's failure is diagnosed — binary sparsity versus the elementwise product itself — by restoring the product under graded activations.

## Position A

**Required.** In TEM the elementwise product is the single line from which non-random remapping, multiple place fields, lap-cells-that-remap-spatially and the whole `g`/`x` dissociation follow; it is presented as a hard consequence of the biological constraint that the same neurons must carry both codes (Whittington et al. 2018, 2020, [[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]])

## Position B

**It blocks the grid code.** Reimplementing the identical architecture in spiking neurons, the `g ⊙ x_LEC` input to CA1 produced place cells but **no grid cells whatsoever**; grid emergence at 59.6% required removing the product and feeding CA1 from CA3 and LEC III separately (Kawahara & Fujisawa 2025, [[wiki/entities/spiking-tem.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether multiplicative gating is the mechanism of hippocampal binding or a rate-model convenience. If B, the wiki's `p = f(g, x)` factorisation is right about the *factors* and wrong about the *operator*, and T40's index reading gains a second independent argument. Also decides whether a binary-spike latent can carry a conjunction at all: an AND over two sparse binary vectors is far sparser than over two rate vectors, which may starve the gradient to `g`

## Status

`LIVE` — one failed reimplementation against one working implementation; the spiking paper reports the failure (S9, S10 Figs) but does not diagnose it, and the confound (binary sparsity vs. the operator itself) is untested
