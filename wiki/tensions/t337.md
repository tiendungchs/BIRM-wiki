# T337 — Is working-memory capacity a duty cycle over a periodic frame, or a rate of aperiodic bursts?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Spec:** `D259` ([[_brainstorm/birm-spec.md]] §11)  
**Closes when:** One preparation measures, on the same trials, (i) gamma burst count *and* burst duration against load, and (ii) the periodicity of burst occurrence — in tissue where load-dependent theta–gamma phase–amplitude coupling is also present. Either the frame is there and the count is fixed, or the count moves and the frame is not.

## Position A

**A duty cycle over a frame.** One gamma cycle is one slot and a slower rhythm supplies the frame, so `slots = period of frame / period of gamma` and the span is a ratio of two clocks (Roux & Uhlhaas 2014, [[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]). The supporting evidence is human and cross-frequency: theta–gamma phase–amplitude coupling in hippocampal depth recordings rises with the number of items held (Axmacher et al.) and is stronger for correctly identified items (Holz et al.); delay theta appears preferentially in *sequential* paradigms and is replaced by alpha, at different sites, for simultaneous arrays. Lisman & Idiart 1995 state the discriminating prediction directly: load should change the *duration* or filling of the burst structure, not the number of bursts. [[wiki/entities/lisa.md]] takes the same shape with the nesting inverted (gamma as frame), and reaches the same 4–6.

## Position B

**A rate of aperiodic bursts.** In macaque lateral prefrontal cortex, load 1→2→3 raises the gamma burst **count** (`p < 10⁻¹²`) with burst **length unchanged** (`p > 0.31`) — the opposite assignment (Lundqvist et al. 2016, `raw/lundqvist-2016-gamma-beta-bursts-underlie-working-memory.md`). And the frame is not observed where it would have to be: the burst auto-correlogram has a central peak and no side peaks, `CV₂ = 0.997 ± 0.028` over the delay, and the 8–10 Hz peak in the gamma *envelope* — the observable a frame is usually inferred from — is produced by the stereotyped 67 ± 19 ms lifetime of individual bursts rather than by periodic occurrence. Cross-site correlograms show only weak coordination, so there is no shared clock across the array either. The paper reports no delay-period low-frequency oscillation at all.

## Where it bites

Decides whether a machine store needs a **frame oscillator as a component**. Under A the architecture has two clocks and the capacity number is a settable ratio, so span is raised by choosing a slower frame and order comes free from position within the frame. Under B there is no clock: capacity is a bandwidth — how many non-overlapping ~67 ms expressions fit before they collide — order needs a separate mechanism entirely ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T32), and the design parameter is a per-item read budget rather than a slot count. The two also disagree about what an averaged spectrogram means: under B, trial-averaged delay power is burst *density* (`r = 0.93`), so every load curve on averaged power in the Position A literature is silent on which of the two moved.

Carried by [[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]; adjacent to T32, which asks what supplies *order* if there is no frame.

## Status

`LIVE` — the two positions rest on different species, tissue and measurement grain: A on human scalp/depth cross-frequency coupling and hippocampus, B on macaque prefrontal single-trial burst statistics with spikes recorded alongside. Neither dataset contains the other's discriminating observable, and B's null on the frame is a null in prefrontal cortex, which is not where A's coupling evidence was measured. B has the stronger instrument (single trials, spikes and field together, both burst parameters reported); A has the wider behavioural coverage (the sequential-vs-simultaneous span difference, which B's account does not predict).
