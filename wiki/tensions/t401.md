# T401 — Should the lower level of a hierarchy be denied the environment's reward?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A hierarchical agent is run at three settings of the lower level's access to extrinsic reward — none (pure concealment), partial (`R + αR^I` with `α` tuned), full (`α = 0`) — on a task where the *bypass* failure is measurable rather than inferred, i.e. where the lower level's policy can be tested for dependence on the goal channel after training (freeze the goal, or feed a shuffled goal, and measure return). Position A predicts concealment wins wherever the frozen-goal test shows the partially-informed Worker had learned a goal-independent policy; position B predicts concealment loses at every `α`, with the gap unexplained by bypass.

## Position A

**Conceal it.** Dayan & Hinton's original feudal reinforcement learning advocates "completely concealing the reward from the environment from lower levels of hierarchy" (as reported by Vezhnevets et al. 2017). The argument is the same one `G59` makes about the observation: a lower level that can see the payoff can learn to collect it directly, leaving the goal channel unused, and the manager then trains against a transition model that its worker is not in fact implementing. Concealment makes the lower level's *only* route to reward the satisfaction of the goal it was handed — non-bypassability enforced by what is denied, not by a penalty. The biological analogue is the medial-wall narrowing ([[wiki/concepts/policy-abstraction-hierarchy.md]], `G59`): pre-SMA receives the tactic and the resolved action and is denied the sensory argument.

## Position B

**Let it through, at a tuned gain.** [[wiki/entities/feudal-networks.md]] runs the ablation. The "purely feudal" variant — Worker trained on intrinsic reward alone — is the *best* of the four alternatives tried, and still measurably worse than full FuN, whose Worker maximises `R_t + αR^I_t`. The paper's verdict is flat: "allowing the Worker to experience the external reward is beneficial". Two mechanisms are available for why. (i) The Manager's goals are wrong early, and a Worker with no independent access to reward has no way to do better than a wrong instruction — extrinsic access is a floor under the manager's incompetence. (ii) The intrinsic reward `r^I_t = (1/c) Σ d_cos(s_t − s_{t−i}, g_{t−i})` is a *direction*-following payment with no terminal state, so it never tells the Worker when the goal was worth pursuing; the extrinsic term supplies the missing grounding.

## Where it bites

**Whether adding a level costs the levels below it their autonomy.** Under A, a hierarchy is a chain of agents each blind to everything but its instruction, and the whole system's competence is bounded by the top level's model — a *deep* hierarchy then compounds instruction error with no local correction anywhere. Under B every level is an opportunist that will take the payoff if it sees one, and the architecture's correctness rests entirely on a gain `α` that nothing derives; set it too high and the lower level farms its own instruction-following ([[wiki/concepts/conditioned-reinforcement.md]]'s subgoal capture, available here because the Worker is paid for movement whether or not the movement pays), set it too low and the goal channel is decorative.

The two also differ in what they deny. A cannot express a lower level that *refuses* a bad instruction — the information needed to evaluate one is withheld — so error correction must live entirely in the manager's own return. B cannot express a level whose objective genuinely differs from the root's, because the extrinsic term reintroduces the root's objective at every level; every want the agent constructs is thereby anchored, which is the opposite of what makes a conditioned reinforcer survive devaluation.

Note the row is about the **reward**, not the observation. `G59` asks whether the lower level should see the *state* its manager saw; this row asks whether it should see the *payoff*. FuN violates `G59` (the Worker receives `z_t`) and takes B here, so no system in the wiki currently tests either denial in isolation.

## Status

`LIVE` — one ablation, in one architecture, on Atari, with no bypass measurement of any kind: the inferiority of the concealed Worker is reported as a learning-curve difference, and nothing establishes that the informed Worker's advantage comes from anything other than a denser gradient. A's side has no machine measurement at all, only the original proposal and the anatomical narrowing, which is about a different quantity.

Rows citing this one: `T367` (its purely concealed variant is the arrangement this row prices — whether the lower level should see the environment's reward at all).
