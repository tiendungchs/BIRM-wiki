# T382 — Does posterior cingulate cortex carry a signed subjective value, or an unsigned salience?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One recording separates value from salience in the posterior cingulate population *and* fits the discount kernel to the same subjects' behaviour, so a discounted-value account and an unsigned-deviation account make different predictions on the same trials.

## Position A

**Signed subjective value** (Kable & Glimcher 2007, [[wiki/concepts/subjective-value.md]]). Posterior cingulate is one of three regions whose blood-oxygen time course carries a hyperbolically discounted value: subjective value outscored amount, delay and choice in ≥85% of posterior-cingulate voxels; fitting all subjects one population discount rate **loses the effect in posterior cingulate entirely**; and the neurometric kernel both covaries with the behavioural one and has zero mean offset from it. That two-part match is a stronger test than a correlation with value.

## Position B

**Unsigned salience** (Foster et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/posterior-cingulate-cortex.md]]). Macaque dorsal posterior cingulate firing does track the *variance* of risky offers — which varies with subjective but not objective expected value — but a later study that explicitly dissociated value (preference for a choice) from salience (absolute deviation of an outcome from baseline) found **salience the better explanation**. Dorsal anterior cingulate neurons encode both; dorsal posterior cingulate responses are dominated by salience. The authors use this to explain why posterior cingulate appears in far fewer human subjective-value studies than ventral striatum and ventromedial prefrontal cortex.

## Where it bites

Whether the region is a **valuation** stage or a **learning-rate** stage, which are different places in an architecture. A signed discounted value feeds a comparator and its output is a choice; an unsigned deviation from baseline feeds an update rule and its output is a step size. Three converging facts favour the second reading and none of them is a valuation fact: the signal outlasts the trial by seconds and sometimes several trials, it rises at context change points and on explore trials, and **muscimol inactivation reduces learning** rather than distorting choice. If B is right, [[wiki/concepts/subjective-value.md]]'s three-region common-currency claim loses a region and [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]] gains its first cortical carrier with a multi-trial memory of outcome surprise.

## Status

`LIVE` — and the two positions are not measured on the same thing, which is why neither displaces the other. Position A is a haemodynamic signal in undifferentiated human "posterior cingulate", at 6–10 s into the trial, on a task where one option was held fixed at $20 (so sum, difference, ratio and chosen-option value are all unidentifiable from each other — that page's own stated limit). Position B is macaque single units in **dorsal** posterior cingulate only (area CGp), with no discount kernel fitted to behaviour. The tripartite parcellation makes the mismatch worse rather than better: the human effect could sit in ventral posterior cingulate, which no macaque study has recorded from, in which case both are right about different subregions and the disagreement is a node-definition artefact ([[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]).
