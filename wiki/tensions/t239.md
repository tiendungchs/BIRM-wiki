# T239 — Is a mean-field population model licensed to describe a network that synchronises?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Yes — it predicts the data.** Neural mass and neural field models built on the mean-field/diffusion approximation give quantitatively verified predictions of EEG time series and spectra, coherence and correlation functions, evoked responses, and the frequency and amplitude structure of absence seizures, and their stationary solutions are what makes parameter selection in a spiking decision network tractable at all (Deco et al. 2008)

## Position B

**Not where it matters.** The derivation requires that afferent currents into a population be uncorrelated, so that neurons sharing a membrane potential are indistinguishable — and the paper's own 250-neuron simulation shows that the *main effect of a stimulus* is to create exactly that correlation: spike-timing variance contracts progressively, kurtosis goes sub- to super-Gaussian, and the mass model consequently misses both the progressive contraction and the ≈800 ms of post-offset ringing the surviving synchrony produces. The authors name the general form of this as "one of the key outstanding problems" — linear mean-field models predict evoked responses well *without* nonlinear dynamics, while microscopic accounts require bifurcations — and both offered fixes (a self-consistent multiscale hierarchy; recursively enslaving fine scales to the macroscopic field) are flagged as elaborate or causally inconsistent

## Where it bites

Every wiki claim that reads a population-level coupling matrix's dynamics as a summary of the spiking network underneath — [[wiki/entities/fcann.md]] most directly, and any whole-brain model whose nodes are regions. Also bounds [[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]: a geometry summarised by its centroid is blind to the moments that are doing the work
