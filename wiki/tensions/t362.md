# T362 — Does an internal state modulate learned value by a multiplicative gain or by an additive offset?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One preparation runs a state manipulation over a **graded family** of outcomes — three or more concentrations of the same reinforcer, spanning the point where the family's valence reverses — and reports the *rank order* and the *spread* of pursuit before and after, not just the mean. A gain predicts the spread scales with the mean and the order can only invert when the sign flips; an offset predicts the spread is preserved and the order survives a sign flip (the once-best member becomes the least-bad). A mixed result — spread scaling within a valence, order preserved across the reversal — says both operators are present and the row becomes a question of which state variable drives which.

## Position A

**Multiplicative gain.** The state register scales the outcome's worth: `Ṽ(s_t) = κ·r_t + γV̂(s_{t+1})`, `κ>1` appetite/sensitization, `κ<1` satiety, `κ=1` plain temporal difference (Zhang et al. 2009, [[wiki/concepts/incentive-salience.md]]). Evidence: amphetamine and mesolimbic sensitization amplify cue-triggered ventral-pallidum firing and cue-triggered lever bursts without changing the unconditioned-stimulus response, i.e. a scaling of the cue's evaluation with the outcome's evaluation left where it was; deprivation potentiates the reinforcing efficacy of food. Architecturally a gain **cannot cross zero** — no state change can make a previously appetitive outcome aversive, only worthless — and it preserves relative ordering within a valence.

## Position B

**Additive offset.** The state moves the origin against which the outcome is scored: `Ṽ(s_t) = (r_t + log κ) + γV̂(s_{t+1})` (Zhang et al. 2009's second sub-type), which is the same operator as the **movable origin** of [[wiki/concepts/affective-opponency.md]] — a context-set zero against which safety scores as reward and omission as punishment (Boureau & Dayan 2010). Evidence: valence reversals actually happen — an intensely salty solution and its cue become *attractive* under a never-before-experienced sodium appetite, in both firing and consumption, with no re-tasting (Zhang et al. 2009, Test 1); taste-aversion devaluation runs the same reversal the other way. The decisive argument is about rank order: reversing valence multiplicatively (`κ<0`) would make the once-most-liked member of a graded family the *most disliked*, where what is observed is that it remains the best of a bad lot — an additive shift of the whole set.

## Where it bites

**What a state register is architecturally denied.** The wiki's one candidate mechanism for state-dependent revaluation without relearning is a run-time register applied to a cached value (`G56`, `G118`), and its operator has never been pinned down. A gain can re-rank options by relative magnitude and can zero an option out; it cannot make an agent flee what it approached. An offset can flip approach into avoidance for the whole set at once; it cannot change how strongly two options are separated, so it cannot express "hungrier means the difference between good and mediocre food matters more". The two also differ in where the operator has to sit: a gain must reach the cue's evaluation at retrieval, an offset can be applied anywhere the outcome is scored, including at the teaching signal.

Zhang et al. 2009 hold both, and their reason for holding both is a *behavioural* argument (rank order under reversal) rather than a neural one — their own assay cannot separate the sub-types, because ventral-pallidum firing rates are non-negative and a zero-to-high univalent change is indistinguishable in them from a bivalent psychological shift. They note that the two operators together generate the **positive affine group**, the transformations that leave the optimal policy invariant — which is the row's sting: under a *fixed* policy-optimality criterion the two are the same object, and they differ only in what they can do to a *set* of options and in what a downstream reader can recover.
