# T360 — Is non-instrumental information demand a drive to resolve uncertainty, or a drive to engage with positive items?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One observing task crosses cue **reliability** with cue **valence** orthogonally — informative-good, informative-bad, uninformative-good, uninformative-bad, all with identical outcome distributions and no action available — and measures sampling choice. A predicts choice tracks reliability with no valence main effect; B predicts a valence main effect and *avoidance* of the reliable bad-news cue. The same design run on the orbitofrontal and midbrain dopamine populations that encode the preference decides which quantity those cells carry.

## Position A

**Early resolution of uncertainty.** The agent assigns utility to the accuracy of its own beliefs, so it buys the most reliable cue whatever the cue says. Evidence: monkeys in a 50:50 large/small-reward observing task prefer informative over uninformative cues with no instrumental incentive; lateral intraparietal neurons encode cue reliability *independently of the reward expected from acting on it*, distinguishing informative cues of different reliability from uninformative cues of equal reward probability (Gottlieb & Oudeyer 2018, [[wiki/concepts/information-sampling-vs-search.md]]). Every epistemic term in the wiki has this shape — information gain scored over a belief, with valence entering only through the pragmatic term ([[wiki/concepts/epistemic-value.md]]).

## Position B

**Engagement with positive items.** The agent is motivated to occupy anticipated-positive states, so it samples in a biased fashion: pleasant-but-less-accurate cues are preferred and accurate bad news is avoided (dread). Evidence: a dopamine prediction error at a reward predictor assigns value to engaging with the *predictor itself*, above and beyond the rewarded action — sign-tracking rats orient to the reward light rather than to the magazine; a reward-predictive cue at one location impairs saccades elsewhere; past-reward associations capture attention as irrelevant distractors; monkeys proactively search for **redundant** reward cues, i.e. demand information with zero predictive utility. With gain and loss-avoidance targets matched for informativeness and operant value, object-selective cortex encodes the gain targets better while the intraparietal sulcus stays uncertainty-driven, and the human N2pc scales with reward probability independently of predictive value ([[wiki/concepts/incentive-salience.md]]).

## Where it bites

**Whether an exploration bonus may be written as a function of the belief alone.** Under A it may: the term reads `q` and nothing else, and the agent's information demand is valence-blind by construction — which is what every implementation in the wiki assumes. Under B the same term must take the *predicted valence* of the answer as a second argument, which makes the drive asymmetric (it seeks confirmation of good outcomes and is repelled by resolvable bad ones) and produces a failure mode no epistemic ablation in the wiki can exhibit: an agent that reliably declines to look where the news is likely to be bad, and so never learns the part of the graph it most needs.

The two are also separable by *what they demand when the information is useless*. A predicts no demand for a redundant cue; B predicts demand, and the redundant-reward-cue search result is the one datum already on B's side. They are not exhaustive — the coexistence reading is that both channels run in the same selection stage with different addresses (intraparietal sulcus uncertainty-driven, object-selective cortex and the N2pc valence-driven), in which case the row becomes a question about weights rather than about identity.

## Status

`LIVE` — both positions are stated in one review as the two live hypotheses in the theoretical literature, neither has been tested against the other in a factorial design, and the neural correlates (orbitofrontal, midbrain dopamine) are compatible with either.
