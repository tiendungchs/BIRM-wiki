# T91 — Is the medial temporal contribution to arbitrary mapping restricted to spatial consequents?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The systematic antecedent × consequent sweep is run — an ablation with a nonmotor, nonspatial consequent, which the review itself says has never been done.

## Position A

**Spatial.** The monkey neuropsychology emphasises spatial factors: premotor cortex is required for visuo*motor* but not visuo*visual* mappings, and on the same logic the "hippocampus" is proposed to subserve visuo*spatial* but not visuovisual mappings — a division of labour by consequent type across the network, with only prefrontal cortex impaired for both (Wise & Murray 2000, reviewing the ablation record)

## Position B

**General.** Human neuroimaging finds hippocampal and parahippocampal activation during *nonmotor* associative learning (Henke et al.), for less-practiced versus well-practiced memories (Petersson et al.), and for novel word pairs (Dolan & Fletcher) — consequents with no spatial or motor content at all. The same studies report prefrontal activation for nonmotor consequents, which *does* agree with the lesion record, so the divergence is specific to the medial temporal claim

## Where it bites

Whether a machine fast store should be typed by *what kind of consequent* it binds (one store per output modality) or be a single modality-agnostic binder addressed by the controller — [[wiki/concepts/arbitrary-sensorimotor-mapping.md]], [[wiki/concepts/complementary-learning-systems.md]]

## Status

`LIVE` — and partly a methods artefact: lesions test necessity in monkeys, imaging tests engagement in humans, so "activates during word-pair learning" and "not required for visuovisual mapping" can both be true. The decisive experiment — an ablation with a nonmotor, nonspatial consequent — is the systematic antecedent × consequent sweep the review itself says has never been run
