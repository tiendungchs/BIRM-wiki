# T17 — Is intelligence universal, or scoped?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Universal.** Intelligence is expected reward over a mixture across *all computable environments*; no task distribution is privileged, and the agent is optimal by construction relative to its prior ([[wiki/entities/aixi.md]], Hutter 2000). If so, human intelligence is an implementation of one algorithm, reverse-engineering the brain is the shortest path, and AI is close-ended

## Position B

**Scoped.** Every measure of intelligence is relative to a stipulated scope of tasks and value weights; the human `g` factor is an evolutionary adaptation, general only in the limited sense that human morphology is (evolved for the savanna, sufficient for Everest and basketball, useless in interstellar space). No Free Lunch bites only under a uniform distribution over problems, which is not the distribution our universe supplies (Chollet 2019)

## Where it bites

Sets the research strategy. Universal ⇒ pursue the algorithm and benchmark against optimality; scoped ⇒ AI is anthropocentric by necessity, must be benchmarked *against humans* on shared priors and a shared task scope, and "general intelligence" without a stated scope is not a claim. The wiki's scoring tables assume the scoped reading already (they compare against human performance), without having argued for it
