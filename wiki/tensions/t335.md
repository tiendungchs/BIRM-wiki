# T335 — Do matrix problems and four-term analogies measure the same relational competence?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One experiment crosses **task format** (4-term analogy vs `3×3` matrix) with **response mode** (evaluate a completed item vs complete an open one) on matched visuospatial material, and reports whether the analogy-specific left rostrolateral cluster follows the format or the response mode — or a machine solver is shown to transfer between the two formats at the rate it transfers within one of them.

## Position A

**One construct.** Four-term analogies and matrix problems are held to measure the same relational reasoning function: both require relational comparison, integration and schema inference, and both are standard indices of fluid reasoning (Krawczyk 2012; Raven's Progressive Matrices is *the* fluid-intelligence instrument). The wiki's entire machine-side abstract-reasoning stack assumes this — [[wiki/entities/raven.md]], [[wiki/entities/pgm.md]] and [[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]] are matrix-or-grid formats scored as evidence about analogy-grade abstraction, and [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]] cites frontal-patient deficits on *two-relation Raven's-type problems* as evidence about relational complexity in analogy.

## Position B

**Dissociable systems.** In the only quantitative meta-analysis of the two formats (Hobeika et al. 2016, 27 experiments / 40 contrasts / 506 subjects / 351 foci, activation-likelihood estimation), visuospatial analogy and visuospatial matrix problems — same domain, same modality — activate contrasting networks. Visuospatial analogy > matrix recovers the analogy-general **left rostrolateral cluster** (BA 10, anterior end of the inferior frontal sulcus, MNI −48/44/−8) and nothing else but cerebellum. Matrix > analogy recovers a large, right-predominant fronto-parieto-occipital system — bilateral posterior/dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (BA 6/44/9), posterior superior frontal sulcus, medial prefrontal cortex, bilateral superior and inferior parietal lobule, precuneus, postcentral gyrus — i.e. the dorsal attention and fronto-parietal control networks, plus a *medial* rostral prefrontal cluster distinct from the analogy one. The matrix map does **not** include the region that every analogy contrast shares.

## Where it bites

Every matrix-format score in the wiki. If B holds, a system's RAVEN/PGM/ARC number is an index of visuospatial load handling, item-set search and multi-relation bookkeeping — the control-network half — and carries no commitment about the second-order integrator that four-term analogy isolates. The two "relational reasoning" literatures the wiki merges (`I36`'s crossed designs, from analogy; `G104`'s relations-per-item load variable, from Penn's Raven adaptation) would then be measuring different components, and [[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]]'s comparative falsification test — which is built on Raven-style matrices — would be testing the format the *integrator* is least engaged by. Conversely, if A holds, the dissociation is a harness artefact and matrix benchmarks stay the cheap proxy the field treats them as.

## Status

`LIVE`, and the source itself names the confound that could dissolve it. Response mode is almost perfectly collinear with format in the 27 studies: all but two matrix studies used **forced-choice completion**, all but four analogy studies used **evaluation**. Wendelken et al. 2008b measured exactly this — left rostrolateral cortex engaged by *evaluating* an analogy and not by *completing* one, format held constant. *Corrected from the primary* ([[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]): it reproduces only the **rostrolateral half** of the meta-analytic dissociation. (i) Completion does not recruit medial prefrontal cortex: medial BA 10 is below baseline in all four conditions and complete > compare survives only at `p < .005` uncorrected. (ii) The control-network half has the **opposite** sign: evaluation recruits right DLPFC, premotor, supplementary motor and superior parietal cortex more than completion, whereas the meta-analysis has the completion-typical matrix format recruiting that system. (iii) The material is verbal, and the dorsal Raven-derived rostrolateral ROIs are *deactivated* on it. So response mode is a live explanation for the rostrolateral result only; the matrix format's control-network excess is not explained by it, which leaves Position B's other half standing. Two further uncontrolled differences point the same way: matrix items display more terms (9 cells + 8 candidates vs 4–6 terms), so visuospatial load and the number of relations to integrate both rise with format. Note the reading is *not* symmetric in cost: the format→construct inference is what benchmarks rely on, and it is the one currently unsupported. The two matrix studies that did use evaluative responses split — one recruited left rostrolateral cortex, one did not, `n = 2`.

**A causal result now pulls against B, from a different evidence type** (Mole et al. 2025; [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]). In 247 unilateral focal-lesion patients, a four-term visuospatial analogy test, an entirely verbal relational-deduction test **and RAPM** all map to right frontal networks, ordered by extent — analogy (right middle/inferior frontal, caudal-dominated) ⊂ deduction (rostral, distributed) ⊂ RAPM (most extensive, superior-frontal-dominated) — with posterior cortex contributing minimally to all three and posterior patients unimpaired. So at the resolution of **necessity** the two formats share a substrate, while at the resolution of **activation** they dissociate. The two are not formally incompatible: a lesion series can only report which regions are load-bearing, and two tasks can require the same network while loading different parts of it. But it costs Position B its cleanest reading — the matrix format is not sitting on a purely control-network substrate that spares the reasoning site, because the reasoning site the lesion data identify is the *same* one. It also relocates part of the argument into `T336`: the lesion evidence and the activation evidence do not agree about the hemisphere either, so `T335` cannot be settled by adding lesion data until `T336` is settled first.

**A within-format test of the relation-count confound** (Hammer et al. 2019, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]], `n = 229`). The Status above names relation count as rising with format. This study holds format fixed (four-term, visuospatial, evaluation) and raises the cued relations from 1 to 3 on identical displays. The sparse whole-brain load classifier (79.5%) selects precuneus, left SPL, left IFG BA 44, dorsomedial BA 8, left anterior insula and visual cortex. That overlaps the matrix-side regions of Position B (parietal BA 7, precuneus, posterior IFS BA 44, medial prefrontal, insula) and contains no rostrolateral parcel. `(brainstorm)` So part of the matrix > analogy excess may be relation count rather than format, which would move the matrix map's control-network half from format to load. Not closing: this is a within-analogy load contrast, not an analogy-vs-matrix one; the classifier is L1-sparse, so the missing rostrolateral parcel is not a null; and response mode is not crossed.

**Response mode is conditional on semantic distance** (second-hand, Holyoak & Monti 2021, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]). Wendelken et al. 2008b's completion null used **close** items (microphone : sound :: camera : ?). **Distant** completion (blindness : sight :: poverty : ?) reliably recruits RLPFC (Green et al. 2012), and evaluation scales parametrically with `A:B`–`C:D` distance (Green et al. 2010). The authors' reading: close items can be completed by spreading activation without comparison. So the response-mode explanation of the rostrolateral half holds only for close items, and a third variable (distance, or more generally whether an associative route can solve the item) must be held fixed in the `Closes when` crossing. Matrix items have no semantic distance in this sense, so the variable does not transfer directly to the format contrast. Caveat: Green's site is mediocaudal (BA 9/10, superior frontal gyrus), not the lateroventral BA 10/47 site of the meta-analytic cluster. Not closing; status unchanged.

**A primary from the matrix side of the pool** (Christoff et al. 2001, `n = 10`, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]). One of Hobeika's forced-choice-completion matrix studies, read first-hand. Not closing; status unchanged.

- **Completion-mode matrices recruit left rostrolateral cortex when width rises.** 2- vs 1-relational items, format and response mode fixed, give left RLPFC (Talairach −34/50/9). It is the only prefrontal effect that survives RT- and accuracy-matching. So response mode does not keep the integrator out of the matrix format. `(brainstorm)` A format subtraction would hide it wherever both formats load integration at the same site.
- **But at a different site.** The peak is middle frontal gyrus at `z = +9`, dorsal to the analogy cluster (MNI −48/44/−8). It is one of the dorsal "Raven" peaks that Wendelken et al. 2008b found deactivated on verbal analogy. Position B therefore survives in a narrower form: the formats share an operation and differ in *where* along the `T338` dorsoventral gradient it peaks. Typical Talairach–MNI offsets are a few millimetres, smaller than this ~17 mm `z` gap.
- **The posterior half of the matrix excess follows duration.** Extrastriate and superior parietal effects appear under a fixed HRF for both 1 vs 0 and 2 vs 1 and vanish once each trial's RT is modelled. The meta-analysis has no duration covariate, so part of matrix > analogy (parietal BA 7, occipital) may be time-on-task. Matrix items took longer here (2.8–5.7 s), but no RT comparison with analogy items exists in the source.
