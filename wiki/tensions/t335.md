# T335 — Do matrix problems and four-term analogies measure the same relational competence?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One experiment crosses **task format** (4-term analogy vs `3×3` matrix) with **response mode** (evaluate a completed item vs complete an open one) on matched visuospatial material, and reports whether the analogy-specific left rostrolateral cluster follows the format or the response mode — or a machine solver is shown to transfer between the two formats at the rate it transfers within one of them.

## Position A

**One construct.** Four-term analogies and matrix problems are held to measure the same relational reasoning function: both require relational comparison, integration and schema inference, and both are standard indices of fluid reasoning (Krawczyk 2012; Raven's Progressive Matrices is *the* fluid-intelligence instrument). The wiki's entire machine-side abstract-reasoning stack assumes this — [[wiki/entities/raven.md]], [[wiki/entities/pgm.md]] and [[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]] are matrix-or-grid formats scored as evidence about analogy-grade abstraction, and [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]] cites frontal-patient deficits on *two-relation Raven's-type problems* as evidence about relational complexity in analogy.

## Position B

**Dissociable systems.** In the only quantitative meta-analysis of the two formats (Hobeika et al. 2016, 27 experiments / 40 contrasts / 506 subjects / 351 foci, activation-likelihood estimation), visuospatial analogy and visuospatial matrix problems — same domain, same modality — activate contrasting networks. Visuospatial analogy > matrix recovers the analogy-general **left rostrolateral cluster** (BA 10, anterior end of the inferior frontal sulcus, MNI −48/44/−8) and nothing else but cerebellum. Matrix > analogy recovers a large, right-predominant fronto-parieto-occipital system — bilateral posterior/dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (BA 6/44/9), posterior superior frontal sulcus, medial prefrontal cortex, bilateral superior and inferior parietal lobule, precuneus, postcentral gyrus — i.e. the dorsal attention and fronto-parietal control networks, plus a *medial* rostral prefrontal cluster distinct from the analogy one. The matrix map does **not** include the region that every analogy contrast shares.

## Where it bites

Every matrix-format score in the wiki. If B holds, a system's RAVEN/PGM/ARC number is an index of visuospatial load handling, item-set search and multi-relation bookkeeping — the control-network half — and carries no commitment about the second-order integrator that four-term analogy isolates. The two "relational reasoning" literatures the wiki merges (`I36`'s crossed designs, from analogy; `G104`'s relations-per-item load variable, from Penn's Raven adaptation) would then be measuring different components, and [[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]]'s comparative falsification test — which is built on Raven-style matrices — would be testing the format the *integrator* is least engaged by. Conversely, if A holds, the dissociation is a harness artefact and matrix benchmarks stay the cheap proxy the field treats them as.

## Status

`LIVE`, and the source itself names the confound that could dissolve it. Response mode is almost perfectly collinear with format in the 27 studies: all but two matrix studies used **forced-choice completion**, all but four analogy studies used **evaluation**. Wendelken et al. 2008b measured exactly this — left rostrolateral cortex engaged by *evaluating* an analogy and not by *completing* one, with completion recruiting medial prefrontal cortex instead — which reproduces the entire meta-analytic dissociation from response mode alone, format held constant. Two further uncontrolled differences point the same way: matrix items display more terms (9 cells + 8 candidates vs 4–6 terms), so visuospatial load and the number of relations to integrate both rise with format. Note the reading is *not* symmetric in cost: the format→construct inference is what benchmarks rely on, and it is the one currently unsupported. The two matrix studies that did use evaluative responses split — one recruited left rostrolateral cortex, one did not, `n = 2`.

**A causal result now pulls against B, from a different evidence type** (Mole et al. 2025; [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]). In 247 unilateral focal-lesion patients, a four-term visuospatial analogy test, an entirely verbal relational-deduction test **and RAPM** all map to right frontal networks, ordered by extent — analogy (right middle/inferior frontal, caudal-dominated) ⊂ deduction (rostral, distributed) ⊂ RAPM (most extensive, superior-frontal-dominated) — with posterior cortex contributing minimally to all three and posterior patients unimpaired. So at the resolution of **necessity** the two formats share a substrate, while at the resolution of **activation** they dissociate. The two are not formally incompatible: a lesion series can only report which regions are load-bearing, and two tasks can require the same network while loading different parts of it. But it costs Position B its cleanest reading — the matrix format is not sitting on a purely control-network substrate that spares the reasoning site, because the reasoning site the lesion data identify is the *same* one. It also relocates part of the argument into `T336`: the lesion evidence and the activation evidence do not agree about the hemisphere either, so `T335` cannot be settled by adding lesion data until `T336` is settled first.
