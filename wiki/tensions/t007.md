# T7 — Are backpropagation-derived local rules worth pursuing at all?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** An experiment separates "intrinsic to gradient approximation" from "artefact of the approximations tested" — a local rule that never approximated a gradient is scaled to the regime where feedback alignment fails.

## Position A

Yes — locality is what makes deployment-time learning, spiking substrates and neuromorphic hardware possible, and each new constraint has generated a new algorithm (feedback alignment → e-prop → neuromodulated credit assignment, each improving on the last)

## Position B

No — measured against backpropagation through time they show **worse and more variable generalization** that does not close by scaling the step size, because the gradient approximation is poorly aligned with the true gradient. A local *approximation* of an optimization process is fundamentally sub-par, and Schmidgall et al. 2023 pose the abandonment question themselves. Bartunov et al. 2018 add the scaling half of the case: on ImageNet with locally-connected (weight-sharing-free) nets, **every** target-propagation variant sits at chance (98–99% top-1) and feedback alignment reaches 93.08% against backpropagation's 71.43% — the family's validation had all been done on a dataset (MNIST) that does not discriminate between the methods

## Where it bites

Decides whether the wiki's slow-**W** learner should pursue local gradient approximations at all, or restrict local rules to the jobs backpropagation cannot do (deployment-time adaptation, delayed sparse reward, spiking substrates)

## Status

`LEANING` — toward B *for the approximation programme specifically*. Rules that never approximated a gradient ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]) are untouched by the result, and no experiment separates "intrinsic to approximation" from "artefact of the approximations tested"
