# T258 — In schizophrenia, is the default mode network over-engaged or under-connected?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Over-engaged.** Three studies converge on an overactive network: positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions) correlate with raised default activity during passive epochs, poor task performance correlates with medial prefrontal activation, and inter-regional correlations are *higher* than in controls — read as failure of mode arbitration in the direction opposite to autism (Buckner, Andrews-Hanna & Schacter 2008, [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]])

## Position B

**Under-connected.** A systematic review of fMRI studies reports hypoconnectivity *within* the network, specifically medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate cortex, plus reduced dorsolateral-prefrontal↔posterior-cingulate coupling correlating with disorganisation symptoms, and impaired default↔executive-control coupling read as the basis of the executive deficits (Azarias et al. 2025)

## Where it bites

Whether the disorder is a mode-arbitration failure (a control variable stuck internal) or a degradation of the network itself. The wiki uses the autism/schizophrenia pair as its main argument that a *separate arbitrator* exists rather than local mutual inhibition — that argument needs schizophrenia to be over-engagement of an intact network, and dies if the network is instead disconnected

## Status

`LIVE` — but the two positions are not measuring the same quantity: A is activity level and task-epoch modulation, B is resting within-network functional correlation, and both are compatible with a network that is *disconnected and disinhibited*, which would be the worst case for the arbitration reading (a controller cannot be inferred from the sign of a symptom correlation when the thing controlled has changed). Neither source is primary; A pools three studies with directions stated, B is a narrative review reporting no effect sizes, so this row should be settled by a primary study rather than by weighing the two reviews. Note the same instability *within* B: PTSD appears as hyperactivity on re-experiencing and medial-prefrontal↔posterior-cingulate hypoconnectivity on avoidance, one diagnosis, opposite signs
