# T342 — Does a cerebellar module emit a motor command, or a prediction of that command's sensory consequence?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Purkinje-cell output from a single identified microzone is shown to be in motor-command coordinates or in sensory-prediction coordinates by a task that dissociates the two (hand vs cursor under a visuomotor reversal), *and* the paired-module reconciliation is tested by whether forward-type and inverse-type cells are interleaved within a microzone or segregated across microzones.

## Position A

**Inverse model — the output is the command.** Monkey ventral paraflocculus during ocular-following responses: simple-spike rate is reconstructed by a linear combination of eye acceleration, velocity and position 10 ms *later*, with a velocity/acceleration coefficient ratio matching motoneurons; the same fit fails on the visual mossy-fibre inputs (medial superior temporal area, dorsolateral pontine nucleus); simple-spike preferred directions lie on the extraocular muscle axes while input preferred directions are uniform over 360°; stimulating at the recording site moves the eye in that cell's preferred direction. Complex spikes are then a *feedback motor command* — muscle-axis spatial frame, retinal-slip waveform (Kawato; Shidara et al. 1993; Gomi et al., Kobayashi et al., in Wolpert, Miall & Kawato 1998, [[wiki/entities/cerebellum.md]])

## Position B

**Forward model — the output is a prediction.** In a mirror task where hand and cursor move in different directions, a significant proportion of directionally tuned Purkinje cells in intermediate cerebellar cortex track the **cursor**, not the hand (goal-tuned cells excluded because they fire before movement onset); Ebner & Fu find the same as a within-trial time course (hand early, cursor late); simple-spike activity **predicts** complex-spike activity ~150 ms later, the prediction interval of that task, as if the output were a prediction corrected after a fixed lag; and a Smith-predictor simulation with a degraded forward model reproduces the tracking deficits of cerebellar ataxia (Miall & Wolpert, same review)

## Where it bites

Whether a learned module's interface is an **action** or a **prediction** — the wiki's most load-bearing L1 decomposition question, asked here about the one circuit uniform enough to answer it. The two positions are not cleanly separable by behaviour, and the review says why: a forward model inside a high-gain internal feedback loop *has the motor command as its output*, so the same tissue satisfies both descriptions. The reconciliation on offer (MOSAIC: both, paired, one pair per microzone, coupled by a responsibility signal) is the strongest architectural claim in the paper and the least tested — it predicts spatial interleaving of the two cell types and nobody has looked. For a builder the stake is whether a module library is a library of *policies* selected by their predictors ([[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]], MOSAIC) or a library of *predictors* from which a policy is derived at runtime ([[wiki/concepts/learned-world-models.md]], [[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]]) — and whether an experiment could tell those apart at all.

## Status

`LIVE` — the two positions rest on different microzones, different effectors and different tasks, so they are not yet even in contact; the review's own reconciliation would make both true and is untested
