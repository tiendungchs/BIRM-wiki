# T414 — Does the hippocampus split space and time across subfields, or carry both in every subfield?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** CA3 (Cornu Ammonis field 3) and CA1 (Cornu Ammonis field 1) are recorded together, and each is lesioned or silenced separately, in one task that crosses a spatial demand with a temporal demand at matched short (≤ 3 s) and long (≥ 10 s) delays. A content × subfield interaction favours A. A delay × subfield interaction with no content interaction favours the duration reading in *Status*. No interaction favours B.

## Position A

**Split by content.** CA3 handles space and the short term (~10 s). CA1 handles time and the intermediate term (5 min–24 h) (Kesner & Rolls 2015, [[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]]). Lesions dissociate in both directions. CA3 lesions abolish object–place and odour–place learning, which CA1 lesions spare (Gilbert & Kesner 2003). CA1 lesions abolish object–trace–odour association, which CA3 lesions spare (Kesner et al. 2005). Recordings agree in part. Over hours and days of foraging, CA3 maps separate boxes and stay stable. CA2 (Cornu Ammonis field 2) maps barely separate boxes but drift over time. CA1 does both (Mankin et al. 2012). On two routes through a maze, CA3 cells keep one field while CA1 cells separate the routes (Ito et al. 2015). Both via Eichenbaum 2017 (`raw/eichenbaum-2017-integration-space-time-memory.md`).

## Position B

**Both in each subfield.** On a treadmill and on maze alleys, elapsed time and location are signalled equivalently by the same CA1 and CA3 populations (Salz et al. 2016). CA1 and CA3 separate overlapping routes equally (Bahar et al. 2012, against Ito et al. 2015). CA2 has robust place fields when the animal is still between foraging bouts (Kay et al. 2016). CA3 is needed for the temporal order of odour pairs at a **3 s** gap when ten pairs must be held, against Kesner et al. 2005's sparing of CA3 on a temporal task (Farovik et al. 2010). Human high-resolution fMRI finds no subfield preference for spatial or temporal order retrieval (Kyle et al. 2015). All via Eichenbaum 2017, [[wiki/entities/temporal-context-model.md]].

## Where it bites

Whether a machine fast store is decomposed into a **frame block per dimension** (a spatial binder, a temporal binder, merged downstream) or is **one population** with each dimension assigned to units independently. Under A, the time code can enter through a separate port (CA1's direct entorhinal input, `T33`) and a model needs no temporal term in its recurrent binder. Under B, the recurrent binder carries time as well as space, and the only split is in how long a gap each stage can bridge.

## Status

`LIVE`. Eichenbaum's reconciling reading, asserted and not tested: segregation appears or disappears **with the behaviour**, so both positions are observed. His narrower reading of the lesion data is that CA3 and CA1 both process time and differ in the **duration** they bridge: CA1 is needed only once the gap reaches 10 s, CA3 at 3 s and at 10 s (Farovik et al. 2010). That would split the store by timescale, not by dimension, which matches Kesner & Rolls's own short-term/intermediate-term rule and drops its space/time rule. The `Closes when` design separates the two. No study has run it. The rows are rat lesion and rat recording work from different labs and tasks, read second-hand through two reviews.
