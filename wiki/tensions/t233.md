# T233 — Is an adaptation variable a memory or the thing that prevents one?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**It is the store.** In the LSNN/DEXAT line a per-neuron adaptive threshold *is* the working memory: a 10-LIF + 10-DEXAT network holds a 1200 ms STORE-RECALL delay at 95% in 50 training iterations, memory span scales with the adaptation time constant and with baseline excitability, and the item is read with no retrieval operation at all — the memory is the depressed firing rate the next input meets (Ganguly et al. 2024 reviewing Bellec et al. 2018/2020, Shaban et al. 2021, Fitz et al.; [[wiki/concepts/spike-frequency-adaptation.md]])

## Position B

**It is the destabiliser.** The wiki's two prior users of the identical equation subtract it from the drive precisely to *stop* anything persisting: in [[wiki/entities/trnn.md]] `τ_v dV/dt = −V + mr` is what forces working memory out of a fixed point and into a moving trajectory of sequentially peaking units, and in [[wiki/entities/adaptive-cann.md]] the same `τ_v dV/dt = −V + mU` destroys the stationary bump into a travelling wave once `m > τ/τ_v`

## Where it bites

Whether a builder adds adaptation to *get* memory or to *break* it. The reconciliation is visible and nobody states it: A-CANN supplies a threshold, so the two readings are **parameter regimes of one mechanism** — below it the variable is a leaky trace that outlasts its input (a store), above it a positive-feedback destabiliser (a sweep generator). What is missing is the same threshold for the discrete case: no one has written down when a DEXAT network stops holding and starts sweeping, and the interference that long adaptation causes between stored items (Fitz et al.) is plausibly that boundary seen from below
