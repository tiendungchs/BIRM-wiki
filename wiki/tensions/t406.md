# T406 — Should a subgoal passed down a hierarchy be a direction in a learned latent space, or a displacement in fixed state coordinates?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One hierarchical agent is run with both goal formats: (i) unit direction in a jointly learned latent space, cosine reward; (ii) displacement in a *fixed* coordinate space, distance reward. Cross them with two domains: low-dimensional fully observed state, and pixels under partial observability. Match the interaction budget. Add a third arm: a displacement in a **frozen pretrained** embedding. A is right if the learned-direction format wins on pixels and the fixed-coordinate arms lose there. B is right if the fixed-coordinate arm (raw or frozen embedding) wins in both domains, which would locate the cost in the *co-training* of the goal space rather than in its dimensionality.

## Position A

**Direction in a learned space.** [[wiki/entities/feudal-networks.md]] (Vezhnevets et al. 2017): the Manager emits a unit vector `g_t` in a latent space `s = f^Mspace(z)` it learns; the Worker is paid `(1/c) Σ d_cos(s_t − s_{t−i}, g_{t−i})`. Its ablation reports **absolute goals significantly inferior** to directional ones on Atari. Two arguments. A direction is a valid instruction over a large region, so one goal generalises. And the space is shaped by what the Worker can cause, so goal semantics and executor competence co-train.

## Position B

**Displacement in fixed coordinates.** [[wiki/entities/hiro.md]] (Nachum et al. 2018): the upper level emits `g_t`, a desired change in the raw positional observation, and the lower level is paid `−‖s_t + g_t − s_{t+1}‖₂`. On four MuJoCo ant tasks, swapping in FuN's components one at a time:

| Arm | Gather | Maze | Push | Fall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Raw-state displacement, L2 (HIRO) | 3.02 | 0.99 | 0.92 | 0.66 |
| Cosine reward | 0.85 | 0.16 | 0.06 | 0.07 |
| Learned-representation goals | 0.03 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |

The source's mechanism: fixed coordinates give the lower level a **usable reward from the first step**, before the upper level or the task reward carries any information. A learned space gives the lower level a reward about a space that does not yet mean anything.
**A third format on B's side** ([[wiki/entities/hac.md]], Levy et al. 2019): an **absolute** raw-state target, a thresholded binary reward and `H`-attempt commitment. It supports three levels on MuJoCo pendulum, arm and ant tasks, and HAC 2-level beats HIRO on three of them. FuN's ablation found absolute goals significantly inferior on Atari. HAC's working absolute goals are again in a low-dimensional, fully observed domain, so this is the same domain confound, not a crossing. The HIRO comparison also changes relabelling and adds HER, so it does not isolate absolute vs relative format.

**A learned space that works, splitting B's mechanism** ([[wiki/entities/near-optimal-goal-representation.md]], Nachum et al. 2019; same lab and benchmark as HIRO, so not independent). On HIRO's agent, goals are displacements in a learned `f`, `d = 2`. `f` is co-trained online by an action-conditioned contrastive prediction of the `c`-step future, scored with the lower level's own distance. Curves illegible; outcomes verbal:

| Arm | Raw state | "Images" (5 × 5 × 3 semantic map, `x,y` zeroed) |
|---|---|---|
| Learned `f`, predictive objective | ≈ `x,y` oracle | ≈ oracle |
| Learned `f`, end-to-end RL gradient | fails | fails |
| Whole observation as goal space | fails beyond point tasks | fails |
| HIRO's positional subset | ≈ oracle | "much worse" |
| Learned `f` frozen, transferred to two new tasks | works (Appendix F) | — |

B's mechanism ("a learned space gives the lower level a reward about a space that does not yet mean anything") holds for an RL-gradient-trained space and fails for a predictively trained one. The arms vary *what trains the space*, a variable neither position names. No direction/cosine arm is run, and the image domain is a 75-value authored map, not pixels, so the crossing in **Closes when** is still unrun.


**Whether the interface between levels needs its own representation learner.** Under A, every level added brings a latent space to train and a bootstrap period in which the lower level's reward is noise. Under B, the interface is free, but its expressiveness is capped by the sensors.

| | Denied under A | Denied under B |
|---|---|---|
| Early training | A meaningful lower-level reward before the space settles | — |
| Goal semantics | Stable meaning: the space drifts as it trains, so a stored `g` changes meaning (the same non-stationarity HIRO's off-policy correction repairs, one level down) | Goals over anything not in `s`: pixels, hidden state, objects not yet segmented |
| Generalisation of one goal | — | A goal valid over a region: a displacement is tied to magnitude as well as direction |

The confound that keeps this a tension and not a settled result: the two ablations are on different domains, and the domain differs on exactly the property A's argument needs (high-dimensional, partially observed input). HIRO's FuN arms are also re-implementations inside HIRO's off-policy agent, with the dilated LSTM dropped.

## Status

`LIVE` — opened by the ingest of Nachum et al. 2018. Each position has one ablation in its own domain; no shared task. Levy et al. 2019 ([[wiki/entities/hac.md]]) adds a working absolute-state format on B's side in the same kind of domain and does not cross formats. Nachum et al. 2019 ([[wiki/entities/near-optimal-goal-representation.md]]) runs a learned-space displacement arm and a frozen-embedding arm, and both work. It does not run a direction arm, and its image domain is not pixels. Its result relocates B's cost to the goal space's training signal (RL gradient fails, predictive objective works); it does not settle the format.
