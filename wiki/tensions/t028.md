# T28 — Is the hippocampus a map, or an index of cortical representations?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Spec:** `D190`, `D195`, `D247`, `D250`, `D257`, `D262` ([[_brainstorm/birm-spec.md]] §11)  
**Closes when:** Whittington et al. 2022's schedule is tested: hippocampus builds a relational map where no cortical structure applies and reverts to indexing once cortex has structured that world, with the transition timed against the behavioural onset of generalisation.

## Position A

**A map.** In state-space models the connections *between hippocampal cells* are the edges of the graph: clone-structured cognitive graphs learn a de-aliased transition structure inside hippocampus with local, fast, biologically plausible rules, and successor-representation models read place fields directly off a discounted future-occupancy matrix ([[wiki/entities/cscg.md]], [[wiki/concepts/successor-representation.md]])

## Position B

**An index.** Models of *generalisation* adhere to hippocampal indexing theory: hippocampal cells are conjunctions binding a cortical structural code to a cortical sensory code, all predictive capability lives in cortex, and remapping is the rebinding that lets one abstraction serve many worlds ([[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides where the fast store sits relative to the graph — whether fast **M** *is* the instance-graph or merely points at it, and therefore what replay consolidates

**A second cortical action→consequence map, this one built from nothing** ([[wiki/entities/m2-auditory-cortex-filter.md]]). The previous paragraph's channel was *rewritten* in cortex; this one is *acquired* in cortex. A mouse given an arbitrary, novel contingency — its own running produces a fixed-frequency tone — installs over 5–9 days a movement-gated notch at exactly that frequency in auditory cortex, implemented by secondary motor cortex learning to recruit the interneurons tuned to it, and pays for it behaviourally (detection of non-reafferent tones during movement improves; the reafferent tone stays impaired). Position A's usual objection to Leinweber — that the reversal only re-tunes a map that development installed — does not apply, since nothing in the animal's history pairs locomotion with a 16 kHz tone. The circuit contains no hippocampal element and the suppression is absent in auditory thalamus, so it is cortical by exclusion at the subcortical end. **The same control is still missing at the other end**: no experiment shows the filter forms, or survives, without the hippocampus. And the same reduction applies as before — the predicted quantity is a coordinate on a one-dimensional sensory axis, not a relational structure.


**And the row's central move is shown to be undecidable from behaviour** ([[wiki/concepts/activity-slots.md]], Whittington et al. 2025). Position B's "all predictive capability lives in cortex, the hippocampus is an index" is a claim about *where a memory is stored*, and the duality proves that on the whole family of structured sequence-memory tasks the synaptic-store solution and the activity-store solution compute the same function and generalise identically to novel state–observation pairings. Two consequences for this row. (i) The authors state the distinction is **synapses vs. activity, not hippocampus vs. prefrontal cortex** — prefrontal cortex reaches hippocampus via entorhinal cortex and can therefore also carry position codes (grid-like coding is reported in medial prefrontal cortex), while any region storing in activity should use slots. So neither position is a claim about anatomy at all until a carrier measurement is supplied. (ii) None of the cortex-side evidence accumulated in this row is of the separating kind: the only instruments that distinguish the two are neuron-budget scaling, sample complexity and cross-task decoding (`G112`). Set against this, the source also reports the datum that cuts the other way: in some rodent working-memory tasks, position representations are **not** decodable from medial prefrontal cortex — early evidence, cited second-hand.

## Status

`LEANING` — Whittington et al. 2022 propose a schedule rather than a winner: hippocampus builds a relational map where no cortical structure yet applies, and reverts to memory once cortex has learned to structure that kind of world, with the transition tied to the behavioural onset of generalisation. Untested. Whittington et al. 2022 (TEM-t) adds a third framing that fits either side: if the computation is transformer-shaped it can run in cortex alone, and the hippocampus is recruited only when memories must persist or must bind across *distant* areas — so hippocampal dependence would be set by memory horizon and binding span, not by whether the task needs a map ([[wiki/entities/tem-transformer.md]]). **Chandra et al. 2023 push B to its limit and remove its main cost**: the hippocampal state is a content-free *hash* of a grid state — a pointer with no predictive content whatsoever, carrying not even a conjunction — and the same architecture nonetheless yields exponential capacity, no catastrophic forgetting across environments, zero-shot spatial inference and ~10⁵-step sequence memory. The index reading therefore no longer trades capacity against generalisation ([[wiki/entities/vector-hash.md]]), which was position A's strongest remaining argument

**Cortex-side evidence for Position B, added at the Keller & Mrsic-Flogel 2018 ingest.** Position B's phrase "all predictive capability lives in cortex" was a modelling assumption inherited from the Tolman–Eichenbaum Machine, not a measurement. It now has a circuit: mouse visual cortex acquires predictions of upcoming input from three separable experience regimes — passive exposure, visuomotor coupling, and experience at a location in a virtual environment — and the resulting mismatch responses are retinotopic, feature-specific and computed in layer 2/3 ([[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]]). The prediction of visual input *at a spatial location* is the case that bites: it is a place-conditioned prediction generated in sensory cortex, with no hippocampal store named in the circuit. What is still missing on this side is a lesion or silencing result showing those cortical predictions survive without the hippocampus.

**An explicit architectural statement of Position B, with no measurement behind it** (Hawkins et al. 2019, [[wiki/entities/thousand-brains-theory.md]]). Position B's "all predictive capability lives in cortex" is asserted here in its strongest form: every cortical column builds its own predictive model of whole objects, and the hippocampal–entorhinal machinery is the *evolutionary ancestor* of that machinery rather than a participant in running it — the hippocampus is not assigned a role in cortical prediction at all. This does not move the row, since the paper contains no equation, no simulation and no data; it matters only because it shows the cortex-only reading is a deliberate architectural commitment with a circuit sketch, not a convenience inherited from one model's construction.

**Position B's strongest cortex-side evidence, added at the Leinweber et al. 2017 ingest** ([[wiki/entities/a24b-m2-v1-projection.md]]). The previous paragraph asked for a circuit; this is one, with a causal test in both directions. A dense topographic projection from anterior cingulate / secondary motor cortex delivers a motor-conditioned prediction of visual flow into V1 in *retinotopic* coordinates; inactivating or lesioning the source reduces both motor-related and mismatch responses in V1; and — the part that bites — training mice in a **left–right inverted** virtual environment **reverses** the axons' turn preference within ≤10 sessions, in proportion to behavioural performance, and only in the condition where flow is coupled to movement. So an action→sensory-consequence map is *stored, and rewritten, in cortex*, with no hippocampal store named anywhere in the circuit. The same projection is separately reported to carry a prediction conditioned on **spatial location** in the virtual environment — a place-conditioned prediction generated cortically, which is the case Position A would most expect to be hippocampal. What is still missing is the same control as before: no experiment shows these cortical predictions survive hippocampal silencing, and the reversal is a map between two low-dimensional variables, not a relational structure.

**A proposed division of labour that is more specific than either position, and it is B's** ([[wiki/entities/state-space-composition.md]], Bakermans et al. 2025 — same group as position B's anchor model). The claim is stated as an architectural commitment: the reusable building blocks (grid, border-, object- and reward-vector codes) *are cortical*, generalising policies from them does **not** require the hippocampus at all, and the hippocampus becomes necessary only when those cortical representations must be bound into a **relational memory** — the same machinery as episodic memory and scene construction. That is position B with the index's job named precisely: not "point at cortex" but "hold the current *configuration* of cortical blocks." Two qualifications keep this from settling the row. **(i) The paper explicitly leaves A's mechanism running alongside.** Where a world cannot be composed from existing primitives, a hippocampus that learns transition structure (position A) can model it, and the authors propose both are invoked — compositional inference for rapid approximate behaviour, transition learning to nuance it. So the source reads the row as a *two-system* answer rather than a winner, which is a different reconciliation from Whittington et al. 2022's schedule and is equally untested. **(ii) The evidence offered is not of the separating kind either.** The recordings show replay writing vector-to-landmark fields into hippocampal rate maps; nothing measures whether the vector codes those fields are built from survive hippocampal silencing, which is the same missing control the Leinweber and Schneider paragraphs above already flag.

**A map built by a sensory predictor with no index and no hippocampal analogue at all** ([[wiki/entities/visual-predictive-coder.md]], Gornet & Thomson 2024). This does not add evidence to either side — it is a simulation in Minecraft, not a measurement — but it changes what the row is arguing about. A generic ResNet-18 encoder–decoder trained on next-frame pixel error alone develops a latent that is both the map (position decodable to ±4 lattice units; latent distance ∝ `log` physical distance at `r = 0.827`) and the predictor, with **no second store of any kind**: no index, no conjunction layer, no separately-learned transition matrix. So the row's dichotomy presupposes an architecture with two parts, and the cheapest system that solves the task has one. Two qualifications. (i) The map is only ever read by an auxiliary decoder trained after the fact — nothing in the paper uses it — so this is decodable structure, not a demonstrated index or a demonstrated map. (ii) The environment is a 2,600-square gridworld with `A*` trajectories; the memory horizon and binding span that TEM-t makes the deciding variables are both trivial here.
