# T412 — Is parietal cortex necessary for verbal relational deduction?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Mole et al. 2025's posterior cohort is re-analysed with an IPL/IPS-targeted score (a BA 7/40 region of interest, or voxel-level mapping limited to posterior lesions) on the Deductive Reasoning Test. **Or** a focal parietal cohort of at least ~20 runs both a three-term and a four-term (distractor-premise) version of the same verbal problems, with timeouts scored apart from errors. Position A predicts a deficit that survives both. Position B predicts none.

## Position A

**Necessary.** Waechter et al. 2013: 7 Vietnam-veteran patients with focal penetrating lesions mostly in BA 7/40 and nothing outside the parietal lobe. On 48 verbal four-term transitive-inference problems (premises on screen, one distractor premise) they score .49, at chance (wiki-computed `t(6) = −0.2`). Controls (`n = 6`) score .70 (`p = .047`, Tukey). Working memory (Wechsler Memory Scale), IQ, education and lesion volume are matched. Focal BA 10 patients (`n = 5`) do not differ from controls. The authors attribute the deficit to building a spatial mental model from the propositions, not to working memory. [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]

## Position B

**Not necessary.** Mole et al. 2025: 74 posterior-lesion patients (32 left, 42 right; tumour or stroke) are unimpaired on a 24-item verbal relational-syllogism test (three sentences, 18 determinate, 6 indeterminate). Their indeterminate-item score is 70.56 against controls' 76.06, n.s. The Bayesian stochastic-block lesion-deficit model gives posterior cortex a minimal contribution to the deduction network, and to the analogy and Raven's networks too. In the same cohort, left-frontal Stroop and fluency networks are recovered (`I40`), so the method can detect lesion effects. [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]

## Where it bites

The wiki's relational decomposition has a parietal **first-order relation store** and a frontal **integrator**, each separately damageable ([[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]], [[wiki/concepts/abstraction-graph.md]] row 1). Under B, the parietal block is a readout or background condition ([[wiki/concepts/constitutive-vs-enabling.md]]). A model can drop it or rebuild the first-order representation elsewhere, and ablating it should cost nothing. Under A, it is a necessary block for propositional input. Its job is plausibly to **construct** the ordered array from the sentences before anything composes it ([[wiki/concepts/problem-framing.md]], `G73`). An architecture handed a pre-built array would then be skipping a required component, not merely an inessential one. The two positions also disagree about where the frontal pole fits: A has BA 10 lesions sparing the task, and B has a right frontal network required for it.

## Status

`LIVE`. The two results come from different evidence grades, and four differences could dissolve the contradiction without either being wrong.

1. **Grain.** Waechter's groups are weighted by Brodmann area: BA 7/40 damage of 7–44%, nothing outside parietal cortex. Mole's posterior group uses a ≥70%-of-lesion lobar rule that includes temporal and occipital cases, and no analysis targets IPL/IPS. A focal effect in 7 patients can disappear in a lobar average of 74.
2. **Load.** Waechter's problems have four terms with a distractor premise, chosen to allow several candidate models. Mole's have three terms. The parietal activation literature tracks relation *count* (this page's IPL row), and that is the variable on which the tasks differ.
3. **Score.** Waechter scores non-responses after 40 s as errors, and parietal response-time SD is 2–3× the other groups'. So part of the deficit may be slowing. Mole reports accuracy, and their time limits are not recorded on the wiki.
4. **Sample.** Waechter's contrast is `p = .047` at `n = 7` vs `6`. The aetiology is traumatic injury ~40 years old, against tumour or stroke in Mole. Chronic reorganisation could act in either direction.

Not available as a dissolver: **verbal vs pictorial format.** Both tasks are verbal. The authors' own format reconciliation is aimed at Wendelken & Bunge 2010's pictorial fMRI result, not at Mole.
