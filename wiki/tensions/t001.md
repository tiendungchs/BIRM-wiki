# T1 — Does the implementation level carry computational content?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `META`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A computational-level specification is rebuilt in spiking form and its central representation survives removal of every implementation-level mechanism — or a second case like Kawahara & Fujisawa 2025 shows implementation mechanisms are load-bearing in a domain outside spatial coding.

## Position A

Only Marr levels 1–2 (computation, algorithm) need transfer; biophysics is substrate detail, deliberately excluded (Hassabis et al. 2017). Lake et al. 2017 push the same position further: understand the "software" before the "hardware", because what we know about the brain is not clear-cut enough to constrain a cognitive theory in either direction — and a hypothetical biological mechanism should be doubted if it is *cognitively* implausible

## Position B

Spiking timing, energy constraints and one-shot synaptic change are computational, not incidental — the position of neuromorphic and biophysical modelling, which the same paper sets itself against. Schmidgall et al. 2023 argue it directly: spikes are theoretically shown to carry *more* information than rate codes despite being binary and sparse; energy is a first-order barrier at scale and on edge devices; the i.i.d. timeless data assumption "does not reflect physical reality" ([[wiki/entities/spiking-neural-networks.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a reasoning architecture may be specified purely functionally, or whether the substrate constrains what is expressible

## Status

`LEANING B` — B now has a controlled test rather than an argument: Kawahara & Fujisawa 2025 rebuild an architecture the wiki already holds at the computational level ([[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]) in spiking neurons, and three mechanisms *inexpressible* in its functional specification turn out to be necessary for its central representation — grid cells fall from 59.6% to 0.00% without a neuromodulatory input gain, to 10.1% without STDP, to 25.6% without theta-phase inhibition — while the spiking version additionally produces phase precession and phase locking, which the rate model has no vocabulary for ([[wiki/entities/spiking-tem.md]]). B also has the sharpest *quantitative* case in the wiki, and it is older than the debate: an integrate-and-fire model of the barn-owl laminar nucleus fires with **20–25 µs** precision from 250 µs postsynaptic potentials, `τ_m ≈ 100 µs` and 40 µs input jitter, with the precision almost independent of `τ_m` — so temporal content is not merely present, it is *not bounded by* the speed of the components, and the variable carrying the answer (arrival-time difference) has no rate-level description (Gerstner et al. 1996; [[wiki/concepts/temporal-coding.md]]). The residual defence of A: the rate TEM obtains the same code *without* any of these, so they may be repairs for costs the substrate itself imposes rather than computational content it adds. **A now has two further data, both from inside the neuromorphic literature.** (i) Whole trained rate networks — multilayer perceptrons, convolutional nets, deep belief networks, an LSTM — are routinely *converted* to spikes by copying the weights and reading activations as firing rates, with accuracy preserved up to CIFAR-10 and ImageNet; on those tasks the functional specification was evidently complete, and spiking bought energy, not content (T231). (ii) subLSTM replaces the LSTM's multiplicative gates with subtractive ones implementable by cortical lateral inhibition and matches — never beats — the original on sequential MNIST and a language benchmark, so at least one piece of the substrate is confirmed detail. The two sides are reconcilable only if the implementation level matters for **what is represented** (grid codes, phase precession, temporal difference variables) and not for **how a gate or a unit is computed** — which is a sharper form of the question than either position currently states (Tavanaei et al. 2019). **A's datum (i) has narrowed since**: conversion still shows a functional specification was *sufficient* for CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, but it is no longer the best way to get there — spike-native surrogate-gradient networks now beat converted ones on both at 4–8× fewer time steps (T231), so "the rate spec was complete" can no longer be supported by "and nothing else works". A's version of that argument now has to be made on parity, not on leadership **The one proof-level datum belongs to B and simultaneously caps it** (Maass 1997, [[wiki/concepts/circuit-size-separation.md]]): a single spiking neuron computes coincidence detection and element distinctness that provably cost `Ω(n)` sigmoidal hidden units — `≥1663` at a 10,000-synapse fan-in — so the substrate difference is real and quantified; but for piecewise-linear postsynaptic potentials the two classes are *mutually simulable* in `O(s)` units, which makes the rigorous form of B a density claim rather than an inexpressibility claim (T234).
