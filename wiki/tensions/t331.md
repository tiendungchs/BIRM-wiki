# T331 — Do generalizable concepts require one transmodal store, or do direct modality-to-modality connections suffice?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Spec:** `D244` ([[_brainstorm/birm-spec.md]] §11)  
**Closes when:** A multimodal system trained with **no** shared latent — only pairwise connections among modality encoders — matches a hub architecture on cross-modal generalization to novel items, on matched data and parameter budget; or the hub architecture is shown to win only where the pairwise system was denied capacity.

## Position A

**A single transmodal hub is required.** Lambon Ralph et al. 2017 ([[wiki/concepts/controlled-semantic-cognition.md]]): the map from modality-specific features to conceptual similarity is "complex, variable and nonlinear", and the evidence for one concept arrives across different modalities, contexts and time points — so a network encoding only direct associations among modality-specific sources cannot recover conceptual structure, while one intermediating layer common to all concepts and modalities can. The empirical leg is semantic dementia: bilateral anterior-temporal atrophy produces impairment across **all** modalities and virtually all concept types, with item success predicted by familiarity, typicality and required specificity and by nothing modality-specific — a profile only a single shared store produces from a single lesion site. Corroborated by modality-invariant anterior-temporal engagement across words/objects/pictures/sounds, by representational merging of modality-specific sources in multivoxel patterns at the same site, and by domain-general slowing under lateral anterior-temporal inhibitory TMS

## Position B

**No hub; connectivity-tuned distributed features suffice.** Mahon & Caramazza's distributed domain-specific hypothesis and the fully-distributed feature-based view hold that semantic representation is the parallel reactivation of modality-specific sources across cortex, with local function tuned by differential connectivity — the same connectivity-shapes-function mechanism A uses, deployed *without* an intermediating layer. Category-specific deficits and differential fMRI activation follow directly. The convergence-zone family (Damasio) grants meeting points but denies they represent anything: zones are **pointers** binding distributed sources, multiple and discrete, differentiated by task (receptive vs expressive) and category, and hemispherically split. Most investigators in the field are reported to endorse some distributed feature-based view

## Where it bites

Decides the top-level shape of any multimodal reasoning architecture the wiki would build. Under A there is one shared latent every encoder writes into, its metric *is* the concept space, and generalization to a novel item is a nearest-neighbour operation in it — which also gives the graded-specialization and lesion results for free. Under B there is no such object: modalities connect pairwise, and "concept" is a stable co-activation pattern with no single address, so there is nothing to probe, nothing to transplant across tasks, and category structure must be read off the connectivity rather than off a representation. The two also predict opposite things about a **new modality**: A adds a spoke and retrains the hub interface; B must learn `n` new pairwise maps. This is the architectural fork sitting underneath [[wiki/concepts/cross-modal-grounding.md]]'s rate–distortion account, which analyses objectives while assuming the shape

## Status

`LEANING` toward A on the clinical evidence and **not** on the machine evidence, which has never been run as a controlled comparison. The asymmetry to keep in view: A's decisive datum is a *lesion* — one site, all modalities, all categories — and B has no equally direct positive result, only the observation that category effects do not require a hub. But three qualifications hold A back from `RESOLVED`. (i) The source is a review by the hub proposal's own authors and states the rivals in its own terms. (ii) A's own framework concedes that representations are hub–spoke *collaborations*, so "hub required" is a claim about a necessary component, not a sufficient architecture, and B's mechanism (connectivity-tuned local function) is imported wholesale into A as the graded-hub story — the positions share more machinery than the dispute suggests. (iii) In machine learning the comparison is confounded in the way the `Closes when` names: every large multimodal system built to date uses a shared space, so the pairwise alternative is untested rather than refuted, and the shared-space systems fail on exactly the content (relations, arrangement) that neither position addresses ([[wiki/concepts/cross-modal-grounding.md]]). A discriminating machine experiment is cheap and nobody has published it
