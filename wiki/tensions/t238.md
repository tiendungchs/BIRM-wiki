# T238 — Does a reservoir's nonlinearity supply the higher-order features it is credited with?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Yes — that is half the definition of a reservoir.** The standard account, carried on [[wiki/entities/liquid-state-machine.md]], [[wiki/entities/hag-reservoir.md]] and [[wiki/concepts/autonomous-pattern-generation.md]], is that one recurrent circuit does two jobs: it buffers the input history *and* its nonlinear dynamics expand that history into a rich feature space, whereupon a linear readout suffices. The LSM's separation property is exactly this claim measured — state distance grows with input distance because the liquid is nonlinear and heterogeneous, and replacing dynamic synapses with static ones at matched firing rate destroys classification of every input segment but the last. HAG's whole programme is improving that feature space by rewiring.

## Position B

**No, not usefully.** Kleyko et al. 2025 ([[wiki/entities/sigma-pi-reservoir.md]]) report, citing the reservoir literature's own analyses, that reservoirs with common **saturating** activation functions mainly cause memory fading and leave a feature space closely resembling a *linear* recurrent network's — the circuit performs the concatenation (sum-similarity) job and only weakly the tensor-product (product-similarity) one. Their evidence is constructive rather than ablative: a bare permutation-ring buffer plus a separate Pi-neuron stage computing explicitly chosen polynomial orders matches or beats an echo state network, and on Loihi 2 a buffer-only network fails a target quadratic in the two most recent states that the buffer-plus-second-order network predicts nearly perfectly. On Lorenz63 the ESN needs more dimensions than the higher-order feature spaces to reach the same error.

## Where it bites

Whether "add a nonlinearity and expand" is a design principle or a folk theorem. If B, then every reservoir result in the wiki is really a result about a *fading linear buffer with a trained readout*, T174's dispute over recurrent topology is a dispute about the buffer only, and the productive lever is naming the polynomial orders a task needs and building a circuit for them. **The two are not yet in contradiction.** (i) A's evidence is at fixed small `n` on spike-train classification, B's is autoregressive prediction of three low-dimensional chaotic systems — B never runs the ablation on a task where A's separation results were obtained. (ii) A's liquid is a *spiking* circuit with dynamic synapses, and "common saturating activation functions" does not cover it; the paper concedes that spiking neurons and short-term plasticity "can create richer representations" and objects only that the richness is not adjustable. (iii) B's reservoir baseline is a `tanh` ESN with `ρ(W)=1`, i.e. exactly the family the claim is made about, so B's positive result is not evidence about A's substrate. The discriminating experiment: run the buffer-vs-buffer-plus-Pi comparison with an LSM as the buffer, or measure directly how much of a `tanh` reservoir's state variance lies outside the span of the linear reservoir's. Unrun.
