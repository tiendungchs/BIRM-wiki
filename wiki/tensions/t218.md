# T218 — Is normalised exact match an adequate acceptance test?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The false-negative rate of the MATH normaliser is measured on MATH itself with its own ruleset — neither source reports it.

## Position A

**Yes, near-completely.** Hendrycks et al. 2021 designed the `\boxed{}` delimiter plus a normalisation ruleset (fraction encodings, units, spacing, factor and variable order, decimal/fraction equivalence) and state these "cover nearly all ways that different generated or actual solutions can be equivalent in practice" — which is what makes free-generation mathematics auto-gradeable at all, and what every RLVR pipeline and every MATH-derived reward model is built on ([[wiki/entities/math-dataset.md]])

## Position B

**No — the false-negative rate is large enough to corrupt training.** Raiyan et al. 2026 report that up to **38%** of responses flagged incorrect by rule-based graders were correct (`12/36` vs `1/3`), which denies the model informative gradient and reinforces whatever surface format the grader happens to accept ([[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]])

## Where it bites

Every outcome-supervised training run and every reported MATH-family score. A 38% false-negative rate on the reward channel is not a measurement nuisance — under RLVR it is an optimisation pressure toward *grader-legible* answers, which is the Goodhart row of that page's failure table entered through the cheapest rung rather than through a learned verifier

## Status

`LEANING B` — B is a direct audit of graders in use, A is a design claim the authors never measured. Neither source reports the rate on MATH specifically with its own normaliser, which is the missing measurement
