# T393 — Is the dentate separator a slope or a threshold?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One preparation delivers a graded input perturbation *measured at the entorhinal stage* and the dentate output similarity is plotted against it with enough resolution to distinguish a monotone ramp from a step — or a machine separator is run both ways and the downstream store's allocate-vs-reuse behaviour is shown to depend on which shape it is given.

## Position A

**A slope. The dentate separates proportionally.** Morphing a recording enclosure from square to circle in graded steps moves the dentate population vector *gradually*, tracking the amount of geometric change, while the three putative medial-entorhinal grid axons recorded in the molecular layer do not move at all (Leutgeb et al. 2007). Under this reading the dentate is an amplifier of input difference — it multiplies `Δ` by a gain > 1 and hands the result forward, leaving the allocate-vs-reuse decision to CA3's attractor landscape.

## Position B

**A threshold. The dentate holds, then breaks.** Under graded local-global cue conflict the dentate population stays coherent at a 45° mismatch and becomes abruptly non-coherent at 90° and above — the authors describe the contrast with the morph result as "sharp" and the nonlinearity is visible in the polar plots, which go from a peak at 0° to near-circular in one step (Neunuebel & Knierim 2014, `raw/neunuebel-2014-ca3-pattern-completion-dentate-gyrus-pattern-separation.md`). Under this reading the dentate is a comparator: it emits "same situation" or "new situation", and the decision has already been taken before CA3 is addressed.

## Where it bites

Where the allocate-vs-reuse decision lives, which is `G38`'s subject. Under A nothing decides anything at the input stage — the separator is a gain, the decision is the shape of CA3's basins, and a controller must set that basin depth. Under B the separator *is* the decision, the knob is a criterion on input similarity rather than a bias on a transfer curve, and CA3's job reduces to executing a call already made. The two also predict opposite failure modes near the criterion: A degrades gracefully into a blended code that is neither the old episode nor a new one, B flips and is therefore capable of splitting one situation into two on a hair's-breadth input difference.

The positions may be separated by the **manipulation** rather than by the tissue: a geometric morph changes the structural (medial entorhinal) input continuously while leaving cue identity alone, whereas a double rotation holds both cue sets intact and only puts them in *conflict* — and a conflict between two self-consistent reference frames is exactly the input for which a step response is the correct answer. If that is the whole disagreement, the dentate is a threshold on *reference-frame consistency* and a slope on everything else, and the transfer-curve abstraction on [[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]] is under-parameterised: one input-similarity axis cannot express it.

## Status

`LIVE` — neither study was designed to resolve the shape. Position A's input measurement is three putative grid axons; Position B samples four mismatch angles, which is the minimum for distinguishing a step from a ramp and leaves the location of the step unresolved between 45° and 90°. Both pool across sessions and animals, and neither can identify which dentate cell types contributed.
