# T141 — Is the brain's discount kernel exponential, or hyperbolic?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The machine construction is run: train an agent with exponential `γ` and refit both kernels to its value units — a hyperbolic neurometric function from an exponential objective would dissolve the row.

## Position A

**Exponential — a settable `γ`.** Every value function in the wiki is `V = E[Σ_k γ^k r]`, the discount is a scalar metaparameter with a proposed chemical carrier (serotonin) and a proposed anatomical substrate (the direct/indirect balance), and the whole closed-loop control story depends on `γ` being one number that multiplies a recursive term (Doya 2002, [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]])

## Position B

**Hyperbolic.** `SV = A/(1+kD)` fits every subject better than a single exponential (median `R² = 0.95`), consistent with a large animal and human literature, and the *neural* discount function refit to value-region time courses matches the hyperbolic behavioural one with zero mean offset (Kable & Glimcher 2007, [[wiki/concepts/subjective-value.md]])

## Where it bites

A hyperbolic kernel is **time-inconsistent**: `SV(D)` is not the composition of one-step discounts, preferences between a fixed pair reverse as both approach, and no stationary value function exists for dynamic programming to converge to. So under B the wiki's rollout scoring, its TD error ([[wiki/concepts/reward-prediction-error.md]]), and its `γ`-as-metaparameter substrate are all descriptions of something the brain does not compute — while under A the behavioural hyperbola must be an artefact of aggregation, uncertainty about receipt, or a nonlinear utility over amount

## Status

`LIVE`, and the two literatures barely touch: A is fitted to trial-by-trial learning in tasks lasting seconds, B to stated preferences over delays of hours to months. The cheap discriminating construction is a machine one — train an agent with exponential `γ` and refit both kernels to its *value units*; if a network optimising `γ^k` returns a hyperbolic neurometric function, B's finding stops being evidence about the algorithm at all
