# T186 — To make role structure readable by a dot product, do you need the *content* of the neighbouring roles or only their *labels*?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Labels and content are ablated against each other on one probe set — contextualization by unbinding versus superposed role code-vectors, currently demonstrated separately.

## Position A

**Content.** Plate's contextualization replaces each filler by a blend of the filler and the **typical fillers** of the other roles that same object occupies, obtained by *unbinding* (`typ^R_r = R ⊛ r†`) — the whole point being that "which other roles an object fills" is invisible to comparison and must be computed and folded in. It is what takes AN off its tie with FA and yields `LS > AN^cm > AN > SS > FA` in 100/100 runs (Plate, NIPS 7, [[wiki/concepts/vector-symbolic-binding.md]])

## Position B

**Labels suffice.** The re-representation `C*_x = C_x^lower ∨ ⋁_r C_r` superposes the *role code-vectors themselves* — no fillers, no unbinding, no content — and that is enough to map all five similarity types of the same probe set correctly, including the cross-mapped and first-order-relations cases that the plain codes fail, plus three classic complex analogies (Slipchenko & Rachkovskij 2009, [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides the cost and the operator requirements of every structural index the wiki might build: a label-set context is one OR over already-held vectors, a typical-filler context needs an inverse and a pass over the episode per role. The two are not measured against each other — different tasks (ranking vs correspondence), different codes (dense real vs sparse binary) — and B's own stated hole is exactly what A's extra content would fix: a *set* of role labels cannot distinguish two episodes with identical relations in different arrangements.
