# T235 — Should two heterogeneous subnetworks be coupled neuron-to-neuron, or through an interface module?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The interface module is ablated: a learnable hybrid unit against a fixed converter at matched architecture, which none of the three demonstrations runs.

## Position A

**Directly.** Every coupling in the wiki is a weight matrix or a fixed converter: ANN-to-SNN conversion copies weights and reads activations as rates, encoding schemes turn a scalar into a spike train, and any end-to-end hybrid simply backpropagates across the boundary. No extra parameters, no extra objective, and the gradient path is unbroken

## Position B

**Through a module with its own parameters.** Zhao et al. 2022 argue that neuron-to-neuron coupling of an ANN and an SNN is "inefficient and unmanageable when their architecture becomes complex", because the two sides differ in synchronisation, timescale and code all at once; decoupling them behind a hybrid unit `Y = Q·F·H·W(X)` keeps each paradigm's techniques self-contained and lets the interface be trained separately ([[wiki/concepts/cross-paradigm-interface.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides the shape of every answer to G21 (composing two specialised modules) and G52 (an edge with its own state). If B, a reasoning architecture's module boundaries are themselves trainable objects with their own losses, and the wiki's habit of drawing them as arrows is wrong

## Status

`LIVE — B asserted, not measured.` B's three demonstrations all beat single-paradigm baselines, but none of them ablates the interface: there is no learnable-HU vs fixed-converter comparison at matched architecture, so the results confirm that *hybrid* helps and say nothing about whether the *module* is why. Cheapest test: HSN or HMN with `H, F` frozen to the identity conversion and everything else unchanged. **Three years on, still not run**: the same lab's CH-HNN ([[wiki/entities/ch-hnn.md]], Shi et al. 2025) uses the simplest possible interface — a multiplicative binary mask, no `Q`, no learned kernel — and beats the baselines anyway, which is weak evidence *against* B's central claim that the interface's parameterisability is what pays
