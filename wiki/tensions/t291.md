# T291 — When a model fails a capability test, has the capability been measured, or its deployment under one frame?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The rule is stated and applied: a reframing that changes what the task asks measures the capability; one that changes only motivation does not. No wiki benchmark documents which its prompt variations are.

## Position A

**The capability.** This is the default reading of every benchmark null in the wiki and the premise of the whole evaluation apparatus: a task is authored to isolate a competence, the model is given the task, and the score is a property of the model. On this reading, re-framing a failed task until it passes is a form of prompt-shopping that inflates the estimate, and [[wiki/concepts/benchmark-contamination.md]] plus [[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]] both argue that the more ways a task is presented, the more likely one of them leaks the answer

## Position B

**Deployment under one frame, and the error can be total.** Comparative psychology ran this experiment on a twenty-year timescale: chimpanzee theory of mind was a settled negative until Hare et al. (2000, 2001) asked the same question in *competition with a conspecific* instead of *cooperation with a human*, at which point it inverted, and several non-ape species followed. The controlled version is within-species: chimpanzees succeed or fail at one cooperation task according to whether the reward is dispersed or monopolisable, and social learning is abolished by dyadic intolerance — the representation is constant and the measured competence moves ([[wiki/concepts/motivation-representation-synergy.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether the wiki's negative results are findings or artefacts, and it bears hardest on exactly the pages that carry the most negatives: the agent-modelling batteries ([[wiki/entities/agent-benchmark.md]], [[wiki/entities/bib.md]], [[wiki/entities/hbtom.md]]) present their problem in one frame each. It also names a cheap protocol — re-run any claimed capability absence under at least one frame in which deploying the capability is advantageous under the model's own objective, and report both — which is `G102`'s evaluative half and needs no new mechanism

## Status

`LIVE` — and the two positions are separable by a rule nobody has stated: A is right when the reframing changes what the task *asks*, B is right when it changes only what the subject is *motivated to do about it*, and no wiki benchmark documents which of the two a prompt variation is. The biological case is unambiguous only because the competitive and cooperative paradigms are provably isomorphic in their inferential demand; the machine analogue of that isomorphism proof does not exist, which is what keeps the row live rather than `BOTH`

**Sibling row (S12 audit, lint 23): T292** is this row's hardest instance — the ape null on shared intentionality, where the *same two experiments* are cited by both sides — and it names the positive prediction (find the frame) that a machine benchmark would have to make before a null could move from A to B.

**The machine analogue of the isomorphism proof, stated for the first time** ([[wiki/concepts/perception-reasoning-dissociation.md]], Wang et al. 2026). This row's `Closes when` asks for a rule separating a reframing that changes what the task *asks* from one that changes only how it is *delivered*, and says no wiki benchmark documents which of the two its prompt variations are. Wang et al. re-frame three abstract-reasoning benchmarks — images replaced by natural-language descriptions — and make the demarcation an explicit design clause rather than a claim: **the transformation is applied to one image at a time, never to a pair and never to the task, and uniformly across the benchmark.** Because the inductive signal in an ARC-style task lives strictly *across* images, a per-image transformation provably cannot carry it, so the reframing is certified to leave the inferential demand intact while changing only the delivery. That is position A's admissibility test given a checkable form, and the score then moves +11 to +12.5 points — a position-B result obtained under a position-A-proof rather than by prompt-shopping.

**What it does for the row.** It supplies the criterion and demonstrates it is cheap, but only where the task's inferential structure has a *known locus* (here: across-image relations). The agent-modelling batteries this row bears hardest on ([[wiki/entities/agent-benchmark.md]], [[wiki/entities/bib.md]], [[wiki/entities/hbtom.md]]) have no such locus — the inference is over an agent's latent goal, and no partition of the stimulus is provably signal-free — so the protocol is a template the row's hardest cases cannot yet instantiate. The row stays `LIVE`; it now has one worked example of what closing it would look like.
