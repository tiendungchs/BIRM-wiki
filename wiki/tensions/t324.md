# T324 — Does network controllability rank nodes by anything degree does not already say?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A control-theoretic quantity is shown to predict something a weighted-degree vector does not, on the same graph — either boundary controllability earning its keep on a real prediction, or a full `E*(driver set, x₀, x_T, T)` computed and validated against a degree baseline.

## Position A

**Yes — controllability is target-relative and centrality is not.** Degree, betweenness, k-core and participation index all rank nodes with no reference to any transition ([[wiki/concepts/connectome-hubs-and-cores.md]]); minimum control energy `E* = (x_T − A^T x₀)ᵀ W_c⁻¹ (x_T − A^T x₀)` is defined only relative to a named source state, target state, driver set and horizon, so it answers "important for getting **where**" and the graph statistics cannot ([[wiki/concepts/network-control-theory.md]], Bassett & Sporns 2017). The framework also supplies control *goals* a ranking cannot express — the seizure result shows distributed vs targeted strategies differ by substrate topology.

## Position B

**No, not as measured.** On the only real-connectome application in the wiki, the node-level diagnostics are near-deterministic functions of weighted degree: average controllability `r = +0.91` and modal controllability `r = −0.99`, reproduced across diffusion spectrum imaging (`n = 8` ×3), an independent diffusion-tensor cohort (`n = 85`) and macaque CoCoMac tract tracing — `r = −0.99` in all three for modal (Gu et al. 2015). At that correlation the control vocabulary is a relabelling: "hub" and "high average controllability" are the same set, "non-hub" and "high modal controllability" are the same set, and every cognitive interpretation drawn from them is available from the degree column alone. Only **boundary controllability** is degree-independent (`r = +0.13` / `+0.008` / `−0.19`, two of three non-significant) — and it is computed by recursive Fiedler bipartition, i.e. from the community structure, without any target state either.

## Where it bites

Whether the wiki should spend architectural effort on control theory as a *measurement* framework or only as a *design* framework. If B, then `G85`'s measurement half is already satisfied by cheap graph statistics and the only thing control theory adds is the actuator specification — a driver set, an input trajectory, a target, a cost — which is a design object, not a diagnostic. It also bites on the one runnable experiment [[wiki/concepts/network-control-theory.md]] proposes (compute `E*` on a trained network): the experiment must report a degree baseline or it establishes nothing. And it bites numerically — the Gramian's smallest eigenvalue is `2.5 × 10⁻²³` against a largest `≥ 1` at `N = 234`, so `trace(W_c⁻¹)`, the quantity that actually equals average input energy, is uncomputable and the literature substitutes `trace(W_c)`; the collinearity with degree is therefore a property of the substitute, and whether the honest measure would break it is unknown.

## Status

`LEANING` toward B on node rankings, with A intact on everything else. The correlations are not marginal and not one dataset, and the paper reporting them draws its cognitive-system conclusions from exactly those two collinear diagnostics — so the default-mode/frontoparietal/attention triple dissociation is, on its face, the statement that the default mode contains the structural hubs and the control systems do not. What rescues A is the interaction term the degree story does not predict: system × diagnostic `F(18) = 39.81`, `p ≈ 0`, driven by the third, degree-orthogonal diagnostic. A resolves in A's favour the moment anyone computes a full source→target `E*` and beats a degree baseline with it; nobody has, on a brain or on a trained network.
