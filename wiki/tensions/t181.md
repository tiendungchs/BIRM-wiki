# T181 — Is a more accurate verifier always a better verifier?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Verifier accuracy is swept against hacking rate in a controlled run, giving the coefficient the survey's claim currently lacks.

## Position A

**Yes — accuracy is the binding constraint.** The whole ladder is ordered by it: process reward models beat outcome models because they localise the error, kernel-checked proofs beat both because they cannot be wrong, and rule-based graders are indicted precisely for inaccuracy (up to 38% of responses flagged incorrect were correct, denying the model informative gradient).

## Position B

**No — accuracy and exploitability are separate axes.** The survey reports that higher-accuracy *learned* verifiers are sometimes **more** susceptible to hacking during training, because a richer signal is a richer surface for the policy to Goodhart against; the three conditions a verifier must meet (accurate, adversarially resistant, informative on partial progress) are stated as independent, and a kernel's advantage is that it meets all three rather than that it scores highest.

## Where it bites

What to spend on when a verifier must be built: a better scorer, or an *independence* property. It also decides whether the wiki's internal-verifier candidates ([[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]], [[wiki/concepts/precision-weighting.md]]) can be improved into the role or are excluded from it by construction, since both are computed by the model they check.

## Status

`LEANING` toward B, on the grounds that the one verifier meeting all three conditions is the one that is not learned — but the survey's hacking claim is reported without a controlled sweep of verifier accuracy against hacking rate, so the coefficient is unknown.
