# T320 — Does a model's own softmax rank its own errors?

**Status:** `BOTH`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The condition separating the two regimes is stated as a measurable property of the task (label-set closedness, type-1 `d′`, or answer-space cardinality) and predicts the sign of the rank correlation between `κ_f` and loss on a held-out domain.

## Position A

**It ranks well enough to sell a guarantee.** Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017 ([[wiki/concepts/selective-prediction.md]]): thresholding `κ_f = max_j f(x|j)` — the raw max-softmax, one forward pass, no training — yields **1% test risk at 78.6% coverage** on CIFAR-10 and **2% top-5 risk at 59.4% coverage** on ImageNet, each with a distribution-free bound holding at `δ = 0.001`. The one alternative tried, MC-dropout uncertainty over repeated perturbed passes, is **identical** on both CIFARs and a factor of two *worse* on ImageNet top-1 (≈10% vs >20% error at 60% coverage). The scalar read off the answer path is not a weak baseline; it is the one to beat.

## Position B

**It can be anti-correlated with correctness, and the wiki has measured that.** [[wiki/entities/olymmath.md]]: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B on OlymMATH-HARD reaches `Pass@64 = 30.0%` with `Cons@64 = 0.0%` — every problem it can solve at least once is solved by a *minority* of its own samples, so the model's own vote is wrong on 100% of its reachable problems. [[wiki/entities/poe-arc-solver.md]] finds that among four aggregators over views, trusting the **most confident** one is worst (63.5 vs 71.6 for the product rule). [[wiki/entities/hle.md]] reports RMS calibration error of 73–89%. On these tasks, rejecting the low-`κ` tail does not lower risk.

## Where it bites

`G89`'s `Closes when` clause is written as *beat a confidence scalar read off the same forward pass* — which presupposes B's regime and is nearly unachievable in A's. And the split decides whether abstention is a solved engineering problem or an open architectural one. The visible difference between the two bodies of evidence is the **response set**: A's tasks are `k`-way closed-set classification where `max_j` is a genuine posterior over the whole answer space and a fluke-correct answer is a real event; B's are open-ended generation where the "confidence" is a sequence log-probability or a sample plurality, the answer space is unbounded, chance is ≈0, and nothing normalises over the alternatives. If that is the whole story, then `κ_f` is not the variable — the closedness of the label set is — and the fix for B is to *construct* a closed candidate set before scoring, not to build a better monitor. Nobody has run the discriminating measurement: score max-softmax-style selective risk on the multiple-choice slice of an open-ended benchmark ([[wiki/entities/hle.md]] ships 24%, [[wiki/entities/gpqa.md]] is 4-way throughout) and compare the risk–coverage curve to the free-generation slice of the same model.

## Status

`BOTH` — A and B are both fully evidenced and have never been run on the same response format; the closed/open label-set split is the leading candidate for the boundary and is untested
