# T350 — Is a memory library a set of discrete atoms, or one continuous family plus a metaparameter?

**Status:** `LIVE`
**Level:** `L1`
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]
**Closes when:** A learner trained on a zero-mean parameter sweep is tested at a parameter setting **outside** the trained range. A parametric family extrapolates along the coordinate; a library of atoms interpolates within the convex hull of what it allocated and degrades to the novel-context prior beyond it. The two make opposite predictions and no experiment in the wiki runs it.

## Position A

**Atoms.** The stored object is a discrete context with a scalar state, allocated by a nonparametric prior and indexed by a posterior. Reduced interference between opposing perturbations follows from the two being assigned to *different* contexts whose responsibilities do not compete, so neither overwrites the other; nothing relates one context to another except a shared transition prior, and the model reproduces savings, anterograde interference and consistency effects with the Kalman gains and states essentially unmoved (Heald, Lengyel & Wolpert 2021, [[wiki/entities/coin-model.md]], [[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]]). The same shape appears with the content upgraded to a whole policy and the posterior collapsed to an argmax, and there it acquires a circuit ([[wiki/entities/c-ts-model.md]]), and 23 years earlier with the content a paired forward/inverse module pair ([[wiki/entities/cerebellum.md]], MOSAIC) — three instantiations, one decomposition.

## Position B

**A family and a coordinate.** The stored object is a low-dimensional subspace `w = f(μ)` of control space plus a metaparameter `μ` that slides along it; two opposing perturbations are two settings of one `μ`, joined by a short in-subspace path, which is why anterograde *and* retrograde interference between ±60° visuomotor rotations both fall after 800 trials of zero-mean random rotations (`p < 0.01`, `p < 0.02`) while a group given the same number of ±60° trials embedded in a looser linear family shows the ordinary interference (Braun et al. 2009, [[wiki/concepts/structural-learning.md]]). The decisive observation for B is not the interference result but the **exploration** one: variance is suppressed orthogonal to the learned structure and, on a task *off* the structure, the initial search still runs along it — a stored coordinate is being traversed, which an exchangeable atom has no way to express.

## Where it bites

What a machine store's slot is, and therefore what "allocate" means. Under A, novelty triggers allocation and the library grows one atom per novel situation — the memorising asymptote [[wiki/concepts/intelligence-density.md]] scores at `ℐ → 0`, and the failure mode outside the library is a confident intrusion. Under B, novelty triggers a *move* along an existing coordinate, the store's size is fixed by the number of structures rather than by the number of situations, and the failure mode outside the family is a search in the wrong subspace — which is exactly what the random-linear group displays, learning feed-forward *more slowly than naive controls*. The two also disagree about what an allocation prior is for: A needs `γ`/`κ`/`α` to set granularity, B needs a criterion for *how tightly to specify the family*, and B has no such criterion at all (`G27`).

## Status

`LIVE`, and the wiki's own text already contains the complaint that B answers: [[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]]'s open problems state twice, independently (Heald et al.; Sanders et al. 2020), that "context 5 cannot be context 3 with one edge flipped" and that nothing implements a hierarchy in which two contexts share structure. Braun et al. supply behavioural evidence that human motor memory *does* share structure parametrically — in the same task, the same perturbation magnitudes, and from the same laboratory. Three considerations that keep the row from resolving. (i) The two need not be rivals: a plausible synthesis is a discrete posterior over **which structure** and a continuous coordinate **within** one, which is neither model and is unbuilt — Sanders et al. gesture at the same synthesis from the A side by proposing the dorsoventral gradient as one inference run at several values of `α` in parallel. (ii) B has no inference procedure, no allocation rule and no readout of `μ`; it is a decomposition supported by variance measurements, not a model that was fit against A. (iii) A's evidence includes an intervention and a neural code ([[wiki/entities/hidden-state-inference-remapping.md]], Mishchanchuk et al. 2024) and B's includes neither, so the asymmetry in support is large and the asymmetry in *what has been asked* is larger — no one has looked for a metaparameter in tissue.
