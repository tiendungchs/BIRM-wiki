# T251 — Does cortical geometry suffice to explain brain dynamics, or are the rare long-range connections load-bearing?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The outlier connections are lesioned in a geometric model and the long-range functional connectivity gain shown to vanish — shuffling their positions already destroys it.

## Position A

*Geometry is enough.* The folded cortical surface is itself the product of a distance-decay wiring rule, its Laplace–Beltrami eigenmodes reconstruct much of resting and task activity, and the heat kernel is provably the optimal locality-preserving solution — so wiring adds nothing the shape does not already encode (Pang et al. 2023, as cited by Vohryzek et al. 2024)

## Position B

*Geometry cannot express an exception.* Adding the >3 SD, >40 mm outliers to the distance rule beats geometry on long-range functional connectivity at 200 modes (`p < 0.0005`) and on 47 task maps within the first 20 modes; shuffling the outliers' positions destroys the gain, so it is their placement and not their number. Folding cannot bring anterior and posterior cortex into adjacency without displacing everything else (Vohryzek et al. 2024)

## Where it bites

Whether a builder's spatial prior can be a pure metric (embed nodes, decay with distance) or must carry a sparse learned residual of exceptions. Three qualifications keep this LIVE rather than LEANING: EDR-binary — no long-range term at all — is statistically indistinguishable from the winner; the geometry-vs-EDR+LR difference vanishes under global signal regression, which much of the field applies by default; and all four bases predict fluid intelligence and processing speed identically (G87)
