# T108 — When a new situation is ambiguous between stored structures, does the learner mix them or commit to one?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Mix.** Expression is a probability-weighted mixture over *all* memories and updating is responsibility-scaled across all of them, with both evidence channels significant and additive and no interaction (cue `p = 3.8×10⁻³`, feedback `p = 1.26×10⁻⁴`) — offered as direct evidence against winner-take-all retrieval (Heald et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]])

## Position B

**Commit.** C-TS collapses the posterior onto its mode each trial, assigns the trial definitively to one task-set, updates only that one, and keeps no history of alternative assignments; the corticostriatal network is stricter still, since only the *gated* prefrontal stripe can learn at all. Restricting C-TS to the network's regime cost little and fit humans about as well (Collins & Frank 2013, [[wiki/entities/c-ts-model.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a machine retrieval layer is a soft router or a hard one — and therefore whether an ambiguous new situation degrades gracefully or produces a systematic wrong-rule error. The two make opposite predictions about partial matches: a mixture blends toward the correct policy, an argmax commits to a rule that is rewarded on half the stimuli and is measurably slow to abandon it (negative transfer, `E(N_new)` 0.84 for the overlapping new context against 0.99 for the incongruent one). Confounded by content: COIN's memories hold one scalar, where committing is nearly free, while C-TS's hold a policy whose credit assignment is ambiguous unless a single owner is named

## Status

`LIVE` — the two were fit to different domains and neither has been run on the other's task; the discriminating experiment is a partial-match probe, which only the argmax account predicts a signature for. **A third position, from a system that does both** ([[wiki/entities/cn-dpm.md]]): commit and mix split by *operation*, not by model — the allocate-or-not decision is an argmax over responsibilities including the novel-component hypothesis (a new expert is created only when "none of mine" is the mode), while expression is a likelihood-weighted mixture over all components and updating scales every component's gradient by its responsibility. That is a coherent resolution shape neither side proposed: hard where the decision is *discrete and irreversible* (allocating a module), soft where it is *continuous and recoverable* (predicting, updating). **A fourth position dissolves the choice by changing the granularity** ([[wiki/entities/ch-hnn.md]]): there are no modules to commit to, only units, and the mixture is expressed as a *binary mask whose overlap between two situations is trained to equal their similarity* — so an ambiguous input recruits the union of the units its neighbours recruit, which is a mixture in the code and a commitment at every individual unit, and it degrades gracefully without any posterior being maintained
