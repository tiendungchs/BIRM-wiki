# T395 — Does a second-order cue retrieve the specific outcome's value, or only a US-general affective state?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** An outcome-**specificity** probe is run on `S2` with sensitivity matched to the same probe on `S1`. Either (a) outcome-specific Pavlovian–instrumental transfer from `S2` — does an `S2` built through a sucrose-paired `S1` selectively potentiate the sucrose-earning lever over a pellet-earning one, or only raise both? — or (b) a two-outcome second-order design with selective devaluation of one outcome, reading `S2` responding. Position A predicts selective transfer and selective devaluation sensitivity at `S2`. Position B predicts general potentiation only and complete devaluation-insensitivity at `S2`, with `S1` showing both in the same animals as the positive control.

## Position A

**`S2` retrieves the specific outcome's current motivational value.** [[wiki/concepts/valuation-system-decomposition.md]] (Cardinal et al. 2002) lists second-order conditioning, alongside reinforcer devaluation and conditioned reinforcement, as an isolating assay for the `CS → US(motivational)` valuation — "the current motivational value of the *specific* predicted outcome, retrievable by its cue" — and assigns it to the basolateral amygdala. The lesion evidence is the support: basolateral damage abolishes second-order conditioning (aversive and appetitive alike) while sparing the first-order CR and sparing sensory preconditioning, so the representation `S2` needs is exactly the one that lesion removes, and that representation is independently characterised as outcome-specific by the devaluation assay. [[wiki/entities/amygdala.md]] states the same inference directly: "a CS can only train a second CS if it retrieves a value."

## Position B

**`S2` acquires a US-general affective state and carries no outcome identity.** Gewirtz & Davis 2000 ([[wiki/concepts/higher-order-conditioning.md]]) run the post-training manipulations that would show identity and get nothing at `S2`:

| Probe | `S1` | `S2` |
|---|---|---|
| US devaluation / inflation | tracks the change | **unaffected** |
| `S1` extinction | abolished | **spared** |
| Keypeck topography, food vs water US (pigeons) | force differs by US identity | **does not differ by US identity** |

Holland & Rescorla 1975, reported independently in [[wiki/concepts/conditioned-reinforcement.md]], is the same devaluation result. S–R is then excluded too — the CR form elicited by `S2` differs from `S1`'s when the modalities differ (a light paired with food gives rearing; a tone paired with that light gives a brief startle-like response and no rearing), and second-order fear-potentiated startle is acquired although the startle reflex is never elicited during second-order training. What is left is a central motivational state: fear, or appetitively "hope".

B also explains A's lesion result without conceding identity, via the insertion-point model. The first-order `S1`/US convergence is in the **lateral** nucleus; second-order plasticity is proposed for the **basal** nucleus, downstream of it. A lesion of "the basolateral amygdala" removes both. So the second-order block is a *depth* effect — the stage downstream of outcome identity is gone — and licenses no inference about what `S2` stored.

## Where it bites

**Whether a self-manufactured subgoal can name the goal it was built from.** Under A, chaining preserves outcome identity, so an agent can plan over its own subgoals with model-based machinery: revalue the goal and every subgoal below it is revalued by inference, and a subgoal can be selected *because* it leads to the outcome currently wanted. Under B, the first chaining step erases identity and every later step inherits only a scalar — subgoals become outcome-blind, cannot be re-sorted when the goal changes, and hierarchical planning over them is unavailable by construction. That is the associative mechanism underneath two results the wiki already carries: the per-link value attenuation [[wiki/concepts/conditioned-reinforcement.md]] measures and cannot explain, and the autonomy of conditioned reinforcers under primary-reward devaluation (`G125`).

What each side **denies**. A cannot express the topography and fear-potentiated-startle dissociations at all: if `S2` retrieved the specific outcome, the conditioned response it drives should be the one that outcome organises, and it demonstrably is not. B cannot express the two documented `S2→S1` exceptions — autoshaping in pigeons, where `S1` extinction *does* reduce `S2` responding, and rodents given **simultaneous** `S2`/`S1` presentation with very few `S1→US` trials — unless the association type is conceded to be set by stimulus statistics rather than by the chaining operation, which is B's own proposed settlement and costs it the claim that second-order conditioning has one content.

Evidence asymmetry: A's support is a lesion-plus-inference chain with the lesion incapable of resolving two serial stages inside one complex; B's support is four converging behavioural dissociations, none of which addresses the anatomy. No experiment on the table was designed to separate them, because the outcome-specificity probes (specific PIT, two-outcome devaluation) that individuate the valuations in A's framework have not been run on `S2`.

## Status

`LIVE` — opened by the ingest of Gewirtz & Davis 2000. The likely settlement is B plus the insertion-point account, with A's assay row corrected to read "second-order conditioning requires the specific-outcome valuation *upstream* and stores something else", but the discriminating probe has not been run. Adjacent rows: `G126` (the opponent term the same training procedure builds), `G125` (subgoal currency with no audit — this row says what the currency is denominated in), `T367` (whether the subgoal error rides its own channel; orthogonal, but B makes the subgoal signal identity-free and so cheaper to route on a shared channel), `T359` (whether the motivational gain is a multiplier or a separate valuation system).
