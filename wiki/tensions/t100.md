# T100 — At which task phase does the hippocampal→prefrontal channel matter — encoding or retrieval?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Terminal inhibition and regional inactivation are run in one task at both phases — no experiment currently runs both manipulations together, and the likely answer is both.

## Position A

**Encoding.** Optogenetic inhibition of ventral-hippocampal *terminals* in medial prefrontal cortex impairs a four-goal T-maze specifically during cue encoding, and pathway gamma (30–70 Hz) tracks successful encoding and correct trials, vanishing under terminal inhibition (Spellman et al. 2015, in Jin & Maren 2015, [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]])

## Position B

**Retrieval.** The best-controlled rodent lesion result puts medial prefrontal necessity at retrieval only: inactivation before training, or during the delay, has no effect on spatial win-shift; inactivation before the test does (Euston et al. 2012)

## Where it bites

Whether a store's *input link* and the store itself can be assigned different task phases. If both hold, ablating a module and ablating its input give opposite phase signatures, and every lesion design that hits the region is blind to the difference — as is every machine memory evaluation, none of which ablates a connection separately from its endpoints ([[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]], gap G52)

## Status

`LIVE` — the likely resolution is BOTH and it is untested. Different manipulations (terminal inhibition vs. regional inactivation), different tasks, and a reading that reconciles them: the channel writes the trial's context→cue mapping into the controller at encoding, and the controller is queried later. Untested, because no experiment runs both manipulations in one task
