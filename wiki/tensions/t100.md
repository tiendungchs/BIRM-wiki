# T100 — At which task phase does the hippocampal→prefrontal channel matter — encoding or retrieval?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Terminal inhibition and regional inactivation are run in one task at both phases — no experiment currently runs both manipulations together, and the likely answer is both.

## Position A

**Encoding.** Optogenetic inhibition of ventral-hippocampal *terminals* in medial prefrontal cortex impairs a four-goal T-maze specifically during cue encoding, and pathway gamma (30–70 Hz) tracks successful encoding and correct trials, vanishing under terminal inhibition (Spellman et al. 2015, in Jin & Maren 2015, [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]])

Human correlational support, with a control worth the citation: hippocampus–ventromedial-prefrontal coupling rises with repetition number *within* a run and is flat across runs, so it tracks assembly of one overlapping structure at encoding rather than time on task — and ventromedial prefrontal encoding activation predicts later inference after premise memory is partialled out (Zeithamova et al. 2012, [[wiki/concepts/retrieval-mediated-learning.md]])

## Position B

**Retrieval.** The best-controlled rodent lesion result puts medial prefrontal necessity at retrieval only: inactivation before training, or during the delay, has no effect on spatial win-shift; inactivation before the test does (Euston et al. 2012)

## Where it bites

Whether a store's *input link* and the store itself can be assigned different task phases. If both hold, ablating a module and ablating its input give opposite phase signatures, and every lesion design that hits the region is blind to the difference — as is every machine memory evaluation, none of which ablates a connection separately from its endpoints ([[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]], gap G52)

## Status

**A third source assigns each phase to a different route rather than adjudicating between them** (Eichenbaum 2017, [[wiki/entities/hippocampal-prefrontal-channel.md]]): ventral-hippocampal terminal inhibition impairs the **sample** phase and is *ineffective* during the delay or the choice phase, while within one trial the lead reverses — hippocampus leads by ~30 ms at context entry and during the delay, prefrontal cortex leads by ~30 ms at object sampling and the choice point, both only on correct trials. That makes "encoding vs retrieval" a question about *which* of three routes is open, not about when one channel matters — which is the routing reading this row's own `Where it bites` anticipated.

**A fourth source silences the channel's *origin population* and finds no phase at all** (Cembrowski et al. 2018, [[wiki/entities/subiculum.md]]). The subiculum→medial-prefrontal projection leaves from the proximal/prosubicular excitatory class, and bilateral chemogenetic silencing of that whole class — including a control that silences *all* proximal cells rather than only the *Klk8*⁺ subset — leaves match-to-sample water-maze performance intact at encoding **and** at retrieval, while silencing the abutting *distal* class abolishes encoding of the new goal and spares retrieval of the old one. Read carefully, this does not adjudicate the row: it is **dorsal** subiculum in mouse, whereas both positions above concern the *ventral* hippocampal → prefrontal route, and a spatial task is exactly where the posterior/distal exit should dominate. What it does add is a constraint on the manipulation lattice the row's `Closes when` assumes — a class-restricted somatic silencing is a third manipulation alongside terminal inhibition and regional inactivation, and here it separates two phases cleanly on the *other* output class while giving a flat null on this one. Any experiment that finally runs both phases in one task should run it on both classes, or it will attribute to the prefrontal channel a phase signature that belongs to the population next to it.

`LIVE` — the likely resolution is BOTH and it is untested. Different manipulations (terminal inhibition vs. regional inactivation), different tasks, and a reading that reconciles them: the channel writes the trial's context→cue mapping into the controller at encoding, and the controller is queried later. Untested, because no experiment runs both manipulations in one task
