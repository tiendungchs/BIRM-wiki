# T47 — Are concurrent structural codes independent frames or one code read out in many places?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A conflict design rotates conceptual and spatial axes relative to one another and asks whether the two grid angles dissociate — single-unit and BOLD instruments cannot currently address each other.

## Position A

**Independent.** Chen et al. 2022 hold that grid, place and head-direction cells in primary somatosensory and secondary visual cortex arise from a generalized path-integration principle running on each area's *own* self-motion signal — optic flow, proprioception, head movement — and are "complementary yet functionally independent" of the entorhinal integrator; the columnar version makes this thousands of object-anchored frames voting ([[wiki/concepts/distributed-reference-frames.md]])

## Position B

**Registered, or singular.** The only case where two regions' frame orientations have been estimated in one task finds them *aligned*: entorhinal and ventromedial-prefrontal hexagonal codes over the same conceptual bird space share a grid angle (`t21 = 2.18`), each stable across sessions more than a week apart (Constantinescu et al. 2016). Alignment is equally consistent with one entorhinal code propagated to prefrontal cortex, which would delete every fMRI row from the replication evidence

## The third option the row never offered, now measured

**Conjunctive.** Alexander & Nitz 2015 recorded 228 retrosplenial neurons while three frames were dissociated by design — egocentric turn direction, route-centric position, allocentric room position — and found them combined **multiplicatively inside single cells** rather than carried by separate populations (A) or by one code read out elsewhere (B). 45.6% turn-sensitive, of which 43% have their turn response scaled by the turn's position in the route, and that scaling is itself scaled by the route's room position; 21.7% of turn-insensitive cells carry a room-invariant route code; ensemble reconstruction 4.51 cm within a room location versus 51.32 cm across locations, in a population with no place fields ([[wiki/entities/retrosplenial-cortex.md]], [[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]). This is the first instrument in the row that estimates the candidate frames in the *same units*, which is what both existing positions lack. It does not settle the row — the frames here are all spatial, whereas the row's discriminating design pits a *conceptual* axis against a spatial one, and single units in one region cannot say whether an entorhinal and a prefrontal grid are the same code.

## What the anatomy does to both positions

Both positions assume frames can be assigned to regions. The pathway that carries the conversion does not respect that (Kravitz et al. 2011, [[wiki/entities/dorsal-visual-stream.md]]): the headline assignment is egocentric-parietal → allocentric-medial-temporal, and then **caudal inferior parietal units are reported coding in world- and object-centred frames** while **head-direction cells sit inside the hippocampal formation** (pre- and parasubiculum). The authors' own resolution is a premise: function is a gradient over connectivity, and densely reciprocally interconnected regions must share some functional properties, so regional frame labels are *biases* rather than contents. This is the anatomical form of the row's measurement problem — if every stage carries some of both frames, no experiment that localises a frame in a region can adjudicate independence against shared coding, and the conjunctive third option (Alexander & Nitz 2015) is what a gradient looks like when it is measured in single units.

## Where it bites

Decides whether a model needs an arbitration mechanism over many `g` modules (G43) or a single `g` with distributed readouts — and if frames are merely *registered*, arbitration collapses from coordinate conversion to weighting

## Status

`LIVE` — and the row now needs a third column. The evidence on each side uses a different instrument (single units for independence, BOLD for alignment) and neither can address the other's claim. Discriminating test: a conflict design where conceptual and spatial axes are rotated relative to one another, asking whether the two angles dissociate. One frame-mixing population is now measured (Alexander & Nitz 2015), which shows the independent/shared dichotomy is not exhaustive but tests only spatial frames within one region
