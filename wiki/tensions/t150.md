# T150 — Should a quantisation codebook be learned from data or installed by construction?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A structured fixed codebook is tested under a *wrong* topology, the case that separates "installed beats learned" from "this topology happens to be right" (T77, `G47`).

## Position A

**Learned.** The entire vector-quantisation lineage since VQ-VAE trains the codebook jointly with the encoder, on the argument that a codebook fitted to the data allocates its capacity where the data are; every discrete world model and tokeniser in the wiki inherits this, and [[wiki/entities/adaworld.md]] pushes it further by learning the action condition itself. Nothing in the wiki previously suggested a fixed codebook would be anything but a handicap accepted for interpretability.

## Position B

**Installed.** [[wiki/entities/gcq.md]] fixes its codebook to the attractor states of a toroidal continuous attractor network and reports that making the same codewords learnable — adding the standard codebook loss `γ‖sg[s] − ŝ‖²`, everything else identical — makes the model *worse* on both metrics (FID_p 43.41 → 47.76, PSNR_p 27.77 → 24.48). The stated cause is structural rather than optimisational: scaffold codewords stand in dynamical relations to one another (`e_i + a` must remain a codeword), and a per-code gradient does not preserve them. Finite scalar quantisation (FSQ) is the independent precedent — a predefined codebook that matches VQ-VAE — and GCQ's static-setting check on ImageNet reports minimal degradation against VQ-VAE at sequence length 1.

## Where it bites

Whether the discrete vocabulary a reasoning system runs over is something to be discovered or something to be supplied — G27's node set, G4's edge labels and G47's manifold are the same question at three levels. If B, the wiki's standing "learn the structure" programme is buying flexibility it does not need in exactly the places where the structure is a group.

## Status

`LIVE`. B's evidence is one ablation inside one architecture, on a codebook whose *whole point* is that its elements are related — it says nothing about unstructured codebooks, and it perturbs code content within a fixed topology while never testing a wrong topology (which is the case that would matter, [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T77, G47). A's position has never been tested against a structured fixed codebook at scale. The decidable version: sweep codebook adaptability against domain–topology mismatch, and check whether learning helps exactly when the installed manifold is wrong.
