# T135 — What is compared when a brain decides whether to plan — the reliability of the two answers, or the cost and benefit of computing one?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Training, reward proximity and task complexity are held fixed while the response deadline varies: a shift toward habitual responding favours the cost-benefit account.

## Position A

**Reliability.** Both controllers are run as approximate Bayesian learners and, per action, the estimate of the one with the smaller posterior variance is adopted; the arbitration variable is already maintained by each learner, nothing is priced in time or calories, and the crossings that produce habitisation fall out of two variance curves (Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005, [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]]).

## Position B

**Cost and benefit.** Arbitration as a *value of computation*: plan when the expected improvement in the choice exceeds the resource cost of the deliberation, which the wiki has been citing alongside Position A as though they were one proposal (Keramati, Dezfouli & Piray 2011).

**Direct evidence, both halves of the comparison** (Kool, Gershman & Cushman 2017/2018, via [[wiki/concepts/effort-based-decision-making.md]]): on the two-step task, raising the **stakes** increases model-based control — and does not do so in the variant where model-based control confers no reward advantage, which rules out arousal and any route from stakes to control that does not pass through an expected benefit. Holding payoffs fixed and raising the **required planning depth** decreases model-based control. Neither manipulation touches either controller's posterior variance, so Position A's arbitration variable is unmoved while behaviour shifts.

## Where it bites

They need different machinery and predict opposite things under a speed constraint. Under A, adding time pressure changes nothing about which controller is *right*, so habitisation should track accumulated data and search depth alone; under B, time pressure is the whole cost term and should shift control to the cache even in a task where the tree's estimate is far more certain. Only A specifies an implementable quantity, and only B explains why deliberation stops when nothing is uncertain.

## Status

`LIVE`, now **asymmetric**. B has two independent behavioural manipulations moving the arbitration with reliability held constant; A has no result in which a variance change moves behaviour while price is held fixed. This does not close the row — B's evidence shows *something priced* enters the arbitration, not that reliability does not — and the discriminating experiment is unchanged: hold training, reward proximity and task complexity fixed and vary only the response deadline. A predicts no shift in devaluation sensitivity, B predicts a shift toward habitual responding
