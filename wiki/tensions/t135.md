# T135 — What is compared when a brain decides whether to plan — the reliability of the two answers, or the cost and benefit of computing one?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Reliability.** Both controllers are run as approximate Bayesian learners and, per action, the estimate of the one with the smaller posterior variance is adopted; the arbitration variable is already maintained by each learner, nothing is priced in time or calories, and the crossings that produce habitisation fall out of two variance curves (Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005, [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]]).

## Position B

**Cost and benefit.** Arbitration as a *value of computation*: plan when the expected improvement in the choice exceeds the resource cost of the deliberation, which the wiki has been citing alongside Position A as though they were one proposal (Keramati, Dezfouli & Piray 2011).

## Where it bites

They need different machinery and predict opposite things under a speed constraint. Under A, adding time pressure changes nothing about which controller is *right*, so habitisation should track accumulated data and search depth alone; under B, time pressure is the whole cost term and should shift control to the cache even in a task where the tree's estimate is far more certain. Only A specifies an implementable quantity, and only B explains why deliberation stops when nothing is uncertain.

## Status

`LIVE` — with a cheap discriminating experiment: hold training, reward proximity and task complexity fixed and vary only the response deadline. A predicts no shift in devaluation sensitivity, B predicts a shift toward habitual responding
