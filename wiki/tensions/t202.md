# T202 — Does installing an inductive bias by sampling it into `p(T)` give you the bias, or only its frequency?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**The bias.** MLC's episode sampler pairs 80% of queries with the algebraic output and 20% with human-like heuristics, and the resulting frozen model reproduces all three measured human biases at close to the human rate on the open-ended task — one-to-one 66.0% (people 62.1%), iconic concatenation 85.0% (79.3%), mutual exclusivity 99.0% (93.1%), modal all-three pattern 65.0% (58.6%) — with nothing written into the architecture, and it acquires ME in the direction *opposite* to the neural-network default (Lake & Baroni 2023, [[wiki/entities/mlc.md]])

## Position B

**Only its frequency, in the context it was measured in.** Probed on contextual factors the sampler never varied, the same frozen model applies ME absolutely — 98.0% of samples against 68.2% of human responses — while people grade it by the number of counter-examples (`β = 1.76, p < 0.001`) and by the size of the response pool (`β = 2.05, p < 0.01`), plausibly by pragmatic inference about what the task is asking; and its iconic concatenation *drops* to 66.7% (people 93.9%) on mappings that violate one-to-one. Re-optimizing the same architecture on those participants recovers the human profile (68.6% / 93.8%), but only after the response pool was added to the input as an extra study example — the model previously had no channel to condition on it (Supplementary Information SI-2)

## Where it bites

Decides whether the wiki's cheapest route to installing a prior ([[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]], third route) scales to priors that must be *applied conditionally*. If B, a sampled prior is a marginal statistic and every context the bias should respond to must itself be varied in `p(T)` **and** be visible in the input — so the sampler inherits the coverage problem of G66 one level down, at the level of the bias's arguments rather than the task's. The constructive reading is the authors' own: a meta-learner "must be optimized for the kinds of generalizations it will be asked to make", which makes bias-context enumeration a design step nothing currently audits

## Status

`LEANING B` — the two positions are measurements of the same model in different settings, and only B's setting is out-of-sample; A's fit is partly calibration (the 80/20 ratio was set to the human accuracy it is scored against)
