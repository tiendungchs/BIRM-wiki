# T254 — Is the degree distribution of neural graphs heavy-tailed (lognormal/power-law) or exponential?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Degree distributions are measured at one scale, one modality and one edge definition across neuron-level and region-level graphs, since the two claims are currently about different objects.

## Position A

**Heavy-tailed.** The standard network-neuroscience presentation: degree distributions in brain networks are close to lognormal or power-law, so a small number of nodes have far more connections than the rest and act as hubs relaying the bulk of communication — the property that Watts–Strogatz graphs, with their near-binomial degrees, fail to reproduce (`raw/talk-nd-small-world-graphs.txt`, an expository talk, **(tentative)**)

## Position B

**Exponential, not scale-free.** Measured on human cortex at 998 ROIs by diffusion spectrum imaging, degree and strength distributions are exponential with only a ~10-fold range from the least to the most connected node, and the graph is assortative — hubs wire to hubs, forming a club rather than a star (Hagmann et al. 2008, [[wiki/concepts/connectome-hubs-and-cores.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a builder should plan for one or a few very large routers or for a distributed club of comparable hubs. Under A, hub capacity must be budgeted separately and the failure of a single hub is catastrophic; under B, integration is *redundantly* carried and no single node is irreplaceable — the difference between a bus and a mesh. Both agree the distribution is *heterogeneous* and that WS's homogeneity is wrong; they disagree on how heavy the tail is

## Status

`LEANING B` for human cortex at region scale, where the measurement exists and the exponential fit is explicit. The two need not conflict globally: they are claims about different scales (neuron-level vs region-level graphs), different modalities (anatomical vs functional) and different edge definitions, and a region-scale connectome is degree-bounded by construction because parcels are of near-equal area. A genuinely resolving test would fit both families to the same graph with a likelihood criterion at several parcellation granularities; the talk cites no fit at all
