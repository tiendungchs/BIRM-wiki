# T223 — Does a cross-lingual score gap measure the model's reasoning, or the harness's answer extraction?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The released Chinese trajectories are re-graded with a language-matched extractor and the Wilcoxon re-run — the data exists and nobody has done it.

## Position A

**Reasoning.** The gap is a property of the model: every one of 14 models (1.5B / 7B / 14B) scores higher on the English than the Chinese version of the *identical* problem, the difference is significant by Wilcoxon signed-rank across all four subjects and both difficulty levels over 582k released trajectories, and the authors' explanation is a pretraining-distribution one — English corpora dominate, so English-language problem solving is generally stronger (Sun et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/olymmath.md]]). Read this way, language is the strongest content-preserving perturbation available (every token changes, no relation does) and the drop is `I14`'s rephrasing axis taken to its limit.

## Position B

**The grader.** The same paper's own trajectory analysis reports that **answer-extraction failures are disproportionately frequent among incorrect Chinese responses** — a presentation-layer defect that the authors themselves call "distinct from reasoning errors". Normalised exact match is assumed language-neutral everywhere in the wiki and is not: what the grader can *find* in a response depends on the language it was written in, so every cross-lingual comparison is a joint measurement of the model and the harness. The confound cannot be re-attributed to leakage either — the paper's `δ` metric is higher in English for OlymMATH, whose English version is an author-produced translation that could not have been in any pretraining corpus, so `δ` is measuring English fluency as much as exposure and is not comparable across languages.

## Where it bites

Whether a brain-inspired reasoner's competence is language-bound, and more immediately whether the wiki may read *any* multilingual score difference as evidence about representation. It also bounds `I14`: a "meaning-preserving" perturbation must preserve the **grader's** competence as well as the problem's semantics, which no perturbation set in the wiki checks.

## Status

`LIVE`. The decisive experiment is cheap and the data already exists: re-grade the released Chinese trajectories with a language-matched extractor (or hand-score a stratified sample of the extraction failures) and re-run the Wilcoxon. If the gap survives at the same magnitude it is the model; if it collapses it was the harness. Nobody has done it, and the trajectories are public.
