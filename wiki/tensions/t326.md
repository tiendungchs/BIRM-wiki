# T326 — Does a disentangled representation buy anything downstream?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Disentanglement is varied and downstream performance measured in a setting where the representations are *verifiably* disentangled (weak supervision, temporal structure, or a hand-constructed factorized code) and the downstream task requires **recombination** of factor values unseen in training — not factor recovery, which is the task the metrics already score.

## Position A

**Yes — factorization is a prerequisite, not an optimization.** The wiki carries this as a premise in several places: constructive recombination of familiar elements into novel scenarios requires compositional/disentangled representations, since "you cannot recombine what is not factorized" ([[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]]); compositional generalisation in world models is stated as *requiring* disentangled representations of entities, relations and physical properties ([[wiki/concepts/learned-world-models.md]]); modelling every factor of variation is one of the named defences against shortcut learning ([[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]]). The lineage's own claims are broader — better sample complexity, transfer, few-shot learning, integrating out nuisance factors, interventions and counterfactuals (Bengio et al. 2013; Schölkopf et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/independent-causal-mechanisms.md]]).

## Position B

**Not measurably.** Locatello et al. 2019 ([[wiki/concepts/disentanglement.md]]), >12,000 models over 6 methods × 7 datasets × 50 seeds: statistical efficiency (accuracy from 100 samples ÷ accuracy from 10,000 samples, gradient-boosted trees and multi-class logistic regression) shows **no reliable relation** to any of the six disentanglement metrics. Disentanglement scores *do* correlate with downstream accuracy on the dSprites family and partly on Shapes3D, but not on Cars3D or SmallNORB — and the authors flag that those metrics may be partly measuring informativeness rather than factorization. Their summary of the field: they are not aware of *any* prior work compellingly showing the usefulness of disentangled representations, and where β-VAE has been applied successfully it is not clear the success came from disentanglement.

## Where it bites

Whether "factorize the latent" belongs in the objective slot at all (`G30`), and whether the wiki's `g`/`x` separation (`G1`) is justified by its downstream payoff or only by its biological warrant. Under **A**, a factorization penalty is a first-class training objective and the impossibility theorem is a reason to buy the inductive bias elsewhere (weak supervision, temporal structure, interaction). Under **B**, nobody has demonstrated the thing being bought, and the impossibility theorem is a reason to stop paying.

The two are not yet arguing about the same experiment. **B**'s downstream task is *recovering the ground-truth factors* from the representation — precisely what the disentanglement metrics score, so the correlation it finds is partly tautological and its *absence* on the efficiency ratio is the informative half. **A**'s claim is about **recombination** — generalising to factor combinations absent from training — which **B** never tests. **B** also runs on models that, by its own first result, are not reliably disentangled, so it may be measuring the null effect of a manipulation that did not take. Against that, **A** has no measurement of any kind: it is stated as a premise in every wiki page that uses it, sourced to position papers.

## Status

`LIVE` — B is the only direct measurement and it is a null result on a task adjacent to the one A claims; A is a widely repeated premise with no experiment behind it. Neither side has run the recombination test the `Closes when` names, and it is cheap on the same seven datasets B already released.
