# T256 — Is the precuneus (area 7m) part of the default mode network?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Membership and centrality are tested on the same parcellation, so a high-degree cortical hub that is not a member of this functional system is distinguishable from a member.

## Position A

**Yes** — the imaging literature's routine usage, and the structural evidence: precuneus is a top-ranked member of the cortical structural core on all six graph measures (degree, strength, betweenness, efficiency, k-core, participation), sits in one of the only two bilateral modules, and is a *connector* hub by participation index (Hagmann et al. 2008, [[wiki/concepts/connectome-hubs-and-cores.md]]); precuneus + posterior cingulate seeds are the pair whose structural strength predicts resting functional connectivity at `r² = 0.53`. **Second source on this side** (Satpute et al. 2026, [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]): the precuneus is treated as a full member and given a *position* — the eulaminate III (most differentiated) end of the posterior medial complex's local laminar gradient, retrosplenium (agranular) → posterior cingulate (intermediate) → precuneus — with neurite-density, myelination and non-DMN-connectivity values tabulated alongside the other two

## Position B

**No, provisionally** (Buckner, Andrews-Hanna & Schacter 2008, [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]): macaque area 7m connects predominantly to occipital/parietal *visual* areas and frontal *motor-planning* areas; the medial temporal regions that project heavily to PCC and Rsp show minimal projections to 7m; and the human deactivation maps usually stop short of the parietal midline edge where 7m sits, with published dissociations between PCC deactivation and the 7m region. Their diagnosis is terminological — "precuneus" in the imaging literature is often a loose label for a posterior-midline region that includes areas 29/30

## Where it bites

Whether the default network and the structural core are the *same object* seen two ways or two objects that overlap. If 7m is out, the core contains a large non-member and the network is not "the set of structural hubs" at either end (medial prefrontal cortex is excluded from the core, precuneus from the network). It also decides what a machine analogue would copy: an integrative bottleneck sized by graph position, or a specific memory-plus-self circuit that happens to sit near one

## Status

`LIVE` — the two claims are made in different currencies (fibre-density graph rank vs tract-tracing target lists plus deactivation topography) and are not directly contradictory: 7m could be a high-degree cortical hub that is not a member of *this* functional system. Nothing in the wiki tests membership and centrality on the same parcellation, and both papers are 2008 — [[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]] is the reason the question is hard to close
