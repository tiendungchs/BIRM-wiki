# T169 — Is the isotropic Gaussian the right target for an embedding distribution, or only the right *shape* at a dimension nobody knows how to choose?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**The right target, and it is proved rather than chosen.** At fixed total variance, an anisotropic embedding always admits a downstream task with higher ridge-probe bias and has strictly higher OLS estimator variance; and among all distributions under a scalar covariance constraint the isotropic Gaussian **uniquely** minimises the integrated squared bias of radius-k-NN and Nadaraya–Watson kernel probes. Isotropy from the linear analysis, Gaussianity from the nonlinear one. Enforcing it removes the predictor, the EMA teacher and register tokens, holds across ~50 architectures in 8 families and to 1.8B parameters, and yields a training loss that ranks checkpoints without labels (Balestriero & LeCun 2025, [[wiki/entities/lejepa.md]])

## Position B

**Only the shape. The target's *dimension* `K` is a free parameter with a measured cost the theorem is silent about.** The same regulariser in a world model loses to both baselines on the wiki's simplest environment — Two-Room, 2D navigation — because the data's intrinsic dimension is far below the 192 the isotropic Gaussian is asked to fill, and its performance band is bounded on both ends (collapse below ≈184, saturation above) with neither bound predicted by anything (Maes et al. 2026, [[wiki/entities/lewm.md]]). The price is charged even where `K` is *not* mismatched: re-running VICReg with the embedding `ℓ₂`-normalised — which fixes each dimension's variance the way `N(0,I)` does — costs **3.5 points** on ImageNet (Bardes et al. 2022, [[wiki/entities/vicreg.md]]). And the theorem's premise is the escape hatch: it is worst-case over *unknown* tasks, so any known structure in the downstream family makes an anisotropy strictly better by the same algebra

## Where it bites

Whether distribution matching is a general anti-collapse provision or one that must be sized per dataset. Under A a practitioner sets `λ` and stops; under B `K` joins the tuning surface the family's whole selling point was eliminating, and the coefficient-count argument for it over VICReg (G34) partly unwinds. It also decides how to read every biological anisotropy the wiki records: if isotropy is optimal only when the read-out is unknown, a power-law cortical spectrum is evidence about which structures read the population ([[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]) Home page for this row: [[wiki/concepts/representational-collapse.md]].

## Status

`LIVE` — the two sides are not in contradiction so much as in different scopes, and no one has run the experiment that would join them: sweep `K` against a dataset's estimated intrinsic dimension under a fixed LeJEPA recipe and see whether the loss↔accuracy correlation that makes `λ` self-tuning also picks `K`. If it does, B dissolves into the same instrument; if it does not, the derivation covers less of the design than its framing suggests
