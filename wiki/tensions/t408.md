# T408 — Is the heading integrator's velocity input the vestibular measurement, or a reconstruction from motor prediction plus the exafferent residual?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Heading cells (anterodorsal thalamus or postsubiculum) are recorded during matched active and passive head rotations, in the same animals, while the motor-expectation route is perturbed, either by a resistive torque during its ~40–50-movement recalibration window or by silencing neck-proprioceptive or efference input. A holds if heading tracks the vestibular-nerve signal in every condition. B holds if heading under active motion follows the cancelled-plus-predicted signal: it drifts or mis-scales during the recalibration window, or fails when the efference/proprioceptive route is silenced while passive tuning survives.

## Position A

**The vestibular measurement.** The classic view: angular head velocity from the semicircular canals passes through the vestibular nuclei and the nucleus prepositus / supragenual nucleus to the dorsal tegmental ↔ lateral mammillary ring, which integrates it. Evidence: bilateral labyrinth lesions, canal occlusion or inactivation, and horizontal-canal-deficient mice all abolish heading in anterodorsal thalamus or postsubiculum. Heading cells keep their tuning under passive rotation, when only vestibular cues are available. Every brainstem stage on the route contains angular-head-velocity cells. Shinder & Taube 2014 add the authors' own argument: head-fixed passive tuning survives with motor and proprioceptive input absent, and vestibular lesions abolish heading under active motion, so non-vestibular cues cannot stand in. Their mechanism: the ~25% of monkey `VO` cells attenuated only 20–75% (McCrea et al. 1999) carry the signal. The wiki is written this way: the lateral mammillary nucleus "turns vestibular input into heading" ([[wiki/entities/mammillary-bodies.md]], [[wiki/concepts/reference-frame-transformation.md]]), and the integrator's `a_t` is a measured self-motion signal ([[wiki/concepts/path-integration.md]]).

## Position B

**A reconstruction: the sensor stage passes on only the unpredicted part.** At the first central stage, vestibular-only neurons are ~70% attenuated during active head movement (monkeys of several species; mice). The cancellation happens only when neck proprioception matches the motor expectation, and it is cerebellum-dependent. The other output classes carry gaze: position-vestibular-pause neurons are suppressed during saccades and pursuit, and the primate nucleus prepositus outputs mostly eye position. So during voluntary behaviour no vestibular-nucleus output carries pure head motion. On the behavioural side, a rat moved passively by cart into a novel room loses stable preferred directions despite a normal vestibular signal, while a rat that walks in keeps them. Something other than the vestibular signal (proprioception, efference copy) carries orientation across. The source's candidate mechanism is context-dependent decoding: the residual ~30% is up-weighted under active motion and/or summed with proprioceptive and efference estimates inside the heading network (Cullen & Taube 2017, [[wiki/entities/head-direction-system.md]]). Shinder & Taube 2014 add two points. The cancelling signal is the **neck** motor efference copy: a monkey turning head and body together by steering its chair shows no suppression (Roy & Cullen 2001). And at the integrator's own input stage, most dorsal-tegmental angular-head-velocity cells *lose* tuning under passive rotation (8/12, Sharp et al. 2001b), the opposite of what a relayed `VO` signal predicts.

## Where it bites

**What rides the integrator's input wire, and therefore where the forward model sits.**

| | Under A | Under B |
|---|---|---|
| Integrator input | The sensor reading | `â_t(efference, proprioception) + e_t` (prediction plus exafferent residual) |
| Forward model | Beside the integrator, used for perception or control | **On the integrator's input path** |
| Effect of a body-dynamics change | None on heading | Transient heading drift or gain error while the forward model recalibrates (~40–50 movements) |
| VR / head-fixed setups | Heading follows whatever vestibular input remains | Heading follows a recalibrated model and may encode the immersed frame |
| Machine consequence | Any integrator can take raw velocity | An integrator fed by a sensor stage that cancels reafference must also be fed the efference prediction, or it integrates only externally caused motion |

It also bites `G92`. Under B the agent/world partition for head motion is computed at the first synapse, and the integrator has to re-add the "agent" half.

## The restraint confound (Jankowski et al. 2013)

The anterodorsal active-versus-passive record, with numbers ([[wiki/entities/head-direction-system.md]]):

| Preparation | Passive-motion effect on `AD` heading |
|---|---|
| Trunk tightly restrained, head free (Taube 1995; Knierim 1995) | Near-complete suppression |
| Unrestrained (Zugaro 2001) | Peak rate −27%, tuning kept |
| Loosely restrained (Bassett 2005) | −23% |
| Trunk and head fixed (Shinder & Taube 2011) | No reduction, also in darkness |

The size of the effect follows the restraint, not the active/passive contrast. Two consequences for the row. (i) The head-fixed result is A's cleanest support: with motor, proprioceptive and visual input absent, heading tuning is intact. (ii) It does not touch B's claim, which is about what the integrator uses *during active motion*, when the first-stage signal is cancelled. A head-fixed passive rotation is the one case where the vestibular-only neurons are not cancelling, so it cannot discriminate. The `Closes when` design (active versus passive *while the motor-expectation route is perturbed*) stands. The review notes active-motion firing may also be raised by theta-state inputs (Zugaro's higher active rates), a third account neither position states.

## Four candidate resolutions (Shinder & Taube 2014)

| # | Mechanism | Integrator input | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distributed sum of vestibular and non-vestibular cell types, summed at `DTN` | `Σ_k w_k s_k` | B |
| 2 | Spared, mildly attenuated `VO` subpopulation | Uncancelled sensor channel | A |
| 3 | Gated switch: `VO` under passive, another source (`PGRNd`, cerebellum) under active; efference selects, does not encode | `g·s_passive + (1−g)·s_active` | **Neither** |
| 4 | Ring reinterprets the altered input per condition | `D(s_t; c_t)` | B |

Mechanism 3 is a third form the row does not state. Under it the forward model sits neither beside the integrator (A) nor inside its input sum (B). It sits on a **selector**, so a recalibration window would cause a channel-assignment error rather than a gain error. The `Closes when` design separates A from B. It does not separate 3 from B unless the perturbation makes a movement partly self-generated, e.g. an active head turn during imposed body rotation. A switch predicts all-or-none errors per condition; a sum predicts errors scaled by the self-generated fraction ([[wiki/entities/head-direction-system.md]], brainstorm).

**Input-stage data** ([[wiki/entities/head-direction-system.md]]): dorsal-tegmental angular-head-velocity tuning changes between active and passive rotation, in both directions across cells, while anterodorsal heading under head-fixed passive rotation does not change. One hand-held study (struggling rats) plus unpublished head-fixed recordings, so it does not move the status.

## The posterior route, measured (Dale & Cullen 2017)

The source this row cites for B's mechanism ([[wiki/entities/head-direction-system.md]]). It records macaque ventral posterior lateral thalamus, which is the **perception** reader of the vestibular nuclei, not the heading reader.

| Finding | Bearing on the row |
|---|---|
| Active rotation and translation ~80% cancelled. In combined motion only the passive component is encoded (0.39 vs 0.35 (spk/s)/(°/s) alone, n.s.) | B's premise holds one stage above the vestibular nuclei: a downstream reader receives the external residual, and the self-generated part is subtracted per component |
| Neck command with the head blocked: no change in firing | The cancellation needs proprioceptive confirmation. A command alone does nothing, so there is no negative image |
| Cortex must rebuild active self-motion from several modalities (the authors' Fig. 7A) | B's reconstruction problem is posed for the perception route by the same authors. Nobody has measured it for the heading route |

It does not decide the row, for two reasons. The heading route might tap a stage or cell group upstream of the cancellation (Position A, mechanism 2). And nobody has recorded `AD` or postsubiculum under these conditions. **It adds a discriminating probe** (brainstorm): an attempted head turn under restraint. Heading that shifts means the integrator adds an ungated efference prediction, which is B in its plain form. Heading that holds is consistent with A, or with a B in which the prediction is gated by confirmation, as in `VPL`. The probe cannot separate those last two. It is cheaper than the `Closes when` design and complements it.

## The cerebellar stage on the same path (Dugué et al. 2017)

Freely moving rats, caudal vermis (nodulus/uvula), 86 units ([[wiki/entities/head-direction-system.md]]). Independent of the Taube and Cullen labs.

| Finding | Bearing on the row |
|---|---|
| Most Purkinje cells code head rotation with a tilt-dependent axis; a subset codes rotation about gravity-fixed axes | A second transformation on the input path, separate from cancellation: the integrator needs rotation **about gravity**, `ĝ·ω`, under A and B alike. Neither position states which frame its input is in |
| Firing predicted from active sessions differs from firing predicted from passive ones (`p` = 0.0021, n = 17) | Active/passive dependence reaches the transform's own cells. Weak: passive motion was hand-generated, lower in frequency and narrower in tilt, and the authors give that as an alternative cause |
| Peak sensitivity at positive lags | Sensory-driven, not a motor command. No efference signal is identified in these cells |

It does not move the row: no heading cells, no perturbation of the motor-expectation route, and the active/passive difference is not separated from the kinematic mismatch. It adds a requirement to the `Closes when` design. Active and passive rotations must be matched in **tilt** as well as velocity, or a frame-transform difference will read as an active/passive difference.

## Status

`LIVE`. The source states this as an open conundrum and has no data that decides it. Shinder & Taube 2014 (same lab) also leaves it open. Two qualifications. The cancellation data are mostly monkey and the heading data mostly rat. And the one species difference reported (mouse vestibular-only neurons carry a static neck-position signal unchanged between active and passive motion) offers a partial answer for rodents that does not hold in primates. That answer is weaker than it looks: during active rotation those mouse cells encode **head-on-body** rotation (Medrea & Cullen 2013, via Shinder & Taube 2014), and heading is head-in-world, independent of neck angle. Heading under active versus passive motion is itself mixed: attenuated passive responses in restrained rats, matched responses in head-fixed anterodorsal recordings. Shinder & Taube 2014 (J. Neurophysiol.) compares passive rotation with no motion, not active with passive, so it does not decide the row. It only repeats A's point that passive vestibular input alone keeps full tuning contrast, which falls by about half once the input stops. Dale & Cullen 2017 shows the cancellation reaching the thalamic perception reader, gated by proprioceptive confirmation. It records no heading cells, so the row stays `LIVE`. Dugué et al. 2017 finds the cerebellar head→gravity stage on the same input path coding active and passive motion differently, with kinematics confounded and no heading cells recorded; the row stays `LIVE`.
