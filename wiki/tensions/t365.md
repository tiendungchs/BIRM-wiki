# T365 — Do topological bottlenecks make the right subgoals, or the wrong ones?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One task graph is decomposed twice — once by the maximum-evidence partition, once by the leading eigenpurposes of its Laplacian — and both option sets are scored on the *same two* measures with the same option count: (i) trials to discover the optimal policy of a randomly drawn task, and (ii) diffusion time under a uniformly random policy over options plus primitives. Position A predicts the bottleneck set wins both; Position B predicts it wins (i) and loses (ii).

## Position A

**The optimal decomposition cuts at bottlenecks.** The option inventory maximising the Bayesian model evidence of the behaviours an agent must produce is provably also the one minimising code length and the geometric-mean number of trial-and-error attempts to discover a randomly drawn task or subtask, and on the four-rooms grid that optimum is the **doorway** partition; the Tower of Hanoi optimum is the three positions of the largest disk. Humans pick bottleneck vertices at 4.4× the chance rate with degree, exposure and salience equalised, and plan through the parse region-first (Solway et al. 2014, [[wiki/concepts/optimal-hierarchy-criterion.md]]). The graph-partitioning family behaves the same way when built: InfoMap communities over a learned transition matrix never straddle true rooms, and top-down planning through them gives paths 25% shorter than flat Dijkstra while size-matched room-ignoring surrogates give paths 35% longer ([[wiki/entities/cscg.md]]).

## Position B

**Bottleneck options are worse than no options for exploration.** In the same four-rooms domain, a doorway-only option set *raises* diffusion time — the expected number of steps a random walk needs to get between two random states — **above the primitive-only random walk**, because an agent shuttling between doorways rarely reaches the outer corners and each sampled option undoes dozens of primitive steps. The eigenpurpose decomposition of the same graph does not find doorways first: it finds **room centres**, and the first option terminating at a doorway is the fifth. Eigenoptions beat doorway options on reward accrual whenever the goal is not itself near a doorway, and the gain is attributable to the diffusion model rather than to temporal extension, since random-terminal-state options raise diffusion time by orders of magnitude (Machado, Bellemare & Bowling 2017, [[wiki/concepts/eigenoption-discovery.md]]).

## Where it bites

`G33` currently leans on the bottleneck reading: the graph-partitioning family is the row's most-built answer and Solway is its normative licence. If Position B holds, that licence is **scoped to planning**, and a system that discovers its own subgoals before it has ever seen a reward is being scored on the wrong axis — because in the pre-reward regime the only thing options can do is explore. The distinction the two positions imply is sharp and unremarked in either source:

| Regime | What an option is for | Which set wins |
|---|---|---|
| Post-reward, graph known | Shortcut a search that already has a target | Bottleneck (A) |
| Pre-reward, graph being estimated | Cover the state space so the target is *found* | Spectral / graded-timescale (B) |

If that split is the resolution, then no single option library serves both and an architecture needs a **retirement and re-derivation schedule** — which nothing in the wiki has (see the stale-option-library failure mode on [[wiki/concepts/temporal-abstraction-options.md]]). If instead the diffusion-time result is an artefact of the *uniformly random* policy over options and primitives — an evaluation choice, not a property of the option set — Position A survives intact and the metric, not the inventory, is what needs replacing.

## Status

`LIVE` — the two sources measure different quantities on the same domain and neither runs the other's. Machado et al. cite Solway et al. approvingly and claim their experiments "confirm this result" while proposing diffusion time as a *different* metric, which is precisely the conflation the row isolates: minimising trials-to-discover and minimising diffusion time are not shown to be the same optimum, and on doorway options they visibly disagree. Adjacent rows: `G33` (nothing decomposes a task into subgoals), `G61` (exploration is always an external schedule — Position B makes the option set itself the exploration mechanism).
