# T130 — In a gated working memory, is the basal ganglia a write-enable or the maintenance loop itself?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The distinguishing dual-task simulation is run — cheap, stated, and never done: a write-enable that drops out and a loop that carries maintenance differ under concurrent load.

## Position A

**A write-enable that then lets go.** The module releases a reverberating loop and drops out — either a thalamo-cortical loop (Monchi et al. 2000; FROST, Ashby et al. 2005) or a cortico-cortical one whose switch it flips (Frank et al. 2001; Moustafa & Maida 2007; [[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]). Four of the five reviewed working-memory models take this position, and the cortico-cortical variant has an argument behind it: for thalamus to carry maintenance it would need as many dedicated cells as prefrontal cortex has, which is implausible (Frank et al. 2001)

## Position B

**The loop runs through it.** In Schroll et al. 2012 the reverberation passes through the direct pathway, so the module *is* the maintenance mechanism, with the hyperdirect pathway supplying the reset that ends it. This is the most recent and most capable of the five — the only one that both learns what deserves maintenance and simulates delayed alternation and 1-2-AX — and it departs from the others without arguing for the departure (Helie et al. 2013)

## Where it bites

Decides whether gating capacity is consumed for the whole retention interval. Under A, interference between maintenance and concurrent action selection scales with the *rate of writes*; under B, with the *number of items held*. No wiki architecture represents the second cost at all, and a transformer's key/value cache is position A taken to its limit

## Status

`LIVE` — no behavioural or lesion datum in the review separates them; the distinguishing dual-task simulation is cheap and has not been run
