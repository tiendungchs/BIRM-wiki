# T290 — Is the human-unique lateralisation an adaptation for the functions that are lateralised, or a consequence of being large?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Degree of **functional** lateralisation is plotted against brain size across primates with the function held fixed. The *structural* half of this is now done (Ardesch et al. 2022, below); what remains is the function term.

## Position A

**Adaptation.** Population-level left-hemisphere dominance for language, ~90% right-handedness for precision tools, and a human-unique interhemispheric asymmetry in *neuropil fraction* in Broca's area, Wernicke's area, M1-hand and V1 — none of which is found in chimpanzee area Tpt or M1-hand. The asymmetry is specific to computationally demanding, human-specific functions

## Position B

**Consequence of scale.** Ringo et al. 1994: interhemispheric conduction delay makes bilateral duplication of a time-critical computation expensive as a brain enlarges, so specialisation is predicted from size alone. Supporting the prior claim: planum temporale leftward asymmetry is present in chimpanzees, bonobos, gorillas, orangutans **and macaque area Tpt**, and inferior-frontal sulcal asymmetry in all great apes — so the asymmetries pre-date language by tens of millions of years and language landed on a scaffold that was already there. **And the size term is now measured** (Ardesch et al. 2022, [[wiki/concepts/connectivity-scaling-bottleneck.md]]): across 14 primates spanning a 350-fold range in cerebral volume, *structural* connectivity asymmetry between spatially homologous left/right regions rises with brain size (standardised β = +0.73, `p = 5.0 × 10⁻³`, adj. `R² = 0.48`; permutation null `p < 2 × 10⁻¹⁶`), controlled for both connection count and total connection strength, with the same effect on cortical surface area (β = +0.73). The mechanism Ringo predicted is measured alongside it: corpus callosum cross-section scales at `b = 0.88` on cortical surface (sub-isometric), from 1 cm² of callosum per 90 cm² of cortex in the galago to 1 per 211 cm² in humans, and the fraction of long connections falls from 63–71% in small brains to 40% in humans

## Where it bites

Decides whether a machine architecture should be given *designed* functional specialisation or should be expected to develop it from a delay/bandwidth cost, and whether observed specialisation in a large model is evidence of a discovered function or of a wiring economy ([[wiki/concepts/small-world-topology.md]], [[wiki/concepts/integration-segregation-balance.md]])

## Status

`LIVE`, but the balance has shifted toward B — the size→asymmetry regression that was "stated nowhere" now exists across 14 primates, together with its predicted causal antecedent (a sub-isometric callosum and a length distribution shifting short). Two things keep it open. First, Ardesch measures **connectivity-profile asymmetry**, not functional lateralisation: a wiring asymmetry does not by itself say a *computation* is confined to one side, and the human-unique neuropil asymmetries in Broca's and Wernicke's areas remain unaddressed by a whole-hemisphere regression. Second, the function is not held fixed, so the regression cannot separate "bigger brains lateralise" from "bigger-brained species have different functional repertoires". Note the two are not exclusive — B can supply the *opportunity* (scale makes duplication costly) and A the *assignment* (which function occupies the freed hemisphere), in which case the machine-side prediction is that specialisation emerges from the cost and its content is arbitrary until the task distribution fixes it
