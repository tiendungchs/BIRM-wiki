# T290 — Is the human-unique lateralisation an adaptation for the functions that are lateralised, or a consequence of being large?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Adaptation.** Population-level left-hemisphere dominance for language, ~90% right-handedness for precision tools, and a human-unique interhemispheric asymmetry in *neuropil fraction* in Broca's area, Wernicke's area, M1-hand and V1 — none of which is found in chimpanzee area Tpt or M1-hand. The asymmetry is specific to computationally demanding, human-specific functions

## Position B

**Consequence of scale.** Ringo et al. 1994: interhemispheric conduction delay makes bilateral duplication of a time-critical computation expensive as a brain enlarges, so specialisation is predicted from size alone. Supporting the prior claim: planum temporale leftward asymmetry is present in chimpanzees, bonobos, gorillas, orangutans **and macaque area Tpt**, and inferior-frontal sulcal asymmetry in all great apes — so the asymmetries pre-date language by tens of millions of years and language landed on a scaffold that was already there

## Where it bites

Decides whether a machine architecture should be given *designed* functional specialisation or should be expected to develop it from a delay/bandwidth cost, and whether observed specialisation in a large model is evidence of a discovered function or of a wiring economy ([[wiki/concepts/small-world-topology.md]], [[wiki/concepts/integration-segregation-balance.md]])

## Status

`LIVE` — the same source (Sherwood et al. 2008) offers both readings on the same page without separating them, and the discriminating measurement is stated nowhere: no one has plotted degree of functional lateralisation against brain size across primates while holding the function fixed. Note the two are not exclusive — B can supply the *opportunity* (scale makes duplication costly) and A the *assignment* (which function occupies the freed hemisphere), in which case the machine-side prediction is that specialisation emerges from the cost and its content is arbitrary until the task distribution fixes it
