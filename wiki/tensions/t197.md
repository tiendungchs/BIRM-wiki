# T197 — Is un-flagged retrieval error a defect to engineer away or a phenomenon to reproduce?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The two literatures share a metric: an approximate matcher is scored against human reminding proportions *and* ground-truth containment on the same items.

## Position A

**Defect.** The wiki's retrieval mechanisms are scored normatively: [[wiki/entities/neuromatch.md]]'s 49% precision at 97.9 AUROC is recorded as the reason G37 is only `PARTIAL`, and the first missing piece named there is a *certificate* that would let an approximate matcher flag its own errors. On this reading a first stage that discards relational structure is a tractability compromise to be removed as soon as something cheap and structure-sensitive exists (Plate 1993, T185)

## Position B

**Phenomenon.** MAC/FAC states **fallibility** as one of four explicit design criteria ([[wiki/entities/macfac.md]]): "being simple, these models will not always produce the best match ... while this might be a disadvantage in a normative model, it could be an advantage in modelling human similarity-based access". The argument against smart indexing is that it "can predict superhuman access behavior", and the model is validated by *ordinal fit to human error rates* (LS ≈ SF ≫ AN ≈ FOR), beating ARCS — a full constraint-satisfaction network — which got the ordering wrong in two places

## Where it bites

Decides what a reasoning model's retrieval stage is being optimised for. If fallibility is a defect, spend the certificate and the extra structure at stage 1 and accept superhuman access. If it is load-bearing — the "primacy of the mundane" argument is that surface matches are usually the *right* guides to action, and a system that always retrieved the deepest structural analog would be wrong most of the time — then a retrieval stage tuned to maximise structural recall is optimising the wrong quantity, and the missing instrument is a measure of *how often the best structural match is the useful one*, which no source in the wiki reports

## Status

`LIVE` — the two literatures never argue with each other because they never share a metric; MAC/FAC is scored against human proportions and NeuroMatch against ground-truth containment
