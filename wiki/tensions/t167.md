# T167 — Is a self-supervised representation's character set by its objective or by its pair sampler?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The pair-sampler lever is shown transferable beyond one method, modality and metric — and a principle for setting it replaces the search that found the winning setting. *(The first clause is met — three methods, two modalities. The second is half met for augmentation magnitude and unmet for mask/target selection; the prediction-horizon axis is the one that looks rule-settable, since its sweep is one-dimensional with an interior optimum.)*

## Position A

**By the objective.** Everything the wiki carries on gap G34 is a catalogue of *losses* — negatives, variance + covariance, distribution matching, EMA asymmetry — and the whole comparison literature ranks methods by which term they use, with a spread of a few points on ImageNet linear evaluation across the entire family. The pair-construction step is reported as an implementation detail: SimCLR's augmentation list, iBOT's mask ratio, V-JEPA's tubelet mask are stated and not ablated.

## Position B

**By the pair sampler, by a wide margin.** Holding the loss, the architecture, the dataset and the epoch budget fixed and varying only which sub-images become context and target, an image JEPA moves **15.5 → 54.2** on ImageNet-1% linear evaluation — a factor of 3.5, where the entire anti-collapse literature spans a few points (Assran et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/i-jepa.md]]). Two of the losing settings are the masked-image-modelling defaults. The mechanism is two opposite failures: targets predictable by local texture continuation make the objective cheap without content, and a context uninformative about its target makes the conditional mean nearly constant.

**And a second, independent instance now exists in a different method and a different objective** (He et al. 2022, [[wiki/entities/mae.md]]): a pixel-reconstruction masked auto-encoder, ViT-L/16, loss and architecture and data fixed, varying only the mask sampler — random-75% **73.5**, grid 66.0, block-75% **63.9** ImageNet linear probe, with the masking *ratio* alone worth another 54.6 → 73.5. The failure modes are the same two and are legible in the training loss: the grid sampler has the *lower* reconstruction loss and sharper outputs, the block sampler the higher loss and blurrier ones, and both represent worse than the setting between them.

**And the lever finally has a principle, for one of its axes** (Jing et al. 2022, [[wiki/entities/directclr.md]]). For a linear model under InfoNCE, gradient flow is governed by `X = Σ̂₀ − Σ̂₁` — a weighted covariance of the *data* minus a weighted covariance of the *augmentation* — and wherever `Σ̂₁` exceeds `Σ̂₀`, `X` has a negative eigenvalue and that direction's singular value vanishes. So "too strong an augmentation" is not a tuning intuition but a per-direction inequality, and the failure it names is a rank deficit in the embedding, verified numerically by sweeping an additive-noise amplitude `k`. It covers augmentation *magnitude* only — not which sub-image becomes the context, which is B's actual 15.5 → 54.2 result — and it is proved for one linear layer.

**And the wiki's oldest instance turns out to predate both, in a fourth modality and on a different axis of the lever** (van den Oord et al. 2018, [[wiki/entities/cpc.md]]). Loss, encoder, autoregressor, dataset and budget fixed; vary only **how far ahead** the contrastive score predicts: 2/4/8/12/16 steps → 28.5 / 57.6 / 63.6 / **64.6** / 63.8 on LibriSpeech phone classification. A factor of 2.3 from one scalar in pair construction, with the same two opposite failures as B's other instances — too short and local smoothness solves the objective, too long and the target is unpredictable. A second sweep in the same table varies only **where the negatives are drawn from** (mixed-speaker 64.6, same-speaker 65.5, within-sequence-only 65.2, mixed-speaker with the current sequence excluded 57.3), i.e. 7.3 points from the noise distribution with the loss unchanged. Two things this adds that the image cases could not: the modality clause of the `Closes when` is met (speech, and the RL and text sections of the same paper use the same lever), and the horizon is the **only axis of the lever so far that is a scalar with a shape** — an interior optimum in a one-dimensional sweep is the kind of object a rule could set, where "which sub-image is the context" and "which mask pattern" are not.

## Where it bites

Where a designer spends effort, and what an emergence claim is a claim *about*. If B holds, the wiki's coefficient-count preference ordering over anti-collapse families ranks the smaller lever, and every "objective X produces representation Y" statement needs the pair distribution reported alongside — an eighth audit item for [[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]]. It also reopens G32 from the self-supervised side: the mask sampler *is* the experience stream, and nothing designs it. Home page for this row: [[wiki/concepts/representational-collapse.md]].

## Status

`LEANING B` — **three** methods now, with three losses (latent L2, pixel MSE, InfoNCE), three anti-collapse regimes (EMA asymmetry, none, negatives) and two modalities (image, speech), all internally controlled and all showing the same two opposite failure modes; the magnitudes span 2.3× to 3.5×, against a few points for the entire anti-collapse literature. The modality clause of the `Closes when` is met; the metric is still a frozen-feature probe in every case. The counter-consideration is unchanged and is now doubled: *both* winning settings were found by search, so B says the lever is large in two places, not that anyone knows how to set it. The MAE case adds one thing the I-JEPA case could not — the objective value is **anti-correlated** with the representation across the sampler rows, so the loss cannot even be used to search the lever it is most sensitive to.
