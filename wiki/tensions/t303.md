# T303 — Is the striatum an action-selection module, or a prediction-error stage *below* prefrontal cortex?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A recording or lesion study types corticostriatal input by layer of origin *and* asks whether spiny-neuron activity tracks a selected option or a residual — i.e. whether striatal firing scales with the *mismatch* between a prefrontal contextual prediction and the sensory evidence, or with the identity of the option about to be released.

## Position A

**A selector.** The module reads cortical ensembles and releases one action program by focused disinhibition of its thalamic target, opponent D₁/D₂ populations pushing the tonically inhibitory output in both directions ([[wiki/entities/basal-ganglia.md]], Gerfen & Surmeier 2011). Every architecture in the wiki takes this reading: [[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]] as a learned write-enable on prefrontal stripes, [[wiki/entities/c-ts-model.md]] as the gate that binds a context to a task-set, T130 arguing only about *how much* of maintenance runs through it. Selection is the primitive; the disinhibition is what the circuit is for.

## Position B

**An error stage, and a low one.** Because basal-ganglia output goes to *thalamus*, the module sits hierarchically **below** prefrontal cortex, which makes the prefrontal→striatal projection a *backward* connection in the predictive-coding sense — and the densest corticostriatal projection does originate in layer V, the deep-pyramidal source predictive coding assigns to predictions. Medium spiny neurons then occupy the prediction-error slot, targeted by backward connections and gated by D₁/D₂ receptors exactly as an error unit gated by precision should be. Focused disinhibition is not a primitive here but a *consequence* of winnerless-competition dynamics on hidden causes — the only kind of dynamics that can prescribe a unique sequential trajectory (Friston et al. 2012, [[wiki/entities/affordance-active-inference-model.md]]).

## Where it bites

What a builder wires the module as. Under A it is a gate on a channel, and its input is whatever the cortex proposes; the design question is how the gate is trained. Under B it is a subtraction node inside a hierarchy, its input is a *top-down prediction* from the level above, and the design question is what precision its residual carries — which is the same register T122 disputes, one level down. The two also disagree about direction of information flow on the same anatomical edge: A reads prefrontal→striatal as a command, B reads it as a prediction to be cancelled. B additionally predicts **antagonistic** dopaminergic control of prefrontal cortex (ventral tegmental area) and striatum (substantia nigra), since the balance between levels is what a hierarchical inference is sensitive to — a segregation of the cells of origin that A has no reason to expect.

## Status

`LIVE` — the layer-V finding is consistent with B and is not evidence *for* it, since a command pathway may equally originate in layer V. No wiki source records striatal activity against a quantity that separates a residual from a selection. Adjacent rows: **T130** (write-enable versus maintenance loop) presupposes A on both sides; **T122** is the same precision question one level up.
