# T394 — What makes map switching discrete: a posterior over hidden states, or a binding to a preconfigured attractor's coordinates?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One model predicts, from the *same* observable, both the Colgin training-history dissociation (abrupt vs gradual under an identical probe) and the graded/partial remapping regimes — or a manipulation dissociates them: hold the inferred partition fixed and vary the coordinate assignment, or the reverse.

## Position A

**Discreteness is inference.** Map identity is a hidden state inferred under a Chinese Restaurant Process prior; the four remapping categories are not kinds but points on one axis of log posterior odds, and an abrupt transition is what a confident posterior looks like when swept across a decision boundary ([[wiki/entities/hidden-state-inference-remapping.md]], Sanders, Wilson & Gershman 2020). The account is agnostic about substrate — "the attractor network describes *how*, this describes *why*" — and its prediction for the morph disagreements is that they are the partial and global regimes of one continuum, reproducible within a lab by probing early versus late in training

## Position B

**Discreteness is geometry.** There is one preconfigured continuous attractor map; the only thing learned is the binding of external cues to coordinates on it, and a jump occurs when the balance of cue evidence tips between two *sufficiently separated* stored coordinates. Where the two endpoints were bound to the same coordinates no separation exists to jump between, and the code changes only in rate (Colgin et al. 2010, [[wiki/concepts/path-integration.md]], [[wiki/concepts/attractor-dynamics.md]]). Evidence: an identical morph probe gives a coherent midpoint jump after corridor training (CA3 sigmoid slope 35.2 ± 12.4, 30/34 fields at one transition point) and a linear rate-only trend after single-location training (slope 7.4 ± 2.2, scattered transitions), and the separation survives moving both enclosures to a common location

## Where it bites

Whether a machine store's allocate-vs-reuse decision (`G38`) belongs in a **latent-variable module** that scores partitions of experience, or in the **address space** as a consequence of how far apart two anchors were placed. The two make opposite demands. Position A needs an explicit posterior, a prior with a concentration parameter, and a store that can be told to split or merge; the discreteness is computed and is available to the rest of the system as a number. Position B needs no inference at all — the store is continuous throughout and discreteness is an emergent property of anchor separation, so nothing ever reports that a new state was allocated, and the decision is made once, at first encounter, by whatever the integrator happened to read ([[wiki/concepts/key-value-memory.md]]: A is a decision about values, B is a fact about keys)

## Status

`LIVE` — the two are not formally incompatible: a coordinate assignment could be the *implementation* of a posterior that has already committed, which is Position A's own stated relationship to attractor accounts. What blocks the merge is that they disagree about the controlling variable of the one experiment both address. A predicts the Wills/Leutgeb divergence is a training-stage effect on a continuum of posterior confidence; B produced both outcomes at the same training stage by manipulating locomotion between enclosures, and reports the resulting separation *persisting against* a later contradicting observation — a binding that refuses correction, which is not what a running posterior does. Neither model has been run on the other's design
