# T35 — Is remapping controlled by the environment or by the animal's inference history?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Remapping type is predicted from inference history against a protocol-based prediction on the same manipulation, in a design that survives the O'Keefe/Moser replication failure.

## Position A

**The environment.** The standard reading of the literature assigns remapping types to manipulation types: global remapping corresponds to a change of physical location, rate remapping to a change of condition at the same location (Leutgeb 2005a; Colgin 2010; Alme 2014; Lisman 2017), which is what makes an experimental protocol a valid instrument **A-side evidence with the control B's argument needs, in one designed experiment.** Where the map *breaks* is set by the environment and demonstrably not by the animal's behaviour or motivation: in a hairpin maze the entorhinal-hippocampal map resets at every compartment boundary, but running the identical zig-zag trajectory in the same box with the internal walls removed produces no reset at all, and the phase is held from the open field (`r = 0.49 ± 0.03`); transparent walls fragment the map as thoroughly as opaque ones, and moving the reward from every arm to the last arm changes nothing (`r = 0.54` vs `0.62`). So trajectory stereotypy, turning, visual access and reward schedule are all excluded by design, leaving physical partition (Derdikman et al. 2009, `T347`). This does not rescue the protocol→remapping-type mapping B attacks — it is a different dependent variable — but it is the wiki's cleanest demonstration that *some* map change is environment-locked rather than history-locked. **And the replication failure B leans on is not one** (Colgin et al. 2010): it is a two-arm designed experiment that produced Wills's abrupt transition and Leutgeb's gradual one *in the same lab, on the same morph-day protocol*, separated only by whether the two enclosures had earlier been placed apart and joined by a walkable corridor. That removes B's strongest single datum and replaces it with a third position — the controlling variable is neither the environment at test nor the animal's whole inference history but the state of **one internal variable at first encounter**, the path-integrator coordinate the enclosure was bound to ([[wiki/concepts/path-integration.md]], `G39`, `T394`). It is history-dependent, as B says, and it is a single manipulable event with a named mechanism, which is what B denies.

## Position B

**The inference history.** Global remapping occurs between conditions at the *same* location, rate remapping between *different* locations, and the *same* manipulation produces both at different points in training (Lever 2002). Worse, the O'Keefe and Moser labs get opposite results from comparable protocols, and Colgin et al. 2010's attempted exact replication of Wills et al. 2005 reversed it. Sanders et al. 2020 conclude that remapping responds to a large array of experiential factors of which the experimenter manipulates a subset, so cross-lab comparison of remapping extent is invalid ([[wiki/entities/hidden-state-inference-remapping.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether "did it remap?" can be used as a controlled dependent variable at all — and whether an engineered analogue should expose an allocate/reuse decision to the designer or accept that it is set by the full training history

## Status

`LEANING` — the reconciliation (categories are one continuum in state uncertainty) is still B's, and B retains the cross-lab protocol disagreements. But the two props under the earlier reading are gone: the Colgin/Wills contradiction is a designed dissociation rather than a replication failure, and A is no longer without an account of the training-dependence — it has a specific one, in which the fitted cue→coordinate binding *persists against* a later contradicting observation (moving both enclosures to a common location did not merge the maps). Whether that binding account and the hidden-state posterior are rivals or the same thing at two levels of description is `T394`
