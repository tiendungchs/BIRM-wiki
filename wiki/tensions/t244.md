# T244 — Is the cortex a set of units with borders, or a continuum with axes?

**Status:** `BOTH`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Neither — the row is `BOTH` by construction: borders and gradients are complementary projections, and it closes only if one is shown to be derivable from the other.

## Position A

**Units with borders.** The parcellation tradition from Brodmann and the Vogts to HCP-MMP1.0: areas have distinct internal architecture, distinct distant-connectivity fingerprints, and borders sharp enough that a multimodal classifier can find them in an individual — and lesion evidence (Broca, Wernicke, Lichtheim) says function is lost in pieces.

## Position B

**A continuum with axes.** Bailey and von Bonin, Lashley and Clark historically; today, gradients: the principal axis of intrinsic functional organisation runs unimodal → transmodal with no borders in it, tracks cortical expansion in primate evolution and genetic/transcriptomic gradients, and a tertiary axis juxtaposes the default mode network against the multiple-demand network. Function and microstructure vary *within* regions along more than one axis, and finer parcellations cannot fix it.

## Where it bites

Whether a reasoning architecture's units should be a partition or an embedding — and whether "which module is this?" is even the right query ([[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]])

## Status

`BOTH` — and not as a compromise; the regimes are two complementary projections, not two claims about one fact. Bijsterbosch et al. 2021: visual areas have well-defined borders *and* gradual connectivity gradients riding on top of them along the dorsal and ventral streams; the representations are complementary projections, not rival ontologies. The live problem is that a result obtained in one representation has no clear implication for the other, and no comparative benchmarking exists ([[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]])

A third representation, from one scale down, is consistent with both and reduces to neither: **serial homology** ([[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]], Harris & Shepherd 2015). Areas hold the same inventory of genetically defined cell classes wired in the same pattern, and differ *quantitatively* — cell counts, synaptic strengths, ion-channel expression, and the elaboration of layer 4 by its own afferents. That yields borders where a class inventory changes discretely (layer-4 cytoarchitecture, thalamic input type) and gradients where only the scalars change, with the graded transcription-factor expression across the cortical sheet as a shared developmental cause of both. It also predicts that neither projection is the natural unit for a model: the unit is a class-typed circuit template plus an areal parameter vector (`T371`)
