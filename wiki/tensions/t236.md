# T236 — Does converting a spike train into a continuous function before measuring error destroy the timing code?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The van Rossum result is reproduced at sub-millisecond precision in a second setup, since one comparison currently carries the whole reversal of the standing reading.

## Position A

**Yes — a smoothed error is a rate-like error.** The wiki's standing reading, carried on [[wiki/concepts/temporal-coding.md]]: of the supervised rules for spike trains, three of four (SpikeProp's kernelised hidden units, SPAN's alpha-kernel regression, ReSuMe's convolved trains) define the discrepancy on a smoothed signal, so they measure a rate-like quantity and inherit exactly the blindness the timing camp exists to argue against; only the Victor–Purpura edit distance scores timing as such (via Tavanaei et al. 2019)

## Position B

**No — and the measurement is direct.** Gardner & Grüning 2016 run the comparison on one setup: exponentially filtering both target and actual trains (`τ_q = 10 ms`, a van Rossum error) gives capacity `α_m = 0.14 ± 0.01` at 1 ms precision against the Victor–Purpura Chronotron's `0.15 ± 0.01`, and still holds ≈0.07 at **0.2 ms** — sub-millisecond, on the smoothed error. The rule that collapses is the *unsmoothed* one (`α_m = 0.07`, and exactly zero below 0.8 ms), for a structural reason: an instantaneous error's attractor sits at the threshold-crossing weight, a property of the postsynaptic-potential peak that is independent of the target time. Longer filtering also *lowers* the achievable latency floor (`τ_q = 0` → 7 ms, `τ_q = 10 ms` → 3 ms)

## Where it bites

Whether the wiki should treat kernel-based losses as second-class. Position B says the real axis is different — not smoothed vs. combinatorial but whether the error at time `t` knows about spikes at other times — which reclassifies ReSuMe and PSD (instantaneous, and unstable) with INST rather than with SPAN, and predicts that any future rule scoring one spike at a time will fail regardless of how sharp its metric is. Both smoothing schemes remain restricted to a single output neuron, so neither position is tested at depth ([[wiki/concepts/spike-train-error-metrics.md]], [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] `G76`)
