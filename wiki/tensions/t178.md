# T178 — Should a multiscale temporal decomposition be imposed on a sequence model, or left for a deep stack to discover?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Plain S4 is run with a band-stratified `A` and no wavelet front end: recovering the ListOps gain makes the front end redundant and the effect an initialisation (T177).

## Position A

**Discover it.** [[wiki/entities/s4.md]] puts one initialisation interval on one linear time-invariant recurrence, stacks the layer, and the decomposition appears anyway: first-layer kernels cover a few steps of local context while last-layer kernels span all 16,384 positions at varying spatial frequencies, with no hierarchy anywhere in the architecture. That stack is state of the art on all six Long Range Arena tasks and the only model above chance on Path-X. Nothing was designed in, and the same weights re-tune to a new temporal resolution from one scalar `Δ`.

## Position B

**Impose it.** [[wiki/entities/ms-ssm.md]] installs the hierarchy inside a *single* layer — a learned undecimated wavelet cascade into `S+2` bands, one SSM per band, eigenvalues initialised into disjoint intervals, an input-dependent mixer — at matched parameters and matched total state, and gains **3.4 points on ListOps** over S4 (63.04 vs 59.60) plus the best sCIFAR, ImageNet-1K and PTB-XL numbers in its tables. Its own ablation attributes the largest share of the gain to the multi-resolution convolution rather than to the recurrence.

## Where it bites

How a builder spends depth. If A, multiscale structure is free at sufficient depth and a front end is redundant machinery; if B, depth discovers it *inefficiently* and one layer of imposed structure substitutes for many layers of search. The split in the evidence is the interesting part and neither paper names it: **the imposed hierarchy wins where the task's structure is explicitly nested** (ListOps, pixel-sequence sCIFAR, ECG rhythm-vs-form) **and loses where it is flat and long** (Retrieval, Path-X — MS-SSM's headline S6 variant averages 86.73 on the five non-ListOps LRA tasks against S4's 91.38, S5's 92.52 and Mega's 93.22).

## Status

`LIVE` — and the discriminating experiment is cheaper than either paper's, because the two mechanisms MS-SSM ships are separable and it never separates them: run plain S4 with a **band-stratified** `A` and *no* wavelet front end. If it recovers the ListOps gain, the front end is redundant and the whole effect is T177's, i.e. an initialisation. If it does not, imposed band-limiting is doing work that no initialisation can do, and A is refuted for nested tasks. A second confound to clear first: MS-SSM's LRA baselines are quoted from a third source (Qin et al. 2024) and its two variants disagree by 5.2 points in opposite directions across the two task families.
