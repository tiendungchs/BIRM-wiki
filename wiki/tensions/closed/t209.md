# T209 — Should an efficiency metric charge for thinking?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The metric charges for deliberation, or a system with unbounded per-action thinking is shown not to score maximally efficient under the action-only rule.

## Position A

**No — actions are the resource.** ARC-AGI-3 counts only environment-changing actions; "internal operations that do not alter the environment, such as tool calls, reasoning steps, or retries within the model itself, are not counted". The stated justification is that action efficiency subsumes data, risk and time efficiency into one scalar that is directly comparable between a biological and an artificial agent, and that it penalises brute-force interaction, which is the failure mode the benchmark is built against. The environment is turn-based for the same reason: the intended difficulty is reasoning, not reflex ([[wiki/entities/arc-agi-3.md]])

## Position B

**Yes — a score attaches to (approach, compute budget) and never to an approach.** T204's claim, from ARC's own 2024 report, and the reason version 2 made cost-per-task a mandatory reporting column on every line. Under an action-only metric a system with unbounded per-action deliberation is maximally efficient by construction, and the exact strategy the metric is meant to penalise — search — simply moves inside the turn, where it is free. The benchmark's own cost axis (dollars per run, X-axis on the leaderboard) and its 5×-human-median action budget are administrative caps, not part of the score

## Where it bites

Decides what "efficiency" names in the wiki's scoring vocabulary. The two metrics are measuring genuinely different resources — interaction with the world versus computation about it — and a human's 2.3-minute median on version 2 bounds *both* at once, which is why the human baseline reads as a single number and an AI's does not. **(brainstorm)** The repair is to report the pair rather than pick one: RHAE alongside compute-per-action, since a system matching human action counts at 10⁴× human deliberation has demonstrated planning and not efficiency, and the current metric cannot tell those apart
