# T36 — Does map selection cause context-dependent behaviour?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The designed experiment is run: two groups trained to remap or not to the same manipulation, verified neurally, then tested for fear-memory generalisation.

## Position A

**Yes — the field's working assumption**, and the wiki's: remapping is the neurophysiological substrate of context-dependent learning (Colgin et al. 2008), which is what licenses treating map retrieval as the mechanism behind context-appropriate action. Correlational support exists (Kentros 2004; Kennedy & Shapiro 2009)

## Position B

**Unproven, and there is a counterexample.** Sanders et al. 2020 state that no causal demonstration exists (see also Kubie et al. 2019), and Jeffery et al. 2003 report task-performance *transfer* between two conditions showing near-global remapping — behaviour generalising exactly where the code did not

## Where it bites

The whole retrieval-and-allocation story: if a separated map does not gate behaviour, then map identity is a representational bookkeeping device and the behavioural context-effects are computed somewhere else. Also decides whether [[wiki/concepts/representation-probing.md]]'s decoded-vs-used split applies to the hippocampus as it does to transformers
