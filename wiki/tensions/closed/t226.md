# T226 — What is a benchmark's error rate, when two audits of the same 2,500 items disagree by a factor of 1.7 — and can a model-in-the-loop audit measure its own effect on model scores?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Either cheap control is run: the revised-answer/plurality-pass@8 agreement rate against its base rate, or a blind repair of a random sample with a model-output-free third team.

## Position A

**15.4%, by the authors' own multi-reviewer-with-rebuttal protocol.** Phan et al. 2025/2026 ([[wiki/entities/hle.md]]) sample 400 items across two rounds, have students solve them in full, and route every flagged error between organisers, original authors and auditors until consensus — an item counts as bad only if the author cannot defend it. The rebuttal round exists precisely to stop a single dissenting reviewer from manufacturing an error, and the same paper shows dropping it inflates the rate on a subset from 18% to 25%. On this reading the higher external number is a *protocol artefact* of an auditor with no author in the loop.

## Position B

**~26% scoring-relevant, 45.7% repairable, 27.6% indeterminate.** Zhai et al. 2026 ([[wiki/entities/hle-verified.md]]) audit **all** 2,500 items component-wise and three-valued, and find only 668 usable unmodified. The scoring-relevant figure is not asserted but derived, and it is unusually robust: the ratio Δ(full set)/Δ(repaired subset) is 0.24–0.29 for each of seven models computed independently, which is hard to produce from an over-eager auditor. Its own weakness is the mirror image of A's: the flagging stage is pass@8 frontier solvers and the repair stage is model-proposed and model-adjudicated, while the reported success metric is **frontier-model accuracy** — so items where the models are confidently and uniformly wrong are exactly the ones at risk of being repaired into agreement, and the post-repair clustering of seven models into a nine-point band is the signature that would show it.

## Where it bites

Every number in the wiki of the form "benchmark X self-reports an error rate of Y". If the rate is a function of the audit's reviewer count, rebuttal rule, unit of judgement (item vs component) and label arity (binary vs three-valued), then `F12` is not a caveat to attach to a benchmark but a statement that **the quantity is undefined until the protocol is specified**, exactly as [[wiki/concepts/human-baseline.md]] concluded for the human denominator (T210/T213/T214 read as one defect). It also decides whether I18-style repair is a fix or a contamination channel: a benchmark repaired by the systems it scores has no remaining developer-blindness on the repaired items.

## Status

`LIVE`. Two cheap controls, neither run, and either settles it: (i) report the fraction of revised items whose new answer equals the plurality pass@8 model answer, against the base rate on gold items the models also failed — the pass@8 outputs are already collected; (ii) repair a random sample **blind**, with a third expert team given no model outputs, and report key agreement with the model-assisted repair.
