# T264 — Is default-mode anticorrelation a neural phenomenon or a product of global signal regression?

**Status:** `RESOLVED`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Anticorrelation magnitude is reported with *and* without global signal regression on the same data and regions — no source currently does.

## Position A

**Substantially manufactured.** Global-signal regression mean-centres the correlation distribution and therefore *forces* negative values to exist; the anchor review states the caveat about its own anticorrelation result and leaves the magnitude without GSR unknown (Buckner et al. 2008); the connectome-methods review lists GSR among rejected practices because it removes task-related neural signal and "spuriously inflates anticorrelations" (Bijsterbosch et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]); and Gao et al. 2013's own check finds all values shift positive without it, with several rest-state core↔salience correlations losing their negative sign. Fox et al. 2009 supply A's strongest same-data fact against their own conclusion: **before any nuisance regression every seed correlates positively with essentially every voxel**, and at the a priori seeds no significant negative correlation exists without global correction

## Position B

**Neural, and GSR is what makes it detectable.** The global signal is a composite of neural and non-neural sources; removal increases anticorrelations by virtue of mean-centering rather than by fabricating structure, and effects that are not reproducible without GSR "may not be analysis artifacts in as much as they reflect neural features whose detection benefits from removal of shared trends" — with a TMS meta-analysis finding subgenual-ACC connectivity↔treatment-response associations carried specifically by patients with large global-signal fluctuations. Recommended practice: report both, especially for anticorrelation research (Tripathi et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]).

**Primary for B, with the same-data comparison this row asked for** (Fox, Zhang, Snyder & Raichle 2009, J Neurophysiol 101:3270, `n = 17`, 4 × 7 min fixation; [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]). The authors concede the algebra (any global correction makes the voxelwise β map sum to zero, so negatives are mandated per subject) and test what the algebra does *not* fix:

| What GSR does not mandate | Result |
|---|---|
| **Where** the negatives fall | After GSR: seed-specific, on the system with the opposite task response. After post hoc distribution centring (same zero mean): in white matter, the same for every seed — the artefact signature |
| **Cross-subject consistency** | PCC seed: consistent negative regions in the random-effects map. White-matter seed: β maps also sum to zero per subject, no consistent negatives |
| **Survival when the networks are outside the mask** | Global signal recomputed after removing 30–95% of voxels most (anti)correlated with MT+; anticorrelation persists at 80% and is still significant at 95%; a simulation confirms the test separates anticorrelation from independence |
| **Presence before GSR** | Not at the a priori seeds. Qualitatively present at a PCC seed re-centred on positive default-network correlations, at lowered threshold (post hoc) |
| **Lagged-correlation mechanism** (Murphy et al. 2009's route to artefactual negatives) | Cross-correlogram between the two networks before GSR shows no lag |

The global signal itself: significant in every grey-matter voxel, *least* in the task-negative system, most in visual cortex; its measured variance exceeds the average of five systems' variances (`2.51 vs 1.55`, `p < 2.94 × 10⁻⁴`), so it is not just the mean of system signals; removing it raises structure↔function correspondence (see Status)

**The magnitude comparison the row asked for, with no global correction at all** (Chai, Nieto Castañón, Öngür & Whitfield-Gabrieli 2012, NeuroImage 59:1420, `n = 15`, 10 min eyes-open rest, one MPFC seed at MNI (−1, 49, −2); [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]). Non-neural noise is removed by regressing principal components of eroded white-matter and CSF (cerebrospinal fluid) signal (aCompCor), so no grey-matter-derived regressor enters and nothing mandates a zero-sum map:

| Test, same data and regions | GSR | aCompCor, 3–10 noise components |
|---|---|---|
| Group peak `t`, six task-positive regions (L/R DLPFC, L/R IPL, SMA, R FEF) | 7.4–10.8, all significant | 5.4–12.3, all significant (SMA at 3 and FEF at 3 or 5 components pass voxelwise FDR only, not cluster correction) |
| ROI anticorrelation strength | stronger | weaker (`p < .05`), still `p < .001` in all six |
| Null reference (bilateral V1-adjacent spheres, no relation expected) | **artefactual negative** (`t(14) = 3.99`, `p = .001`) | not significant (`p > .14`) |
| Specificity, anticorrelated ROIs | — | no difference (`p > .1`) |
| Positive correlations (PCC, lateral parietal, MPFC) | lower magnitude; lower specificity than 5–10 components | higher |

No negatives appear with motion regression alone. With one noise component only IPL survives voxelwise (ROI level: DLPFC `p < .005`, FEF `p = .03`, SMA `p = .11`), and the null region still shows artefactual coupling (`t(14) = 3.21`) — the sign needs enough non-neural noise removed, not the global mean.

## Where it bites

Whether `G90`'s two-mode competition has any measured basis. The internal/external anticorrelation is the wiki's main evidence that the brain runs two mutually suppressing modes at all; if the negative sign is a centering artefact, what remains is a graded reduction in coupling, and an architecture needs a gain rather than an opponent process. Note the positions are not symmetric: side B does not claim GSR is unbiased, only that the bias is a shift whose direction is known, which makes the dispute settleable by reporting both — and almost no source in this literature does

## Status

`RESOLVED`, toward B on the **sign** and toward A on the **magnitude**. Chai et al. 2012 meet `Closes when` as written: anticorrelation reported with and without GSR on the same data and regions, as voxelwise peak `t` per region. Without any global correction, the six task-positive regions anticorrelate with the medial prefrontal seed once ≥ 3 white-matter/CSF components are removed, and a null reference region stays at zero. With GSR the same regions anticorrelate more strongly, *and* the null region goes negative — so A's charge that GSR inflates and manufactures negatives is confirmed at the null region, and refuted for the default↔task-positive pair. The Fox et al. 2009 crux (is the global component additive?) is bypassed rather than answered: the sign no longer depends on removing it.

Qualifications carried into page prose, not kept as a row. (i) One seed, `n = 15`, one site; ROI tests use masks that are partly the GSR maps (union across methods), so only the voxelwise peaks are independent of GSR. (ii) The noise components could share variance with the global signal; the authors' counter-test (global-signal components decay spurious correlation slower and true correlation faster than aCompCor) is in a supplementary figure lost in conversion. (iii) Not reaching sign is not reaching neural origin: the neural claim rests on cited work (Popa et al. 2009 cat LFP). (iv) Anticorrelation is weaker than positive correlation and fluctuates within a session (Chang & Glover 2010, cited), so the stationary estimate is a time-average. For `G90`: the opponent relation has a measured basis, and GSR's inflation means its strength is over-stated in every GSR-based figure the wiki carries.

**Since closure, qualification (iii) is answered** (Keller et al. 2013, J Neurosci, `n = 5`, resting ECoG; [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]). The 0.1–1 Hz envelope of 50–150 Hz power shows significant anticorrelations with no regression, in 2.7% of electrode pairs against 35.6% positive. They fall preferentially under anticorrelated BOLD: 25.1% of BOLD-anticorrelated pairs, against 10.1% of null-BOLD and 11.4% of positive-BOLD pairs. So the sign has a neuronal counterpart. A side-B fact appears in neural data too. Regressing the mean high-γ signal raises negatives to 30.6% of pairs, so the global component is partly neuronal. GSR on BOLD raises agreement with unregressed high-γ maps (`r = 0.35 → 0.40`) and also creates BOLD negatives with no neural counterpart. That is the same both-ways verdict as Chai et al. 2012, now scored against a non-haemodynamic reference.

Prior state, for the record: `LIVE` after Fox et al. 2009 — same-data comparison existed qualitatively, magnitudes lost in conversion; pro-B tests rested on an additive-global-signal assumption; Gao et al. 2013 found state *changes* survive without GSR while absolute signs do not; on Honey et al. 2009's data GSR raised structure↔function correspondence (all pairs `r = 0.36 vs 0.32`; connected pairs `0.53 vs 0.45`; path length `0.47 vs 0.35`).
