# T136 — Does a hierarchical learner deliver a subgoal-specific reward of its own?

**Status:** `RESOLVED`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A reward-like signal at subgoal attainment is looked for in a hierarchically structured task — the discriminating measurement the source names and nobody has made.

## Position A

**Yes — pseudo-reward.** The options framework and MAXQ give each subtask its own reward function, delivered on reaching that subtask's subgoal, and it is what shapes the option-specific policy when external reward is silent; the framework therefore predicts a reward-like response to *subgoal attainment* in structures responsive to exogenous reward, and phasic dopamine at subtask boundaries scaled by the option-level prediction error (Botvinick, Niv & Barto 2009, [[wiki/concepts/temporal-abstraction-options.md]]).

## Position B

**No.** The HAM framework shapes subtask policies with ordinary exogenous reward plus fixed structural constraints on the machine, and has no pseudo-reward at all; MAXQ additionally decomposes the value function into components rather than keeping one `V_o` per option, so the prediction errors computed in a given domain differ between frameworks.

## Where it bites

It decides whether a machine hierarchical learner needs an *internal reward generator* per subgoal — with all of the specification burden that carries (who sets the subgoal, what its pseudo-reward is worth relative to the real one, what stops the agent from farming it) — or whether structure alone suffices. It also decides whether the flagship neural test is a test of HRL at all: a null result at subgoals refutes options/MAXQ and leaves HAM untouched.

## Status

`RESOLVED` in favour of position A. Ribas-Fernandes et al. 2011 made the measurement ([[wiki/concepts/pseudo-reward-prediction-error.md]]): in a delivery task whose geometry holds distance-to-goal fixed while varying distance-to-subgoal, a displacement *away* from the subgoal produces a fronto-central negativity at Cz (EEG, `n` = 9, `p` < 0.01) and parametric activation in dorsal anterior cingulate cortex and bilateral anterior insula scaling with the error's size (fMRI, `n` = 30), plus habenula and right amygdala at region-of-interest threshold; a displacement *toward* the subgoal activates right nucleus accumbens (fMRI, `n` = 14). A separate behavioural experiment (`n` = 22) shows subgoal distance carries **no** weight in package choice while goal distance carries a large one (`M = −7.6`, `p` < 0.001), which rules out the flat account in which participants attached primary reward to the subgoal (mean Bayes factor 4.31 for the hierarchical model).

Three qualifications the close carries into the prose rather than keeping as a row. (i) The signal measured is the per-step error *en route* to the subgoal, against `V_o`, not the reward-like response *at attainment* that this row's `Closes when` literally names and that the options framework separately predicts — attainment regressors were in the design matrix and are not reported. (ii) Position B is narrowed rather than refuted: HAM remains internally consistent, but it no longer describes this learner. (iii) The result does not separate the pseudo-reward error from the ordinary reward prediction error by anatomy — both appear in the same four structures — which is the live successor question, carried at `L2` by `T367`.
