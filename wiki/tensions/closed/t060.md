# T60 — What substrate defines a large-scale attractor landscape — anatomy or activity covariance?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The functional-coupling model is tested where structure and covariance dissociate — a lesion or a developmental case in which anatomy and activity covariance make opposite predictions.

## Position A

**Anatomy.** The standard whole-brain modelling programme couples neural-mass models through the *structural* connectome (diffusion MRI) and derives dynamics from it; the landscape is a consequence of physical wiring, and structure–function coupling is the thing to be explained (Deco et al. 2012; Golos et al. 2015; Hansen et al. 2015, cited in Englert et al. 2026)

## Position B

**Activity covariance.** Use the *functional* connectome directly as the coupling matrix (`J = −Σ⁻¹`) and skip the structure–function problem entirely: the resulting model has two hyperparameters instead of dozens, converges ~9× faster than degree-preserving permutations of itself, replicates at `r = 0.93` across three scanners, and predicts task and disease perturbations without refitting ([[wiki/entities/fcann.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a builder derives a memory/dynamics landscape from wiring constraints or from observed co-activation. Position B buys accuracy and simplicity and gives up every mechanistic (polysynaptic, biophysical) claim; it also cannot be falsified by the covariance fit alone, since a Boltzmann-form model fitted to covariance reproduces covariance by construction — its evidence has to come from convergence speed, eigenvector alignment, flow and perturbation. The two are not exclusive: the stated next step is to use structure as a *sparsity mask* on `J` and learn the rest
