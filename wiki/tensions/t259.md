# T259 — Is the cortical predictive hierarchy a chain of command, or an apex that broadcasts across levels?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The chain and the broadcast apex are separated on one parcellation: a chained hierarchy with a broadcast top satisfies both, so the test is whether any level below the apex broadcasts.

## Position A

**A chain.** The formalism every predictive architecture in the wiki uses: layer `S_i` couples only to `S_{i−1}` and `S_{i+1}`, predictions descend one level at a time and errors ascend one level at a time ([[wiki/concepts/predictive-coding-free-energy.md]] equation 1); the anatomical support is the laminar feedforward/feedback rule and the SLN%-graded hierarchy ([[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]]), both of which are relations between *adjacent* areas

## Position B

**An orchestra conductor** (Satpute et al. 2026, [[wiki/concepts/broadcast-hierarchy.md]]): the highest system receives **selectively** — proportionally more input from high-level association cortex (eulaminate I) than from sensory areas — while its long-range output is **diffusely distributed across multiple networks and laminar types simultaneously**; and Barbas' laminar type rule (similar laminar profiles connect preferentially) makes level-matched channels run in parallel rather than serially, i.e. a heterarchy. Supporting anatomy: the default mode network spans agranular through eulaminate III with no koniocortex, and is broader in cross-network reach without being more densely connected

## Where it bites

The cost of a global model revision — `O(L)` relays versus one act — and therefore whether an architecture can afford to throw out its current frame mid-stream, which is what a changed goal, a wrong assumption or a new interlocutor demands. Also what the update *scope* variable attaches to: a chain has no way to express "revise every level now" ([[wiki/concepts/event-segmentation.md]])

## Status

`LEANING B for the output side` — and the two claims are not straightforwardly exclusive: a broadcast apex sitting on top of otherwise chained levels satisfies both. Position B no longer rests only on a review with no new data: Paquola et al. 2025 measure it on one parcellation — effective **input** to the default mode network covaries with its cytoarchitectural axis (`r = −0.54`, `P < 0.001`) and structural communication efficiency with it (`r = −0.60`, `P_spin = 0.001`, steepest to koniocortex), while effective **output** does not (`r = −0.18`, n.s.) and is distributed across all six cortical types with the lowest KL divergence from a null of any cortical network. What is still missing is causal: the output balance is a null-model comparison over couplings fitted by a *linear* dynamic causal model with a fixed haemodynamic response, and no measurement of the laminar type of the connections themselves exists

**A third reading, added at the Keller & Mrsic-Flogel 2018 ingest.** Neither a chain nor an apex: cortex is not arranged as a hierarchy at all, and *any* two areas holding a learned transformation between their coordinate systems exchange predictions and errors — in both directions simultaneously, since the direction of a message is a property of the emitting cell class rather than of the edge ([[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]]). Under this reading the chain/apex question is malformed for most of cortex and only well-posed near primary sensory areas, where a hierarchy demonstrably exists. It also admits the reversed variant — predictions ascending, errors descending — as a live architecture.

**A fourth consideration, added at the Rao & Ballard 1999 ingest.** In the linear case the descending feedback term is algebraically interchangeable with recurrent lateral inhibition inside the receiving level (`W = UᵀU`, equation 8). A level able to substitute its own horizontal connectivity for its superior's prediction is not straightforwardly a link in a chain of command, and the substitution is invisible to any dynamical measurement — see [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] **T343**. This does not favour either position; it says the chain, where it exists, may be shorter than the number of areas suggests.

**A fifth consideration, added at the Larkum 2013 ingest.** The dispute has a cellular port. Feedback to any level arrives on horizontal fibres running along layer 1 and terminates on the distal apical tufts of that level's pyramidal cells, where ~90% of the synapses are long-range — and Larkum's account of where those fibres come from is explicitly *not* the level immediately above: "feedback inputs come not only from regions immediately above in the hierarchy but also from many other higher-level cortical and thalamic regions". Because the tuft is a second spike-initiation zone rather than a remote perturbation ([[wiki/concepts/apical-amplification.md]]), an area writing to that port gates the receiving level's output directly, with no relay through the intervening levels. This does not decide between A and B, but it removes the strongest *architectural* argument for A: a chain is not forced by the anatomy of the feedback arm, and the cost of addressing a distant level is one axon rather than `O(L)` relays. What it leaves open is whether the layer-1 traffic actually carries level-skipping *content* or only level-skipping *gain*, which is the same content/gain ambiguity the mechanism itself has.

**A sixth consideration, added at the Markov et al. 2014 ingest** ([[wiki/concepts/counterstream-organization.md]]). Position A's anatomical support was the laminar rule and the SLN%-graded hierarchy, both described above as "relations between *adjacent* areas". The quantitative version of that hierarchy does not support the adjacency reading:

- Descending pathways **outnumber** ascending ones ~2:1 and **cross more levels**, at equal cumulative weight per target area — so most of the descending traffic is level-skipping by construction, and ~60% of neurons in projections longer than 10 mm are feedback.
- SLN is **continuous and approximately uniformly distributed** above 0.1 with no clustering, so there are no discrete levels for a chain to step through; the fitted levels carry ±1–2 level confidence intervals.
- A cell may carry the same descending message to several targets — double-labelling among purely feedback populations rises from 6% to **30%** with hierarchical distance — while a cell essentially never carries one message up and another down (<1–2%). Divergence is a property of the descending arm specifically.

This is direct quantitative support for the *output* side of Position B on ordinary visual cortex, not only on the default mode network, and it does so without needing an apex: a level-skipping broadcast arm is a property of the descending stream at every depth. What it does not settle is content versus gain, and it adds a constraint the broadcast reading has to absorb — the long-range descending stream originates from layer 6, whose cells have no apical tuft in layer 1, so the broadcaster does not itself listen on the port it writes to ([[wiki/concepts/apical-amplification.md]]).
