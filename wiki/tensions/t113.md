# T113 — Is a control-band oscillation the control resource, or the receipt of one?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A theta-band-targeted manipulation with a matched off-band control is run — the stimulation was at 50–130 Hz and theta was only measured.

## Position A

**A resource whose amount matters.** Raising the gain of the human control loop (ventral internal capsule/ventral striatum deep brain stimulation) raises task-induced prefrontal theta power, and the per-subject theta increase tracks the per-subject response-time improvement on a conflict task and predicts clinical outcome (`n = 8, r = 0.76, p = 0.03`, AUC 0.87) — the authors' framing is that "amplifying those oscillations facilitates control", and their proposed clinic titrates stimulation *to the theta biomarker* (Widge et al. 2019, [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]])

## Position B

**A receipt of a process running elsewhere.** Theta-band coupling is precisely the component that *survives* cutting the direct projection between two regions, so in the wiki's one causal decomposition theta indexes a clock supplied by a third structure rather than the content or drive on the link (Spellman et al. 2015; Yamamoto et al. 2014, [[wiki/concepts/inter-areal-synchrony.md]]); and in monkey prefrontal–hippocampal recordings the *band*, not the amount, codes outcome (Brincat & Miller 2015)

## Where it bites

Whether a machine controller should have a scalar "control amount" worth reading out and optimising, or whether band power is a coordination artefact and the transferable variable is timing. Under A a closed-loop system can titrate control to a measured oscillation, which is the entire clinical proposal; under B such a system tunes to an epiphenomenon and any behavioural gain came from something the stimulation also did

## Status

`LIVE` — the two are not directly opposed (power at one site is not coupling between two), and the decisive design has not been run: the stimulation was delivered at 50–130 Hz and theta was only ever *measured*, so a theta-band-targeted manipulation with a matched off-band control is what would separate the readings. What the human result does establish independently of the band question is the gain claim itself — control performance rose with no change to the task model
