# T109 — Is medial premotor cortex (SMA/pre-SMA) part of the arbitrary stimulus→action binding network?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A medial premotor ablation is run on a *higher-order* mapping task — sets rather than single cues — which nobody has done; until then neither row should be cited as settled.

## Position A

**No.** Monkey ablation of medial premotor cortex — supplementary motor plus anterior cingulate motor areas — leaves arbitrary visuomotor mapping essentially intact; the region sits in the review's explicit *specificity* list, the one that makes the positive network a network rather than a list of everything (Wise & Murray 2000, [[wiki/concepts/arbitrary-sensorimotor-mapping.md]])

## Position B

**Yes, and centrally.** Human fMRI of higher-order categorical stimulus→response learning finds midline SMA/pre-SMA in every subject as one of four acquisition-selective regions; its activity declines across runs only while the rule is being acquired, is greater for a learnable set than for a matched ruleless one, correlates negatively with trial-by-trial accuracy, and its blockwise coactivation with dorsolateral prefrontal cortex accounts for **63%** of between-subject variance in accuracy — the largest effect reported (Boettiger & D'Esposito 2005)

## Where it bites

Whether an architecture needs a separate module holding the *set of candidate responses* on which the controller's bias acts, or whether the controller can bias premotor/striatal storage directly. Position B assigns SMA/pre-SMA exactly that role and makes controller↔candidate-set coupling the performance-limiting quantity; position A says the slot can be deleted with no cost. Partly reconcilable — ablation asks whether a region is *necessary*, imaging whether it is *engaged*, and a redundant region can be both engaged and dispensable — but the effect size on the imaging side is too large for the null to be read as "not involved", and the two used different tasks: monkey one-to-one cue→action edges against human higher-order categorical rules over 10-member sets, which is precisely the manipulation the human study argues made the prefrontal signal visible at all

## Status

`LIVE` — the discriminating experiment is a medial premotor ablation on a *higher-order* mapping task (sets, not single cues), which nobody has run; the wiki should not cite either row as settled
