# T246 — Does the age-related rise in global coupling `G` measure compensation or decline?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A measurement distinguishes compensation from dedifferentiation independently of the fluidity-maximisation criterion, which is itself the assumption under test.

## Position A

**Compensation.** In the Virtual Aging Brain (`n` = 649, ages 55–85), inferred `G` rises with age and with structural-connectivity deterioration; since the working point is chosen to maximise fluidity, a higher `G` is read as a global (hypothesised dopaminergic) modulation that preserves the fluid dynamics linked to cognitive flexibility as interhemispheric tracts degrade (Hashemi et al. 2025, Lavanga et al.).

## Position B

**Decline, or unreadable.** The same study finds `G` rising *faster* in low performers on concept shifting — the opposite of what a successful compensation would predict, and reinterpreted post hoc as "acceleration of dedifferentiation". Independently, a single global coupling gain reorganises every graph statistic of a simulated functional network without touching structure, so `G` is the parameter every disconnection pathology moves and is not a localiser (Cabral, Kringelbach & Deco 2014).

## Where it bites

Whether a fitted global gain can be given a functional sign at all. It is the one parameter every whole-brain model in the wiki sweeps, and the ageing, anaesthesia, schizophrenia and metastability literatures each attach a different interpretation to the same scalar ([[wiki/entities/virtual-brain-twin.md]], [[wiki/concepts/metastability.md]])

## Status

`LIVE` — the two readings are not distinguished by any measurement in the source: both predict `G` rising, and the fluidity-maximisation criterion used to place the working point is itself the assumption under test.
