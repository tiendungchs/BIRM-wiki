# T355 — Is the second midbrain dopamine channel a valence-blind salience magnitude, or a signed aversion channel?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Optogenetically tagged, projection-identified dopamine neurons — mPFC-projecting versus NAc-lateral-shell-projecting — are recorded in one animal across four trial types: reward delivered, aversive event delivered, reward omitted, aversive event omitted. A is right if the non-value population is excited by both deliveries and silent on both omissions; B is right if it is excited by aversive delivery and not by reward delivery. A mixed result partitions the population again.

## Position A

**A rectified, valence-blind *salience* magnitude.** Bromberg-Martin, Matsumoto & Hikosaka 2010 ([[wiki/concepts/broadcast-channel-decomposition.md]]): a dorsolateral substantia nigra pars compacta population is excited by rewarding **and** aversive events alike, weakly by neutral ones, and is **silent on omission** — `salience ≈ |outcome| · 1[outcome occurred]`, not `|δ|`. Its proposed targets are the engagement machinery (accumbens **core**, dorsolateral prefrontal cortex), and the quantity it carries answers *how hard to engage* without committing to whether the news was good. Evidence: monkey single-unit recordings across valences, with the channel identity read off the firing profile.

## Position B

**A signed *aversion* channel.** Lammel et al. 2012 ([[wiki/entities/ventral-tegmental-area.md]]): the non-NAc-lateral-shell dopamine population is the medial posterior VTA → mPFC line, it is driven monosynaptically by the lateral habenula at 100% connection probability, phasic drive of that afferent produces conditioned place **aversion**, and a D₁ antagonist in mPFC abolishes the aversion. Its authors conclude mPFC-projecting cells "may be the primary subpopulation of dopamine neurons preferentially activated by aversive stimuli." Evidence: mouse circuit tracing plus optogenetic sufficiency plus target-receptor necessity, with the channel identity read off connectivity and behaviour.

## Where it bites

**Whether a two-channel broadcast system needs a sign bit.** Under A the second channel is unsigned and the architecture gets *value* on one wire and *gain* on the other — two quantities of different type, and `G117`'s double dissociation (lose one, lose outcome learning; lose the other, lose effort mobilisation) is the test. Under B the second channel is the same type as the first with the opposite valence — two rectified halves of one signed quantity — and the right machine analogue is a non-negative `head_+`/`head_−` pair, not a value/gain pair. The two designs differ in what is *architecturally denied*: A cannot express "this specific bad thing" on the second channel at all, B cannot raise engagement for a neutral-but-important event.

The evidence classes do not overlap, which is why the row is not already decided. A has firing across valences and no connectivity; B has connectivity, causal sufficiency and target-receptor necessity, and **no firing record at all** — nothing in Lammel et al. measures what the mPFC line does to a reward. A's own authors flag VTA dopamine identification by firing properties as unreliable, which is where A's monkey data would have to be extended to meet B's mouse anatomy.

**The founding record excluded this row's population by protocol.** Matsumoto & Hikosaka 2007 ([[wiki/entities/lateral-habenula.md]]) — the monkey work the A-side lineage is built on — state that they recorded from 62 dopamine neurons selected for phasic *excitation to reward-predicting stimuli*, and that "dopamine-like neurons that were not sensitive to reward-predicting stimuli were not examined further". The cells this row is about are, by construction, the discarded ones. The same study also cuts against B's exclusivity: habenular stimulation inhibits 82% of the *reward-responsive* cells, so habenular drive is not confined to a putative aversion line — one non-negative source writes to both, as [[wiki/entities/lateral-habenula.md]] has it, which means "driven by the habenula" cannot by itself identify a channel.

Species and region are a confound the closing experiment must absorb: A's split is clearest in substantia nigra pars compacta (monkey), B's is in VTA (mouse), and a reading on which both are right — three or more populations, a salience one in SNc and an aversion one in VTA — is admissible and would make the wiki's "two channels" shorthand wrong in a third way.

## Status

`LIVE` — the discriminating trial type (omission, on a projection-identified non-value population) has not been run. Adjacent rows: `T122` disputes what the *value* channel carries; `T313` (retired) asked whether dopamine responds to aversive events at all and was resolved by topography — this row is the next question that resolution opens, namely what the second topographic population's code actually is. `G116` bites here too: conditioned place preference and aversion are approach/avoid measures, so B's behavioural evidence cannot separate a valence signal from an action signal.

**`T361` attacks B from upstream.** B's channel is defined by its afferent — the lateral habenula — and Hu, Cui & Yang 2020 ([[wiki/entities/lateral-habenula.md]]) report that habenular lesion abolishes the dopamine negative prediction error caused by **reward omission** while leaving the one caused by aversive stimuli intact. If that holds, B's "aversion" line is driven by a *disappointment* signal, and the omission trial — the one trial type this row says nobody has run on a projection-identified cell — is not merely missing evidence but the condition under which B's own afferent is known to be necessary.
