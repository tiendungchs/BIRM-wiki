# T353 — Does a sample-efficiency result measure the agent, or the benchmark's exact-state-revisit rate?

**Status:** `LIVE`
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]
**Closes when:** Any benchmark in the wiki used to support a data-efficiency or one-shot-learning claim is annotated with (i) the fraction of evaluation-time queries that exactly match a stored or previously seen training item under the agent's own representation, and (ii) for any retrieval-based agent, the score-versus-read-width curve. The row resolves if agent rankings are stable once results are stratified by (i), and inverts if the ranking changes on the low-revisit stratum.

## Position A

**The results measure the agent.** Episodic control's Atari advantage is reported as one-shot learning and read that way throughout the literature and the wiki: [[wiki/entities/neural-episodic-control.md]] leads every parametric agent below 20M frames, and [[wiki/concepts/complementary-learning-systems.md]] takes the early-win/late-loss signature as confirmation of a normative theory that predicted it before the architectures existed. The Labyrinth arm supports the same reading from the other side — a 3-D environment with <0.1% exact revisits, where MFEC still reaches the Forage-and-Avoid target in <3M frames against A3C's >40M, and reaches positive reward on Double-T-Maze where A3C never does.

## Position B

**A large part of the Atari result is literal table lookup, and the number is published.** Blundell et al. 2016 ([[wiki/entities/model-free-episodic-control.md]]) measure the fraction of state-action pairs exactly matched in the agent's own buffers: **60%** on Q\*bert, 50% Ms. Pac-Man, 45% Space Invaders, 10% Frostbite, 10% River Raid — against **<0.1%** on all three Labyrinth levels. Atari is deterministic with 30 possible initial states. So on the two games where episodic control's margin is largest, the majority of decisions are retrievals of a value the agent has already observed for that exact state, which is not generalisation under any definition on [[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]]'s ladder — it is the "unlimited experience" gaming channel, measured. The same paper supplies the agent-side confirmation: on Atari, increasing `k` from small to large **does not improve the score** on four of five games, i.e. the neighbours carry no information and the read is a lookup; on Labyrinth the same sweep improves the score substantially.

## Where it bites

On every sample-efficiency claim in the wiki, because no other benchmark here reports the statistic. `G17` says no evaluation protocol can certify structure discovery; this row is the cheap, concrete half of that — before an agent is credited with generalising, report what fraction of its test-time queries it has literally already seen, and stratify the score. It bites hardest on the retrieval-based architectures whose advantage *is* retrieval ([[wiki/entities/hami.md]], [[wiki/entities/neural-episodic-control.md]], [[wiki/entities/sparse-distributed-memory.md]]) and on [[wiki/entities/dqn.md]]-lineage Atari comparisons generally, where determinism plus a 30-state initial distribution is a property of the emulator that every agent on the leaderboard is silently priced against. It also bites on the abstract-reasoning benchmarks the wiki cares about most, where "the same item seen before" must be defined under a learned representation rather than as pixel equality — which is the hard part of the instrument and the reason nobody has built it.

## Status

`LIVE`, and it is an instrument row rather than a disagreement about facts: both positions are held by the same paper, and Position B's numbers are Position A's authors' own. What makes it live is that the measurement has been made exactly once, in 2016, and never since — the statistic appears on no other benchmark page in the wiki, and no result here has been stratified by it. The `k`-sweep half is cheaper still (no labels, no retraining, no held-out split, and it measures the deployed read path rather than a surrogate), and it has not been run on any of the wiki's other fast stores although each has a read width that would support it.
