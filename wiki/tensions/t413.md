# T413 — Is hippocampal pattern separation domain-general, or one per-stream operation among several?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Graded-similarity discrimination of visual objects (lures at several similarity levels, as in the human bias-score design) is scored after damage confined to the dentate gyrus, in the same species as an activity measurement of dentate/CA3 lure responses, with perirhinal cortex intact. A deficit graded with lure similarity favours A. Intact performance at every similarity level, with the dentate lure signal still present before the lesion, favours B.

## Position A

**Domain-general.** In humans, CA3/dentate gyrus treats near-duplicate *object photographs* as new items. Its lure bias score is 0.15, against 0.59–0.83 in every other medial-temporal region. The task is incidental and non-spatial. So the store's de-aliasing operation already runs on arbitrary content (Bakker et al. 2008, [[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]]). The perforant path → dentate expansion is described on that page as the substrate of "domain-agnostic" separation.

## Position B

**One stream among several.** In rats, lesions that include the dorsal dentate impair separation graded by similarity for places, enclosure geometry and grey-shade context. Ventral dentate lesions impair it for odours. Hippocampal lesions do **not** impair separation of visual objects, reward values or motor responses. Those are assigned to inferior temporal and perirhinal cortex, orbitofrontal cortex and amygdala, and caudate. The authors' reading is that the hippocampus separates the spatial and temporal frame (and odour ventrally), and each cortical stream separates its own content (Kesner & Rolls 2015, [[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]]).

## Where it bites

Whether a machine needs **one** separating store or a separator **per module**. Under A, one expansion-and-randomise front end in the fast store de-aliases every content type, and the cortical encoders can stay structure-preserving. Under B, each content encoder must separate its own near-duplicates before the hippocampal stage. The fast store's separator then de-aliases only the frame (where and when) that episodes are bound to, and a store fed un-separated object codes would conflate near-duplicate objects without any hippocampal fault. It also decides what `G38`'s knob is a knob *on*: one global setting, or one per stream.

## Status

`LIVE`. The two results differ in four ways, and each could dissolve the contradiction without either result being wrong.

1. **Activity vs necessity.** A shows that dentate/CA3 *responds* as a separator to object lures. B shows the hippocampus is not *necessary* for object separation. If perirhinal cortex also separates objects, both hold. A store can separate redundantly with a cortical stage that suffices.
2. **Species and content.** A is human; B is rat. Rodent object vision is far less developed, and the source itself notes the rat hippocampal representation is dominated by place.
3. **Measure.** A's bias score is a contrast on mean activity and cannot tell a separator from a site *receiving already-separated input* (the estimator's own stated limit). A perirhinal stage that separated objects upstream would make dentate/CA3 score as a separator without separating anything. That reading converts A into B.
4. **Task.** A is incidental viewing with no memory demand. B's object-separation claims come from discrimination and recognition tasks whose primary studies the clipped review does not cite by name.
