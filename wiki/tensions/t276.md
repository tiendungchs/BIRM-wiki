# T276 — What does a transthalamic cortico-cortical route carry — a copy of the content, or the sender's state?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The route is tested across postsynaptic targets: if LP→PM carries state and LP→postrhinal carries content, the disagreement is between triples and both positions hold.

## Position A

**Content.** Higher-order thalamus is a genuine second route for cortico-cortical communication: the posterior medial nucleus relays S1→S2 with driver-type synapses (Theyel et al. 2009), higher-order thalamus is proposed as a synchroniser of cortical responses across areas (Saalmann et al. 2012), and thalamocortical relay cells receive very strong driving input from layer-5 pyramids — the anatomy of a relay, not a modulator ([[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]]). LP→postrhinal cortex is reported to carry robust, precise visual information

## Position B

**State.** For V1/LM→LP→PM in awake mice, the transthalamic route carries almost none of the content and nearly all of the arousal signal: LP→PM axons are weakly visually responsive and poorly time-locked to PM events despite *higher* calcium event rates than either cortico-cortical afferent; silencing LP terminals leaves PM visual responses near-intact (as does ablating LP→PM neurons) while significantly cutting PM's pupil-linked modulation — and cutting nothing of its locomotion modulation. Silencing V1/LM terminals does the reverse. The state signal is inherited: V1 layer-5/6 cortico-thalamic cells are more arousal-modulated than V1 cortico-cortical cells (Neske & Cardin 2025, [[wiki/concepts/transthalamic-context-routing.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a model's inter-areal graph needs one edge per module pair or two typed ones. Under A the transthalamic route is redundancy or a synchronisation path and can be folded into the direct edge; under B every edge is a pair — a fast tuned content wire and a slow untuned context wire with its own destination — which is a different wiring diagram, a different cost model, and the only substrate in the wiki for an *addressed* rather than broadcast global variable ([[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]])

## Status

`LIVE`, and plausibly **both, resolved by target**: the source itself insists the role of LP is postsynaptic-area-dependent (LP→PM contextual, LP→postrhinal precise-visual, LP→AL optic-flow-sensitive and locomotion-suppressed), so the disagreement may be between triples rather than between theories. Two unrun discriminators: (i) whether the split is a labelled line or a **low-pass property of the thalamocortical synapse** — test with fast vs slow modulated carriers on the same axons; (ii) whether the layer-1 terminals, unimaged here, carry something different from the layer-2/3 ones

**A primate triple adds a fourth reading: an enabling loop, whatever the cargo** (Zhou, Schafer & Desimone 2016, [[wiki/entities/pulvinar.md]]). The ventro-lateral pulvinar → V4 route carries content *in* the relay: 35% of sites are object-selective, and receptive fields are a convergent copy of V4's. That content is inherited, not originated, since V4 leads at gamma by 12.2 ms. Pulvinar → V4 influence dominates below 30 Hz. The pre-stimulus alpha result of Saalmann et al. 2012, which this row cites under A, is replicated, and then **reverses sign during stimulus processing**. Removing the route with whole-nucleus muscimol cuts V4's evoked response by 59.7% and puts that cortex into a local slow-wave-like state. That is the opposite of B's content-sparing silencing result. Neither A nor B predicts it as stated. The route's cargo can be mostly a copy while its *presence* is required for the receiver to respond. This adds a discriminator the row lacks: **whole-relay removal versus pathway-specific terminal suppression**, run on one triple. Until that is done, the LP→PM and pulvinar→V4 results differ on instrument, species, target and task engagement at once, and none of these can be isolated.
