# T242 — Which carrier frequency's power do resting BOLD fluctuations track — and can the question be settled at all?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The question is accepted as unsettleable with fMRI: the haemodynamic transform removes every candidate carrier before fitting, so arbitration needs a non-haemodynamic instrument.

## Position A

**Gamma, positively.** Intracranial and intracortical recordings in monkeys and humans find local BOLD increases accompanied by increases in gamma-band (40–100 Hz) local-field power and in firing rate, with slow (<0.1 Hz, 1/f) envelope fluctuations coherent between distant electrodes and selective for specific resting networks (Logothetis; Leopold; Shmuel & Leopold; Nir et al., in Cabral, Kringelbach & Deco 2014). The metabolic argument supports it: high-frequency oscillations are the expensive ones, and BOLD measures metabolic demand. This is the bridge [[wiki/concepts/inter-areal-synchrony.md]] uses to connect a <0.1 Hz human correlation to a 1–100 Hz rodent result.

**Within-subject primary for A, on the pattern only** (Keller et al. 2013, J Neurosci, `n = 5`, subdural ECoG and resting fMRI in separate sessions, 53,383 electrode pairs; [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]). Each electrode seed's map of 50–150 Hz power-envelope correlations matches its BOLD correlation map at mean `r = 0.40` (max 0.77), across sensory, motor and default networks. The 0.1–1 Hz envelope matches better than the 1–10 Hz one (`0.40` vs `0.28`, `p < .05`). In preliminary data (not shown), the slow cortical potential matches less well, especially for long-range pairs. Alpha/beta were **not** tested, so this does not arbitrate.

## Position B

**Alpha/beta, negatively.** Nearly every non-invasive study — simultaneous EEG–fMRI and MEG — finds BOLD *inversely* correlated with alpha and beta (8–32 Hz) power, and recovers RSN spatial maps, including the DMN, from **beta-band amplitude envelopes** via ICA on source-projected MEG (Nikouline; Mantini; Brookes; Hipp; de Pasquale, same review). Frequency-specific hubs differ by band (medial temporal at 4–6 Hz, lateral parietal at 8–23 Hz, sensorimotor at 32–45 Hz), so there is no single carrier to find. A reconciliation exists — gamma and alpha/beta power are themselves anticorrelated and contribute independently to BOLD variance (Scheeringa et al.) — but it dissolves the bridge rather than repairing it, since the sign of the BOLD–power relation then depends on which band dominates locally.

**A difference between the carriers that does not depend on BOLD.** Resting high-γ envelopes carry significant anticorrelations without any regression (2.7% of pairs; Keller et al. 2013). MEG β-band envelope studies reportedly find none (de Pasquale et al. 2012, as cited there). The authors attribute the divergence to band or to technique. If it is band, the two positions predict different **sign structures** of the neural correlation matrix itself, not only different signs of the BOLD–power relation. That comparison needs no haemodynamics, so it can be run electrophysiologically without simultaneous fMRI.

## Where it bites

Every cross-scale claim that leans on a human functional connectome to support an electrophysiological mechanism ([[wiki/concepts/inter-areal-synchrony.md]]'s timescale-gap row), and the six whole-brain models on [[wiki/concepts/metastability.md]], three of which posit a gamma carrier and three of which posit no oscillation at all yet fit the same BOLD data.

## Status

`LIVE`, and **structurally so**: the arbitration cannot be done with fMRI. The haemodynamic transform is measured to be a 0.35 Hz low-pass of population rate with a 1.6 s lag (`r = 0.9` against the full Balloon–Windkessel model), so every candidate carrier is removed from the observable before it is fitted; the review's own statement is that settling it requires simultaneous whole-brain electrophysiology and fMRI, which does not exist. Keller et al. 2013 is the nearest substitute, a within-subject non-simultaneous comparison with partial cortical coverage. It tested one band, so the status is unchanged. It adds an electrophysiology-only test (anticorrelation present or absent per band, in one recording) that sidesteps the haemodynamic transform for the sign-structure part of the question. That part could settle without `Closes when` being met.
