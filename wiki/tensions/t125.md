# T125 — Does the epistemic term help or hurt once it is implemented?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The stated experiment is run: a deep implementation with horizon > 1 and a parameter-novelty term, in the regime where A's tabular results hold and B's horizon-1 estimators degenerate.

## Position A

**Helps — it is the whole point.** Three structural results in the wiki agree the term is derived rather than tuned and that it pays: `−H(ρ_t)` is the objective's only nonlinearity and its gradient is a coverage bonus (Milosevic et al. 2026); `w = 1` sits at the Pareto knee on every one of eleven benchmarks and its advantage *grows* with state-space size (Tileworld 8×8: 66.5% vs. 2.5%; Cooper & Velasquez 2026); ablating the novelty correction takes rock retrieval from 98.7% to 0.0% (Nuijten et al. 2026)

## Position B

**Hurts, in every form anyone has shipped.** Five one-step neural estimators of `G` crossed with three action-selection rules on dSprites: the *only* configurations that solve the task are the ones with the epistemic term deleted (`G⁴ = −ψr`), one variant diverges to `NaN`, and the "principled" estimate `D_KL[P_θs ‖ Q_φs] − ψr` collapses the policy onto a single action — action-prior entropy → 0, transition-network variance low for that action alone — because a divergence between the agent's forward model and its own encoder is minimised by *not acting differently* (Champion et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/deep-active-inference-agent.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a reasoning agent's exploration drive can be derived from its planning objective or must be supplied separately (ε-greedy, an entropy bonus, a tuned coefficient). If B holds in general, the wiki's cleanest theoretical answer to exploration is unavailable in code

## Status

`LIVE` — but the two sides may not be testing the same object. A's evidence is tabular/tree-search, over horizons > 1, and A's own novelty term is over *parameters*; B's five definitions are all horizon-1 and all over *states*, in a regime Cooper & Velasquez independently report as degenerating to myopic information gain. **The discriminating experiment is stated and unrun:** a deep implementation with depth > 1, an information gain over `θ` rather than over `s`, and a sign audit — *can an agent that freezes its own policy drive this term to zero?* If yes, the term is a self-consistency penalty, not an information gain . **A's side also now has a datapoint that is not an agent at all:** on N=25 humans in a contextual two-armed bandit where information carries an explicit price, active inference with all three `G` terms beats both model-free and model-based reinforcement learning by BIC, and participants pay for the cue far more often than they decline it — behaviour a reward-only learner has no reason to produce (Zhang et al. 2025, `raw/zhang-2025-novelty-variability-active-inference.md`). This is *fit to a biological decision-maker*, not task performance, and the paper concedes the complete class theorem (Wald 1947) makes it weak evidence: some prior always exists that reproduces any behaviour. **A's side also has a gradient-optimised entry, which narrows B to amortised neural estimators:** minimising a variational free energy with epistemic priors by Adam on a factorized categorical posterior — no message passing, no tree search — takes a stochastic cue maze from 0–17% to 100% success and beats tree-search Sophisticated Inference (94%), so the failure mode is not "gradients on an epistemic term" but specifically a one-step neural `G`-estimator built from generative rather than variational factors (Nuijten et al. 2026, `raw/nuijten-2026-efe-planning-variational-inference.md`)
