# T255 — Is the brain's default self-evaluation accurate information-gathering or self-serving bias?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Accurate.** The adaptive story that motivates the default-mode account: idle time is spent on chronic, generalized updates of one's strengths and weaknesses so that future action can be conditioned on them — which is only useful if the estimates are unbiased. Moran et al.'s data are read this way: medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate cortex track *self-descriptiveness* (a cognitive judgment) while valence is carried elsewhere (ventral anterior cingulate), so the region doing self-evaluation is not the region doing flattery

## Position B

**Biased.** Participants call more positive traits highly self-descriptive and rate negative traits as less essential to self. Beer 2007's objection: one esteem-maintaining mechanism acting on *both* valences — positive attributes important, negative attributes irrelevant — produces a main effect of self-description with no valence interaction, i.e. exactly the observed pattern, so the design does not identify the two accounts. Orbitofrontal lesion patients' inflated self-views against expert judges show the *default* is inflated when uncorrected

## Where it bites

Whether a machine can use its own competence estimate at all. Under A the estimate is usable and calibration is a refinement; under B the uncorrected estimate is signed-wrong and needs an external referent before it can gate anything ([[wiki/concepts/default-self-model.md]], `G89`)

`LIVE` — the discriminating experiment is stated and unrun: add a **neutral** trait condition and test the three-level valence × self-description interaction
