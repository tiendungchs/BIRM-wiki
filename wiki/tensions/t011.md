# T11 — Is an installed prior a hard constraint or a strong default?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Hard.** Core principles operate all-or-none and *delete* candidate structures rather than reweighting them: an entity that violates cohesion is not admitted to the object system at all, and non-object substances (sand, liquids) never enter it ([[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]])

## Position B

**Defeasible.** The same principles are false at the smallest and largest scales — objects are neither cohesive nor continuous, space is not Euclidean or 3-D — and they are demonstrably overridden: by physics and non-Euclidean geometry in adults, by learning to count in preschoolers, by learning about eating and breathing in children's agent concepts (Spelke & Kinzler 2007)

## Where it bites

Decides how a prior is implemented. A hard constraint has no path to a domain where it is wrong; a soft regularizer is washed out by data and buys no sample efficiency. The override channel in every documented case is an **external symbol system**, which is also T8's candidate for composition

## Status

`LEANING` — toward defeasible, with the open question being the *mechanism* of override, not its existence; note the two positions are the same source's, at the scale of action vs. outside it
