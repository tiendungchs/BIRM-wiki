# T227 — Does a near-zero train−test familiarity disparity license the verdict "this model did not train on the benchmark"?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The triple `(δ_train, δ_test, δ_train-test)` is reported and *high-and-flat* treated as the both-leaked signature, with the absolute scale calibrated against a documented corpus.

## Position A

**Yes, as reported.** Xu et al. 2024's headline instrument is `δ_train-test`, models are *ordered* by it across 31 systems, and the paper's own reading is that "models such as the LLaMA series and Mistral-7B-v0.1 exhibit nearly zero `δ_train-test` scores, suggesting minimal potential for benchmark leakage". Every downstream citation of a leakage ranking inherits that ordering ([[wiki/concepts/benchmark-contamination.md]])

## Position B

**No — the statistic's null and its worst case coincide.** A model that ingested *both* splits reads the same near-zero disparity as one that ingested neither, and the paper supplies the counterexample in its own table: ChatGLM3-6B at `δ_train-test = 0.55` sits among the clean models on `δ_train = 36.85` / `δ_test = 36.30`, ~3× LLaMA-7B's absolute familiarity. The instance-level backstop fails too, and on a *known positive*: Aquila2-34B, documented as having seen the entire GSM8K test set, returns **zero** suspicious items on it because it learned the content under a different answer delimiter

## Where it bites

Every leakage ranking, and every wiki claim of the form "benchmark X is uncontaminated for model Y". It also bounds [[wiki/entities/olymmath.md]]'s self-audit, which reports `δ` on a single split with no disparity available, and it is the reason `F14` was added to [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]] rather than being folded into `F1`

## Status

`LEANING` to B — B is the authors' own stated limitation, and the ChatGLM3 and Aquila2-34B rows are in their own tables. The repair is arithmetic and unrun: report the triple `(δ_train, δ_test, δ_train-test)` and treat *high-and-flat* as the both-leaked signature, then calibrate the absolute scale against a model with a documented corpus
