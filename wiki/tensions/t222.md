# T222 — Does keeping the evaluation set permanently private solve contamination?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Residual leakage is measured rather than prevented, as OlymMATH does with `n`-gram `δ` against rewritten variants — making privacy an auditable quantity rather than a claim.

## Position A

**Yes — it is the only defence that cannot decay.** Glazer et al. 2024 ([[wiki/entities/frontiermath.md]]) commission every item new from research mathematicians under an originality requirement, route submissions through encrypted channels and password-protected archives, scan the whole set with two plagiarism services (no significant matches), release **five** problems and keep the rest unpublished with the authoring organisation running all evaluations. There is no public split to be i.i.d. with, so the distributional-leakage channel [[wiki/entities/arc-agi-2.md]] documents (T207) has no route in, and no item can be memorised because no item is available to memorise.

## Position B

**No — it relocates the failure from leakage into unauditability.** `F1` in [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]: developer-blindness is a depleting stock, and a fixed private set absorbs leakage through repeated scoring regardless of publication (ARC-AGI-1's 100 private tasks absorbed ~10,000 reported scores). The specific cost here is measurable: the same paper estimates its own **critical error rate at ~10%** from a 35-item second-review sample against a 0–2% spread between the six models it ranks, reports **6/35** items with missing hypotheses and **2/35** violating its own guessproofness bound — and nobody outside the organisation can check any of it, reproduce a score, or re-audit an item. A measured error rate is a property of the audit, and this audit is not repeatable by a third party.

## Where it bites

Every score reported on a closed benchmark, and the design choice facing any successor to a saturated public one: the two properties (contamination resistance, external auditability) are traded directly against each other, and the wiki's other self-auditing benchmark ([[wiki/entities/gpqa.md]], 73.6–76.4% objectivity) sits on the opposite side of the trade with a canary string as its only leakage defence.

## Status

`LIVE` — and a **third position** now exists that neither A nor B anticipated: [[wiki/entities/olymmath.md]] sources its 350 items from printed, non-digitised material, publishes all of them, and then **measures** the residual leakage (`n`-gram `δ` against LLM-rewritten variants: 0.90% / 17.59% for two base models in English, against 34.84% / 38.81% for PolyMath). That buys auditability and contamination resistance at once, at the price of a defence that decays from the day of release — to which the paper's answer is to open-source the *construction pipeline* (the Lean formalisation agent, the sourcing protocol) so the set can be refreshed, i.e. **a benchmark as a renewable process rather than an artefact**. The unsolved part is comparability: successive editions share no anchor subset, so a score trend across refreshes means nothing. [[wiki/entities/hle.md]] takes the same third position (all 2,500 items public, a private set held back, and HLE-Rolling as the refresh mechanism) and adds the measurement that most weakens **A**: publishing the set is what makes a **15.4%** error rate knowable at all, and the number turns out to depend on the reviewing rule (18% → 25% on the same items), so FrontierMath's unauditable ~10% is not comparable to it in either direction — `F12`.
