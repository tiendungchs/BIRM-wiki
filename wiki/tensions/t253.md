# T253 — Is slow connectome dynamics the accumulated expression of fast states, or an independent parallel stream?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**One stream, filtered by the instrument.** The prevailing multimodal position: infraslow BOLD network dynamics are the cumulative/low-pass expression of fast, short-lived electrophysiological states, which is why HRF-convolving EEG components, spectra or state time courses recovers fMRI networks at all. The wiki's own mechanistic version is stronger still — in Ghosh et al. 2008 the 0.1 Hz signal *is* the amplitude modulation of a ~10 Hz carrier, and in Deco et al. 2009 the slow envelope is the switching of fast cluster-synchronisation states, so in both the slow observable is a derived quantity of the fast dynamics ([[wiki/concepts/dynamic-repertoire.md]], [[wiki/concepts/temporal-coding.md]])

## Position B

**Six parallel streams, no shared clock.** With one authored 126-state alphabet scored at each modality's native rate, the temporal overlap of *identical* states between infraslow fMRI and each EEG band does not exceed a lifetime-preserving shuffle null (`t(25)` = 0.04–1.09, and 0.02–0.25 after HRF convolution of the EEG correlation time series), with the 6 s haemodynamic lag applied; EEG↔EEG overlap is < 1.5% of frames. Meanwhile the *relative* transition, occupancy and lifetime structure is shared across all six (`r = 0.90–0.96` within EEG), so the streams share an alphabet and a sequencing rule but not a phase (Alderson et al. 2026, [[wiki/concepts/parallel-timescale-streams.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a multi-timescale architecture needs one state stream read out at several rates (a hierarchical RNN with a slow layer ticking every `k` fast steps, phase-locked by construction) or several asynchronous streams over a shared codebook — which decides whether `k` must be chosen at all, and whether cross-timescale coupling is a synchronisation barrier or a shared alphabet ([[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] G67)

## Status

`LIVE` — and B's evidence is a **null result** at `N` = 26: failure to exceed a shuffle null excludes strict alignment, not partial coupling below the null's resolution. Two further asymmetries keep it open: A's mechanism is simulated and B's is measured, but B never simulates its own arrangement, and B's fMRI↔EEG shared-structure correlations (`r = 0.17–0.19` for lifetimes) are the weakest numbers in its paper — plausibly because ~3 s states under-sample 126 blueprints in 10 min. **The decisive experiment**: run a whole-brain model of type A, extract band-limited envelopes and BOLD from the *same* simulation, and score cross-timescale state overlap with the same shuffle null — A predicts it exceeds chance by construction
