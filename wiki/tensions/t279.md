# T279 — Does decoding an attended feature from a population imply that population carries a feature-tuned gain signal?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Feature-tuned modulation is measured directly rather than inferred from decoding — the model shows the decoding passes with no modulation present. Sibling of T114.

## Position A

**Yes** — the wiki's default reading everywhere, and the empirical basis of *spatially global* feature-based attention: an attended feature is decodable from visual areas whose receptive fields cover unstimulated parts of the field (Serences & Boynton 2007), and neurons are reported to be modulated by the similarity between their preferred feature and the attended one irrespective of receptive-field location (Treue & Martínez-Trujillo 1999; Serences & Boynton 2007) — so a spatially global feature-space bias signal exists and reaches early sensory cortex

## Position B

**No — the same decoding accuracy arises with no feature-tuned modulation present.** In a spiking model with near-random reciprocal connections between mixed-selectivity control units and feature-tuned sensory rings, unstimulated rings show (i) **chance** decoding from a decoder trained on stimulus-evoked patterns, at every top-down modulation strength, (ii) **above-chance and rising** decoding from a within-task SVM, and (iii) **chance** cross-generalisation between two unstimulated rings — i.e. consistent, idiosyncratic, per-region random shadows of the control signal, not a shared feature code. Only a small departure from randomness (κ ≈ 0.1) produces genuinely stimulus-like modulation, and κ ≥ 0.2 makes it as strong as a real stimulus (Park & Serences 2025, [[wiki/concepts/random-feedback-addressing.md]])

## Where it bites

Every decoding-based attention claim in the wiki, and by extension every MVPA claim about a modulatory signal: a classifier trained and tested inside one condition certifies *presence of information*, never its **format**, and B is a constructive existence proof that the two come apart in exactly the regime the field reads as global feature attention. The discriminating instrument is already standard and rarely used here — train the decoder on stimulus-evoked responses and require it to cross-generalise, or fit an explicit encoding model ([[wiki/concepts/representation-probing.md]]). B is a simulation, so it establishes that A's evidence is insufficient rather than that A is false; the empirical version (does global feature attention in unstimulated cortex survive a cross-generalising encoding-model test?) has not been run
