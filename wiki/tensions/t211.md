# T211 — Should an intelligence benchmark equalise human priors, or withhold them?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The cheap unrun experiment: take an existing prior-equalised benchmark and re-author its tasks in a world with altered physics, scoring humans and machines on both.

## Position A

**Equalise.** A fair human/machine comparison requires the prior term `P` to be the same on both sides, and Core Knowledge is the only enumerated candidate for what humans start with — so a benchmark states its priors exhaustively, restricts every task to them, and thereby makes the comparison mean something ([[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]], [[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]]). Chollet's supporting argument is that intelligence must be tied to a defined area of application, and only the human one is accessible

## Position B

**Withhold.** An artificial agent is not bound to the human domain or to the physical world, so it can be scored in arbitrary formalisable worlds — different dimensionality, altered physics or causality — and in such a world an installed human prior is a *mis-specified constraint*, not a head start. Objectness, action-on-contact and classical physics are named: classical physics is itself a pragmatic human model that contradicts quantum mechanics, so supplying it as an axiom would cap the agent at a known-false belief. Anchoring to humans also re-invites the skills-over-intelligence failure the field keeps repeating (Pfister & Jud 2025, [[wiki/concepts/problem-framing.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether [[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]] is an ingredient of a reasoning architecture or only a scoring control — and whether the wiki's whole brain-inspired-prior programme is buying generality or buying a good fit to one world. It also decides what a benchmark may hold fixed: `P` can be equalised only by fixing it, and fixing it is what makes the framing constant (G73)

## Status

`LIVE` — the positions are not symmetric in what they can be tested. A benchmark over arbitrary worlds does not exist, and the source concedes its own residual (its worlds are still formalisable, executable and human-imagined), while the equalise side has five years of measurements. **(brainstorm)** The discriminating experiment is cheap and unrun: take an existing prior-equalised task set and *re-render it under a violated prior* (non-cohesive objects, non-Euclidean adjacency), then compare human and machine degradation. If installed priors are a head start, humans fall further than a prior-free learner; if they are a constraint, the ordering flips
