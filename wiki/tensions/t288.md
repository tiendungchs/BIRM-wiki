# T288 — Can a domain-general learner acquire hierarchical syntactic structure from positive evidence alone?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**No — this is the classical learnability argument.** A finite sample is consistent with infinitely many mutually inconsistent target grammars, so unless the search space is constrained the selection is impossible; "no known general learning mechanism can acquire a natural language solely on the basis of positive or negative evidence, and the prospects for finding any such domain-independent device seem rather dim". The constraint must be installed, and the comparative data agree at the capability boundary: cotton-top tamarins match human infants on transitional-probability segmentation and on algebraic rule learning over consonant-vowel sequences, and then fail the phrase-structure grammar `AⁿBⁿ` in three separate experiments while readily mastering the finite-state `(AB)ⁿ` variant on identical stimuli (Hauser, Chomsky & Fitch 2002; Fitch & Hauser 2002; Gold; Nowak et al.)

## Position B

**Yes, apparently.** Transformers trained on next-token prediction over positive evidence alone reach competence on the phrase-structure phenomena the argument declared unreachable, with no grammar installed ([[wiki/entities/transformer.md]]) — and the wiki's own compositional-generalisation results show the deciding variable is the *training distribution*, not the architecture: the same architecture scores 0.30 and 0.778 on productivity depending only on whether the episode sampler varied length ([[wiki/entities/mlc.md]], [[wiki/entities/pcfg-set.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a reasoning architecture must contain an installed generative operator (`G70`, `G11`, `G99`) or may expect one to be induced by an appropriately designed experience stream (`G32`). The two sides also disagree about what evidence would count: A is a claim about acquiring an I-language from a child-sized corpus, B is a claim about behavioural competence from a corpus several orders of magnitude larger

## Status

`LIVE` — the three escape routes are unadjudicated: (a) corpus size differs by orders of magnitude from the child's, so the poverty-of-stimulus premise is not shared; (b) the transformer's architecture *is* a constraint, so it is not domain-general in the sense the argument requires, and no one has stated what makes a learner domain-general enough to be a counterexample; (c) competence on the phenomena is not acquisition of the grammar (T8's discriminator problem, unsolved here too). Note the sides are not symmetric in what they measure: A's evidence is a *failure* profile in a nonhuman primate, B's is a success profile in a machine, and no experiment runs both protocols on one system
