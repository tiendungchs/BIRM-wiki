# T371 — Is the cortical circuit's invariant unit the layer or the genetically defined cell class?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One of the functional laminar assignments is re-run with the two class-mixed layers split by marker rather than by depth — record or silence L5B IT separately from L5B PT, and L6 IT separately from L6 CT, during the task the laminar claim was made on. A holds if the laminar term survives (both classes in a layer carry the same assigned quantity). B holds if the assignment tracks the class across layers and the layer term is a mixture of two different quantities.

## Position A

**The layer.** Every functional model the wiki carries is keyed to depth: Douglas & Martin's explore-in-superficial / commit-in-deep split, Bastos et al. 2012's one-quantity-per-population table (L4 spiny stellate = error on causes, L2/3 pyramid = broadcast error, L5/6 pyramid = the prediction), the SLN% distance rule that measures hierarchy from the *layer of origin* of a projection, Felleman–Van Essen feedforward/feedback typing by laminar termination, and the laminar shift of remote-memory readout ([[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]]). Lamination is also defended as a wiring-cost solution — a scaffold that constrains which cells can connect (Mitchison 1991) — so depth is not an arbitrary coordinate but the one the connectivity was built against.

## Position B

**The cell class.** Harris & Shepherd 2015: *"lamination is not the sole or even primary organizing principle of neocortex. Instead, what different regions share is their hodology: the patterns of connection between different genetically defined cell classes."* The evidence: (i) manipulating the class-specifying transcription factors (*Fezf2*/*Ctip2*, *Satb2*, *Tbr1*) in **postmigratory** neurons — cells already at their laminar destination — changes their connectivity and intrinsic physiology, so class and not position is the determinant; (ii) two layers are class-mixed — IT and PT in L5B, IT and CT in L6 — and the mixed classes have opposite profiles (PT has the highest firing rate in cortex and no local output, CT is near-silent with no corticocortical axon); (iii) in rodent, feedforward and feedback projections arise from distinct populations that need not occupy different layers, and a latexin⁺/*Nr4a2*⁺ deep-layer subclass carries feedback to the matching primary area; (iv) agranular motor cortex expresses L4 markers with no granular layer, so the "missing" layer is a missing cytoarchitecture rather than a missing cell type; (v) primate/rodent laminar disagreements may be a re-layering of conserved classes rather than a different circuit.

## Where it bites

**Whether a module in a brain-inspired architecture is indexed by depth or carries a type label.** Under A, a stack index is the right coordinate and the existing laminar assignments transfer as written. Under B, the transferable object is a set of typed units — an input-adaptor type, a recurrent integrator type, a broadcast-output type with no local return, a modulatory-return type with no peer edges — replicated per module, with depth an implementation detail of packing them; inter-areal connectivity then factorizes as `k` port types × an areal embedding rather than as `N²` weights, and hierarchy is a consequence of port typing rather than a designed depth. It also decides whether any laminar measurement is interpretable: a depth-resolved probe in L5B or L6 averages two classes that B says do opposite jobs.

## Status

`LEANING` toward B for *what determines connectivity* — the postmigratory transcription-factor manipulations are causal and A has no comparable experiment — but the two are not straightforwardly exclusive, and nothing yet re-runs a functional laminar claim under a class split. A keeps two things B does not deny: layers constrain *which inhibitory channels reach a cell* (an L3 pyramid gets far more chandelier input than an L6 pyramid because L3 has more chandelier cells), and laminar termination remains the only measurement from which hierarchy has actually been estimated at scale. The honest reading is that the layer is a **proxy for a type label** that is accurate wherever a layer holds one class and fails where it holds two.
