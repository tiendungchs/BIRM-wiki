# T67 — Where does mixed selectivity come from — wiring, or learning?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Mixed selectivity is measured before and through training: present and stable favours wiring, growing with exposure and tracking input correlation favours learning.

## Position A

**Wiring; it is free.** Conjunctive coding appears with no learning at all: fan-in restriction in a small dendritically wired network makes every unit see too little of the input to be class-specific (Chavlis & Poirazi 2025, [[wiki/entities/dendritic-ann.md]]), and lateral-patch/dendrite size matching means most superficial pyramids sample a patch/non-patch mixture by construction (Douglas & Martin 2004, [[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]])

## Position B

**Learning; random connectivity is not enough.** Prefrontal models do not reach measured levels of mixed selectivity under random connectivity, and do reach them once a Hebbian plasticity term is added (Wikipedia, *Hebbian theory*) `(tentative)`

## Where it bites

Whether a reasoning architecture should *build in* an expansion/mixing stage or let one emerge from a local rule — and, if the second, whether the mixing directions inherit Hebb's PCA objective and therefore its bias toward high-variance nuisance structure ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]], [[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]])

## Status
