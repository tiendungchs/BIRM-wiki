# T213 — What is the human baseline on ARC-AGI-1?

**Status:** `RESOLVED/LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One crowd study reports per-subject first-attempt accuracy on the *evaluation* split — the only quantity commensurable with a machine `pass@2` score.

## Position A

**~97–99%, and the gap to machines is ~30 points.** Two testers scored 97% and 98% on the private evaluation set and 100% between them; ten Mechanical Turk workers per task solved 99% of the public evaluation set between them. [[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]] and [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T205 both quote this range, and it is the number the "unsolved for five years" framing is measured against

## Position B

**~60–84%, and the gap is much smaller.** Johnson et al. 2021 measure **83.8%** mean per-task accuracy over 95 participants on 40 public *training* tasks with 3 attempts (8.38 of 10 tasks per participant, hardest task 38.1%), and the ARC Prize's own reported *average* human on the evaluation set is **60.2%** — below several 2025 systems

## Where it bites

Every "super-human" and "below human" claim on this benchmark, including the ARC Prize's framing of [[wiki/entities/poe-arc-solver.md]]'s 71.6% as above human. The three numbers are not in conflict about the world; they measure different things — **union over testers** (solvability of the task), **per-subject rate on the easy split** (difficulty for an individual), and **mean over a crowd on the hard split** (population competence) — on three different task pools. What is wrong is treating any of them as *the* baseline. It also bears on Chollet's own G31 workaround, which proposes human success rate as the empirical proxy for generalization difficulty without specifying which of these it is **Root cause:** aggregation order (B3) left unstated — union-over-testers, per-subject mean and crowd mean are three different estimands, and the fix is the five-parameter specification at [[wiki/concepts/human-baseline.md]].

## Status

`RESOLVED` as a category error, `LIVE` as a reporting defect: no source in the wiki reports per-subject first-attempt accuracy on the *evaluation* split, which is the only quantity commensurable with a machine's `pass@2` score on the same tasks. Costs one crowd study to fix
