# T106 — Do task-trained prefrontal-like recurrent networks settle into attractors, or avoid them?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One architecture is shown to require an attractor for planning and to require its absence for maintenance, or the two results are shown to be the same network under different training regimes.

## Position A

**They avoid them, and the avoidance is what makes them cortex-like.** Trained with adaptation currents, sparse recurrence and block topology, a delay network suppresses fixed points entirely and carries the memory in a transient trajectory, beating its persistent-activity twin on distractors, item load and navigation ([[wiki/entities/trnn.md]], Liu et al. 2025); and fixed-weight networks that *do* converge on point/line attractors are more fragile to synapse ablation and *less* like recorded prefrontal populations than short-term-plasticity networks with no persistent delay activity ([[wiki/entities/stsp-working-memory-rnn.md]], Kozachkov et al. 2022)

## Position B

**They settle into attractors, and here the attractor is the computation.** RNNs meta-trained on planning with within-trial reward change converge on a spacetime attractor: perturbation of the settled state decays if weak, flips the representation to an entire alternative path if strong, and does nothing in random directions; networks too small to build one fail the task rather than finding another solution ([[wiki/entities/spacetime-attractor.md]], Jensen et al. 2026)

## Where it bites

Whether relaxation is the wiki's default prefrontal mechanism or an artefact of easy delay tasks. The candidate reconciliation is that the two literatures train on different objectives — *hold an item* (where a moving trajectory is cheaper and more robust) versus *satisfy a set of constraints over a future trajectory* (where a fixed point is what "consistent plan" means) — which predicts that the same network should abandon attractors when the delay task is made purely mnemonic, and is untested
