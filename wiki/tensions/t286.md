# T286 — Is write-side randomization content-blind?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The linearity-breaking statistic is measured separately on known and unknown names, controlling for salience — costs nothing new and settles whether the separator consults content.

## Position A

**Yes, and that is what makes it work.** Mossy-fibre hashing is a *wiring* property — ~46 synapses per CA3 cell, sparse and effectively random, plus neurogenesis supplying fresh random connections — so it destroys input similarity without consulting it, handing CA3 an address drawn at random. The wiki's prescription follows from exactly this: **hash on write, embed on read** ([[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]]), and the hash's independence from the data is what guarantees a novel entity is separated as hard as a familiar one

## Position B

**No, not when the separator is learned.** The one measured machine instance is content-dependent on both available tests: **known** names produce a larger MLP output norm than unknown ones — already at MLP1, a layer outside the circuit — and a linear probe for the answer rises *smoothly* through the layers that were supposed to be pure hashing rather than sitting at chance until a lookup stage. Gradient descent does discover randomization (trained MLPs break linear structure 60–70% of the way, shuffled weights 20–40%) but it discovers a *biased* one, which is what a few optimisation steps applied to a pure hash would produce: making known entities' hashes more prominent and more mutually orthogonal is a loss improvement (Nanda et al. 2023, [[wiki/concepts/memorisation-vs-generalisation.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides what a machine store should implement at the write port. Content-blind hashing is cheap, needs no training and generalises to novel keys by construction; a learned separator gets better capacity on the distribution it saw and has undefined behaviour off it. `G97` (the read of a sparse store is dense) assumes the write side is settled; this row says it is not. It also bites on [[wiki/entities/sparse-distributed-memory.md]] and [[wiki/entities/vector-hash.md]], both of which use a fixed random expansion and would, if B is right, be leaving capacity on the table

## Status

`LIVE`. Asymmetric evidence: position A is anatomy, position B is one measurement in one pretrained transformer on one task, with no control for whether the norm difference is *separation* rather than mere salience. **The experiment that would settle it costs nothing new:** measure the linearity-breaking statistic separately on known and unknown names. If the learned transform separates known entities *more*, the hash is allocating capacity by frequency and the wiki's prescription needs a frequency term; if it separates both equally and only the norms differ, the extra norm is signal amplitude and content-blind hashing survives intact
