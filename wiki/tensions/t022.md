# T22 — Does a ranking metric on balanced samples say anything about a retrieval mechanism?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A retrieval mechanism is scored by precision at its deployment prior rather than AUROC on balanced samples, on the same datasets.

## Position A

**Yes — the model is accurate.** NeuroMatch reports 89.3–97.9 AUROC on balanced positive/negative pairs across seven datasets, ~20% above adapted neural graph matchers and +18.4% over approximate heuristics, and the abstract sells this as "accurate, efficient, and robust" (Ying et al. 2020)

## Position B

**No — the same paper's confusion matrix shows the deployment number is precision, and it is at chance-plus.** Matching a small query against *all* neighbourhoods in DD gives TP 68.2 / FN 8.3 / FP 70.5 / TN 1030.9: 89% recall but **49% precision**, because subgraph hits are rare and the balanced evaluation sample destroys that skew. The paper reports the imbalance itself as "extreme"

## Where it bites

Any use of structural retrieval against a real library ([[wiki/concepts/subgraph-matching.md]], G37): one in two returned schemata would be spurious, and the mechanism has no way to flag which. Generalises past this source — every AUROC-scored retrieval or detection result in the wiki inherits it

## Status

`LEANING` (B: it is the same source's own data, on the same datasets)
