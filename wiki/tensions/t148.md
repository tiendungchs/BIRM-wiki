# T148 — Should a discovered action alphabet be discrete or continuous?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The codebook size is swept: the hypothesis that 8 codes index but cannot carry an arbitrary transition is testable and nobody has tested it.

## Position A

**Discrete.** The wiki's standing position everywhere the vocabulary question comes up: a finite symbol set is what makes planners, PDDL rules and formal verification applicable ([[wiki/concepts/affordance-grounded-symbols.md]] — DeepSym's binary bottleneck, LatPlan's discrete state autoencoder), what T147 position A demands for inspectability, and what Genie-style VQ codebooks assume for playability. A discrete edge label is also the only kind [[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]] is literally written over.

## Position B

**Continuous, with discretisation as optional read-out.** A VQ-8 codebook reimplemented under identical conditions transfers barely better than *no action condition at all* — LIBERO FVD 1504.5 against 1545.2 action-agnostic and 767.0 continuous — while continuity is what makes the two operations that matter possible: averaging 100 encoded samples to initialise a new action, and interpolating two codes to compose one. Discrete controls are recovered afterwards by clustering the continuous codes, in whatever number is wanted (Gao et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/adaworld.md]]).

## Where it bites

Gap **G4**: whether the induced edge-label alphabet is a codebook or a vector space, and therefore whether composition over it is symbolic or arithmetic. It also decides whether G27's discretisation problem must be solved for *edges* as well as nodes, or only for nodes.

## Status

`LIVE`, and the evidence is internally split: the same VQ-8 variant that collapses on cross-context transfer is second-best and within 1% of the continuous model on unseen-environment fidelity (Habitat 23.31 vs 23.58 PSNR). The natural reading — 8 codes suffice to *index* a small discrete action space after finetuning but cannot *carry* an arbitrary demonstrated transition — is a hypothesis about codebook size that nobody has swept

**A third design point, from the opposite direction:** [[wiki/entities/gcq.md]] uses a codebook that is discrete *and metrically ordered* — codewords are bumps on a torus, so `e_i + a` is another codeword and differences between codes are actions. That dissolves the dichotomy the row is written over: lookup and composition-by-arithmetic are both available, because the discreteness is a sampling of a continuum rather than an unstructured symbol set. The cost is that the metric is installed rather than discovered, so it buys the composition property by assuming the domain's topology (G47). **A fourth reading says the axis is wrong.** [[wiki/entities/neo-neural-theorizer.md]] runs both design points as baselines under one protocol that holds out *compositions* rather than scenes, and **both score 0.000** — discrete VQ and continuous β-VAE alike, with test-time gradient refinement of the continuous code not helping. What transfers (0.933) is a *sequence* of discrete codes executed by a shared operator. On that evidence the load-bearing axis is **monolithic vs compositional**, not discrete vs continuous, and this row has been comparing two ways of building the same failure. It also settles the codebook-size hypothesis flagged above, in the compositional case and against the intuition: a 6× over-complete codebook does *not* degrade into one code per observed composition — it still allocates primitive-level codes, and scores slightly better. What keeps position B alive is that NEO's domains are synthetic and its encoder is frozen, so nothing yet shows a discrete compositional alphabet surviving the 2000M-frame regime where the continuous code won.

**The `Closes when` sweep is now cheap and there is a reason to expect the collapse was not about codebook size** (Jang et al. 2016, [[wiki/concepts/discrete-relaxation-gradients.md]]): single-sample relaxation removes the `O(k)` cost that makes a large codebook expensive, so "VQ-8 because more is unaffordable" is not a live defence of position B's evidence — but nobody has attributed the transfer collapse to gradient variance either, so this changes what is testable rather than what is known.
