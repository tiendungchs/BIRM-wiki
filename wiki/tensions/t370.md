# T370 — Does uninstructed free play converge on an intermediate-difficulty set-point, or does it under-challenge?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One free-play experiment reports the **distribution** of individual operating points — each participant's steady-state success rate on the activity they chose, on a difficulty ladder that includes an unlearnable rung — instead of a group mean, under no instruction. A is right if that distribution is unimodal around 70–80% correct. B is right if it is bimodal or mass-heavy at the easy end, with the intermediate mode confined to the subgroup that ends up mastering the ladder. The same design with the instruction manipulated within participants separates "the set-point exists and instruction shifts it" from "the set-point is a subgroup property".

## Position A

**Free play settles at an intermediate difficulty on its own.** Adults given a set of games of varying difficulty and **no instructions** survey the whole set, hold at **70–80% correct** for most of the session, and then progress to harder games (Gottlieb & Oudeyer 2018, [[wiki/concepts/information-sampling-vs-search.md]]). This is the operating point a learning-progress maximiser is predicted to converge to, and the same number is the wiki's only human target for a self-generated machine curriculum ([[wiki/concepts/curriculum-learning.md]]). Converging behavioural evidence for a preference for intermediate complexity is cited across infant attention, sensorimotor choice, trivia curiosity and aesthetic preference.

## Position B

**Uninstructed learners under-challenge; the intermediate point is a property of the subgroup that succeeds.** Ten et al. 2021 ([[wiki/concepts/learning-progress.md]]) gave 186 uninstructed participants four activities (three learnable, one unlearnable) and 250 free-choice trials. The group allocated **33.00%** of trials to the *easiest* activity — significantly above chance, `t(1520) = 5.330, p < 0.001` — and only **29.59%** mastered all three learnable activities. Participants who were told to maximise learning self-challenged more and learned more (`dwfPC` 0.756 vs 0.721, `t(379.4) = 2.679, p = 0.008`). Final performance is an **inverted U** in self-challenge, and the uninstructed failures sit on the *rising* arm — they failed by choosing activities that were too easy, while the instructed failures sit on the falling arm. Intermediate self-challenge with no instruction was observed, statistically indistinguishable from the instructed group's — but only in the 29.59% who mastered everything.

**A third datum, at 8 months, that splits the set-point's argument.** Poli et al. 2020 ([[wiki/concepts/realised-information-gain.md]]) found an inverted U in 43 uninstructed infants — look away if the sequence is too predictable *or* too unpredictable — which is A's shape, at an age where instruction is impossible, so B cannot attribute it to a written competence weight. But the U sits on **environmental entropy** `−H(p^j)`, a property of the world, not on the learner's own success rate, which is what A's 70–80%-correct claim is about; and it is the *weakest* of three simultaneous terms (`e^{|β|} = 1.27`, against `7.02` for realised information gain and `2.44` for surprise). The row does not close on it: no individual-level distribution of operating points is reported, and there is no unlearnable rung — every sequence has a modal target location, so the design cannot see the failure mode `A4` was built to expose. What it does establish is that a self-organised intermediate set-point and a dominant progress-like drive coexist, with the set-point as the minor term.

## Where it bites

**Whether an intrinsic-motivation-only agent self-organises an adequate curriculum, or needs a terminal objective to set its competence weight.** Under A, `G32`'s curriculum generator can be an intrinsic drive alone and the 70–80% number is a target to hit. Under B, the same drive run without an external objective spends its time on already-mastered activities, and what the instruction supplies is specific and cheap: in the fitted two-term utility it flips the sign of `w_PC` (uninstructed `+0.255` → instructed `−0.232`, `p < 0.001`) while leaving the learning-progress weight statistically unchanged (`p = 0.799`). So B does not say the intrinsic drive is absent — it says one of its two coefficients is set from outside, and the wrong setting is the default.

The positions are not straightforwardly reconcilable by task difficulty. A's games were freely chosen from a graded set with no unlearnable member; B's ladder contains one, and the unlearnable activity is what pulls the *instructed* group off the optimum (36.92% of EG trials, above chance) while the easy activity pulls the uninstructed group off it. A single set-point claim has to account for both arms of the U, and neither source reports an individual-level distribution that would.
