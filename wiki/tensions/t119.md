# T119 — Where does the variability in a learning task enter the generative model?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**At the output.** This is what the backpropagation limit of a predictive-coding network assumes: a chain of latent layers in which one variance parameter `Σ^(0)` dominates all others, so the residual is entirely at the level where the target is supplied. It is the regime in which the local update provably converges to backpropagation, and the regime every result on [[wiki/concepts/biologically-plausible-credit-assignment.md]] is scored against

## Position B

**At every level, and the graph is not a chain.** Classifying letters has variability in stroke angle and stroke length as much as in pixel colour; and a child learning letter sounds has a latent *letter* that causes both the seen shape and the heard sound — a two-armed tree with noise on both arms, not a chain with noise at one end. Predictive-coding networks with **no** variance parameter dominating are therefore argued to be the better model of cortex, and they are the ones that learn bidirectional associations (Whittington & Bogacz 2017)

## Where it bites

Decides whether backpropagation-approximation is the target for biologically plausible learning or merely a calibration point of it. If the second column is right, the wiki's whole scoring of local rules by "how close to backpropagation" measures the wrong thing, and the precision ratio becomes a design knob rather than an approximation error

## Status

`LEANING` — toward the second column. The argument is structural rather than empirical (no experiment measures cortical precision ratios), but it is supported by the fact that the equivalence limit is *anti-biological*: `Σ^(0) → ∞` drives error-node activity to zero, which spikes cannot encode, while the usable setting `Σ^(0) = 1` costs nothing measurable on MNIST (0.00% train, 1.7–1.8% validation, same as backpropagation)
