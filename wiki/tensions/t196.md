# T196 — Should a compound's meaning depend only on its immediate children, or on the whole structure it sits in?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The localism test is re-run with the training argument-length distribution controlled, since over-length application failure has a diagnosed cause independent of the composition regime.

## Position A

**Local (strong / first-level) composition.** Meaning is built bottom-up per node, so any sub-expression is substitutable by its own value without changing the result — `14 − (2+3)` = `14 − 5`. Every program-induction and parse-tree architecture in the wiki assumes it: [[wiki/concepts/program-induction.md]]'s straight-line compositions, [[wiki/entities/bayesian-program-learning.md]]'s part/sub-part hierarchy, [[wiki/entities/neo-neural-theorizer.md]]'s executor applied to intermediate states — and it is what makes an intermediate node an *estimate of something* rather than a hidden activation (G10). Hupkes et al. 2020 make it testable: unroll the expression against the model and compare

## Position B

**Global (weak) composition.** Meaning follows from the *total* structure plus the atoms, so a compound can mean different things inside different wholes. Carnap's case: any two tautologies `X`, `Y` are truth-conditionally identical, yet `Peter thinks that X` and `Peter thinks that Y` need not agree — the local reading forces a false identity, the global one does not. The source calls the localist account "highly controversial" for natural language, and the three architectures tested behave globally (localism consistency 0.46 / 0.59 / 0.54): they learn *chunks of adjacent functions* and apply them at once, which the authors decline to call a mistake — "imagine computing `5 + 3 − 3`" ([[wiki/entities/pcfg-set.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a low localism score is a defect to fix or a strategy to permit, and therefore whether the wiki's per-node composition assumption is a requirement or a modelling convenience. Concretely: if global composition is admissible, then G10's "unreliable intermediate nodes" is partly a category error — the model was never obliged to have them — and the wiki's cheapest structure probe (ask the model to parse, or to evaluate a sub-expression) loses its warrant. If local composition is required, then chunking is exactly the shortcut that must be trained out, and PCFG SET's systematicity drop (0.92 → 0.72) is its measured cost

## Status

`LIVE` — and the two positions are separable by an experiment nobody has run. The observed chunking has a *diagnosed* cause that is independent of the local/global question: applying a function to an over-length argument fails, so unrolling breaks for a reason having nothing to do with which composition regime is correct. Re-run the localism test with the training argument-length distribution wide enough that unrolling never produces an out-of-range argument; if consistency stays low, the model genuinely composes globally, and if it rises, the whole result was G70/G69 in disguise
