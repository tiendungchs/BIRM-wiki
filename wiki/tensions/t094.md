# T94 — When a behaviour becomes automatic, does the controller drop out or hand over?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Drops out.** Repeated biasing strengthens the direct sensory→motor pathway until it no longer needs prefrontal cortex; automaticity is the same control mechanism run to convergence, and it *releases* controller capacity (Miller et al. 2002, [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]])

## Position B

**Hands over to another prefrontal region.** Prelimbic cortex mediates voluntary, goal-directed initial responding; the incremental effect of overtraining that makes behaviour autonomous is mediated by **infralimbic** cortex — an adjacent prefrontal subregion with its own limbic and autonomic output ports, not a subcortical bypass (Heidbreder & Groenewegen 2003, [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]]) — **Third position — it neither drops out nor moves subcortically; it moves *within* prefrontal cortex.** Novel tasks activate anterior prefrontal cortex while overlearned tasks activate medial and slightly more posterior prefrontal regions (Koechlin et al. 2000), and prefrontal neurons respond to *known* actions, not only new ones — so the reviewers reject both the guided-activation prediction that the controller approaches irrelevance for frequent rules and Fuster's claim that prefrontal representation is reserved for non-habitual actions. No theory in the table specifies the transfer step either way (Wood & Grafman 2003, [[wiki/concepts/controller-knowledge-vs-process.md]], gap G51)

## Where it bites

Cost accounting for skill acquisition ([[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]]): under A a mastered skill frees the control layer, under B it occupies a different part of it, and the two predict opposite things about how many automatic behaviours can run at once. Also decides what a controller lesion does to an overtrained policy — A predicts it survives, B predicts it survives only if the lesion spares the receiving subregion. Not a clean contradiction: A is primate lateral prefrontal cortex with a rule-learning task, B is rat medial wall with instrumental overtraining, and neither measured the other's variable
