# T333 — Can long-range theta coherence carry content between two modules, or does it only index a shared relay?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The direct projection between a semantic store and a prefrontal binding site is silenced during a relational task, and the theta–gamma phase–amplitude coupling in the prefrontal site is measured: if it survives, the channel is a relay and Position B holds; if it goes with the axons, low-band coherence carries drive after all.

## Position A

**Theta is the long-distance channel, and cross-frequency coupling is the routing mechanism.** Knowlton, Hummel & Holyoak 2012: synchrony in low bands including theta is detectable between sites separated by several millimetres, while local circuit synchrony sits in gamma — so the band a coupling appears in is set by the *distance* it has to travel. The proposal built on this is that semantic information in posterior cortex modulates local gamma in prefrontal cortex *via* theta, the theta phase setting when the local gamma bursts occur, and the gamma phases then holding the mutually desynchronized role bindings. [[wiki/entities/lisa.md]] has no other way to get semantic content into its prefrontal RB units, and the monkey data offered are consistent — prefrontal cortex shows ≈3 Hz spike synchronization alongside the 32 Hz item-phase structure

## Position B

**Theta coherence is what a common input looks like; gamma coherence is what direct drive looks like.** Cutting the wire discriminates them and does so twice in the same direction: optogenetic silencing of ventral-hippocampal terminals in prefrontal cortex abolishes gamma synchrony and leaves theta **unaffected** (Spellman et al. 2015); genetic silencing of the entorhinal → CA1 monosynaptic input does the same (Yamamoto et al. 2014); and inactivating the ventral hippocampus — a candidate *relay* rather than an endpoint — is what disrupts prefrontal–dorsal-hippocampal theta (O'Neill et al. 2013). Under this rule, low-band coherence between two areas is evidence of a third structure driving both, not of a message passing between them ([[wiki/concepts/inter-areal-synchrony.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a machine that binds by phase needs an explicit **routing bus** or can rely on pairwise alignment. Under A, two modules that must exchange bindings need only be phase-aligned in a band chosen for their separation, and cross-frequency coupling is a free scheduler — the cheap architecture, and the one LISA assumes. Under B, any long-range coordination requires a *third* component that broadcasts the slow phase to both endpoints (the medial septum and [[wiki/entities/nucleus-reuniens.md]] are the biological candidates), which means the semantic store and the binder cannot be wired to each other alone: a synchrony architecture needs a clock distributor, and LISA does not have one. The two also disagree about what a coherence measurement licenses — under B the fronto-parietal and fronto-temporal coherences the relational-reasoning literature reports are not evidence that those areas are exchanging anything.

## Status

`LIVE` — the two claims come from different preparations and neither has been run on the other's link. Position B's evidence is causal but entirely rodent hippocampal–prefrontal; Position A's is correlational and human/monkey fronto-posterior, and its distance-sets-the-band rule is a conduction argument that B's experiments do not directly refute — a relay could be how low-band coupling is *achieved* over distance without that making the coupling contentless. What makes the row load-bearing rather than terminological is that LISA needs the coupling to carry *which semantics*, and a shared relay by construction carries the same phase to everyone.
