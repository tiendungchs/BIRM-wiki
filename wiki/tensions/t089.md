# T89 — Is a working memory cleared by decay or by an addressed erase signal?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The beta channel is stimulated or blocked and information tested for persistence past its usual clearing point — the current erase evidence is entirely correlational.

## Position A

**Decay.** Every fast store in the wiki forgets by a time constant or by a capacity rule and none has an erase primitive driven by relevance: short-term-plasticity traces lapse at `τ_f`, `τ_r` ([[wiki/entities/stp-flickering-cann.md]], [[wiki/entities/stsp-working-memory-rnn.md]]), a maintained attractor drifts once the adaptation gain exceeds `τ/τ_v` ([[wiki/entities/adaptive-cann.md]]), and the one explicit free list in the wiki reclaims slots by **usage** and read-order bookkeeping rather than by whether the content still matters ([[wiki/entities/differentiable-neural-computer.md]])

## Position B

**An addressed signal.** In macaque prefrontal cortex the largest time × frequency effect in the whole dataset is a post-trial elevation of beta bursting *at the informative sites only* — non-informative sites are relatively suppressed — while spiking information about the last object drops sharply; and before a test object that an earlier mismatch has made irrelevant, beta rises and the gamma/information ramp is cancelled (Lundqvist et al. 2018, [[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]). The same prospective, relevance-driven deletion appears in single units: whichever of two locations has just become irrelevant decays while the other improves, with the switch *preceding* the saccade that makes it irrelevant (Lebedev et al. 2004)

## Where it bites

Whether a machine fast store needs a third primitive alongside read and write, and what its addressing mode is: under A capacity is recovered by waiting or by eviction policy, under B the controller must be able to name a target and cancel it — which is the operation gap G48's prospective clear and gap G42's occupancy accounting both require. It also changes the failure analysis: a decaying store fails by losing what it should have kept, an erasing store can fail by clearing something still needed, and only the second shows up as a *control* error

## Status

`LIVE` — the erase evidence is entirely correlational (local-field-potential burst rates, no causal manipulation of beta), and decay is not ruled out by it: post-trial beta could accompany a lapse it does not cause. The discriminating experiment is stimulating or blocking the beta channel and asking whether information persists past its usual clearing point
