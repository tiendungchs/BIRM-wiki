# T116 — When a functionally defined subensemble and its parent population rank two channels in opposite orders, which reading is the pathway's function?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A downstream reader is shown to act on the population average or on the subensemble — the projection's function is whichever the receiving structure actually reads.

## Position A

**The whole population.** It is the unbiased sample of what the projection carries, and any subensemble is defined by a criterion imported from a different task — here, amygdala-projecting prefrontal cells separate social from non-social stimuli better than accumbens-projecting cells across all recorded neurons (`P < 0.001`), which is the claim a downstream reader receiving the whole projection would act on

## Position B

**The subensemble.** Averaging over a population masks the cells that carry the state — the same dataset finds the ranking reversed within centre-ON cells (`P < 0.001`), and it is the subensemble, not the population, whose activity tracks anxiogenic-zone entry, unfamiliar-open-arm entry and social preference across three assays a day apart (Lai et al. 2026)

## Where it bites

Every claim of the form "module X encodes Y" in the wiki, and every model-vs-brain comparison that fits a decoder to all units: the cell-selection rule is a free parameter that can flip the sign of the result, and nothing in the data picks it. A downstream structure receiving the axons has no way to apply the experimenter's subensemble criterion, which argues for the population — unless the subensemble is itself defined by a projection or cell type the reader can address
