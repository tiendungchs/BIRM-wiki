# T231 — Is the accuracy of deep spiking networks evidence about spiking *learning*, or only about spiking *inference*?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The rewrite is what the row now carries — offline versus online credit assignment — and closes when a network learned wholly in spikes matches a converted one past MNIST.

## Position A

**Learning — the gap to conventional deep nets is closing.** The standard summary of the field, and the one this wiki's SNN page carried until now: spiking networks lag but the gap is decreasing, vanishes on some tasks, and is bought with far fewer operations (Tavanaei et al. 2019, abstract)

## Position B

**Inference only.** The same review's comparison table splits into two tracks, and they are anti-correlated. Offline: train a rate network by backpropagation, copy the weights, replace activations with firing rates — highest accuracy, the only track reported past MNIST (CIFAR-10, ImageNet), and **nothing was learned in spikes**. Online: genuine multi-layer spike-based learning — lower accuracy, no result beyond MNIST, and the strongest entries (99.05%) are layer-wise local training with a hand-crafted difference-of-Gaussian front end and an SVM readout. The best *spike-native* end-to-end number is 98.88% on MNIST via surrogate gradients, which is offline BPTT wearing a membrane potential

## Where it bites

Decides what the SNN literature's headline numbers license. If B, then every conversion result is an argument that a rate specification is *sufficient* for those tasks — a datum for T1 position A supplied by the neuromorphic literature itself — and the case for spiking substrates has to be made on energy, on delayed-error learning, or on representations rate models cannot express ([[wiki/entities/spiking-tem.md]]), never on benchmark accuracy

## Status

`LIVE` — re-scoped, not settled: as written the row is answered, but the rewrite at the end of this cell (offline-vs-online credit assignment) is open and is what the ten pages citing T231 now rely on. The split is the review's own finding, not an outside criticism; A is a summary that averages over the split. **A third track now sits outside the split and lands on the same side** (Zhao et al. 2022, [[wiki/concepts/cross-paradigm-interface.md]]): the HRN reasons on CLEVRER at 91.65 / 95.27 / 85.96 / 78.81% across four question types — far past MNIST, on a spiking network — and nothing in the spiking half was learned at all. The graph of symbol and operator nodes is hand-authored, the instruction set manually specified, the object–attribute edges written by a Hebb rule, and every gradient lives in the ANN front ends and in one interface module. So the competence is borrowed from a **designer** rather than from a converted rate network, which is a different mechanism reaching B's conclusion and a warning that "spike-native" needs to mean *learned* in spikes, not merely *run* in spikes. **The empirical half of B has since expired** (Wang et al. 2024, [[wiki/concepts/neuronal-parameter-heterogeneity.md]]): HIFI trains end-to-end through the spiking dynamics by surrogate gradients — no conversion, no hand-authored graph — and reports **95.98% CIFAR-10, 79.32% CIFAR-100 and 69.11% ImageNet at 4 time steps**, above every conversion baseline in its table including QCFS (67.73% ImageNet at 32 steps); and its entire 2021–2024 baseline set (PLIF, Dspike, DSR, GLIF, TET, SLTT, Diet-SNN, OSR, TAB) is spike-native too, so this is a field-wide reversal rather than one model. B's factual premise — *the online track never leaves MNIST* — was a 2019 statement and is now false. **What survives, and it is a narrower claim than B made:** these gradients are computed by backpropagation through time, offline and non-locally. The line that still separates the two tracks is not converted-vs-spike-native but **offline-vs-online**, and no local or online rule has moved past MNIST. Rewritten that way the tension is about credit assignment, not about accuracy
