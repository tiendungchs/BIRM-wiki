# T3 — Is backpropagation biologically realizable?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

Not as stated: it needs weight transport and non-local error signals, neither of which cortex provides

## Position B

Feedback alignment, predictive-coding and energy-based nets approximate it with fixed random feedback and purely local updates, with correspondences to spike-timing-dependent plasticity

## Where it bites

Decides whether the slow-**W** meta-graph learner can exist in a neural substrate, and whether "biologically plausible" still rules anything out

## Status

`LEANING` — toward realizable approximations, but **narrowed** by Schmidgall et al. 2023: plain feedback alignment is suboptimal at ImageNet scale, and only sign-symmetry — which still transports sign information — matches backpropagation on large data. The surviving approximation is the least biologically defensible one. **And the two objections must now be scored separately** (Whittington & Bogacz 2019): the four models that most convincingly answer *non-locality* — contrastive learning, continuous update, predictive coding, dendritic error, all reaching ~1.7–3% MNIST error with purely Hebbian updates — every one of them **assumes symmetric feedforward/feedback weights** and relies on its plasticity rule to maintain the symmetry. So the family that solves locality does not touch weight transport, and the family that solves weight transport (sign-symmetry) transports the load-bearing part. No model currently answers both. **A partial escape, asserted not measured:** in predictive coding the reciprocal prediction and error synapses obey the same pre×post rule with the roles swapped, so approximate symmetry is a fixed point the two synapses reach independently rather than an assumption — which, if it holds under nonlinearity at depth, converts the symmetry assumption into a *consequence* of the locality solution (talk-nd-brain-learning-algorithm, **(tentative)**) **And the question may be malformed** (Whittington & Bogacz 2017): the predictive-coding equivalence is not approximate-with-unknown-error but exact in the limit `Σ^(0) → ∞`, which is the limit that drives error-node activity to zero and so *removes* the spiking implementation. Realizability and equivalence trade off along a single parameter, so "is backpropagation realizable" has no fixed answer — see T119.
