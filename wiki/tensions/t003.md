# T3 — Is backpropagation biologically realizable?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The weight-transport and non-local-error objections are scored separately, and an approximation that transports neither matches backpropagation at ImageNet scale — sign-symmetry does not count.

## Position A

Not as stated: it needs weight transport and non-local error signals, neither of which cortex provides

## Position B

Feedback alignment, predictive-coding and energy-based nets approximate it with fixed random feedback and purely local updates, with correspondences to spike-timing-dependent plasticity

## Where it bites

Decides whether the slow-**W** meta-graph learner can exist in a neural substrate, and whether "biologically plausible" still rules anything out

## Status

`LEANING` — toward realizable approximations, but **narrowed** by Schmidgall et al. 2023: plain feedback alignment is suboptimal at ImageNet scale, and only sign-symmetry — which still transports sign information — matches backpropagation on large data. The surviving approximation is the least biologically defensible one. **And the two objections must now be scored separately** (Whittington & Bogacz 2019): the four models that most convincingly answer *non-locality* — contrastive learning, continuous update, predictive coding, dendritic error, all reaching ~1.7–3% MNIST error with purely Hebbian updates — every one of them **assumes symmetric feedforward/feedback weights** and relies on its plasticity rule to maintain the symmetry. So the family that solves locality does not touch weight transport, and the family that solves weight transport (sign-symmetry) transports the load-bearing part. No model currently answers both. **A partial escape, asserted not measured:** in predictive coding the reciprocal prediction and error synapses obey the same pre×post rule with the roles swapped, so approximate symmetry is a fixed point the two synapses reach independently rather than an assumption — which, if it holds under nonlinearity at depth, converts the symmetry assumption into a *consequence* of the locality solution (talk-nd-brain-learning-algorithm, **(tentative)**) **And the question may be malformed** (Whittington & Bogacz 2017): the predictive-coding equivalence is not approximate-with-unknown-error but exact in the limit `Σ^(0) → ∞`, which is the limit that drives error-node activity to zero and so *removes* the spiking implementation. Realizability and equivalence trade off along a single parameter, so "is backpropagation realizable" has no fixed answer — see T119. **The dendritic-error member now has in vivo support for its error channel and none for its transport story** (Francioni et al. 2026, [[wiki/concepts/vectorized-instructive-signals.md]]): apical tufts of layer-5 pyramidal cells carry a signed, cell-specific, pre-outcome error whose optogenetic removal blocks learning — so *something* addressed reaches individual neurons in cortex, but the measurement says nothing about whether that address is `Wᵀ`, its sign, or a random `B`, which is the half of the question this row turns on. The authors read their own data as closer to difference target propagation than to backpropagation, which points the row at a family Bartunov et al. 2018 measured at chance on ImageNet.
