# T271 — Does the workspace commit on stimulus offset?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The offset is made task-relevant so awareness of it is enforced: a second ignition must then appear, or the framework's prediction fails.

## Position A

**Yes — two ignitions per stimulus.** The COGITATE adversarial collaboration's preregistered formalisation of the global workspace theory drew two successive peaks, one at stimulus onset and one at offset, and reported that only the first appeared (Cogitate et al. 2025, Nature, as described in `raw/naccache-2025-gnw-adversarial-testing.md`)

## Position B

**No — the commit fires on change in the *attended content*, not on change in the input.** Naccache et al. 2025: offset ignition is expected only if the participant consciously attends the offset, and no simulation (1998–2011) or recording in the framework's own literature ever produced one. Onset ignition was confirmed at 200–800 ms, independent of stimulus duration and of task relevance

## Where it bites

Which signal a machine step-boundary generator should listen to. Under A a boundary is emitted at every input transition (two per stimulus, computable from the input stream alone); under B a boundary is emitted only when a coalition *wins* the bus, so a fading incumbent produces no event and the boundary count depends on the controller's state, not the stimulus ([[wiki/concepts/ignition.md]], [[wiki/concepts/event-segmentation.md]])

## Status

`LEANING B` on the framework's own record — the absence of offset ignition in every prior simulation is auditable and is not a post-hoc claim. But the reply names the discriminating experiment and it is unrun: make the offset task-relevant so awareness of it is enforced, and a second ignition must appear. Until then B is unfalsifiable in the direction that matters. **A complication for B's rationale**, from the one recording of a commit with no input change at all: B argues no event fires when an incumbent merely fades, because *"nothing wins by default"* — but in no-report rivalry the release event fires **before** the incumbent's dominance ends (−198 ms vs −97 ms), so the fade is preceded by a dateable event rather than following one. Whether that event is a *commit* or only a permission is exactly what [[wiki/concepts/cortical-state-bistability.md]] leaves open

**The primary record is now in the wiki and it strengthens A's instrument while leaving B's claim standing** (Ferrante et al. 2025, `raw/ferrante-2025-gnwt-iit-adversarial-testing.md`): the offset null is **0 of 655** prefrontal electrodes with `BF₀₁ > 3` on every one, and the same model recovered the onset-plus-offset profile in **10 striate/extrastriate electrodes**, so it is not an analysis failure. Critically, the null held **in the task-relevant condition too**, where category information was stronger, more stable and longer lasting — the closest approximation available to B's demanded "attend the offset" condition, though attention was on category and not on the offset itself. B's positive half also survives: prefrontal onset decoding generalised over only ~0.2–0.4 s, i.e. an event and not an occupancy state.
