# T341 — Does a cortical area carry one signed prediction-error population, or two sign-specific ones?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Visual tuning curves and mismatch responses are quantified in the **same** L2/3 cells. Two populations require that the cells driven by unexpected added input and the cells responding to omitted expected input have non-overlapping feature preferences; one population requires the same cohort with the same tuning for both signs. The baseline-rate half of this row is answered (see Status) — what remains is the tuning half, which whole-cell recording could not reach in its available recording time and which existing two-photon datasets may already contain.

## Position A

**One population, signed by rate.** Every formalism in the wiki uses a single error variable: `e^{S_{i−1}} = y^{S_{i−1}} − (W^{S_i})^T·y^{S_i}` has one sign per unit and takes both ([[wiki/concepts/predictive-coding-free-energy.md]] equation 2), and the laminar assignment gives L2/3 pyramids `ξ^(i)` — one precision-weighted residual on causes, broadcast forward ([[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]], Bastos et al. 2012). The existence proof that a nervous system can do it this way is dopaminergic: midbrain neurons carry a bidirectional reward prediction error on one population, increases above and decreases below a sustained baseline ([[wiki/concepts/reward-prediction-error.md]]). Every machine predictive-coding network in the wiki is built this way and none reports a defect traceable to it.

## Position B

**Two populations, wired in opposite directions.** Cortical L2/3 principal cells have baseline rates far below the dopaminergic case, so a rate decrease cannot carry the negative half; the error computation must be split into a circuit that responds when bottom-up excitation exceeds top-down inhibition (positive error) and one that responds when top-down excitation exceeds bottom-up inhibition (negative error), with a somatostatin-expressing interneuron subset supplying the bottom-up inhibition to the latter in mouse V1 (Keller & Mrsic-Flogel 2018, [[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]]). The two are functionally dissociable in their targets — negative errors net *suppress* the internal representation, positive errors net *activate* a new cohort — and the observation the split was built to explain is a response to the **absence** of an expected input, which has no reading as a decrease from a near-zero baseline.

**The proposal predates the rate-budget argument by nineteen years, on independent premises.** Rao & Ballard 1999 already proposed two cortical populations for positive and negative residuals — arguing from the fact that the residual is *signed* plus the anatomical precedent of ON-centre/OFF-centre splitting in the early visual pathway, with no appeal to firing rates ([[wiki/concepts/predictive-coding-free-energy.md]]). Position B therefore does not stand or fall with the baseline-rate premise alone.

## Where it bites

`L1`: how many blocks a cortical module has. Under A a machine area is one estimator plus one residual channel, and the sign is carried by the number on the wire; under B the residual channel is two channels with different input wiring, different learned inhibitory circuits, and *opposite* effects on the receiving module — which means the receiver must know which channel a message came on, and a rectified single channel silently discards half the update. It also decides what a silencing experiment means: under A, suppressing the error population removes both directions of update; under B it removes only one, and the model drifts in a direction the experiment cannot infer. Downstream of it sit [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] **T118** (what a superficial pyramidal spike means, which presupposes there is one answer) and every wiki claim that reads a scalar surprise off a model ([[wiki/concepts/violation-of-expectation.md]]).

## Status

`LEANING` — **toward B for the wiring and the transmitted message, toward A for the variable inside the cell.** Jordan & Keller 2020 (`raw/jordan-2020-opposing-topdown-bottomup-layer23-v1.md`, [[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]]) ran the measurement this row was opened for, intracellularly, in mouse V1 under closed-loop visuomotor coupling:

| Result | Moves |
|---|---|
| 78% (25/32) of L2/3 cells show a significant **subthreshold** mismatch response, and the distribution of response amplitudes is **unimodal** from hyperpolarizing to depolarizing | **→ A.** A signed residual exists as a graded per-cell quantity; `V_m` in one cell moves in both directions, so the negative half needs no separate carrier at the level of the computation |
| The distribution of input-sign *angles* — corr(`V_m`, visual flow) against corr(`V_m`, locomotion) — is **bimodal**, 17/22 cells in an opposing-sign quadrant, corr-vs-corr R = −0.65 | **→ B.** The two inputs arrive with opposite signs and the cells fall into two wiring classes, which is the decomposition B asserts |
| Only **10%** of L2/3 cells produce a *spiking* mismatch response against 78% subthreshold; baseline rates confirmed low | **→ B, at the output only.** The spike threshold half-wave-rectifies: each cell can transmit only the depolarizing half of its own signed residual |
| Six intrinsic electrophysiological properties predict **77%** of the variance in mismatch response | **→ B.** The sign classes differ biophysically, i.e. they are plausibly distinct cell types rather than two ends of one continuum |

**The synthesis the wiki now carries:** the residual is *one signed scalar per cell* and *two rectified channels between cells*. B's rate-budget premise survives — but it constrains the output stage, not the comparator, so refuting it would no longer collapse the decomposition even before the Rao & Ballard 1999 sign-representation argument is invoked.

**What keeps the row open.** The `Closes when` test above was explicitly not run: quantifying mismatch responses and visual tuning curves in the same neurons is beyond the recording time of whole-cell work, so "the two sign classes have disjoint tuning" is still asserted. A purely visual account of the dMM/hMM difference — dMM responses as relief from cross-orientation suppression — is ruled out only indirectly, by the fact that the *locomotion*-side correlation anticorrelates with the visual-side one, which no bottom-up-only model produces. Two method limits also bound the verdict: current clamp cannot separate an increase in excitation from a decrease in inhibition, and somatic recording under low awake input resistance underestimates the apical-dendritic somatostatin inhibition the negative-error circuit depends on.

**Superseded reasoning (kept for the record).** The dispute was not about data — both sides accept the mouse V1 mismatch responses — but about whether the low cortical baseline rate is a real constraint or an artefact of how baselines are measured. It is unusually cheap to move because Position B's *2018* form rests on a single quantitative premise (the rate budget) rather than on an interpretation (though refuting that premise leaves the 1999 sign-representation argument standing), and because the two positions differ in a tuning statistic that existing two-photon datasets may already contain: under A the cells responding to unexpected added input and the cells responding to omitted expected input are the same cohort with the same feature preference; under B they are disjoint.
