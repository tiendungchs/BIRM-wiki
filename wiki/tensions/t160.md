# T160 — Does the geometric quality of a representation predict whether the model uses it?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A geometric statistic is found that predicts steering success — the current ranking inverts, so no published statistic does.

## Position A

**Yes — that is the working assumption of the whole find-a-direction-then-steer-it pipeline.** Clean antipodal structure, high probe accuracy and high variance-explained are what identify a direction as a feature; the Othello case is the existence proof, where the 99.6%-accurate direction is also the one that installs a counterfactual board at 0.10 errors ([[wiki/entities/othellogpt.md]]).

## Position B

**No, and the ranking inverts.** In the same model, same site, same fitted subspace, same 100-trial protocol: `in front ↔ behind` is the *best*-aligned inverse pair at layer 24 (cos 0.9950) and steers at **5%**, while `above ↔ below` is the *worst* (0.9779) and steers at **100%**; `left` 100% vs `right` 79% splits a single axis. Geometry scores a fitted summary, steering scores a handle, and the two are different measurements (Tehenan et al. 2025).

## Where it bites

Any interpretability pipeline that selects which features to intervene on by geometric quality — i.e. the default one. It also weakens aggregate steering rates as evidence: 74.3% overall is a mixture of three saturated relations and one that fails almost completely, so the aggregate describes no relation in the set. Competing explanations for the failure are untested and cheap to separate: multi-token surface form (the source's own), or an unstable reference frame on the only axis whose convention varies ([[wiki/concepts/distributed-reference-frames.md]]).
