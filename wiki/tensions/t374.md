# T374 — When a top-down prediction meets matching feedforward drive, does the receiving cell burst or fall silent?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One preparation reports, for the *same* cortical area under closed-loop conditions, the response of identified L5 thick-tufted pyramids and identified L2/3 pyramids to a top-down input that matches their feedforward drive — with dendritic Ca²⁺ events measured alongside somatic rate, so "burst" and "cancel" are distinguished at the compartment that is claimed to do the arithmetic. A layer-wise split (L2/3 subtracts, L5 amplifies) would settle it by partition rather than by verdict.

## Position A

**Amplification.** Coincident basal drive and apical-tuft feedback halve the apical Ca²⁺ threshold and produce a plateau and a 2–4 spike ~200 Hz burst; under noisy input the same mechanism raises the gain of the feedforward transfer function. A match is therefore the cell's *maximal* output, the matching population is the content, and no downstream stage is needed to detect the agreement (Larkum 2013, [[wiki/concepts/apical-amplification.md]]). Supporting in vivo: motor-cortex feedback to layer 1 is necessary for whisker-position tuft plateaus in L5 (Xu et al. 2012), and figure–ground enhancement in V1 is late, feedback-dependent, and absent on misses (Supèr et al. 2001)

## Position B

**Cancellation.** The descending glutamatergic projection is converted by a local learned inhibitory circuit into a subtraction, so a matched prediction *silences* the error population and only the residual ascends; end-stopping and surround suppression are this circuit at the scale of one receptive field, and removing the descending term in the model deletes end-stopping from 28/32 error units (Rao & Ballard 1999). The cell-class version splits the error by sign across two populations with opposite wiring (Keller & Mrsic-Flogel 2018, [[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]]), and the laminar assignment puts the error on superficial pyramids (Bastos et al. 2012, [[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]])

## Where it bites

What travels on the ascending wire, and therefore what every level above is computing on. Under A the feedforward message is the *confirmed content* weighted by how well it was predicted, and a reader downstream gets the interpretation; under B it is the *unexplained part*, and a reader downstream gets a residual it can only interpret against a prediction it must hold itself. The two also invert the cost model: A spends bandwidth in proportion to how well the model fits and can afford no compression, B spends bandwidth in proportion to how badly it fits and makes a well-modelled world nearly free. It further decides what a burst means for plasticity — a confirmation signal under A, an error signal under B ([[wiki/concepts/biologically-plausible-credit-assignment.md]], `T328`) — and hence the sign of the write a coincidence licenses.

## Status

`LIVE`, and the obvious reconciliation is untested rather than refuted: the two claims are made about different cell classes (L5 thick-tufted for A, L2/3 for B) that the laminar assignment already types differently — representation units versus error units — so a cortex in which superficial cells subtract and deep cells amplify satisfies both. What blocks scoring that reading is that nobody has measured the two populations' responses to a matched prediction in one preparation, and that A's evidence is largely *in vitro* coupling physiology plus correlative perceptual recordings while B's is closed-loop mismatch imaging plus a model — the two literatures share no experiment. Interacts with `T341` (which asks how many error populations there are, presupposing that the ascending signal is an error at all) and with `T259` (A's layer-1 port makes any level addressable by any higher area, which a chain does not permit).

**Half of the settle-by-partition option has been measured, on the cancellation side.** Under closed-loop visuomotor coupling in mouse V1, superficial cells integrate top-down (locomotion) and bottom-up (visual flow) drive with **opposing** signs while cells at 480–750 µm integrate both **positively**, with no difference between the tiers in the magnitude of either input (Jordan & Keller 2020, [[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]], [[wiki/concepts/canonical-cortical-microcircuit.md]]). That is the L2/3-subtracts half of the partition, and it makes the deep tier's job a sum rather than a difference. It does not deliver the row's test: the recordings are somatic current clamp with no dendritic Ca²⁺ measure, only 2 of 14 deep cells were below 600 µm, thick-tufted L5 cells were not identified as such, and a positively integrating cell is not thereby an *amplifying* one. The authors also note that somatic recording underestimates apical inhibition, which is the compartment Position A's arithmetic happens in — so this measurement is systematically blind to the mechanism it would have to rule out.

**Sibling row (S12 audit, lint 25): `T376`** — the same apical–basal junction read at two grains, and `T376` already cites this row. This row asks what one cell does when a prediction meets matching drive (burst or silence); `T376` asks whether the coupling that decides it is a global state variable or a per-column gate. A per-column answer there makes this row's sign a *settable* property rather than a fixed cellular rule.
