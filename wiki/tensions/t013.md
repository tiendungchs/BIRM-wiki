# T13 — Is long-term potentiation the mechanism behind behavioural learning?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A behavioural learning event is shown to require a plasticity mechanism whose timescale matches it, with the LTP-decay objection answered by measurement rather than by the eligibility-window mechanism alone.

## Position A

**Yes** — long-term potentiation and spike-timing-dependent plasticity are the standard biological warrant for the Hebbian rule family, cited as biologically supported (Bi & Poo 2001) and used as the substrate story for fast **M** ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]])

## Position B

**The timescales do not match.** LTP's critical interstimulus interval is orders of magnitude shorter than behaviourally relevant intervals, and manipulating interstimulus and intertrial intervals together shows **no critical interval exists**; behaviour persists for weeks or months while LTP decays to baseline in days (Power et al. 1997); learned behaviour is rapidly reacquired after extinction (Bouton 2004) with no matching facilitation in LTP (de Jonge & Racine 1985). Implementing structured model building with purely Hebbian mechanisms would be "especially challenging" (Gallistel & Matzel 2013, via Lake et al. 2017)

## Where it bites

Decides whether "biologically supported" is an argument for a local coactivity rule at all. If B holds, the plasticity family keeps its engineering case (deployment-time writes, locality, spiking substrates) and loses its biological one — and the wiki's fast **M** needs a different substrate story

## Status

`LEANING A` on the interval sub-claim — **behavioral timescale synaptic plasticity supplies the missing mechanism**: a seconds-wide eligibility window gated by a dendritic plateau, producing a reproducible place field from one traversal, measured in CA1 and CA3 in vivo (Bittner et al. 2017; Li et al. 2023; Gonzalez et al. 2023, via Liao & Losonczy 2024). So the family is no longer restricted to a 20 ms kernel and the interval mismatch stops being decisive. B's persistence and reacquisition-after-extinction sub-claims are untouched, and BTSP inherits its own version of the problem: the molecular substrate of the seconds-long trace is unidentified (αCaMKII is a candidate)
