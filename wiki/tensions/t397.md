# T397 — Is the effort cost term one block serving both domains, or two separately-addressed costs for cognitive and physical effort?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One agent is run through *matched* valuation procedures for cognitive and physical demand — same reward scale, same duration, same performance contingency, indifference points elicited in both — under a manipulation that is predicted to move one cost and not the other (dopamine antagonism is the named candidate). Position A predicts the two discount curves shift together and that a lesion or blockade moving one moves the other. Position B predicts a double dissociation, with each domain's curve independently movable.

## Position A

**One cost term, domain-general.** [[wiki/concepts/expected-value-of-control.md]] (Shenhav et al. 2013) states it as a property of the theory: `Cost(signal)` is a function of control **intensity**, and the same dorsal anterior cingulate signal that tracks cognitive demand also tracks physical effort and revises expected reward downward for it. The equations are written over "control signals" with motor and cognitive signals undergoing the same optimisation, which is why the theory predicts dorsal anterior cingulate engagement for a motor action and an abstract strategy alike. Supporting datum from the other side: an incentive study finds ventral-striatal motivation signals common to physical and cognitive demand (Westbrook & Braver 2015's own citation).

## Position B

**Two costs, separately addressed.** [[wiki/concepts/effort-based-decision-making.md]] (Westbrook & Braver 2015) assembles three lines:

| Line | Content |
|---|---|
| Pharmacological dissociation | In a rat cognitive-effort task, **dopamine antagonism decreased preference for physical effort and not for cognitive effort**; amphetamine's effect on cognitive-effort preference is non-monotone |
| Metabolic asymmetry | Physical action has a real energetic cost; cognitive control does not — global cerebral glucose consumption rises ≤ ~1% during vigorous engagement, and astrocytic glycogen serves local transient demand. The resource whose opportunity cost is paid is not the same resource in the two domains |
| Computational asymmetry | The vigour account (average reward rate reported by striatal dopamine tone) regulates a **goal-general** quantity and predicts vigour *falls* when errors accumulate, while control theories predict intensity *rises* on errors — so the regulating computation differs, not merely its input |

B's positive proposal is that systems evolved to price physical exertion may have been **recruited** for cognitive engagement, which would produce partial overlap in valuation regions (A's evidence) without a shared cost variable.

## Where it bites

**Whether an architecture needs one price or a price per resource.** Under A, an agent holds a single scalar budget and every controller — motor vigour, working-memory maintenance, rollout depth — draws on it, so raising one lowers the others automatically and a single dial tunes the whole system's willingness to work. Under B, each resource has its own price with its own units and its own modulator, and the agent needs an exchange rate it can only get by learning one — which is a separate mechanism nothing in the wiki supplies, and which reintroduces the common-currency problem [[wiki/concepts/subjective-value.md]] solves for outcomes but not for costs.

What each side **denies**. A cannot express the dopamine dissociation at all: if the cost is one term, a manipulation that moves the physical curve must move the cognitive one. B cannot explain why the same dorsal anterior cingulate tissue tracks both without conceding either that the region hosts two separately-addressed costs — which nothing has resolved at that resolution — or that its signal is downstream of both and therefore not the cost itself.

Evidence asymmetry: A's support is a theoretical commitment plus co-activation in coarse imaging; B's support is one rodent pharmacology result in a single novel task plus two arguments from disanalogy. Neither side has run the matched-valuation design, because no valuation procedure for cognitive effort has been run alongside one for physical effort in the same subjects.
