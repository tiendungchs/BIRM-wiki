# T73 — Should a one-shot store allocate memories to *overlapping* cell populations, or to independently sampled ones?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Plateau incidence is measured conditioned on a cell's recent excitability tag — the direct test of whether allocation is independent or biased toward recently tagged cells.

## Position A

**Overlapping, deliberately.** Allocate-to-link: a CREB-dependent excitability tag decaying over hours biases the next memory into the cells that just encoded, and the resulting overlap *is* the linkage — demonstrated behaviourally by transfer of fear to a linked-but-never-paired context (Lisman et al. 2018, [[wiki/concepts/memory-allocation-excitability.md]])

## Position B

**Independently, and that independence is what makes the store work.** BTSP's plateau gate is modelled as an input-independent Poisson process at rate `f_q ≈ 0.005`, which spreads memory load uniformly and is credited with the flat recall curve across 10,000 sequentially learned items; the postsynaptically-gated one-shot rule it is compared against recruits the same cells repeatedly and degrades early traces badly (Wu & Maass 2025, [[wiki/entities/btsp-cam.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a fast store's write-target rule may carry information at all. If A, temporal linkage is free and interference is the price; if B, non-interference is free and *every* edge between episodes must be built by a separate mechanism. Both are claims about hippocampal encoding, and both are supported — so the likely resolution is that they operate at different timescales (seconds-wide gating within an episode, hours-wide tagging across episodes) rather than that one is wrong

## Status

`LIVE` — no experiment has measured plateau incidence conditioned on a cell's recent excitability tag, which is the direct test
