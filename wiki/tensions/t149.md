# T149 — Must a vision encoder be trained with language supervision to align well with a language model?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The question is re-posed as VL-JEPA poses it — language as the space a predictor predicts into rather than as encoder supervision — and tested on appearance content, where B's advantage is unsettled.

## Position A

**Yes — the standing assumption of the whole multimodal-LLM stack.** Essentially every encoder used for video question answering (CLIP, SigLIP, Perception Encoder) is pretrained on image–caption pairs, chosen explicitly for the semantic alignment that supervision buys; the field treats language-supervised features as the ones an LLM's input embedding space can absorb.

## Position B

**No.** In a controlled comparison — same Qwen2-7B backbone, same 18M alignment samples, frozen encoder, only the encoder swapped — a video encoder pretrained with *no* language supervision at all averages 52.3 against Perception Encoder's 49.1, SigLIP2's 48.1 and DINOv2's 45.7, winning every benchmark but PerceptionTest, with the largest margins on the temporal ones (MVP 31.1 vs 26.7, TemporalBench 33.3 vs 27.5). Scaled to 88.5M alignment samples it is state of the art at the 8B class on five benchmarks (Assran et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]]).

## Where it bites

Whether language is a *source* of conceptual structure or only a *readout* of it — the same question [[wiki/concepts/compositionality.md]] asks about whether syntax supplies the binding format (T8). If a purely self-supervised visual objective produces features an LLM can consume, then alignment is a projection problem solved at fine-tuning time, and the case for language-supervised pretraining is about data efficiency rather than about representation. Limits: one architecture, alignment data held fixed but pretraining data not, and the losses (PerceptionTest, TVBench, MVBench) cluster on the *appearance*-biased benchmarks, which is exactly where this encoder is independently weakest. **A second, independent B-side data point, on a quantity rather than a benchmark** (Oquab et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/dinov2.md]]): a 300M self-supervised iBOT beats a 1.8B language-supervised OpenCLIP ViT-G on all three monocular-depth benchmarks (NYUd linear RMSE 0.417 vs 0.541), and DINOv2 ViT-g reaches 0.344 — with the qualitative failure being OpenCLIP omitting objects entirely from its depth map. Depth is precisely the content [[wiki/concepts/cross-modal-grounding.md]]'s rate argument says a 77-token caption never carries, so the two sides may not disagree about supervision at all: language supervision looks like a *filter* on which quantities survive, winning where captions are informative (SUN397, Stanford Cars, ImageNet-R/Sketch) and losing where they are silent (depth, fine-grained species, temporal structure).

## Status

`LEANING` — toward B on temporal content, unsettled on appearance. **A third system suggests the question is mis-posed** (Chen et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/vl-jepa.md]]): it keeps a language-free video encoder *frozen* and puts language in as the space a downstream predictor predicts **into**, and beats the best language-supervised encoder on 8 zero-shot video classification sets (52.5 vs 44.7) and 8 retrieval sets (63.7 vs 58.1) at **26× fewer image–text pairs** (3.3B vs 86B). If the rate bound is a property of what the *encoder* was trained on, then language is free to be a target without ever being a bottleneck — and the appearance-side losses persist exactly as the frozen encoder predicts (K400 64.8 vs 76.4)
