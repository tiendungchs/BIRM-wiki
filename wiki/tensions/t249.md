# T249 — Is more integration always better, or is there an interior optimum?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Integration is pushed past the range humans occupy in a model fitted to human data, locating the interior optimum the coherence argument predicts and the graded-demand data has never reached.

## Position A

**Monotone, at least over the range humans occupy.** Position along the between-module-connectivity axis is graded by task demand (motor least integrated → N-back most), the *most* integrated windows have the highest global efficiency, the fastest drift rate and the shortest non-decision time, and the resting brain already spends 70.32 ± 1.4% of its time in the integrated state — with no sign of a turning point anywhere in the observed range (Shine et al. 2016, [[wiki/concepts/integration-segregation-balance.md]])

## Position B

**Interior optimum, and the far end is catastrophic.** A network whose coupling gain is raised until global coherence `R → 1` has a repertoire of size one: every unit does the same thing, no coalition structure exists, and `σ_R → 0`. Six whole-brain models with incompatible node dynamics all fit empirical resting connectivity *only* at the edge of the bifurcation, not past it (Cabral et al. 2014, [[wiki/concepts/metastability.md]]), and clinically the fully-integrated end is the seizure

## Where it bites

The two are probably measuring different things — participation coefficient is a *routing* statistic that says how a node's edge mass is distributed, phase coherence is a *state* statistic that says how similar the units' trajectories are, and a network can be maximally integrated in the first sense while remaining desynchronised in the second. But the wiki cannot currently show that: no source computes both on the same data, and the pupil result explicitly proposes a single global gain as the driver of `B`, which is the same knob `k` that drives `R`. Until they are measured together, a builder who takes T249-A's advice and turns the routing dispersion up is running toward T249-B's cliff with no instrument that would show it approaching. **The decisive experiment is cheap**: sweep one coupling gain in any whole-brain model and plot `mean B` and `σ_R` against it on the same axis
