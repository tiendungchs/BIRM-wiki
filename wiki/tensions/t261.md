# T261 — Does dorsal medial prefrontal cortex (medial BA 8/9/10) increase or decrease from a passive baseline?

**Status:** `BOTH`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The two caveats are closed: reaction-time-matched external and self-referential judgments across the full dorsal-ventral extent, with the ventral tier's uniform fall confirmed.

## Position A

**Increases.** Gusnard et al. 2001: a self-referential judgment (pleasant/unpleasant on affective pictures) produced significant increases along dorsal-to-mid medial Brodmann areas 8, 9 and 10 relative to visual fixation, with reaction times matched to the external-judgment control (1.14 s vs 1.12 s, p = 0.36) so the effect is not time-on-task; a mood-induction study (Pardo et al.) reports the same direction from a passive baseline ([[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]])

## Position B

**Decreases.** Shulman et al. 1997's meta-analysis of task-induced decreases finds these areas among the regions that consistently *fall* from a passive baseline across a wide range of tasks — the finding the whole default-mode literature is built on, and the reason medial BA 8/9/10 is a default-network node at all ([[wiki/concepts/activity-baseline.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether "deactivates under task" is a property of a default-mode node. If the sign flips with task *content* at constant attentional demand, then internal/external mode is not a single gain applied to a network, and a machine copying the switch needs an orientation parameter separate from a load parameter ([[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] `G90`)

## Status

`BOTH — the threshold is what the task is *about*, not how hard it is.` The region's dynamic range is bidirectional around its own baseline: down when attention is directed externally, up when the object of processing is the self, while the ventral tier (BA 24/25/32) falls in *both* cases. Two caveats keep this from being fully resolved: most studies reporting increases use an attention-demanding control task, which cannot distinguish an increase from the control's decrease (demonstrated in primary data in this same paper), and the self-referential manipulation is confounded with affective judgment
