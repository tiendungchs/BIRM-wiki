# T151 — Where do a model's objects live — in its architecture, or in a read-out of its predictions?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Slot architectures and post-hoc correlation read-out are compared on the same real-world imagery, where the slot lineage is reported to fail and the read-out has not been tested against a strong slot baseline.

## Position A

**In the architecture.** The object-centric world-model lineage routes information through a fixed number of competing latent slots with soft attention bottlenecks (MONet, Slot Attention, SAVi, C-SWM, FOCUS), trained on prediction or contrast; entities are a *factor of the latent*, which is what makes them available to a downstream reasoner as variables. [[wiki/concepts/compositionality.md]] and gap G21 both assume this shape

## Position B

**In a read-out.** SpelkeNet has no object variable, no slot count `K` and no bottleneck: the network is an unconstrained 7B autoregressive flow model, and entities are recovered post hoc as clusters of the pairwise correlation between its counterfactual responses — which beats a supervised segmenter on point-prompted Spelke segmentation (AR 0.541 vs 0.482) on real internet imagery where the slot lineage is reported not to scale at all, since too many slots degenerate and too few make reconstruction ill-posed (Venkatesh et al. 2025)

## Where it bites

Whether entity discovery is a training-time architectural commitment or an inference-time query. Position B costs `R × T` full rollouts per segment and delivers no variable a planner can bind to, so a downstream symbolic consumer would have to re-run the probe or distil it — and distilling deletes the query. Position A delivers variables and cannot get them from real scenes

## Status

`LIVE`

**A third stance the row has no column for** ([[wiki/concepts/visual-indices.md]], Pylyshyn 2001): objects live in **neither** — they live in a *tracking* mechanism, and objecthood is defined by what that mechanism can hold onto. Four or five pointers are seized by feature clusters and keep referring to the same individuals as their properties and locations change; a sequence of locations counts as one object exactly when early vision groups it that way. Two consequences for the row. **(i)** It supplies a criterion neither position uses — not segmentation quality against a ground-truth mask (B) and not a reconstruction bottleneck (A), but **sustained correspondence across change**, which is measurable and discriminating: targets survive deletion/accretion along a contour and are lost to a shrink-and-regrow at the same time and place (Scholl & Pylyshyn 1999), and two objects sharing one spatial locus can be tracked through feature space (Blaser et al. 2000). A slot model and a correlation read-out would both have to be re-derived per frame on those stimuli; neither has been run on them. **(ii)** It delivers what B cannot and A can — a variable a planner can bind to, since the index *is* an addressable reference — and gives up what both have, since it holds no content and dies when the referent leaves the field. What it does not supply is the clusterer whose output it indexes, so it does not settle the row so much as relocate it one stage earlier.

**A fourth stance, and the first with a clock** ([[wiki/concepts/incremental-grouping.md]], Mollard, Bohte & Roelfsema 2026): objects live in **neither a slot nor a read-out nor a pointer, but in a transient activity tag** spread over the base representation itself — extra firing injected at a cued location and propagated through whatever a separate, frozen feedforward population declares locally unambiguous, stopped by the absence of that permission. Three things it contributes to the row. **(i)** It is the only entry that *prices* the parse in a currency the biology also pays: timesteps to arrival, linear in distance travelled through the object, and scored by how well that count predicts human reaction times (`R² = 0.55` against a `0.67` noise ceiling on scrambled shapes; `0.15` against `0.24` on COCO masks), beating the hGRU models that had to convert an uncertainty measure into an RT (`0.21` / `0.07`). Neither position A nor position B predicts any reaction time at all, so the row has been adjudicating representation format with no cost axis. **(ii)** It pays for that with the thing both positions deliver: **no object variable exists anywhere**. The output is a binary same-object query about two cued points; counting, comparing, or binding an object to a role each require re-running the flood, and nothing retains the result. So the row's real trade is bindability against traversability, and A and B both bought the first. **(iii)** It looks like the learned grouping criterion the row lacks and is not one. What reward teaches is the *routine* — propagation, gating, and which of four scales to use; what decides *what groups with what* is a supervised label ("is everything in this receptive field collinear and connected?") trained on 50,000 stimuli and then frozen. The authors tried joint training, got it at one scale in earlier work, and report it did not consistently elicit grouping across all four here. The criterion has therefore moved from the architecture into a pretraining objective — which is a third place for it to hide that the row has no column for, and the same residue [[wiki/entities/spelkenet.md]]'s learned segmenter carries one level down.

**A fifth entry the row can only score by its absence** ([[wiki/entities/vdm-i2i.md]], Acuaviva et al. 2025). A frozen video diffusion model adapted to ConceptARC has **no object anywhere** — no slot, no correlation read-out, no pointer, no activity tag; the entire pipeline is denoising a two-frame transition and reading the last frame. It nevertheless records **0.83** (Wan2.1-14B) on ConceptARC's **Count** group — against 0.17 for the best scale-matched language model, 0.60 for the 2020 Kaggle winner and 0.13 for GPT-4, with humans at 0.88. (Frontier reasoning models score higher on Count in the *textual* modality; the comparison that matters here is against the systems that are given the same grids.) Counting is the operation both positions cite as requiring individuals — position A to justify slots, and the `T151` entry for incremental grouping to explain why a flood that retains nothing cannot count. A system with no individuals of any kind nearly matching the human rate on a counting concept is a problem for that shared premise, and the row should carry it as one. Two limits on how far it goes: ConceptARC's Count group is *concept coverage*, not a cardinality report, so the required output may be reproducible without the count ever being formed; and the same model scores **0.23** on Extract Objects — the one concept that demands an object be isolated and emitted — which is where a no-object system should fail and does.
