# T411 — Does above-chance `B–D` choice after five-term premise training measure an ordering of the items, or item-level reward history?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A five-term series is run with `B`'s and `D`'s reinforcement histories equalised *by construction* (identical reward and non-reward counts, not matched accuracy), value transfer is varied separately (change the reward rate of the item `B` is paired with while holding `B`'s own history fixed), and `B–D` is scored against an ablated twin that keeps the premises. Item-value accounts and ordering accounts then predict different `B–D` outcomes.

## Position A

**Reward history.** Choice transitivity falls out of differential reinforcement of the individual items, with no relation among the stimuli represented (Halford et al. 1998b; De Lillo et al. 2001; Wynne 1995, via Penn et al. 2008, [[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]]). The specific form for a five-term series is Couvillon & Bitterman 1992's: if `AB` is answered better than `CD`, `B` loses less reward strength on errors than `D`, and `B` is preferred with no ordering at all. Value transfer is a second form: `B` inherits value from `A`, which is always rewarded and always paired with it. REMERGE puts value transfer aside for transitive inference (TI) for this reason and treats only paired associate inference (PAI) and acquired equivalence (AE) as reward-proof by design ([[wiki/entities/remerge.md]]).

## Position B

**An ordering, which needs the hippocampus.** Dusek & Eichenbaum 1997 (rats, odours `A>B>C>D>E`, n = 8 controls / 6 perirhinal+entorhinal / 4 fornix after exclusions):

| Control | Result |
|---|---|
| Couvillon–Bitterman account | Test-phase accuracy on `AB` does not differ from `CD`, so error-driven decrements on `B` and `D` are equal |
| Learning from probe rewards (`B` and `A` rewarded on probes, 2 probes per 40-trial session) | Controls choose `B` on **88%** of first `B–D` presentations. Novel spaced pairs `WX`, `YZ` stay at chance in every group, and controls' `B–D` beats them |
| A reward-history solver is present | After fornix transection or perirhinal+entorhinal ablation, premise pairs, the end-anchor curve (`AB`, `DE` best) and `A–E` are all intact. `B–D` is at chance (**50%** first presentation) |

The lesioned rat is an empirical item-value solver, and it fails `B–D`. So whatever solves `B–D` in the intact rat is something item value does not supply in that protocol ([[wiki/entities/remerge.md]], [[wiki/entities/temporal-context-model.md]], [[wiki/concepts/recollection-familiarity.md]]).

## Where it bites

`B–D` is the wiki's most-used probe for relational structure in a store: TEM's first-presentation result ([[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]), REMERGE's settling account, TCM's context gradient, schema property (iii) ([[wiki/concepts/schema-assimilation.md]]), and the long-axis binding-window test ([[wiki/concepts/hippocampal-long-axis.md]]). Under A, a machine that passes `B–D` has shown nothing about relational inference unless item reinforcement is equalised. Under B, `B–D` against an ablated twin is a valid instrument. Neither position bears on P8: an ordering of the items is the "ordered analog representation" the deflation already grants.

## Status

`LIVE`. Position B's design rules out the error-count form of A only. **Value transfer is untested**, and it is an item–item link: `B` gets value because it co-occurs with `A`. Item–item links are what the hippocampus is held to carry (Bunsey & Eichenbaum 1996, associative inference). So the lesion result does not separate value transfer from an ordering. Both are between-item links, and both would be lost with the hippocampus. Equal *accuracy* is also not equal *history*: matched `AB`/`CD` scores over 18 animals is a null result, not a construction. The samples are small (fornix n = 4). What the row now needs is the `Closes when` protocol; the machine version is cheap ([[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]], ablated-twin brainstorm).
