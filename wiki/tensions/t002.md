# T2 — Is the fast level a separate store or recurrent activity?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The fast level's degrees of freedom and decay timescale are measured in both a separate-store and a recurrent-activity system and shown to differ — or shown identical, which dissolves the row into a change of basis.

## Position A

Complementary learning systems: a second anatomical system with a sparse conjunctive code, coupled by replay

## Position B

Meta-RL: the fast learner is the *activity dynamics* of a network whose weights are fixed — no second store required. Reinforced by Schmidgall et al. 2023: in-context learning changes activations not weights, yet parameter-sharing in a meta-learner yields the *interpretation of activations as weights*, and self-attention's outer/inner products can be cast as learned weight updates that implement gradient descent

**Where a carrier question can and cannot be asked.** Whatever the fast level is made of, the instrument that reaches it may have to be a *perturbation*: an activity-stored item is decodable from ongoing activity, a weight-stored one is not decodable at all until something drives it. Wolff et al. 2017 ([[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]) show the asymmetry directly — the same human memorandum is at chance in delay activity and decodable from the response to a content-free ping — so a null from a passive decoder is evidence about the *read-out*, not about the store. Any resolution of this row that cites "not decodable during the delay" is citing an instrument that cannot see one of the two options (`G112`, `G113`).

## Where it bites

Determines what fast **M** is built from, and whether one-shot binding and fast policy adaptation are the same mechanism

## Status

`LIVE` — but possibly **dissolving**: if activations and weights are interconvertible under parameter sharing, the question is a change of basis, and the real variables are the fast level's degrees of freedom and decay timescale ([[wiki/concepts/meta-optimized-plasticity.md]])

**A built composition, which weakens the either/or reading** (Ritter et al. 2018, via Botvinick et al. 2019; [[wiki/concepts/meta-learning.md]]). In episodic meta-RL, recurrent activity is the fast *learner* (B). A separate context-keyed store holds that learner's past hidden states and reinstates them when a context recurs (A's store, with new contents). A new task gets meta-RL speed; a repeated task gets one-shot retrieval with no re-exploration. So the two positions can be two roles in one system — learning in activity, keeping what was learned in a store — rather than rival answers. Status unchanged: neither system's degrees of freedom or decay timescale were measured, which is what the closing test asks for.
