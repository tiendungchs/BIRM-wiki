# T14 — Are structured representations real, or emergent descriptions of sub-symbolic processes?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Emergent.** Graphs, grammars, rules, objects, structural descriptions and programs are "useful yet misleading metaphors" — more epiphenomenal than real, emergent properties of sub-symbolic processes; the parallel-distributed-processing strategy is to train a generic network and add ingredients only when forced, and it has produced networks behaving *as if* they had past-tense rules, balance-beam rules, or a tree of living kinds (McClelland et al. 2010, reported in Lake et al. 2017)

## Position B

**Explicit.** Human-level performance requires explicit representations of objects, identity, relations and causal structure; the caption failures (objects right, relations wrong) and the sample-efficiency gaps (924 h vs. minutes; 23% vs. 4% error at matched pre-training) are read as evidence that the emergent version is not the same representation despite matched behaviour on the trained task (Lake et al. 2017)

## Where it bites

Decides whether the wiki should specify `g`, edges and node types as architecture, or train a generic model and look for them afterwards. Note the two sides make the *same* prediction on any single trained task and differ only on transfer, re-goaling and query richness — so the tension is settled by [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] G17/G28 instruments or not at all

## Status

`LIVE` — and this is the wiki's clearest case of Morgan's Canon biting on both sides: matched behaviour licenses no inference about the representation in *either* direction
