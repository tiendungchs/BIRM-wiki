# T171 — Does climbing the moment ladder pay?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The spectrum both tests are supposed to fix is measured, in a self-supervised setting where no label term removes the degenerate solutions, with seed variance reported.

## Position A

**Yes — pin the whole distribution.** [[wiki/entities/lejepa.md]] §5.2 shows the anti-collapse loss families are one ladder indexed by how many moments of `N(0,I)` the test constrains, and argues for the top rung on **identifiability**: finitely many moments do not determine a distribution (Carleman), so a family of non-Gaussian solutions satisfies a second-moment objective exactly and the encoder may sit anywhere in it — "a phenomenon already observed in VICReg". The Epps–Pulley characteristic-function test leaves no such family, at the same coefficient count and `O(N)` cost (Balestriero & LeCun 2025).

## Position B

**Not measurably.** Head to head on CIFAR-100, a sketched **second-moment** test `‖Cov(ZS^⊤) − I‖_F` matches or beats the full Epps–Pulley test in 5 of 8 settings — +1.8 on the collapsed ViT, **+6.2** on the vanilla 6-layer MLP, +4.4 on ViT+Muon — and its three losses are all on already-stabilised baselines and ≤1.1 points. Whatever the extra moments buy, it is not stability and not accuracy in these runs (Akbar 2026, [[wiki/entities/lejepa.md]] §Part 8).

## Where it bites

Whether the wiki's recommended anti-collapse provision is the characteristic-function test or [[wiki/entities/vicreg.md]]'s covariance term with a random sketch bolted on — a real cost difference (17-knot quadrature and complex exponentials vs one Frobenius norm) and a real conceptual one, since only the top rung makes the trained object *identifiable*. Home page for this row: [[wiki/concepts/representational-collapse.md]].

## Status

`LIVE` — B is one dataset, one author, no seed variance, no measurement of the spectrum either test is supposedly fixing, and its setting is **supervised**, where a label term already removes the degenerate solutions identifiability is meant to exclude. A's argument is a theorem about the solution *set*; B's evidence is about the *trajectory*, so they may not be about the same thing (cf. T164, T168)
