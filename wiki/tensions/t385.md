# T385 — In a delayed-match task, which region holds the item and which one filters — prefrontal or parietal?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One design inserts distractors into the delay of a spatial *and* a non-spatial delayed-match task in the same animals, recording prefrontal and parietal cortex simultaneously, and reports which representation survives the distractor in each.

## Position A

**Prefrontal filters, parietal leaks.** In spatial working-memory tasks prefrontal neurons respond only mildly to distractors while posterior-parietal neurons respond strongly and encode the physical properties of any stimulus irrespective of relevance. The standard reading follows: suppression of distracting information in prefrontal cortex is the mechanism protecting the store and gating access to it, and parietal delay activity is an inferior correlate of working memory. This is the assignment every control/storage architecture in the wiki assumes — a controller that admits and protects ([[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]], [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]])

## Position B

**Prefrontal is overwritten and regenerates; parietal holds.** With a behaviourally irrelevant number inserted into the delay of a delayed-match-to-numerosity task and simultaneous lateral-prefrontal and ventral-intraparietal recording, prefrontal neurons **do not resist interference** — sample information is overwritten and the cells respond strongly and in a tuned manner to the distractor, sometimes with a different preferred numerosity — while ventral-intraparietal neurons are mostly unaffected and retain the sample. The prefrontal sample representation is *regenerated* in the second delay, in time to solve the task; monkeys resist the distractor behaviourally with only a slight error increase. The reading: prefrontal cortex is a selection stage in a global workspace that must represent relevant *and* irrelevant items in order to filter, and the maintenance lives parietally — which is why prefrontal lesions impair interference resistance without losing stored information (Nieder 2016, [[wiki/concepts/numerosity.md]])

## Where it bites

Which block in a two-block controller/store architecture is which, and therefore what a lesion or an ablation is evidence of. Under A a controller ablation should lose *access* and leave the store intact; under B it should leave the content intact and lose *selection*, with the observable signature being a delay representation that is destroyed and then reappears — a regeneration event no wiki architecture emits or consumes. It also decides whether "the delay code was overwritten" is a failure mode or a design feature: under B, being overwritten is what a selection stage is *for*, and a machine store evaluated on distractor robustness would be scored on the wrong module. Compounds `T88`, which already splits prefrontal delay activity into item and pointer; B adds a third reading in which prefrontal activity is neither, and locates the item one region back

## Status

`LIVE` — the two positions differ in **content domain** (spatial location vs cardinality), in **parietal subregion** (posterior parietal broadly vs ventral intraparietal area specifically) and in **task**, so the cleanest reconciliation is that the store follows the content: a spatial item is held where spatial codes live and a numerical item where number codes live, with prefrontal cortex always the selector. That reading is consistent with both sides and with `T88`, and it predicts that the "prefrontal protects the store" result should reverse whenever the memorandum is a variable prefrontal cortex does not natively represent — untested. Two limits on B: the regeneration is inferred from the population recovering its tuning, with no test of whether the recovered code is the original or a re-read from the parietal copy; and the distractor was a stimulus of the *same* type as the sample, which is the condition most likely to overwrite
