# T107 — Which structure holds the graph that a plan runs on — the hippocampal formation, or the cortical controller?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Spec:** `D205` ([[_brainstorm/birm-spec.md]] §11)  
**Closes when:** The prefrontal-graph model is given a non-stub hippocampal component — remapping and forward sweeps included — so its sufficiency claim becomes evidence about locus rather than about a cortical graph alone.

## Position A

**Hippocampal-entorhinal.** The wiki's whole cognitive-map account puts the relational structure in the medial temporal lobe: place and grid codes are the map ([[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]]), the Tolman–Eichenbaum Machine factorises structure from sensory content in entorhinal-like cells ([[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]), the successor representation is a hippocampal predictive map, and chained retrieval during two-stage inference is abolished by silencing dorsal CA1 (Barron et al. 2020, [[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]]). Prefrontal cortex reads the map and valuates

## Position B

**Prefrontal.** The hippocampal space code is *too redundant and too distributed* to be a compact topological map; it supplies instances (places, routes, episodes) and the cortical network learns the graph over them. Measured along the cascade, population size falls and receptive fields grow from place cells → prefrontal columns, and every unit type the graph-planner needs (state, distance-to-goal, prospective-sequence, strategy-switch) is present in navigating-rat medial prefrontal recordings and recoverable by blind clustering; the hippocampus is modelled as pure localisation input (Martinet et al. 2011, [[wiki/entities/pfc-columnar-planning-model.md]])

## Where it bites

Where a builder puts the adjacency matrix, and what the fast episodic store is *for*. Under A the fast store holds the graph and the controller queries it, so consolidation moves a graph. Under B the fast store holds instances and the controller *abstracts* a graph from them, so consolidation is a change of representational format and the two structures should carry measurably different graphs — the coarse one only in cortex. The two are not fully exclusive (both may hold a graph at different resolutions), and that reconciliation is exactly what the coarse/fine column split predicts: multi-scale codes exist in entorhinal cortex too (Hafting et al. 2005; Kjelstrup et al. 2008), so the open question is whether cortical chunking is redundant with hippocampal scale, or orthogonal to it (chunking by *action structure* rather than by distance)

**A third locus, with a stated scope limit.** Both positions above are about *cortical or hippocampal* tissue, and neither names the structure holding ~80% of the brain's neurons. Wolpert, Miall & Kawato 1998 ([[wiki/entities/cerebellum.md]]) place forward and inverse internal models in the cerebellum, with efference copy as the forward model's input and one paired module per microzone. This does **not** supply a third position on where a *plan graph* lives — a cerebellar forward model is a model of the plant, not of task structure, and it is fitted over a scalar-to-low-dimensional state. What it does supply is a warning about the question's framing: the prediction machinery a planner consumes need not sit in the structure that holds the graph, so "which structure holds the graph" and "which structure predicts the consequence of an action" can have different answers, and the wiki has been treating them as one question. The review's own closing sentence proposes exactly the extension that would make the cerebellum a real third position — "internal models of the external world, of other people's mental processes" — and offers no evidence for it.


**A fourth locus, and the split the third one warned about, now made concrete.** Leinweber et al. 2017 ([[wiki/entities/a24b-m2-v1-projection.md]]) identify a *cortical* structure that predicts the sensory consequence of an action: A24b/M2 → V1, delivering a motor-conditioned prediction of visual flow in the receiver's own retinotopic coordinates, learned and re-learnable in days. Read against the cerebellar entry above, the two differ on the one thing that matters for a module interface — the cerebellar forward model predicts the **plant's state** and its error is claimed to arrive in motor-command coordinates, while this one predicts **sensory content** and is measured to arrive in sensory coordinates. That confirms the warning added at the Wolpert 1998 ingest and sharpens it: "which structure predicts the consequence of an action" is itself not one question, since body-state prediction and image prediction are computed in different structures with different output formats. Neither supplies a position on where the *plan graph* lives; both subtract from the assumption that whichever structure holds the graph must also generate the predictions a planner consumes.

**The fourth locus, measured a second time — and the two measurements disagree about where the map is kept.** Schneider et al. 2018 ([[wiki/entities/m2-auditory-cortex-filter.md]]) find secondary motor cortex delivering a movement-conditioned prediction into *auditory* cortex, acquired de novo in 5–9 days for an arbitrary running→tone contingency. Read against the entry above, this splits "which structure predicts the consequence of an action" once more, and along a new axis: not *which* structure, but **which side of the wire holds the model**. In V1 the sender's axons already carry the prediction in the receiver's coordinates; in auditory cortex the sender can be driven in bulk, bypassing any sender-side selectivity, and the receiver's frequency-tuned interneurons are still preferentially recruited — so the map is in the projection's synapses ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] `T346`). Neither result bears on where the *plan graph* lives; together they add that even a single named sender is not a single answer, since the same area's forward model is realised differently depending on what it predicts.

## Status

`LIVE` — the prefrontal-graph model's hippocampal component is a deliberate stub (no remapping, no forward sweeps), so it cannot be evidence *against* a hippocampal graph; it establishes only that a cortical graph is sufficient for detour behaviour and consistent with prefrontal single-unit data
