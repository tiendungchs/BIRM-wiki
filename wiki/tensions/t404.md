# T404 — Is the dopamine prediction error the teaching signal for stimulus–reward learning as such, or only for the attribution of incentive value to a cue?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One preparation records dopamine across the full terminal field (accumbens core **and** shell, plus a dorsal site) in animals matched on predictive accuracy but differing in cue-directed approach, *and* runs receptor antagonism during acquisition with a drug-free probe session. A is right if a declining US-evoked signal is found somewhere in the projection for the goal-tracking learner, or if antagonism at a sufficient dose blocks acquisition of both conditioned responses. B is right if no terminal field shows the error signature in goal-trackers and the dissociation survives dose escalation up to the point where performance floors. The artificial-agent analogue is cheap: train one network on a CS→US prediction loss and a second head on cue-approach value from the same `δ`, then remove `δ` from one head only and check which behaviour survives.

## Position A

**A general teaching signal.** `δ = r + γV(s′) − V(s)` is the error term that stimulus–reward learning is driven by, wherever that learning happens: the burst transfers from US to the earliest reliable CS, the dip appears at omission, and the whole temporal-difference literature reads the record this way ([[wiki/concepts/reward-prediction-error.md]], Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997). Every model in the wiki that learns from reward runs it as the only teaching signal it has. Under A, an individual who learns the CS→US contingency has computed prediction errors, and the failure to *record* them is a sampling problem — the voltammetry here is one electrode in accumbens core, and the projection has many terminal fields with different afferents and different targets ([[wiki/concepts/broadcast-channel-decomposition.md]], [[wiki/entities/ventral-tegmental-area.md]]).

## Position B

**Selective for incentive attribution.** Dopamine participates in stimulus–reward learning only where Pavlovian incentive value is assigned to the cue; the predictive association is learned by a system that does not need it. Evidence (Flagel et al. 2011, [[wiki/concepts/sign-tracking-and-goal-tracking.md]]): with the CS an **equally good predictor** in both phenotypes — matched acquisition rates and latency curves — (i) US-evoked accumbens dopamine declines across training in sign-trackers and is *maintained* in goal-trackers, so the defining signature of a prediction error is absent in the group that nonetheless learns; (ii) CS-evoked dopamine grows only in sign-trackers, so the signal does not index associative strength; (iii) systemic flupenthixol during sessions 1–7 abolishes *acquisition* of sign-tracking measured on a drug-free session 8 while leaving goal-tracking acquisition fully intact, which is a necessity test, not a correlation. The pattern replicates in outbred rats with matched baseline dopamine, so it is not an artefact of the bred lines.

## Where it bites

**Whether one broadcast scalar may be the architecture's only teaching signal.** Under A it may, and the wiki's universal use of `δ` is licensed; the open work is addressing (`G19`, `G117`). Under B the architecture needs a **second teaching signal** for the CS→US map — one nobody has identified, in biology or in the wiki — and `δ` is demoted to the trainer of a cue-directed motivational channel that sits beside the predictive one. The designs differ in what they are architecturally denied: an A-architecture cannot produce an agent whose world model is intact after its reward channel is removed during training; a B-architecture cannot explain why removing `δ` degrades *performance* of both conditioned responses, which it does, unless the same signal is also a run-time gain on behaviour that it did not teach.

The two are not exhaustive. A third reading — **address by axon** — keeps A's identity claim and explains B's null by saying the goal-tracker's error rides a dopamine population the electrode did not sample. The systemic-antagonist arm argues against it (a drug reaching every terminal field cost the goal-tracker nothing) but does not close it, since the acquisition dose was chosen to spare gross activity and a receptor population may survive it. That makes the dose–response curve the decisive missing measurement.

## Status

`LIVE` — B has a direct necessity test that A has no counterpart for, and A has the entire firing record plus an untested localisation escape.
