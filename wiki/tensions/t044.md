# T44 — Is a periodic `g` necessary for structural generalization, or just one solution among several?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** An intervention rather than a seed correlation: grid score is manipulated at fixed architecture and imagination accuracy re-measured, and the non-hexagonal high-accuracy solutions are characterised.

## Position A

**Necessary, or near enough.** The wiki's whole case for grid-like codes is that periodicity is what buys extension to unvisited space, error correction and cheap transfer ([[wiki/concepts/path-integration.md]], [[wiki/concepts/abstract-structural-codes.md]]), and Li et al. 2026 supply the first quantitative support: across mm-TEM runs, grid score in the path-integration units predicts 512-step imagination accuracy at `r = 0.647, p = 2e-4` (Spearman `ρ = 0.783, p = 1e-6`)

## Position B

**One solution among several.** The same figure contains models with *low* grid scores and *high* accuracy, whose autocorrelation maps show non-hexagonal alternative solutions; the paper describes them and does not characterise them (the sentence is truncated at a page break in this conversion). Nothing was intervened on — the correlation is over seeds and hyperparameters, so it is equally consistent with grid score and accuracy being two readouts of how well the action→`g` map was learned

## Where it bites

Decides whether periodicity is a *design target* for `g` or an epiphenomenon of a well-trained integrator. If B, then every emergence result the wiki uses as evidence for grid codes ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T38) is measuring the wrong thing, and the objective slot (G30) should be written against path-invariance and long-horizon accuracy directly rather than against gridness

## Status

`LIVE` — first evidence on either side; the correlation is real and the counterexamples are in the same scatter plot ([[wiki/entities/mm-tem-hippoformer.md]])
