# T304 — Should a joint-embedding's output be ℓ₂-normalised onto the hypersphere?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Normalisation is varied as the *only* factor under a loss that does not require it — e.g. VICReg or LeJEPA run with and without ℓ₂ projection at matched effective variance — separating "the sphere is a good feature space" from "the softmax needs a bounded norm".

## Position A

**Yes.** Nearly every method that works normalises: contrastive learning, face embeddings, hyperspherical VAEs, prototype methods. Three arguments. (i) **Necessity** — without a norm constraint the softmax in `L_contrastive` is sharpened arbitrarily by scaling all features, so the loss is minimisable without learning; normalised outputs measurably give better representations. (ii) **The instrument** — well-clustered classes on a sphere form spherical caps, which are linearly separable from the rest of the space, where the same clustering in `R^m` is not; linear separability is the field's quality criterion. (iii) **The target is then derivable** — `σ_{m-1}` is the unique minimiser of the average pairwise Gaussian potential, and the `N`-point minimisers converge weak\* to it, so "spread out" has an exact meaning on the sphere and none in `R^m` (average dot product or Euclidean distance is minimised by *any* zero-mean distribution) (Wang & Isola 2020, [[wiki/concepts/alignment-uniformity.md]])

## Position B

**No, and it is priced.** VICReg is the first joint-embedding method in the wiki whose embeddings are neither batch-standardised nor projected onto a sphere, and **not normalising is the best setting**: re-running it with the embedding ℓ₂-normalised — which implicitly fixes each dimension's batch standard deviation at `1/√d` — costs **3.5 ImageNet points**. Left unconstrained the covariance entries take a wider range than a correlation matrix's `[−1,1]`, "which seems to facilitate training". Standardisation survives only *inside* the expander as an optimisation aid (≈ −1.2 when removed), not as an anti-collapse device (Bardes et al. 2022, [[wiki/entities/vicreg.md]]). The lineage agrees: [[wiki/entities/lejepa.md]]'s derived target is `N(0, I_K)` in `R^K`, whose norm is χ_K-distributed rather than fixed

## Where it bites

Whether the embedding's **norm** is a channel or a nuisance. A's geometry discards it by construction; B's leaves it free to carry information and measures 3.5 points for that freedom. The two targets otherwise **agree exactly** — an isotropic Gaussian's directional marginal is `σ_{K-1}` — so this is not a dispute about anisotropy, it is a one-dimensional disagreement about the radius, which makes it unusually cheap to settle. It also decides whether the wiki's uniformity results transfer to unnormalised architectures at all: A's necessity argument is a property of dot-product-in-a-softmax and does not apply to any loss without one, so half of A may be an artefact of the loss rather than a fact about the space

## Status

`LIVE` — the two sides are measured on different losses, which is exactly why neither settles it. A's necessity argument is loss-specific and A's separability argument is instrument-specific; B's 3.5 points is a single ablation inside one method, and it changes the *variance target* at the same time as the geometry, so it does not isolate normalisation either. **(brainstorm)** The mechanistic guess that would decide it: the norm in an unnormalised embedding is doing confidence/precision work — inputs the encoder is sure about land far out — which the sphere throws away and which a downstream linear probe can use, predicting that A's cost is concentrated on ambiguous inputs and vanishes on clean ones
