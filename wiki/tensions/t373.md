# T373 — Does the cortical slow oscillation schedule the consolidation window, or does the thalamus?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Spindles are induced at a *cortical location* chosen by the experimenter, independently of where the slow oscillation is, and memory for material encoded in that network is measured — i.e. the addressing is manipulated with the timing held fixed.

## Position A

**Cortex leads.** The slow oscillation is generated in cortical layers 2/3 and 5, propagates anterior→posterior, and its depolarising up-state drives thalamic spindle generation; effective consolidation requires spindles *inside* that up-state, and induced spindles outside it buy nothing behaviourally (Steriade; Latchoumane et al. via Klinzing et al. 2019, [[wiki/concepts/sleep-oscillation-nesting.md]])

## Position B

**Thalamus leads.** Spindles predict memory gains more consistently than slow oscillations, support consolidation in stage 2 with no prominent SOs, appear *earlier in development* than slow waves and carry the infant consolidation correlation alone; optogenetic thalamic spindles phase-lock hippocampal ripples whether or not an SO up-state is present, induce SOs within ~200 ms, and a sensory relay nucleus (LGN) can encode a stimulus-specific change during waking and instruct cortex to reproduce it during subsequent sleep (Klinzing et al. 2019, [[wiki/concepts/sleep-oscillation-nesting.md]])

## Where it bites

Where a machine's consolidation scheduler lives. Under A it is a global phase variable emitted by the slow learner, and offline transfer is a property of the receiver's state — the design already implied by [[wiki/concepts/complementary-learning-systems.md]]'s prefrontal slow-wave-amplitude result. Under B it is a **separate gating module** that also runs online attention, so the offline write is addressed to whichever subnetwork was attended, and the same key-query circuit serves both ([[wiki/concepts/transthalamic-context-routing.md]]). The two make opposite predictions about *addressing*: A has no mechanism for it at all — the SO's location does not predict the location of the spindle nested in its up-state — while B predicts the offline update should concentrate where waking attention was, independent of reward or prediction error

## Status

`LIVE`, and not a clean dichotomy — the source's own reading is a loop with both arms measured (SO→spindle→ripple top-down, ripple→SO and spindle→SO bottom-up), so "who leads" may be ill-posed for the rhythm and well-posed for the two functions separately. The sharpest split available: **timing** favours A (behavioural benefit requires SO-up-state phase-locking even when thalamic spindles alone already phase-lock ripples), **addressing** favours B (localisation, development, the LGN→V1 instruction, and the SO's failure to predict spindle location). Klinzing et al. label the thalamic-leadership account "highly speculative" in their own text. Note that the `Closes when` experiment has never been attempted because no method targets spindle induction to a chosen cortical site
