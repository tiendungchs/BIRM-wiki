# T154 — Does a control-usable visual world model need foundation-scale pretraining?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Yes, and freezing it is what makes JEPA training tractable.** DINO-WM and [[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]] both avoid collapse by not training the encoder at all, and the physical-probing advantage tracks the corpus: DINOv2's ~124M images buy velocity and rotational quantities that the small end-to-end models do not recover. The wave's premise is that the encoder is the expensive part and should be amortised across tasks.

## Position B

**No, at least below 3D visual complexity.** A 15M-parameter ViT-Tiny trained end-to-end from pixels on one GPU beats DINO-WM on Push-T *even when DINO-WM is given proprioception*, wins under matched FLOPs on both Push-T and OGBench-Cube, plans ~48× faster, and has lower seed variance (96.0 ± 2.83 vs 92.0 ± 1.63) — while losing on the visually richest 3D environment and on the *simplest* one (Maes et al. 2026, [[wiki/entities/lewm.md]]). **The classification-side version, and it is the cleanest evidence either column has** (Balestriero & LeCun 2025, [[wiki/entities/lejepa.md]]): a 21M ResNet-34 trained from random initialisation on Galaxy10's 11k images beats DINOv3 ViT-S/16 and DINOv2-S under frozen probing (78.2 / 71.4 / 67.6), under full finetuning (83.3 / 81.6 / 78.3) and at every shot count down to one sample per class, and beats a 630M I-JEPA pretrained on ImageNet-22k by 14 points — while *losing* to that same I-JEPA on flowers102, a 1020-image natural-image dataset. Only the encoder varies, so B generalises beyond control: transfer's advantage is a function of **distributional distance from the pretraining corpus**, not of corpus size, and it inverts once the target domain leaves it.

## Where it bites

Where the wave should spend: on encoders reused across environments, or on per-environment models cheap enough to retrain. It also decides how to read every probing table in this literature, since the frozen-encoder systems differ from the end-to-end ones in corpus, parameter count and objective at once ([[wiki/concepts/representation-probing.md]]). **Side A's encoder now has its primary source, and it moves one term.** DINOv2's advantage over a same-size corpus is not size but *shaping*: 142M curated images beat 142M uncurated ones from the same crawl at matched compute by ~14 points on ImageNet-A and iNat2018, while the uncurated corpus wins on segmentation (Oquab et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/dinov2.md]]). So "foundation-scale pretraining" is doing two things the comparison cannot separate — a large corpus and a curated distribution — and the curation targets were the evaluation benchmarks' own train splits.
