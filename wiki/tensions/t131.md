# T131 — When a behaviour becomes automatic, is the policy distilled into a new substrate or migrated to another loop?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Distilled and the module bypassed.** In SPEED (Ashby et al. 2007) the basal ganglia's role is to fire the correct premotor plan consistently enough for Hebbian learning to build a direct sensory-association → premotor cortico-cortical association; once that shortcut is strong the module is no longer in the path. This is the literal version of [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]'s withdrawal step, and it predicts that practice *returns* controller capacity

## Position B

**Migrated, and the module still required.** Control moves from an anterior visual-coordinate striatal loop to a posterior motor-coordinate one but never leaves the striatum (Nakahara et al. 2001), or the habitual action keeps being selected by the same direct/indirect competition (Chersi et al. 2013). Miyachi's muscimol dissociation — anterior caudate/putamen disrupts early production, middle-posterior putamen disrupts late production — is direct evidence that a striatal site is still load-bearing after overtraining

## Where it bites

Decides the parallel capacity of automatic behaviour: distillation into cortical weights is unbounded, migration is capped by the number of loops. It also decides whether "handing a skill over" frees the controller, which is the assumption [[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]] prices skills with

## Status

`LEANING A` — Desmurget & Turner 2010 inactivated the motor segment of the internal globus pallidus in trained monkeys, disconnecting the module from frontal cortex: movement kinematics were impaired, **sequence knowledge was not**. That is what A predicts and what both B models fail. Not decisive: the result is about an overlearned motor sequence, and B's late-phase striatal site is upstream of the inactivated output **Sibling row (unlinked until the 284-source duplicate audit): T94** asks the same question from the prefrontal side — drops out versus hands over. Both anchor on [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]'s withdrawal step, and the two literatures answer it without reference to each other.
