# T131 — When a behaviour becomes automatic, is the policy distilled into a new substrate or migrated to another loop?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The inactivation is repeated at B's late-phase striatal site, which is upstream of the pallidal output Desmurget & Turner disconnected, and on a non-motor overlearned skill.

## Position A

**Distilled and the module bypassed.** In SPEED (Ashby et al. 2007) the basal ganglia's role is to fire the correct premotor plan consistently enough for Hebbian learning to build a direct sensory-association → premotor cortico-cortical association; once that shortcut is strong the module is no longer in the path. This is the literal version of [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]'s withdrawal step, and it predicts that practice *returns* controller capacity

## Position B

**Migrated, and the module still required.** Control moves from an anterior visual-coordinate striatal loop to a posterior motor-coordinate one but never leaves the striatum (Nakahara et al. 2001), or the habitual action keeps being selected by the same direct/indirect competition (Chersi et al. 2013). Miyachi's muscimol dissociation — anterior caudate/putamen disrupts early production, middle-posterior putamen disrupts late production — is direct evidence that a striatal site is still load-bearing after overtraining

**A late-phase striatal site inactivated in experts, on a stimulus–response skill rather than a motor sequence** (Greenstreet et al. 2025, [[wiki/concepts/action-prediction-error.md]]). Mice learn an auditory two-alternative choice:
- Tail-of-striatum (TS) muscimol impairs experts, and dorsomedial-striatum muscimol does not.
- Optogenetic silencing of either TS spiny-neuron class has no effect early in training. The effect grows once performance passes ~65% and is largest in experts.
- Control moves *into* a striatal territory with training, and that territory is still required once the task is mastered.
- The source's mechanism is migration between two striatal learners: an action-prediction-error store takes over from a reward-trained actor whose weights decay.

## Where it bites

Decides the parallel capacity of automatic behaviour: distillation into cortical weights is unbounded, migration is capped by the number of loops. It also decides whether "handing a skill over" frees the controller, which is the assumption [[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]] prices skills with

## Status

`LIVE` — was `LEANING A` until Greenstreet et al. 2025, which supplies the B-side inactivation this row asked for: a late-phase striatal site, upstream of the pallidal output, on a skill that is not a motor sequence, and it impairs experts. The two results now point opposite ways on different skill types: a motor sequence survives disconnection of the pallidal output, while a learned sound→action mapping does not survive TS inactivation. A split by skill type, with sequences distilled into cortex and stimulus→response maps kept in striatum, is admissible and untested. Caveats on the new evidence: expert mice are not shown to be automatic (no devaluation, dual-task or omission test), and training (thousands of trials) is shorter than the monkey overtraining. Prior evidence: Desmurget & Turner 2010 inactivated the motor segment of the internal globus pallidus in trained monkeys, disconnecting the module from frontal cortex: movement kinematics were impaired, **sequence knowledge was not**. That is what A predicts and what both B models fail.
