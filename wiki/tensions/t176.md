# T176 — Should a continuous-time model keep its differential equation, or compile it away?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The two are evaluated on a task that needs invertibility, symbolic readability or interventional identification — every experiment cited so far makes all three irrelevant.

## Position A

**Keep it.** The ODE *is* the semantics, and three properties follow from it and from nothing else: it is invertible under uniqueness conditions, so it can be run backwards in time — the basis of continuous normalizing flows; it has a vector field that can be read as an equation ([[wiki/entities/kan-ode.md]] recovers a PDE source term to 0.5% by symbolic regression on it); and it is a differential operator of the bilinear DCM form that neuroimaging fits to infer effective connectivity ([[wiki/concepts/causal-model-building.md]]), which is the stated motivation for [[wiki/entities/ltc.md]]'s equation. Its state-boundedness theorems are proved *about the ODE's compartments*. Implicit ODE/PDE models also remain the right choice for continuously defined physics and control.

## Position B

**Compile it away.** [[wiki/entities/cfc.md]] replaces the one remaining integral with a closed form and gets 1–5 orders of magnitude in training/inference (8,752% over Latent-ODE-ODE on human activity; 160–220× on PhysioNet; >150-fold accuracy-per-compute-time) with no accuracy loss, SOTA 98.09% on event-based sequential MNIST and +18% over transformers on Walker2D. Approximation error from a `p`-th order integrator stops existing as a concept, the hidden hyperparameter that is the solver disappears, and the pathology ODE-RNNs show on irregularly sampled data — which they fail *for every solver* — is fixed by a gate `σ(−f·t)`, showing it was never an integration problem.

## Where it bites

Whether "continuous-time" in this wiki names a *computational commitment* or a *derivational origin*. Under B, every continuous-time architecture here should be compiled to closed form and the ODE retained only as the thing the closed form was derived from — which costs the wiki its one readable transition function and its one causal-inference-shaped architecture. Under A, the 1–5 orders of magnitude are the price of keeping properties that no benchmark in either paper measures.

## Status

`LIVE` — **and the two positions have never been evaluated on a task that needs A's properties.** Every experiment cited by B is time-series regression or classification, where invertibility, symbolic readability and interventional causal identification are all irrelevant; B's own authors concede all three losses in their limitations section rather than contesting them. The discriminating experiment is a task requiring backward-in-time rollout or an intervention on a fitted connectivity matrix, run on an LTC and its compiled CfC at matched accuracy. Note also that B's compilation is doubly approximate — Lemma 1's bound is *sharp*, i.e. worst-case relative error is total (saved only by a shared `e^{−w_τ t}` envelope), and Algorithm 1's per-synapse superposition of a scalar, self-connection-free theorem across an arbitrary graph is not bounded at all.
