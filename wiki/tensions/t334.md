# T334 — Is an unobserved relational path written at encoding, or composed at retrieval?

**Status:** `LIVE`
**Level:** `L2`
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]
**Closes when:** A single study measures reinstatement of the absent element at encoding *and* manipulates retrieval-time composition demand (e.g. varies path length between test and premise trials) in the same subjects, so that write-time integration and query-time composition make opposite predictions rather than the same one.

## Position A

**Written at encoding.** Zeithamova et al. 2012 read out the *unseen* third element of an `A–B–C` triad by classifier during re-presentation of `A–B`: null on the first repetition (before any `B–C` exposure), significant on the second and third, with the across-subject reactivation index predicting later `A–C` accuracy (r = 0.46). Critically, ventromedial prefrontal encoding activation predicts inference **after partialling out premise memory** (r = 0.38 → partial r = 0.53), which is the control the on-the-fly account needs to survive and does not. The composite is built during learning, for a query that has not been asked ([[wiki/concepts/retrieval-mediated-learning.md]]). **A second primary source adds a latency signature** (Shohamy & Wagner 2008, acquired equivalence, n = 24, intermixed training). The early → late rise in hippocampal and VTA/SN (ventral tegmental area / substantia nigra) activity during learning predicts generalization. The response-time gap between untrained and trained probes predicts generalization negatively (r = −0.69), and the effect holds on the first encounter. In the top quartile the gap is 39 ms. Test-phase hippocampal activity shows no inference signal, though this is a null reported in the supplement

## Position B

**Composed at retrieval.** Premise memory and inference accuracy correlate at **r = 0.76** in the same dataset — most of the variance in inferring is variance in having learned the two edges, and stronger direct associations alone yield better test-time composition with nothing integrated. Independently, the temporal context model derives ordering across non-co-occurring items purely from *retrieved-context overlap at test*, with no composite ever stored, and reads rodent transitive inference as this associative gradient rather than as inference ([[wiki/entities/temporal-context-model.md]]); TEM answers first-presentation transitive inference from a structural code with no write-time integration of the queried pair at all ([[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]). **And B now has a mechanism written for exactly this dispute, by the theory A is an amendment to**: REMERGE recirculates an exemplar-style similarity computation over pattern-separated traces — output combined with the external input, fed back, iterated to an attractor — so the `A–C` link is a property of the settled state and the store keeps its separated code. The primary source (Kumaran & McClelland 2012, [[wiki/entities/remerge.md]]) turns B's strongest number into a mechanism. Good vs poor paired-associate generalizers (73% vs 54% inference, Zeithamova & Preston 2010) are fitted by premise weights of 1.52 vs 1.47 and nothing else. Inference *trails* premise accuracy at every weight ("lag generalization"), so the r = 0.76 premise–inference correlation is what the mechanism requires. **Correction to this row's earlier wording.** The review's claim that weak traces with poor recognition still give near-normal generalization (Kumaran et al. 2016) holds in the primary data only for prototype *categorization*, which is first-pass similarity and fits with recurrence disabled. For inference the primary data show the reverse: weakening premise traces hurts inference first. So B's distinctive signature is inference being lost before premise memory, not inference surviving weak traces

## Where it bites

Where a multi-hop capability is paid for, and therefore what the reader must contain. Under A the store returns the answer to a one-step lookup and no composition operator is needed; under B the store holds only observed edges and the reader needs a traversal operator running inside the query's latency budget, with error compounding per hop. This is the architectural choice behind every retrieval-augmented model's multi-hop failure, and it decides whether [[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]]'s path queries are a write-time or a read-time cost. It also sets what an ablation means: ablating the composition operator is free under A and fatal under B

## Status

`LIVE` — **and the source that proposes B's mechanism says the distinction is not even absolute.** Kumaran et al. 2016 state that no experiment separates encoding-based from retrieval-based models, that both plausibly run as a function of paradigm, training amount and training–test delay, and — the part that changes this row — that **generalized replay converts one into the other**: simultaneous offline reactivation of several related traces can write the settled state back as a new trace, so a retrieval-based mechanism manufactures over time the composite an encoding-based account assumes. Under that reading the two positions are one loop sampled at different points, and the row's real question becomes *how long after learning* the composite exists, which is a delay manipulation nobody has run. Two constraints survive the merge. **On B**: the reactivation curve in A's dataset is measured with *no query pending*, so a pure settling account owes an explanation of why the absent element is reinstated during plain re-presentation. Part of that is now paid. REMERGE fits the hippocampal encoding effect of the 2010 dataset (`BC`-trial activity predicts later `A–C`) as ordinary completion of the stronger `AB` trace when `B` reappears, with no integrated unit. A settling store therefore *does* reinstate the absent element at encoding whenever the cue overlaps a stored trace. What it still does not explain is the vmPFC effect surviving the partialling-out of premise memory. **On A**: an ablation of the composition operator is free only if the composite is written for *every* overlapping pair, which is combinatorially hopeless.

**And the honest reading is that both run, with the open question being the selector, not the winner.** Write-time integration over every pair of overlapping episodes is combinatorially hopeless, so it cannot be the whole story; query-time composition over an unbounded graph is the observed multi-hop failure, so it cannot be either — though in machines that failure is partly one of *read count*. One retrieval per hop, with nothing written, cuts factual-error chains by 40–50% against a single read ([[wiki/entities/ircot.md]]), so pure query-time composition is costlier in reads than it is in accuracy. Position A's own data contains Position B's strongest evidence (the r = 0.76), and the partial correlation removes it for vmPFC but *not* for bilateral hippocampus (r = 0.51 → partial r = 0.22, n.s.). The candidate selector the wiki can state and nobody has tested: integration is triggered where the fast store completes anyway, so pattern completion — not a policy — decides which paths get precomputed ([[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]]). The discriminating experiment is cheap in a machine: probe for the absent third item's content across repetitions of the first pair, where a query-time composer returns the first-repetition null at every repetition and a write-time integrator returns the rising curve

**Two new discriminators from the primary source, one already pointing to A over a delay** (Kumaran & McClelland 2012). (i) **Circle closure**: after training `A>…>F`, add `F+ A−`. Query-time composition predicts chance on `B–E`. A cached ordering (temporal context model, value transfer) predicts `B` still preferred. Both predict `B > D`. Only small-sample animal data exist (Gillan 1981; Davis 1992), and they show disruption. This is a cheaper test than the row's `Closes when`, and it runs in any machine without an internal probe. (ii) **Sleep favours distant pairs**: 12 h after training, `B–E` improves more than `B–D` (Ellenbogen et al. 2007). REMERGE reproduces the emergence of inference after the delay but **cannot** reproduce the distant-pair advantage, which is the signature of a stored linear structure. So the delay question this Status raises has one measured answer: after a night, a composite is plausibly present.

**The selector has a candidate in the training schedule** (Shohamy & Wagner 2008, [[wiki/concepts/retrieval-mediated-learning.md]]). Their intermixed overlapping pairs give encoding-phase prediction and equal test latency. Blocked designs give test-phase hippocampal effects (Heckers et al. 2004; Preston et al. 2004). Transitive inference shows test-time effects only when non-overlapping pairs are trained first (Titone et al. 2004, via the source). The source's own reading is that both mechanisms run and the structure of the experience picks one. Two limits. (i) Schedule and paradigm are confounded across these studies (acquired equivalence vs associative and transitive inference), so no single study varies schedule alone. (ii) Equal latency rules out slow composition at test, not a fast settling store. REMERGE does not report settling time against response time. This adds a third cheap discriminator for machines: compute spent on the unobserved pair vs on trained pairs **(brainstorm)**. `Closes when` is not met: no study yet measures reinstatement and manipulates composition demand in the same subjects.

**When nothing is stored, query-time composition is frontal, not hippocampal** (Wendelken & Bunge 2010, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]). With all premises on screen there is nothing to write, so composition is query-time by construction. Chaining two relations raises right RLPFC activation, while hippocampal activation is flat for inference vs direct trials in every ROI (`n = 16`). Hippocampus encodes the relations (posterior, ordered > symmetric) and couples more with RLPFC on inference trials. So when a query-time traversal operator exists, it sits **outside** the store, which is not the place Position B's REMERGE mechanism puts it. Two second-hand points from the same source bear on the trained-premise case. (i) Mouse hippocampal lesion before training impairs transitive inference, and lesion after training spares it (Van der Jeugd et al. 2009). That fits a composite or ordering built during learning (Position A, or replay-written per the merge above). It does not fit a settling loop that must run in the hippocampus at test. (ii) Memorised-premise studies report no RLPFC. The authors read this as repeated exposure caching an ordering that replaces online integration. Neither point is a primary measurement here.
