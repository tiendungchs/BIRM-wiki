# T310 — Does a frozen linear probe rank self-supervised representations?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The wiki's flagship latent-vs-pixel-target measurement (I-JEPA 66.9 vs 40.7, ImageNet-1% linear evaluation) is re-run at a non-zero read-out depth, and either survives or inverts.

## Position A

**Yes — it is the field's instrument and the wiki's.** Nearly every representation claim the wiki carries is a frozen-feature number: the anti-collapse family comparison ([[wiki/concepts/representational-collapse.md]]), the augmentation-versus-masking trade ([[wiki/entities/i-jepa.md]]), the +26.2 points that make the case for predicting in representation space (T162). The justification is principled — restricting the decoder is what stops the *probe* from doing the work ([[wiki/concepts/representation-probing.md]]) — and a weak decoder therefore reads out usable format, not merely surviving information.

## Position B

**No — it measures format, and format is uncorrelated with content.** (He et al. 2022, [[wiki/entities/mae.md]].) On ViT-L, tuning a **single** Transformer block moves MAE from 73.5 to **81.0**, and tuning half of one (its MLP sub-block only) to 79.1. Across that sweep the ordering of two methods **inverts**: MoCo v3 has the higher frozen linear probe and is worse than MAE at every partial depth tested, by 2.6 points at four blocks — and worse again on COCO detection and ADE20K segmentation. Two of MAE's own ablations are *only* visible to the frozen probe and vanish under fine-tuning (decoder depth 1 vs 8: 65.5 → 73.5 linear, 84.8 → 84.9 fine-tuned; mask token in the encoder: 59.6 → 73.5 linear, 84.2 → 84.9 fine-tuned), which is the same statement from the other side — the instrument is reading how much of the representation sits in one already-readable format, and the encoder's last layers can be re-specialised for free.

**And the sample is no longer two encoders on one architecture.** (Ericsson et al. 2021, [[wiki/entities/ssl-transfer-benchmark.md]].) Fourteen encoders — 13 self-supervised plus supervised — all ResNet-50, all pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, read out both frozen and fully adapted on the same downstream tasks. Rank correlation between the two read-outs, **computed here from their Tables 1 and 4** (the paper reports both rankings and never correlates them):

| Downstream block | frozen vs. adapted, `n` = 14 | Spearman `ρ` | Pearson `r` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Many-shot recognition, 11-dataset average | linear probe vs. full fine-tune | 0.736 | 0.736 |
| PASCAL VOC detection, AP | frozen backbone vs. full fine-tune | **0.174** | **−0.007** |

MoCo-v2 is 2nd best of 14 frozen on detection and **worst** fine-tuned (AP 54.22 → 44.74); InfoMin 3rd best → 2nd worst (53.45 → 44.92); BYOL 4th → 1st. On recognition the top three (DeepCluster-v2, SwAV, BYOL) hold their positions and the mid-field moves 5–6 places (SeLa-v2 up 6, PCL-v1 up 5, MoCo-v2 and SimCLR-v2 down 4).

**And a third source reaches B from a new direction: the labelled probe loses to a *label-free* criterion on a dense task.** (Garrido et al. 2023, [[wiki/concepts/label-free-model-selection.md]].) Selecting a hyperparameter by `RankMe` — the entropy of the embedding's normalised singular values, no labels, no training — against selecting it by an ImageNet linear-probe oracle and by the downstream task's own oracle:

| Downstream benchmark | Task oracle | ImageNet linear oracle | RankMe (no labels) |
|---|---|---|---|
| VOC07+12 detection AP50, VICReg cov. | 79.7 | 78.2 | **79.7** |
| VOC07+12 detection AP50, SimCLR temp. | 81.8 | 81.0 | 81.0 |
| ImageNet-1% semi-supervised, SimCLR temp. | 34.6 | **31.3** | 30.9 |

Two readings, and both belong to B. On detection, the ImageNet frozen linear probe is beaten by a statistic that never sees a label — so whatever rank information the probe carries about that task, an unsupervised spectrum measurement carries at least as much. On ImageNet-1%, the *same-dataset* full-label linear oracle is 3.3 points worse than the task oracle, which the authors state directly: full-ImageNet linear accuracy is not well correlated with few-shot performance on ImageNet itself. That is the instrument failing without any domain shift at all, which neither of the two sources above tests.

## Where it bites

Every comparison table in the wiki's self-supervised wave. If B holds, the T162 headline is a claim about linear separability rather than about what latent targets contain, and the reconstruction pole is systematically underrated by the only instrument that has been applied to it — while the pole's *transfer* numbers (COCO 53.3 vs 49.3, ADE20K 53.6 vs 49.1) are the ones that agree with the partial-fine-tuning ordering rather than with the probe.

**The bite is now sortable.** Split the wiki's frozen-probe claims by what read-out the downstream consumer will use. Recognition-like read-outs (the anti-collapse family table, the augmentation/masking trade, T162's +26.2): `ρ` = 0.74, mid-field displacements of 5–6 places out of 14 — so a top-vs-bottom claim survives and a neighbour-vs-neighbour claim does not. Spatially sensitive read-outs (every dense-transfer and detection number quoted from a frozen encoder): `ρ` = 0.17, `r` = −0.007 — no rank information, so those claims have no instrument behind them at all ([[wiki/entities/ssl-transfer-benchmark.md]]).

## Status

`LEANING B, and the question has changed shape` — B's inversion is no longer a single method pair on a single architecture. Across 14 ResNet-50 encoders the frozen/adapted rank correlation is **0.74 on many-shot recognition and 0.17 on detection**, and the transformer evidence (MAE vs MoCo v3, continuous depth sweep) points the same way on the same task split. So the instrument is neither valid nor invalid in general: **its validity is a function of how far the downstream read-out is from linear classification on ImageNet-like data.** Where the read-out *is* a linear ImageNet-like classifier, the frozen probe is close to a sufficient statistic and the wiki's recognition comparison tables largely stand; where the downstream task is spatially sensitive, the frozen probe carries no rank information at all and every conclusion drawn from it is unsupported.

**The third source narrows where the repair has to be run.** RankMe's fine-tuning benchmarks are two more points on the read-out-depth axis, on a fresh 110-model sweep and with a *selector* rather than a ranking — and they agree with Ericsson's split: recognition-like read-outs largely track, the dense read-out does not. Its distinctive contribution is the ImageNet-1% row, where the probe and the task disagree on the *same dataset*, so "the probe is valid when the downstream task is close to ImageNet classification" is too generous a statement of position A's remaining ground: it also has to be close in *label budget*.

Two counter-considerations survive. (i) Fine-tuning spends labels, so an adapted ranking is a statement about *adaptation efficiency*, and a system that must be consumed frozen is correctly ranked by the frozen number — the split above says which of those two questions each downstream consumer is actually asking. (ii) In Ericsson's suite the encoders differ in epoch budget, augmentation list and projector, so a rank displacement is not attributable to the objective alone; the MAE sweep, which is controlled, is the one that licenses the causal reading.

The repair is unchanged and still unrun by anyone: report read-out accuracy as a **curve over read-out depth** rather than a point. Ericsson supplies its two endpoints on 14 encoders, MAE supplies the interior on 2 — nobody has both.

**This row does not close.** Its `Closes when` names one specific measurement (I-JEPA 66.9 vs 40.7 at non-zero read-out depth) and no source has run it. What the new evidence changes is the prior: on a *recognition* read-out, which is what that measurement uses, `ρ` = 0.74 says the +26.2 gap is more likely than not to survive one adapted block — the gap is far larger than the mid-field churn that the 0.74 leaves room for. The detection column is the warning: I-JEPA's and MAE's dense-transfer numbers disagree with their probe numbers, and that is exactly the block where the probe's rank information goes to zero.
