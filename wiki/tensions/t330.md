# T330 — Is analogy a pipeline of separable subprocesses, or one search whose "subprocesses" are facets of it?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A design isolates *one* analogy subprocess against an explicit baseline and shows a region or population that responds to it and not to the others — or a mapper is built in which access and mapping are the same operation and reproduces the retrieval/mapping dissociation (domain-blind mapping, domain-sensitive retrieval) that the pipeline reading explains for free.

## Position A

**Four subprocesses, separately implementable and separately manipulable.** Every structure-mapping architecture in the wiki is built this way: retrieval is a distinct cheap stage feeding a distinct expensive stage ([[wiki/entities/macfac.md]]), mapping emits candidate inferences as a separate output ([[wiki/entities/sme.md]]), schema induction is a further operation over a completed mapping ([[wiki/entities/lisa.md]]). The behavioural evidence supports separability: mapping is domain-blind while retrieval is not (transfer ~86% either way once cued; 88% vs 12% retrieved at same vs remote domain, Keane 1987), and task designs turn single subprocesses on and off — presenting three terms instead of four adds inference, presenting the source as a stated rule instead of an instance removes induction ([[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]])

## Position B

**One constrained search; the four names are facets of it.** Hummel & Holyoak 2003: "analog retrieval, mapping, inference, and schema induction, as well as rule-based reasoning, are all special cases of the same sequential, directional algorithm for mapping." ARCS and MAC/FAC require access and mapping to run **simultaneously**, since structural, semantic and pragmatic constraints have to shape the memory search while it runs; inference is an *outcome* of the search rather than a stage after it. The whole process completes in hundreds of milliseconds by spreading activation. The neural record is consistent with B and not with A: in two phase-decomposed fMRI studies the pre-mapping *encoding* window produced **more** RLPFC/DLPFC/VLPFC activation than the mapping window (Krawczyk et al. 2010; Volle et al. 2010), and completion (three terms, infer the fourth) produced **lower** RLPFC activation than comparison (Wendelken et al. 2008b — *corrected from the primary:* VLPFC shows no such effect, and the authors' task analysis does not nest the two tasks, so this is not "adding a demand"; see Status) — no study has ever measured all four, and access has never been imaged at all (Parsons & Davies 2022, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a machine analogy system is four components or one. Under A the wiki's current division of labour is correct and the open work is filling boxes — `G37` supplies retrieval, [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]] supplies correspondence, [[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]] supplies the inference filter — and each can be built and evaluated alone. Under B that factorisation is the mistake: a retrieval stage that cannot see the structure it is retrieving for is not a cheap approximation of the right system, it is a different system, and MAC/FAC's measured cost (1.5 items surveyed per probe, structure-blind by design, `T197`) is the price of the split rather than a design win. B also predicts that any *stage-wise* evaluation of an analogy model is measuring an artefact of the harness, which is exactly what the encoding-phase anomaly says about the human experiments

## Status

`LIVE`, and the two positions are supported by different measurement grades. A rests on behavioural dissociations that are strong and replicated but are all *between-task* — they show that the demands separate, not that the mechanisms do. B rests on a theoretical claim from the architectures themselves plus three neuroimaging anomalies, each of which has an alternative explanation on the table: the encoding-phase excess may be anticipatory analogy-making (which would make it evidence *for* B) or ordinary goal-state maintenance (which would make it no evidence at all), and the inference-lowers-activation result is attributed by its own reviewers to a missing baseline. **The primary for that third anomaly does not support B's reading of it** (Wendelken et al. 2008b, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]): compare = retrieve two relations and compare them, complete = retrieve one, transfer it, generate a filler, so each task has an operation the other lacks; the two are matched in response time and accuracy; and RLPFC is above the fixation baseline for both compare cells and neither complete cell, with no task × cue interaction. Read without the nesting assumption it is a *Position A* datum — one operation (relation comparison) with a region that responds to it and not to relation transfer/generation, nor to whether the relation had to be induced from an exemplar. It does not meet the closing condition: fixation is not a matched task baseline, the per-condition null is threshold-dependent, and access is untested. **The row cannot currently be settled by fMRI**: subprocesses that overlap within a few hundred milliseconds are below the temporal resolution of the method that produced all of A's neural support, and the one ERP attempt (Qiu et al. 2008 — rostral BA 10 negativity at 900–1200 ms, dorsal BA 9 at 2000–2500 ms) buys timing at the cost of localisation. The discriminating instrument named by the source is representational rather than temporal: MVPA, which asks whether a region's pattern *encodes* a correspondence rather than how much it responds, has never been run on the subprocesses. **A note on the wiki's own exposure**: `I36`'s second reversal is Position B's second anomaly, read in the opposite direction — see the caveat box on [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]

**Two second-hand additions from a review** (Holyoak & Monti 2021, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]). Not closing; status unchanged.

- **A causal dissociation of a support process from the comparison, for Position A.** Cathodal tDCS to right DLPFC during retrieval practice abolishes retrieval-induced forgetting in later analogy generation and leaves analogy performance unchanged (Valle et al. 2020). This is a perturbation that moves one process and not another, which the activation evidence on both sides lacks. It does not meet `Closes when`: interference control is not one of the four named subprocesses, and the read-out is behaviour, not a region's response.
- **The "mapping" stage of every four-term design is a comparison stage.** An `A:B::C:D` item supplies the correspondences (`A→C`, `B→D`), so no correspondence search runs (the authors' footnote 1). The phase-decomposed anomaly in Position B (encoding > mapping window; Krawczyk et al. 2010; Volle et al. 2010) therefore compares encoding with relation *comparison*, not with mapping. Whichever position holds, no neural study in this row has measured mapping in the structure-mapping sense.

**The primary of Position B's quotation** (Hummel & Holyoak 2003, [[wiki/entities/lisa.md]]). Not closing; status unchanged.

- **Access and mapping differ by one switch.** During retrieval the driver activates recipients through the semantic units exactly as during mapping, but no mapping hypotheses or weights are kept, since LTM holds too many candidates. The authors report that this single difference lets Hummel & Holyoak 1997 reproduce the human dissociation between surface-sensitive access and structure-sensitive mapping (Ross 1989). That is the second branch of `Closes when` as written. The wiki has not read the 1997 paper, so the claim is second-hand for this row and does not close it. **Ingesting Hummel & Holyoak 1997 would decide whether it does.**
- **Inference and schema induction are mapping plus one rule each**: minting on unmatched inhibition, then intersection by normalized semantic feedback. They run inside the same phase sets as mapping, which is B's architecture claim instantiated. They still run *after* a 70% mapping threshold is met, so inference is staged even here, a small concession to A.


**A first-hand mapper in which retrieval and mapping read one objective** (Lu, Ichien & Holyoak 2022, [[wiki/entities/pam.md]]). Not closing; status unchanged.

- **The construction.** PAM's retrieval score `G` is its mapping log-likelihood at the MAP mapping. Mapping is the objective's `argmax`, retrieval is its `max`, and there is no separate access mechanism.
- **The dissociation, reproduced at toy grade.** On Keane 1987's materials both the near (surgeon) and far (general) source map correctly onto the radiation target, and `G` ranks near above far (2.216 vs 1.665). Humans: 88% vs 12% retrieved, about 86% solve once cued. On Turney's 20-analog pool `G` returns the intended source top-1 for 17/20, and all three misses are a nearer analog. `(brainstorm)` The mechanism is that the edge term fixes the `argmax` while the node term adds to the value.
- **Why this does not meet the second branch of `Closes when`.** (i) "Same operation" holds only because the model maps the target against *every* stored source. The authors call that implausible and propose a "finalists" filter in front of mapping, i.e. a pipeline. (ii) The dissociation is a rank difference on one target and two sources. `G` is not size-normalised and has no inter-source competition (footnote 3), so it predicts rank order, not the 88/12 magnitude. **Judgement call:** a reader who takes (i)–(ii) as scope limits rather than failures could close the branch on this evidence.
