# T330 — Is analogy a pipeline of separable subprocesses, or one search whose "subprocesses" are facets of it?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Spec:** `D237` ([[_brainstorm/birm-spec.md]] §11)  
**Closes when:** A design isolates *one* analogy subprocess against an explicit baseline and shows a region or population that responds to it and not to the others — or a mapper is built in which access and mapping are the same operation and reproduces the retrieval/mapping dissociation (domain-blind mapping, domain-sensitive retrieval) that the pipeline reading explains for free.

## Position A

**Four subprocesses, separately implementable and separately manipulable.** Every structure-mapping architecture in the wiki is built this way: retrieval is a distinct cheap stage feeding a distinct expensive stage ([[wiki/entities/macfac.md]]), mapping emits candidate inferences as a separate output ([[wiki/entities/sme.md]]), schema induction is a further operation over a completed mapping ([[wiki/entities/lisa.md]]). The behavioural evidence supports separability: mapping is domain-blind while retrieval is not (transfer ~86% either way once cued; 88% vs 12% retrieved at same vs remote domain, Keane 1987), and task designs turn single subprocesses on and off — presenting three terms instead of four adds inference, presenting the source as a stated rule instead of an instance removes induction ([[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]])

## Position B

**One constrained search; the four names are facets of it.** Hummel & Holyoak 2003: "analog retrieval, mapping, inference, and schema induction, as well as rule-based reasoning, are all special cases of the same sequential, directional algorithm for mapping." ARCS and MAC/FAC require access and mapping to run **simultaneously**, since structural, semantic and pragmatic constraints have to shape the memory search while it runs; inference is an *outcome* of the search rather than a stage after it. The whole process completes in hundreds of milliseconds by spreading activation. The neural record is consistent with B and not with A: in two phase-decomposed fMRI studies the pre-mapping *encoding* window produced **more** RLPFC/DLPFC/VLPFC activation than the mapping window (Krawczyk et al. 2010; Volle et al. 2010), and adding an inference demand **lowered** RLPFC and VLPFC activation (Wendelken et al. 2008b) — no study has ever measured all four, and access has never been imaged at all (Parsons & Davies 2022, [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether a machine analogy system is four components or one. Under A the wiki's current division of labour is correct and the open work is filling boxes — `G37` supplies retrieval, [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]] supplies correspondence, [[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]] supplies the inference filter — and each can be built and evaluated alone. Under B that factorisation is the mistake: a retrieval stage that cannot see the structure it is retrieving for is not a cheap approximation of the right system, it is a different system, and MAC/FAC's measured cost (1.5 items surveyed per probe, structure-blind by design, `T197`) is the price of the split rather than a design win. B also predicts that any *stage-wise* evaluation of an analogy model is measuring an artefact of the harness, which is exactly what the encoding-phase anomaly says about the human experiments

## Status

`LIVE`, and the two positions are supported by different measurement grades. A rests on behavioural dissociations that are strong and replicated but are all *between-task* — they show that the demands separate, not that the mechanisms do. B rests on a theoretical claim from the architectures themselves plus three neuroimaging anomalies, each of which has an alternative explanation on the table: the encoding-phase excess may be anticipatory analogy-making (which would make it evidence *for* B) or ordinary goal-state maintenance (which would make it no evidence at all), and the inference-lowers-activation result is attributed by its own reviewers to a missing baseline. **The row cannot currently be settled by fMRI**: subprocesses that overlap within a few hundred milliseconds are below the temporal resolution of the method that produced all of A's neural support, and the one ERP attempt (Qiu et al. 2008 — rostral BA 10 negativity at 900–1200 ms, dorsal BA 9 at 2000–2500 ms) buys timing at the cost of localisation. The discriminating instrument named by the source is representational rather than temporal: MVPA, which asks whether a region's pattern *encodes* a correspondence rather than how much it responds, has never been run on the subprocesses. **A note on the wiki's own exposure**: `I36`'s second reversal is Position B's second anomaly, read in the opposite direction — see the caveat box on [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontoparietal-network.md]]
