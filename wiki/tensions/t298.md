# T298 — Should a discrete node be smoothed or differentiated exactly?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A relaxed estimator and an exact event-based adjoint are run on the *same* discrete-latent architecture and task, and scored on both final loss and gradient variance.

## Position A

**Smooth it in the backward pass.** Replace the discontinuity with a differentiable proxy and anneal the proxy toward the discrete object. Gumbel-Softmax does this for a categorical argmax and beats every control-variate score-function estimator by 4.0–5.5 nats on categorical latents (Jang et al. 2016, [[wiki/concepts/discrete-relaxation-gradients.md]]); surrogate gradients do it for a spike threshold and, as of 2024, hold the entire CIFAR-10/100/ImageNet spiking leaderboard at 4–8× fewer time steps than conversion ([[wiki/entities/spiking-neural-networks.md]]); the DNC's allocation sort does it by simply ignoring the discontinuities, "apparently harmlessly" ([[wiki/entities/differentiable-neural-computer.md]]). Three literatures reached the same manoeuvre independently, which is the position's main argument.

## Position B

**Do not smooth anything — split the loss at the event.** EventProp applies the adjoint method of optimal control to the hybrid dynamics, computing the *exact* gradient of `L = l_p(𝒮) + ∫ l_V(V,t) dt` via a backward ODE system with error transmitted only at saved event times. The discontinuity is handled by integrating around it rather than replacing it, so there is no bias, no temperature, no schedule, and no train/test mismatch ([[wiki/entities/spiking-neural-networks.md]]).

## Where it bites

Every wiki row that wants a trainable discrete node: `G27`'s binary-bottleneck family, `G91`'s missing discrete commit, `T147`/`T148` position A, and the router argmax of [[wiki/concepts/sparse-expert-routing.md]]. Under A a builder accepts a biased gradient and buys a hyperparameter (`τ` and its schedule) plus a regime mismatch — the relaxed estimator is trained continuous and evaluated discrete, and Jang et al.'s objective is admitted to be no longer a variational bound. Under B the gradient is exact but the method is offline, non-local, and blind to event *creation and deletion* — it cannot move a discrete choice that does not currently exist, which is precisely what allocating a new symbol requires (T23).

## Status

`LIVE` and untested as stated: the two positions come from different literatures on different discontinuities (an argmax over `k` classes vs. a threshold crossing in continuous time) and have never been run against each other. What makes it a real row rather than a domain difference is that B's stated limitation — blindness to event creation and deletion — is the *same* limitation as A's fixed `k`: neither route can grow the discrete alphabet, so the axis they disagree on may be less load-bearing than the one they share
