# T308 — Is the momentum encoder necessary to prevent collapse, or only the stop-gradient?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One codebase runs the predictor-asymmetric joint embedding with and without the EMA target while holding the optimiser (SGD vs LARS), batch size, projector depth, output-BN and predictor learning-rate schedule fixed — isolating which implementation difference converts 0.3% into 68.1%.

## Position A

**The momentum encoder is necessary.** BYOL's own ablation sets `τ = 0`, i.e. target = the online network with stop-gradient only, and reports **0.3%** top-1 — chance. Removing the predictor instead (leaving the EMA) gives 0.2–0.3%. The wiki has carried the conclusion "both stabilisers are necessary; removing either collapses at ~0.3%" on [[wiki/entities/byol.md]], in G34 and in T164 ever since, and the conditional-variance mechanism that explains BYOL's non-collapse *requires* the EMA to ratchet the target's information content upward (Grill et al. 2020)

## Position B

**Only the stop-gradient is.** SimSiam is BYOL with the momentum encoder deleted — shared weights, one predictor, stop-gradient — and reaches **68.1%** at 100 epochs, *above* BYOL's own 66.5 at the same budget, with per-channel std at `1/√d` and no sign of collapse across batch sizes 64–2048, two similarity functions, and symmetric and asymmetric losses. Deleting the stop-gradient from the same system gives 0.1% instantly. The momentum encoder buys **accuracy at long horizons** (BYOL 74.3 vs SimSiam 71.3 at 800 epochs) and is not the collapse provision; the discovery was obscured because a momentum encoder is *always* accompanied by a stop-gradient (Chen & He 2021, [[wiki/entities/simsiam.md]])

## Where it bites

Every design in the wiki that inherited the EMA teacher — the whole JEPA lineage, DINOv2/v3, V-JEPA-2 — pays for a second copy of the encoder and a `τ` schedule on the grounds that it is load-bearing against collapse. Under B that copy is an accuracy and stability purchase that can be traded away, which makes the lineage's anti-collapse story one component simpler and removes the wiki's cleanest example of a provision with *two* necessary parts. It also cuts at T305: if the EMA is not needed for non-collapse at all, "the EMA keeps the predictor near-optimal" loses the phenomenon it was invented to explain.

## Status

`LEANING B` — B's system trains and A's does not, and the two are not the same system: BYOL's `τ = 0` row uses LARS at batch 4096, a 2-layer projector with **no** BN on its output, and a predictor on the same schedule as the encoder, while SimSiam uses SGD at batch 256, a 3-layer projector **with** output BN, and a bottleneck predictor whose learning rate is *not* decayed (worth +0.4 on its own, and the one setting the paper attributes to keeping the predictor from converging). Chen & He concede the gap is "subject to many implementation differences" rather than claiming BYOL's number is wrong. **(brainstorm)** The most likely single culprit is the output BN on the projector, which is locus 5 — a normalisation the wiki already knows can hold a representation up on its own — in which case both papers are right and the honest statement is that *the EMA is one of at least three interchangeable ways to keep the target branch from being dragged along*, alongside output standardisation and a predictor running at a different rate. That reading is consistent with the solved linear dynamics, where `τ < 1`, `α_p > 1` and predictor-only weight decay are all sufficient and none is necessary (Tian et al. 2021, [[wiki/entities/byol.md]]).
