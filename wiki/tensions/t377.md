# T377 — Which structure initiates a coordinated cortico-hippocampal replay event?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Initiation is manipulated rather than cross-correlated — clamp one area's activity during an otherwise normal frame and measure whether the other area's sequence replay survives; or record enough cells in both areas simultaneously to time the two *sequences* (not the two frames) against each other within single events.

## Position A

**Cortex initiates.** Cortical population frames start ~50 ms before hippocampal ones (`P = 2.2 × 10⁻⁸`) and end ~40 ms before them, hippocampus has no reported intrinsic up/down mechanism, and hippocampal interneurons phase-lock to cortical up/down transitions — so the window is opened from outside the store (Ji & Wilson 2007, [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]). The same arm carries content: cortical slow-oscillation up-states drive thalamic spindles that phase-lock ripples, and auditory-cortex patterns precede ripples by ~200 ms and *predict their content* (Klinzing et al. 2019, [[wiki/concepts/sleep-oscillation-nesting.md]]).

**A's content claim is now read first-hand and it is a double dissociation, not a lead time** (Rothschild, Eban & Frank 2017, [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]). Simultaneous CA1 and auditory-cortex tetrode recording in sleeping rats, cross-validated GLM predicting single-cell spike counts from the other area's ensemble vector: pre-ripple AC predicts CA1 **during** the ripple (`z = 4.69`, `P = 2.7 × 10⁻⁶` at −200–0 ms; `P = 0.0006` at −400–−200 ms; n.s. by −600 ms), while pre-ripple CA1 predicts AC during the ripple in **no** window; CA1 during the ripple predicts AC **after** it (`P = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵`) while AC during the ripple predicts CA1 after it not at all; and neither direction predicts anything in matched no-ripple epochs, ruling out slow shared covariance. Magnitude ~20–40% of the within-CA1 gain, and a lower bound given sparse ensembles. **And the cortical leg has a manipulation**: sounds in NREM sleep bias pre-ripple AC patterns (decodable, `P = 0.0013`), the bias persists 2–14 s past the stimulus, and the biased patterns predict ripple content through a GLM fitted on silent sleep (`P = 0.00068`) — while sound identity is *not* decodable from CA1, so the cue reaches the store only as a bias on selection

## Position B

**The hippocampus initiates the content, inside a window it did not open.** Ji & Wilson's own data trend toward hippocampal *replays* leading cortical ones within a frame (their Supplementary Fig. 10), and they state the direction is not established; hippocampal ripples can directly induce slow oscillations in medial prefrontal cortex, leading the mPFC slow-oscillation–spindle event by ~130 ms; mPFC replay correlates with hippocampal sharp waves directly (Euston et al. 2012, [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]]). The indexing and complementary-learning-systems accounts both require the store to nominate what is reinstated — under B the cortical frame is permission, not selection

## Where it bites

Where a machine consolidation scheduler lives, and who owns the replay distribution. Under A the receiver both times and selects, so the sampling policy is a state variable of the slow learner and every criterion on [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]] is computed in the wrong place. Under B the store selects and the receiver only gates, which is the architecture every wiki replay implementation already assumes. It also decides whether [[wiki/gaps/g014.md]]'s channel needs a return path for selection requests, or only for cargo

## Status

`LEANING A`, changed from `LIVE` by Rothschild et al. 2017, which measures the one thing the row was built around not being measured. The **content** direction before a ripple is cortex→hippocampus, the reverse arm is null, and the cortical end can be *set from outside* by a sensory cue whose effect survives seconds and shows up in ripple content — which is as close to A's "the receiver selects" as a non-clamp experiment gets. Three reasons the row stays open rather than closing. (i) The `Closes when` asks for a **clamp**; a sensory cue biases the cortical pattern without silencing anything, so a third variable driving both areas is not formally excluded — though the no-ripple control kills the slow-covariance version of that. (ii) The prediction is **cell-by-cell**, not sequence-by-sequence: it shows cortex biases *which cells* fire in the ripple, not that it sets the replayed *order*, and B's claim is about the sequence. (iii) B's arm survives intact for the second leg — hippocampus→cortex after the ripple is equally significant here, so the settled picture is a loop with the legs time-separated and cortex owning the first one, rather than A defeating B. The practical upshot for a builder is already actionable and does not wait on (i)–(iii): **a machine consolidation scheduler needs a receiver-side selection input**, and the wiki's replay implementations have none.

The row's original sharpest datum, now superseded, was a **dissociation inside one paper rather than a disagreement between two**: Ji & Wilson measure the *frame* (window) direction as cortex→hippocampus with high significance, and explicitly decline to establish the *replay* (content) direction, which trends the other way in their own supplementary analysis. So "who leads" was two questions, and that source answered the timing one for A and left the content one open — the same split T373 reaches for the cortex-vs-thalamus arm of the same loop. The standing reconciliation in the review literature is a loop with both arms real (cortical events initiate hippocampal replay, which then reinforces ongoing cortical replay), which is compatible with both positions and therefore adjudicates neither; Euston et al. 2012 note directly that some measurements put hippocampal events first and others cortical. Note that the interval analysis that establishes the coordination in the first place is direction-blind by construction — it correlates cross-area spike-timing intervals against the behavioural template, which is symmetric in the two areas
