# T318 — Is a network's depth a property of the data distribution?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Someone computes the IB bifurcation points of a joint distribution with known structure and shows that a network with that many layers beats networks with one more and one fewer at matched parameter count — or shows the bifurcation count and the useful depth are uncorrelated.

## Position A

**Yes — architecture is computable from `p(X,Y)`, and the computation is an eigenvalue problem.** The IB information curve has bifurcation points at critical `β`, where the optimal representation changes topologically (a cluster splits, a dimension is added). These are properties of the joint distribution alone, *independent of any modelling assumption*. The critical `β` is set by the largest eigenvalue of the second-order correlations of `p(X,Y|X̂(β))`; the condition under which a sigmoidal unit can compute the exact posterior — conditional independence of inputs given the label — breaks down at the **same eigenvalues**. Tishby & Zaslavsky 2015 therefore conjecture that the optimal layers sit just after each bifurcation, that linearly independent transitions may be merged into one layer, and that "the optimal DNN architecture is also determined solely by an information theoretic analysis of the joint distribution of the data" ([[wiki/concepts/information-bottleneck.md]]).

## Position B

**No — architecture is an independent lever, and it is pulled by hand.** The wiki's control-surface argument (`G16`) puts the heaviest lever in architecture precisely because no functional of the data distribution selects the intended decomposition: two models with identical predictive distributions are indistinguishable to any such functional ([[wiki/concepts/divergence-objectives.md]]). `G29`'s record is that architecture search is expensive, non-gradient and mostly unpaid-for, with hand-design the standing alternative. And the constructive results run the other way: a single ring with one shared recurrent weight approximates any time-invariant fading-memory filter, so within that class *no* topology is selected by the target at all ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T174, [[wiki/entities/simple-cycle-reservoir.md]]), while at fixed topology and fixed state the decisive variable is the spectrum of the recurrent operator (T311, [[wiki/entities/lru.md]]) — a design choice, not a reading of the data.

## Where it bites

Every wiki page that treats depth, module count, or level count as a design decision — [[wiki/concepts/broadcast-hierarchy.md]]'s levels, `G67`'s timescale bands, `G29`'s whole framing. If A, `G29` is not a search problem but a computation, and the same eigenvalue argument should say how many timescales a model needs (`G67`) as a by-product. If B, the bifurcation structure is real but says nothing about what to build, because a network is not the IB optimum and never sits on the curve.

**The two are not yet in contradiction, for three reasons.** (i) A is a conjecture with **no experiment anywhere in the source** — the paper reports no measurements at all. (ii) A's argument runs through the linear-separability condition of a *sigmoidal* unit and finite-support variables; B's constructive results are for linear state-space systems. (iii) A is about the layers of the IB-*optimal* representation, and nothing shows a trained network's layers land near it — the paper's own information-plane path is drawn as a hypothesis. The discriminating experiment is cheap and unrun: take a synthetic `p(X,Y)` whose bifurcation count is computable by deterministic annealing, then sweep depth.
