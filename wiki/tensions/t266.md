# T266 — Can a working memory held in synaptic weights be operated on, or only stored?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Only stored.** Mashour et al. 2020: the attended item is conscious and globally broadcast as distributed persistent firing; unattended items sit in weak local firing or in activity-silent synaptic traces (Mongillo et al. 2008). The claim is a *capability* boundary — "only active neural states can be mentally transformed, e.g. by mental rotation, whereas activity-silent states merely store previously computed states (and thus should be more appropriately termed short-term memory)". Evidence: whenever information in working memory must be transformed, an active decodable state re-emerges together with the classical signatures of conscious access (Trübutschek et al. 2017, 2019)

## Position B

**The distinction is a read-out artefact, not a capability limit.** The wiki's synaptic-store models make maintenance and computation properties of the same substrate: [[wiki/entities/stsp-working-memory-rnn.md]] reproduces prefrontal cortex's collapse of delay decoding while still performing the task, and survives ablation of half its synapses because the memory is not in the trained weights; [[wiki/entities/stp-flickering-cann.md]] holds content with no reverberatory trace and expresses it when a probe releases inhibition; and [[wiki/concepts/meta-optimized-plasticity.md]] argues that under parameter sharing activations *are* weights and attention *is* a weight update, so there is no basis for a transformability asymmetry

## Where it bites

The design of the fast store, directly. Under A a weight-based memory is a register file and every manipulation costs a reinstatement into activity — which prices mental rotation, re-planning and any multi-step edit in broadcast events, and predicts a hard serial bottleneck on how many items can be *changed* per unit time regardless of how many can be *held*. Under B the same substrate computes and stores, and the bottleneck disappears. The two also disagree about what the ~300 ms signature at each refresh is: a necessary reinstatement, or an epiphenomenon of the report

## Status

That is consistent with reinstatement being *necessary* and with it being a *correlate* of the task demand that also drives report.
