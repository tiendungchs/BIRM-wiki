# T315 — Is an uncorrected self-model *inflated*, or merely *insensitive*?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A group difference in self-assessment — orbitofrontal-lesion vs control, low-skill vs high-skill, or a machine system vs its own ideal-observer bound — is measured with trial-by-trial confidence and correctness and reported as bias **and** meta-`d′`/`d′` separately, so a criterion shift and an evidence loss can be told apart on the same data.

## Position A

**Inflated — a signed bias.** Orbitofrontal-lesion patients rate their own social skill as "unrealistically positive" against expert judges, while lateral-frontal-lesion patients and healthy controls do not; the failure is specific to the lesion and points in one direction, so the uncorrected self-model is optimistic rather than noisy and the fix is a correction term supplied by a separate module (Beer 2007, [[wiki/concepts/default-self-model.md]]). The wiki reads this as an architectural prediction: any system that estimates its own competence while being trained on a signal correlated with claimed competence should be expected to inflate. Kruger & Dunning 1999 is the behavioural version — "their incompetence robs them of the ability to realize it".

## Position B

**Not identified by that measurement.** Both results are **one-shot discrepancy scores** — a single self-rating compared against an external performance measure — and Fleming & Lau 2014 show such a score cannot separate metacognitive *bias* (a reluctance to rate oneself low) from metacognitive *sensitivity* (a genuine blindness to one's own performance), let alone *efficiency*. Worse for A, the Dunning–Kruger pattern is partly **predicted by the null**: type 2 sensitivity is mathematically bounded by type 1 `d′` (Galvin et al. 2003), so worse performers must produce noisier self-ratings whether or not anything about their self-model is biased. The same objection applies to the clinical insight literature the lesion result belongs to (David et al. 2012, via Fleming & Lau). Nothing in either result distinguishes a shifted estimator from a low-resolution one.

## Where it bites

Which repair a machine's self-assessment needs, and where it goes. Under **A** the estimate is complete and the scale is shifted, so a calibrator with an external referent applied *after* the estimate fixes it — cheap, post-hoc, and exactly what `G89` asks for. Under **B** the estimate may carry no usable discrimination at all, in which case no affine correction recovers anything and the fix is upstream in what the confidence path is allowed to see ([[wiki/concepts/metacognitive-efficiency.md]]'s evidence-loss reading, and Beer's own mechanism **B** — suppressed negative evidence). The two are separable on data the wiki's own benchmarks could already produce: [[wiki/entities/hle.md]] logs a confidence with every one of 2,500 answers and reports only the calibration term. **The repair under A is now priced and is trivial**: on closed-set classification a modern network's entire calibration defect is removed by one fitted scalar, with its error ranking — and hence its discrimination — provably unchanged (Guo et al. 2017, [[wiki/concepts/confidence-calibration.md]]). That does not adjudicate the row, but it makes the stakes asymmetric: if A holds, the fix is one float; if B holds, nothing post-hoc helps at all.

## Status

`LIVE` — this is a measurement dispute, not a clash of findings: B does not claim the self-model is unbiased, it claims the instrument cannot see the difference. It stays live rather than leaning because the discriminating analysis is cheap, unrun on humans and unrun on machines, and because A retains one piece of evidence B's objection does not reach — the lesion **specificity** (lateral-frontal damage does not produce the effect), which a purely mechanical `d′`-ceiling account does not predict, though a sensitivity account does.
