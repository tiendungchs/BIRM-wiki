# T312 — Is compositional generalisation one tunable scalar, or an irreducible facet vector?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A single split family is scored *both* by compound divergence and by the five-facet suite on the same models, and either the facet scores are recoverable from `D_C` (A wins) or two splits at matched `D_C` produce systematically different facet profiles (B wins). Nobody has run both instruments on one dataset.

## Position A

**One scalar, computed from derivation subgraphs, is the difficulty.** [[wiki/entities/cfq.md]]: sweep target compound divergence `D_C` in 0.1 steps and mean accuracy tracks it monotonically for LSTM+attention, Transformer and Universal Transformer, on **two** datasets, at `R² = 0.81–0.88` — against `R² = 0.11–0.22` for the input/output length ratios that the productivity facet is defined by. The examples in train and test are not distinguishable by eye at `D_C = 0.694`, so the difficulty is not carried by any observable the facet suite names. The correlation survives every training size, and the same scalar retro-explains SCAN's published splits, including why `output pattern` (`D_C = 0.008`) is a weak compositional test despite being the field's default hard split. On this reading the facets are *regions of one axis*, and reporting five numbers is reporting five samples of one curve.

## Position B

**Facets come apart, and one of them is immune to everything.** [[wiki/entities/pcfg-set.md]] (Hupkes et al. 2020) splits the adjective into systematicity / productivity / substitutivity / localism / overgeneralisation and the five dissociate on the *same* three architectures (0.53–0.72 vs 0.30–0.50 vs 0.14–0.98 vs 0.46–0.59). [[wiki/entities/mlc.md]] is the decisive case: identical architecture, one training-distribution change, systematicity goes to ≤0.22% error and productivity stays at **exactly 100% error** on SCAN's length split. A scalar that ordered difficulty would have to call those two splits comparable; the intervention moves one to zero and the other not at all. Position A's own data agrees in one place — CFQ's output-length split scores *worse* than its `D_C` predicts, which the authors attribute to length ratio plus elevated `D_A`. And `D_C` is computed from a rule set the benchmark's authors chose: change the grammar's factorisation and every divergence in the table moves, with no independent criterion for the right granularity (`G16`).

## Where it bites

Directly on `G17` and on how any wiki result is reported. If A holds, an o.o.d. score is meaningless without its `D_C`, benchmarks become comparable across domains for the first time, and "architecture X composes" reduces to "X's accuracy-vs-`D_C` curve is shifted right" — a two-parameter summary. If B holds, `D_C` is an aggregate that averages over the one facet nothing in the wiki has ever moved, and a high-`D_C` benchmark can be passed by a system that is still 100%-wrong on length; the certification-instruments conclusion that **an honest instrument returns a facet vector, not a number** stands, and `I32` is a difficulty *knob* rather than a difficulty *measure*.

They are also not symmetric in cost: A's instrument needs a generator that logs derivations, B's needs a hand-designed suite per task. A is cheap and possibly averaging away the interesting dimension.

## Status

`LIVE` — the two instruments have never been run on the same data. The strongest evidence for A (`R² = 0.81–0.88`) is computed over MCD splits that Table 3 shows differ in their *own* facet mixture (output-pattern coverage 0.023–0.079 across MCD₁₋₃), so the curve may be a well-fit average over a varying composition; the strongest evidence for B (MLC's 0%/100% dissociation) was never scored for compound divergence.
