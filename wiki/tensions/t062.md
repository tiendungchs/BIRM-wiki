# T62 — Should a fast associative store hold instances, or model a distribution?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A single-phase local rule that predicts its own correlations is shown to fit `p(x)` at one-shot cost, or shown not to — the two rules differ only in the subtracted term.

## Position A

**Instances, written in one shot.** The wiki's fast store is defined by one-episode acquisition: the Hebbian outer product is closed-form in the patterns ([[wiki/entities/hopfield-network.md]]), behavioural-timescale plasticity writes a receptive field from a single traversal ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]), and the argument against gradient learning as the episodic mechanism is exactly that 10⁴–10⁶ exposures cannot be one episode ([[wiki/concepts/complementary-learning-systems.md]]). The position now has a *search* result behind it as well as an argument: meta-learning over a plasticity family that spans Hebbian, anti-Hebbian and pre-/post-only rules, on a store that could in principle have accumulated statistics, converges instead to pure one-shot instance overwrite — a presynaptic-only key write and a Hebbian value write, with slot reuse as the only forgetting ([[wiki/entities/three-factor-key-value-memory.md]], Tyulmankov et al. 2021).

**And the `Closes when` clause can now be checked against a measured biological one-shot rule, which answers it in the negative.** The subtracted term in bidirectional behavioural-timescale plasticity is `W·k⁻·q⁻(ET·IS)` — proportional to the synapse's *own current weight*, not to any prediction of its correlations (Milstein et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]). Its fixed point is therefore a **target receptive-field shape** set by the timing of the instructive signal, not an estimate of `p(x)`: the same store run on wildly different initial states converges to near-identical final tuning (final-vs-initial `V_m` slope `m = 0.04`), which is instance overwrite with a built-in stabiliser rather than distribution fitting. So biology's cheapest known one-shot rule pays the cost of the subtracted term and does *not* buy B with it

## Position B

**A distribution, learned iteratively.** Recall of what was shown is not generation of what was not: to produce novel states in the same style the store must fit `p(x)`, which forces a `−log Z` term whose gradient is only obtainable by free-running the network, hence many passes and two phases with no closed form ([[wiki/entities/boltzmann-machine.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether one substrate can be both the episodic buffer and the generative model, or whether the wiki needs two write rules over the same weights. The stakes are concrete for replay: position A replays *stored* episodes, position B must replay *model samples*, and [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]] reports biology doing the latter (replay of never-experienced trajectories) while the fast-store literature assumes the former

## Status

`LIVE` — no source has tried both writes on one matrix; the nearest partial answer is that the two rules differ only in the subtracted term, so a single-phase local rule that predicts its own correlations ([[wiki/entities/fcann.md]]) may implement B at A's cost. The subtracted-term framing now has a third case to discriminate against: `−W` (weight decay to a timing-set target, measured in vivo) is neither `−⟨xxᵀ⟩_model` (B) nor nothing (A), and it is what biology actually runs
