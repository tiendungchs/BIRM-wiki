# T220 — Is best-of-`k` monotone in `k`?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The reconciliation is tested: whether `d(score)/dk` flips sign exactly when the acceptance test admits a wrong candidate, against the HLE length-binned measurement that does not fit it.

## Position A

**Yes — more search is more score.** The wiki's headline inference-scaling numbers are all monotone and taken at large budgets: o1 at 93% with 1,000 candidates plus a learned scorer, DeepSeek-Prover-V2 at 88.9% with 8,192 against a Lean kernel, [[wiki/entities/neo-neural-theorizer.md]] at ~0.70 with 1,024 and a majority vote, [[wiki/entities/poe-arc-solver.md]] still gaining coverage at 6.5× runtime ([[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]])

## Position B

**No — it is an inverted U, measured.** Cobbe et al. 2021 rank up to several thousand completions per GSM8K problem with a learned 6B verifier: accuracy rises to about **400 candidates and then falls**, the authors' stated cause being that a large enough search *finds adversarial solutions that fool the verifier* ([[wiki/entities/gsm8k.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides whether inference budget is a dial a builder can simply turn up, and whether a reported best-of-`k` score needs a *peak* reported alongside it. It also sets which rung of [[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]]'s ladder can absorb a large budget at all: if the turn-down is a property of learned scorers, then sound checkers (kernel, execution, invariance veto) and learned ones have qualitatively different scaling laws, and Goodhart is not a risk that grows with `k` but a *regime* that begins at a measurable `k`

## Status

`LEANING` toward a reconciliation neither source states: the sign of `d(score)/dk` is set by whether the acceptance test can be satisfied by a candidate that is wrong. **A third measurement now constrains that reconciliation and does not fit it:** [[wiki/entities/hle.md]] bins output length on a `log2` scale and finds accuracy log-linear in reasoning tokens and then *falling* beyond **2^14 ≈ 16,384** — with no candidate set, no scorer and no selection, so nothing can be fooled. The budget there is spent inside one trajectory, which makes the turn-down a per-step-reliability ceiling (T217's `p`, compounding as ~`(1−ε)^L`) rather than a Goodhart threshold. Two inverted-Us, two causes: the verifier peak should move with a sounder checker, the length peak only with a lower `ε`. Both monotone rows above use a sound or generator-independent test; the turn-down row uses a learned scalar. **The sweep this row called unrun is now in the wiki, and it splits the learned scalar in two** ([[wiki/entities/prm800k.md]], Lightman et al. 2023): over one candidate pool of up to **1,860** samples per MATH problem, the **ORM's** accuracy *falls* with `N` on the easiest difficulty quintile — the authors' stated cause being adversarial solutions that fool it, the same mechanism as Position B — while the **PRM** on the same pool is flat-to-rising at every quintile out to 1,860 with the ORM gap *widening* in `N`. So the peak did not move with scale; it moved with **what the scorer can see**, which is the reconciliation this row leans toward, obtained without a controlled sweep of verifier accuracy. Two caveats keep it `LEANING`: the PRM's own maximum is simply not reached (1,860 is where the experiment stops), and the ORM turn-down appears on the *easy* end, where the finding is that a large search over easy problems surfaces convincing wrong answers faster than the scorer's margin can absorb — a statement about candidate-pool composition, not only about the checker. The HLE length-binned turn-down still fits neither reading
