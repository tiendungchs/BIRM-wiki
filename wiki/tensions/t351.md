# T351 — Does an easy-to-hard curriculum pay at *fixed* capacity, or only when capacity is small and then grows?

**Status:** `LIVE`
**Level:** `L0`
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]
**Closes when:** One task, one learner, a 2×2: {curriculum, random order} × {capacity fixed at full size, capacity grown on a schedule}, scored at matched final size and matched total updates. The row resolves if the curriculum main effect is non-zero in the fixed-capacity cell, and inverts if the gain is carried entirely by the interaction.

## Position A

**Order alone is worth points, with capacity fixed throughout.** Bengio et al. 2009 ([[wiki/concepts/curriculum-learning.md]]) hold architecture, parameter count and hyper-parameters constant across every comparison — hyper-parameters are even tuned for the *no-curriculum* baseline — and still get: a 3-hidden-layer network whose best test error comes from spending 128 of 256 epochs on the easy set, and a ranking language model at 2.78 vs 2.83 mean log-rank. The two controls exclude the obvious confounds: training on the **union** of easy and target sets is significantly worse than the curriculum, and training on the easy set alone is poor. The proposed mechanism needs no growth — a curriculum is a continuation method, so the schedule selects a basin of `C_1` that a full-capacity network descending `C_1` from step 0 does not reach.

## Position B

**The gain is an interaction with capacity, and disappears when resources are fixed.** The lineage the term descends from is *"starting small"* (Elman 1993), where the result was explicitly attributed to a network that begins **restricted in complexity and then expands its resources as it learns** — the claim was about limited capacity, not about example order. Krueger & Dayan 2009, re-running shaping from the cognitive side, report faster convergence *but worse final results than no curriculum when neural resources are held fixed* — the same design as Position A's, with the opposite sign on the outcome that matters. Rohde & Plaut 1999 separately failed to reproduce Elman's starting-small advantage. Bengio et al. cite all three and state that "the reasons for these differences remain to be clarified."

## Where it bites

Directly on which of two open rows is the real one. `G100` asks for a **capacity** schedule and `G32` for a **data** schedule, and the wiki carries them as independent levers on the strength of Position A. If B holds, they are one lever: a data curriculum is a way of *simulating* a capacity constraint (an easy, low-entropy `Q_λ` makes most of the network's expressivity irrelevant early), and building a growth schedule without one — or a curriculum without growth — buys the smaller half of the effect. The 2×2 also settles what [[wiki/concepts/developmental-heterochrony.md]]'s per-module phase offsets are *for*: under A they are an independent prior about when a block commits, under B they are the mechanism the data curriculum was standing in for all along.

## Status

`LIVE`, and asymmetrically evidenced rather than genuinely balanced. Position A has the wiki's only primary source with matched-capacity controls and published effect sizes; Position B is held **second-hand** — Krueger & Dayan 2009 and Rohde & Plaut 1999 are known here only through Bengio et al.'s citations, neither has been ingested, and neither effect size is in the wiki. Three things stop that asymmetry from settling the row. (i) A's own effect sizes are small where the objective is easy (0.8 points on a convex SVM criterion, 0.05 log-rank on the language model) and large only in the deep non-convex case — which is the regime where "capacity effectively available" is least well defined, so A's strongest cell is B's most plausible one. (ii) The two positions disagree about an *interaction* and neither experiment ran both factors; A never grew a network and B never (as reported) varied order at several fixed sizes. (iii) [[wiki/entities/progressive-neural-networks.md]] is the wiki's only architecture that actually grows, and its matched-size control varies stored *content*, not schedule — so the capacity arm of the 2×2 has an implementation but has never been crossed with an ordering arm. Re-probe for the two missing primaries before treating B as weak.
