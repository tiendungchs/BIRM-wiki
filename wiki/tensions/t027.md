# T27 — Is the brain's map metric or topological?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Metric.** O'Keefe & Nadel's original proposal was a Euclidean coordinate system; entorhinal cortex tracks *Euclidean* distance to a newly set goal independently of barriers, grid cells tile space with a fixed metric, and place-field position is set by distances to walls (Epstein et al. 2017)

## Position B

**Topological.** Posterior hippocampus tracks *path* distance during travel and scales with the current node's degree centrality, anterior hippocampus with its closeness centrality in the street network; replay of place cells over connected tracks preserves the tracks' topology rather than their geometry; and the hippocampal-entorhinal system codes spaces defined only by transitions between discrete items, with no continuum

## Where it bites

Decides what a learned map is *for*. Under A the map is an embedding and planning is vector arithmetic plus obstacle correction; under B it is a graph and planning is search, with the metric demoted to a heuristic. The review states the relation between the two codes is unclear. **The wiki's reading (brainstorm):** they coexist as an A\* pair — an admissible Euclidean lower bound in entorhinal cortex guiding a topological search in hippocampus — which would explain why the two quantities are dissociable by region and why the Euclidean one updates at *goal-setting* while the path one updates *during travel*. Untested as such ([[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]], [[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]])
