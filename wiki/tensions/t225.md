# T225 — Can a benchmark whose label-noise floor exceeds every score reported on it order the systems it ranks?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Yes — the noise is unbiased and the ordering survives it.** Phan et al. 2025/2026 report 2,500 items and an 8-model leaderboard spanning 2.7–13.4% ([[wiki/entities/hle.md]]) alongside a self-measured **15.4%** expert disagreement rate. If mislabelled items are equally hard for every model, the error rate is a constant offset that cancels in a *difference* of scores, and the binomial standard error at `n = 2,500` is ~0.7pp, so a 10-point spread is many standard errors wide. This is the implicit position of every leaderboard built on the dataset, and of the wiki's other self-auditing benchmarks ([[wiki/entities/gpqa.md]] at 73.6–76.4% objectivity, [[wiki/entities/frontiermath.md]] at ~10% critical errors), none of which withdraws a ranking after measuring its own floor.

## Position B

**No — the offset is not constant, and the floor is not even a fixed number.** The disagreement rate is a function of the audit protocol, not of the items: the *same* biology/chemistry/health subset yields **18%** under multi-reviewer-with-rebuttal and **25%** under a single dissenting expert. A rate that moves 7 points on the reviewing rule cannot be subtracted. Worse, the errors are unlikely to be model-independent — an ambiguous item is one where several answers are defensible, which is precisely where a model's chance of matching the recorded key depends on its output style and on the LLM judge's matching behaviour (T218). And the authors themselves disclaim the ordering on the *other* ground (`F7`): inflections near zero "are not strongly indicative of progress".

## Where it bites

Every score in the 2.7–13.4% band, and by extension every frontier-benchmark leaderboard placed deliberately on the floor. It is distinct from `F11` ([[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]): F11 is insufficient `n`, which shrinks with more items; this is a bias term of unknown sign that does not. It also decides whether "measure your own error rate" is a virtue or a formality — three benchmarks in the wiki now report one, and no reported score anywhere has been adjusted by it.

## Status

`LEANING`, and the answer is split. **The decisive measurement has now been run** — Zhai et al. 2026 ([[wiki/entities/hle-verified.md]]) repaired 1,143 items and re-scored seven frontier models on both the full set and the repaired subset. **A wins on the ordering**: all seven hold rank after repair, so a floor exceeding every score does not by itself invalidate a ranking. **B wins on the offset**: the gains span +7.78 to +11.50, so the noise is not the constant that cancels in a difference, and adjacent gaps halve (Claude-Opus4.5 over DeepSeek-V3.2: 5.10 → 2.40). And on the defective quarter alone, B wins outright — the same models compress from a **2.5× to a 1.22× spread** and the top rank changes hands, so a sub-leaderboard restricted to the hardest items was almost entirely an artefact. The same audit puts the floor itself at ~26% scoring-relevant rather than 15.4% (T226). Corrected statement: *the ordering survives, the gaps do not, and the harder the subset the less of it is real.* Still open for benchmarks that have not been repaired, which is all of the others.
