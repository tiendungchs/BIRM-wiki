# T101 — Can the control layer address the episodic store directly, or only through a relay?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The direct anterior-cingulate→CA1/CA3 route is tested for the traffic the relay account assigns to nucleus reuniens, establishing whether the relay is necessary or merely present.

## Position A

**Only through a relay.** The direct hippocampus→medial-prefrontal projection is monosynaptic and essentially unreciprocated, so the return route must be nucleus reuniens or entorhinal cortex — the premise of [[wiki/entities/nucleus-reuniens.md]] and of this wiki's whole controller→store story (Heidbreder & Groenewegen 2003; Jin & Maren 2015)

## Position B

**Directly.** A monosynaptic anterior-cingulate → dorsal CA1/CA3 projection exists in mouse and is optogenetically necessary *and sufficient* for spatial-memory retrieval, preferentially innervating learning-created hub neurons (Rajasethupathy et al. 2015, in Sigurdsson & Duvarci 2016; [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]])

## A third reading: two relays, typed by cortical source

Aggleton & O'Mara 2022 ([[wiki/entities/anterior-thalamic-nuclei.md]]) adds a case that is neither A nor B. Prelimbic, anterior-cingulate and retrosplenial cortices largely **do not** innervate the hippocampal formation directly, and trans-synaptic tracing in rats makes the **anterior thalamic nuclei** a major monosynaptic route from them into dorsal hippocampal formation — a second relay, distinguished from reuniens by which cortical region it serves rather than by what it carries: reuniens is the principal thalamic relay from rostral/ventral prefrontal sites, the anterior thalamic nuclei increasingly take that role for cingulate and retrosplenial ones. Their terminations in shared cortical targets are separated by **topography and lamina**, and anterior thalamic lesions are the more disruptive of the two in rodent spatial memory. So the relay is not one module with a gating parameter; it is a **set of source-typed channels**, and "does the controller need a relay" is the wrong grain of question — the answer depends on which part of the control layer is asking.

## Where it bites

Whether the controller→store write needs an intermediary module at all. If both hold, the resolution is that the *tiers* differ — the ventral tier receives from the store and reaches it only via relay, the dorsal tier writes to it directly — which makes the controller's read port and write port different modules, an asymmetry no architecture has
