# T187 — Must a binding operator be invertible?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A task is exhibited that analogy needs and thinning cannot do — slot-level query without decoding — or the invertible operator's extra cost is shown to buy nothing analogy uses.

## Position A

**Yes — the decode direction is the more valuable half.** The wiki's reading of HRRs is that `†` is what makes a bound structure *readable* rather than merely comparable: "who was the agent?" is `P ⊛ role†` plus an attractor cleanup, and that is the property that would make one vector-symbolic store serve as a fast `M` supporting both similarity ranking and slot-level query. Contextualization itself is built out of unbinding ([[wiki/concepts/vector-symbolic-binding.md]])

## Position B

**No, for everything analogy actually needs.** Context-dependent thinning is `⟨Z⟩ = Z ∧ ⋁_k Π_k(Z)` — lossy, with no inverse — and it supports retrieval, mapping *and* inference by similarity alone: candidate inferences are produced by copying base **code-vectors** with substitution and validated by comparing them to the target episode, so nothing is ever decoded. Its non-invertibility is the same property that makes it work: because thinning keeps a subset of the constituents' bits, a composite stays similar to its parts and nested structural similarity propagates upward for free — which convolution's randomizing property destroys (Slipchenko & Rachkovskij 2009, [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether a brain-inspired fast store must support slot-level interrogation or only comparison. If B, the cleanup-memory requirement ([[wiki/entities/hopfield-network.md]]) disappears and the substrate can be sparse binary; if A, everything downstream of retrieval — reading off *which* object filled a role, instantiating a schema with named arguments, answering a query about a stored episode — needs the inverse, and B's systems can only ever return a ranking.
