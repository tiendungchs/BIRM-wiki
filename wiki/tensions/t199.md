# T199 — Is relational retrieval bought by building indices, or by making the encoding uniform?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Encoding uniformity and index construction are varied independently, at a scale where an index is not already unnecessary — B's demonstration is 18–36 hand-encoded items.

## Position A

**Indices.** The case-based reasoning tradition retrieves the structurally right case by indexing memory on themes, principles and goals — the vocabulary of the index carries higher-order abstractions, and retrieval is a lookup in it. This is what makes a large case library usable, and it is the assumption behind every schema-retrieval mechanism in the wiki that presumes a candidate set ([[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] G37)

## Position B

**Uniform encoding, and indices are then unnecessary.** MAC/FAC's index is *computed from* the representation (a count over the functors the item was encoded with), so it updates automatically whenever encoding changes. Relational retrieval rises with expertise and with intensive encoding, and the proposed cause of both is a **uniform relational vocabulary** — a comprehensive domain theory makes everyone's episodes share higher-order predicates, so relational overlap becomes surface overlap in the index. With enough domain structure the behaviour converges on a richly indexed case-based reasoner with no indexing machinery built. The failure route confirms it: Bassok's verb-interpretation effect ties relational encodings idiosyncratically to surface content, and relational access collapses (Forbus, Gentner & Law 1995, [[wiki/entities/macfac.md]], [[wiki/concepts/schema-assimilation.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides where effort goes in a schema-retrieval system: into a retrieval structure over a fixed encoder, or into canonicalising the encoder. Position B also predicts that indexing psychology is *superhuman* — a well-indexed memory would retrieve the best structural match, which people demonstrably do not

## Status

`LEANING B` — B explains the expert/novice and encoding-depth data with no extra machinery and generates the falsifiable prediction (uniformity, not index construction, is the causal variable); but the two accounts have never been separated experimentally, and B has only been demonstrated at 18–36 hand-encoded items, which is exactly the scale at which an index is unnecessary anyway
