# T34 — Does hippocampus→cortex transfer happen during sleep or during waking?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Recall-gated consolidation is tested against ripple-driven replay on the same memories: A's selection criterion on B's online schedule, with the two schedules separately blocked.

## Position A

**Sleep (and quiet rest).** Sharp-wave-ripple replay during sleep and rest is the measured reinstatement channel, its content is filtered rather than veridical, and the whole machine replay literature is built on it (Marr; Schwindel & McNaughton; Liao & Losonczy 2024, [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]). **A's founding recording is now read first-hand and it is stronger than the wiki had it** (Ji & Wilson 2007): in slow-wave sleep, running-evoked multicell firing *order* replays in CA1 **and** in deep-layer V1/V2, experience-dependently (POST > PRE in both), at 5–10× compression, and the two areas' replays match the same trajectory — with the coordination holding for 11/11 trajectories by interval analysis against shuffled templates, not merely in the nine detected event pairs the wiki previously carried as the whole of the evidence

## Position B

**Waking.** Recall during waking retrieves the *currently relevant* episodes, which are the ones worth turning into semantics; unguided noise-driven firing in sleep would build the bizarre representations we dream about; and non-retrieval during waking is what marks a memory for overwriting, so the retrieval schedule and the forgetting policy are one mechanism (Rolls 2013)

## Where it bites

What a machine consolidation loop should sample. B makes *use frequency* the criterion — computable online, no labels, no offline pass — where A's criteria (reward, inverse visitation, cross-episode recurrence) all need a separate offline process. It also decides whether gap G14's channel runs on the same schedule as the store's forgetting policy

## Status

`LEANING A` on evidence — direct recordings all favour ripple-driven offline replay and Rolls offers a normative argument with no data. B's *criterion* survives independently of its schedule and no source in the wiki tests it. **The dispute is also narrower than it looks**: recall-gated consolidation implements A's selection criterion (recurrence) on B's schedule (event-by-event, online, no replay), so the criterion and the schedule are separable and selective replay is one implementation of the gate rather than its definition (Lindsey & Litwin-Kumar 2024, [[wiki/concepts/recall-gated-consolidation.md]]) . **A dose–response result now strengthens A where it was weakest — between-subject variance.** Prefrontal NREM slow-wave activity predicts overnight word-pair retention at `r = 0.81` across a 50-year age span, absorbs the effects of age and prefrontal atrophy on retention, and negatively predicts next-day hippocampal retrieval activation, while sleep duration, efficiency, stage composition and spindles predict nothing — so a variable expressed only during sleep carries the retention differences, which waking recall alone cannot explain. Still correlational, and it does not touch B's *criterion* (Mander et al. 2013, [[wiki/concepts/complementary-learning-systems.md]]). **And a split that gives each side half the question (tentative):** ripple content decoded on a behaviour-fitted manifold shows awake ripples replaying the trial just completed and the following sleep's ripples revisiting those same trials — selection computed awake, on the awake state's access to current relevance (B's point), transfer executed asleep, where cortex is receptive and the repetition budget exists (A's point) (`raw/talk-nd-memory-gating.txt`, [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]) **And A's schedule now has a causal manipulation with the timing as the variable** (Klinzing et al. 2019, [[wiki/concepts/sleep-oscillation-nesting.md]]): optogenetically induced thalamic spindles during post-learning slow-wave sleep enhance context-conditioned fear memory **only when phase-locked to spontaneous slow-oscillation up-states**, and a slow oscillation whose up-state contains no nested spindle *depotentiates* cortical synapses. That converts A from "transfer is observed during sleep" into "transfer requires a sleep-specific temporal conjunction that waking does not supply", which is the first argument on A's side that B's schedule cannot absorb — waking recall has no equivalent of the up-state window. It also explains the ageing result cited above at mechanism level: what degrades is the *coupling* of spindles to the up-state, not the amount of either rhythm. Neutral on B's criterion, as before. **And the offline window's duration is now bounded**: the POST replay enhancement decays back to the PRE baseline after ~40 min in cortex and ~1 h in hippocampus (Ji & Wilson 2007, [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]), so A's schedule is not "during sleep" but a short post-encoding interval inside it — which converges with the 0–2 h medial-prefrontal critical window and makes the two positions' *budgets* comparable for the first time: B's waking recall has hours, A's channel has tens of minutes.
