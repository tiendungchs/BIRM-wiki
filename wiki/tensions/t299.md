# T299 — Does prioritising replay by surprise speed learning up, or destroy it?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One agent, one buffer implementation, and the surprise-priority exponent `α` swept in both a single-task and a multi-task sequence — showing whether the sign of the effect flips with the number of tasks or with something else.

## Position A

**It is the largest free win in deep reinforcement learning.** Sampling `P(i) ∝ (|δᵢ|+ε)^α` over an otherwise unchanged Double DQN gives ~2× faster learning, median normalised Atari score 111% → 128%, and improvement on 41/49 games over DQN; on an artificial sparse-reward chain the gap between uniform and well-ordered replay is *exponential* in the number of states, with data, architecture, loss and optimiser all held fixed (Schaul et al. 2016, [[wiki/concepts/replay-prioritisation.md]]).

## Position B

**It is the worst rule on the shelf.** In a DreamerV2 agent over 4- and 8-task grid-world sequences, weighting mini-batch episodes by the model's own ensemble disagreement — the surprise/TD-error analogue — performs *like the non-continual baseline*: low performance, low forward transfer, high forgetting. Reward-proportional sampling buys nothing, and uniform coverage of everything seen (reservoir admission) wins (Kessler et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/continual-dreamer.md]]). Biology's measured criterion points the same way as the winner: upsample the under-visited, suppress the salient-but-idiosyncratic ([[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]).

## Where it bites

Decides whether a replay buffer's read policy has one setting or needs a task-boundary-aware one. If A is right within a task and B across tasks, then any agent that both learns fast and retains needs *two* concurrent sampling distributions over one buffer — which nothing in the wiki has, and which turns the arbitration problem [[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]] raises into a concrete two-term mixture. It also decides whether `|δ|`-prioritisation may be used at all inside gap `G14`'s consolidation channel.

## Status

`LIVE`, and the two results may not actually contradict: they optimise different objectives (within-task sample efficiency vs. across-task retention), use different priority statistics (TD error vs. ensemble disagreement, the latter computed **once at insertion** and never refreshed), and differ in whether the bias is corrected — Schaul anneals an importance-sampling weight to `β = 1`, Kessler applies none. **The stale-priority difference is the cheapest candidate explanation and it has not been tested**: an un-refreshed priority is exactly the failure mode Schaul's greedy baseline exhibits and his stochastic `α < 1` exists to prevent. Schaul's own Appendix A also predicts B's regime from A's side: `|δ|` is a bad proxy wherever transitions are *unlearnable*, and a task the agent has moved on from is unlearnable in precisely that sense. Nobody has run the sweep that would separate these.
