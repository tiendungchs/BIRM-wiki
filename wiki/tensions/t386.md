# T386 — Is the sensory render on the retrieval path, or an optional strategy a system can pass the task without?

**Status:** `BOTH`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Aphantasic and strong-imager groups are run on one visual working-memory task with graded load, the load at which the aphantasic group breaks is estimated, and the aphantasic group's delay content is tested for decodability in early visual cortex — establishing whether the weak-imagery route renders at all or renders below report.

## Position A

**Retrieval *is* rendering.** Imagery content is decodable from V1/V2 by classifiers trained on perception and constrained by voxel-wise perceptual feature models, so the recalled item is reinstated in the sensory format; imagery and visual working memory show similar capacity functions, both bias subsequent perception, and decoders trained on visual-working-memory delay content and on imagery cross-decode. The wiki's own implemented memory architecture takes this as constitutive: in [[wiki/entities/bb-model.md]] recall *is* the rendering of a viewpoint through the transformation bank run backwards, with no separate decoder and no non-rendering read path, and [[wiki/concepts/reference-frame-transformation.md]] generalises the claim to one weight set whose direction is set by a scalar (Pearson 2019, [[wiki/concepts/mental-imagery.md]]; Bicanski & Burgess 2018)

## Position B

**The render is one strategy among several, and the task does not require it.** Congenital aphantasics score at floor on vividness questionnaires *and* significantly below controls on the objective binocular-rivalry measure of imagery sensory strength — so the render is genuinely absent, not merely unreported — yet they perform easy and medium visual working-memory tasks, report episodic memories (rated low on "reliving"), and score *slightly higher* than controls on spatial imagery. In the general population, only participants with good imagery are impaired by irrelevant visual information during visual working memory, i.e. visual-cortex recruitment in that task is a **per-individual strategy**. Two people at identical performance can be running different mechanisms, and the review's stated conclusion is that imagery ability must be measured before any visual-memory result is interpreted (Pearson 2019)

## Where it bites

Whether a memory architecture must pass its read-out through a sensory-format generator, or whether the generator is a monitor tap hanging off a path that could be read some other way. Under A, the generator is load-bearing and its fidelity bounds recall — which is what [[wiki/entities/bb-model.md]] predicts and measures (recall correlation lowest at the rendering window). Under B, the sensory render is an *optional* read-out and a system without one loses vividness and high-load precision only, so ablating the generator in a machine model should barely move task scores — an experiment that is cheap and, on the wiki's architectures, unrun. It also puts a validity charge on every decoding result the wiki cites in support of A: human imaging cohorts are unselected for imagery strength, so a V1 decode of "memory content" may be a measurement of the strong-imager subpopulation only, generalised to the mechanism ([[wiki/concepts/human-baseline.md]]). Bears directly on [[wiki/concepts/constitutive-vs-enabling.md]], which uses imagery as its example of content surviving with the input pathway silent, and now has to carry the converse case: the content absent while the dependent function survives

## Status

`BOTH` — the separating variable is not the task but the **individual**, measured by binocular-rivalry imagery strength, with a second threshold inside the weak-imagery group: aphantasics pass easy and medium visual working-memory load and fail hard load, so the render appears dispensable below a precision demand and not above it. Three limits keep this from being a settled boundary. The load threshold is reported categorically (easy/medium/hard) and has never been estimated on a graded scale; it is unknown whether aphantasic delay content is absent from early visual cortex or present below the threshold of report, which is the difference between a missing generator and a missing read-out; and the episodic evidence is a two-person case report. Note also what the tension does *not* dispute — that imagery, where it occurs, is in the sensory format: Position B concedes the format entirely and disputes only whether the path is obligatory
