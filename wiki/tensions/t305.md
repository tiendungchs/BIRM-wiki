# T305 — Should a joint-embedding's predictor be kept *optimal*, or merely *fast*?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One run sweeps the two quantities independently — the predictor's residual `‖W_p f − sg(f_a)‖` against its update rate relative to the online network — at matched everything else, and reports which of the two the downstream probe tracks. The cheapest version: DirectPred with `p_j = √s_j` versus DirectPred with `p_j` set to the least-squares solution on the same `F̂`, same schedule, same seed.

## Position A

**Optimal.** BYOL and SimSiam both state the design rule that the predictor should always achieve minimal `ℓ₂` error predicting the target branch's output from the online branch's ([[wiki/entities/byol.md]]). The evidence is an ablation ladder: with **no** target network, a closed-form optimal linear predictor recomputed per batch reaches 52.5% where the standard configuration reaches 72.5% and removing the predictor collapses at 0.2%; raising *both* the predictor's and the projector's learning rates gives ≈25%, so what matters is optimality **relative to the projector**. The mechanism follows: with `q⋆(z_θ) = E[z′_ξ | z_θ]` the online update descends the expected conditional variance, and since `Var(X|Y,Z) ≤ Var(X|Y)` discarding information can never help — an argument that *requires* the predictor to be at its optimum to hold at all. The wiki's reading — the EMA exists to keep the predictor near-optimal, so the two stabilisers are one mechanism — is built on this (Grill et al. 2020; [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]])

## Position B

**Fast, and optimality is actively harmful.** Solve the predictor's regression exactly (`W_p E[ffᵀ] = ½(E[f_a fᵀ] + E[f f_aᵀ])`, expectations by EMA) and plug it in every minibatch: STL-10 top-1 after 100 BYOL epochs is **40.67 ± 0.50** with EMA and 39.45 ± 1.26 without, against **74–75** for the identical linear predictor trained by gradient descent. Plugging in less often is worse, not better (every 2/3/5 minibatches: 35.29 / 34.60 / 35.63). The rule that works — DirectPred — sets `W_p = Û diag[√s_j + ε max_j s_j] Ûᵀ` from the eigendecomposition of the predictor's own input correlation `F̂`, which is the fixed point of the *learning dynamics* (eigenspace alignment plus the invariant `s_j = p_j²/α_p`) and **not** the least-squares solution; it reaches 77.38 on STL-10 and 72.4/91.0 on ImageNet-300ep, matching BYOL's two-layer predictor. Interleaving gradient steps between direct settings improves it further (80.28). In the solved linear dynamics the load-bearing quantity is the relative rate `α_p`, which appears in the escape condition `s_j(0) > p_j²(0)/α_p` and in the alignment bound, and never as a residual (Tian, Chen & Ganguli 2021, [[wiki/entities/byol.md]])

## Where it bites

Every two-timescale design in the wiki that pairs a fast amortiser with a slower target — recognition net vs. generative model, policy vs. planner, fast **M** vs. slow **W**, the whole JEPA lineage's predictor-plus-EMA-teacher. Under A the amortiser has a well-defined job (approximate a conditional expectation) and its lag is a tolerated defect to be engineered away; under B the lag *is* the mechanism and closing it destroys the system, which makes "train the amortiser to convergence" — the default in every amortised-inference method — a bug rather than a target. It also decides what the EMA is for: a device for keeping a regression solved (A) or a **curriculum** that raises the predictor's goal `τ/(1+σ²)` as the features stabilise (B), and only the second explains why raising `α_p` or applying weight decay to the predictor alone substitutes for the target network

## Status

`LIVE` — and it is a genuine disagreement rather than a scale gap, because the two sides' numbers are compatible while their readings are not. A's 52.5% is an exact predictor *without* an EMA target; B's 40.67% is an exact predictor *with* one, and both are far below the 72–75% of a gradient-trained predictor, so **every measurement in the wiki has the exactly-optimal predictor losing**. What A actually has is the ordering 52.5 > 25 (predictor faster than projector beats both fast), which B explains without optimality at all. The reason it stays open: B's account is derived in a bias-free two-layer *linear* model with isotropic data, a symmetric predictor and no ℓ₂ normalisation, and the wiki already knows that normalisation (locus 5) is load-bearing on its own ([[wiki/concepts/representational-collapse.md]]); and A's conditional-variance mechanism, if abandoned, leaves BYOL's non-collapse with no account outside the linear regime. **(brainstorm)** The reconciliation that would satisfy both: optimality is the right target for the predictor's *eigenbasis* and the wrong one for its *eigenvalues* — DirectPred takes `Û` from the data and `p_j` from the flow, and that split is exactly what a residual-based rule gets wrong
