# T364 — Is the dorsomedial control layer a level-blind evaluator of control, or a level-specific selector organised by abstraction?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One design crosses **abstraction level** with **control demand** orthogonally in the same subjects, along the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) rostro-caudal axis — an easy superordinate task, a hard superordinate task, an easy subordinate task, a hard subordinate task, with automaticity matched by training rather than assumed. Position A predicts the rostro-caudal locus is set by **demand alone** and the level factor produces no spatial shift once demand is equated; position B predicts the locus is set by **level alone** and the demand factor changes amplitude without moving it. The existing gradient reports confound the two, because more abstract demands were also harder. A crossed interaction — locus moving with level *and* amplitude with demand — settles it as two superposed variables and turns the row into a question of what the second one is for.

## Position A

**Level-blind: one evaluator, applied wherever a process cannot run on prespecified parameters.** The expected-value-of-control model states the claim directly — dACC "should be engaged by control-demanding behaviours irrespective of their level of abstractness or temporal extent, whether these involve individual motor actions, more abstract strategies, or temporally extended tasks", the only determinant being non-automaticity (Shenhav et al. 2013, [[wiki/concepts/expected-value-of-control.md]]). The criterion is `signal* = argmax_signal [Σ Pr(outcome|signal,state)·Value(outcome) − Cost(signal)]`, which contains no level argument: `signal` ranges over identities and intensities, and an identity at any grain is an admissible member of the candidate set. The theory's positive case is coverage — conflict monitoring, value coding, task selection, error responses, effort cost, foraging, exploration and intertemporal choice fall out of one equation with no hierarchy postulated — plus the observation that dorsomedial cytoarchitecture grades continuously into pre-supplementary and supplementary motor area, suggesting one function applied over a range of control-signal abstractness rather than a stack of different functions.

## Position B

**Level-specific: the dorsomedial wall is ordered by abstraction, like the lateral wall.** The hierarchical-reinforcement-learning account (Holroyd & Yeung, as cited by Shenhav et al. 2013) assigns dACC specifically to the control of **superordinate, temporally extended actions** — options rather than primitive actions. The reviewed anatomy agrees: progressively anterior dACC regions signal progressively more complex demands, from conflicts between specific motor actions caudally to conflicts between high-level strategies rostrally; dACC and pre-supplementary motor area differentially encode **block-level** versus **trial-level** incentives, and dACC–lateral-prefrontal connectivity is modulated by which incentive type is in play, anterior with anterior and posterior with posterior; and those dACC subregions show resting-state connectivity with exactly the lateral bands Koechlin's cascade model orders by abstraction. This is the medial-wall counterpart of the lateral rostro-caudal ordering the wiki already holds, where the band engaged is set by the **order** of the structure in the task and not by its difficulty ([[wiki/concepts/policy-abstraction-hierarchy.md]], Badre et al. 2010).

**B now has one measurement that is not confounded with difficulty** ([[wiki/concepts/pseudo-reward-prediction-error.md]], Ribas-Fernandes et al. 2011). Dorsal anterior cingulate cortex activation scales parametrically with a prediction error about distance to a **subgoal** while distance to the rewarded goal is held exactly constant by the task's ellipse geometry, and the effect survives regressing out subgoal displacement magnitude, response accuracy and response latency. Difficulty is not the manipulated variable here — the two compared jump types (D and E) are matched visual events of equal motor consequence — so this is a dACC signal individuated by *level* with demand unmanipulated. It does not satisfy this row's `Closes when`, which asks for level and demand crossed orthogonally with a rostro-caudal locus reported, and it does not touch position A's coverage argument: the expected-value-of-control equation contains no term that would move with subgoal distance, but neither does it forbid one.

## Where it bites

**Whether a control-cost term is one scalar or one per level.** Under A a builder attaches a single `Cost(signal)` to whatever the controller is currently specifying, and the option hierarchy of [[wiki/concepts/temporal-abstraction-options.md]] needs no separate accounting — a temporally extended option is just a control signal with a long-lived identity, priced by its intensity like any other. Under B the cost and the specification are *replicated per level*, and a subgoal's price is not commensurable with a primitive action's without a conversion, which is precisely the bookkeeping `G33` needs and does not have.

The two also differ in what is architecturally **denied**. A cannot express a control decision that is unavailable at one grain and available at another — under A any identity can win the `argmax`, so an agent can always choose to control at the finest grain, which is exactly the failure mode a hierarchy exists to prevent. B cannot express the level-general effects A's coverage rests on: dACC responses to physical effort, to pain, and to trial-level conflict between two button presses have no superordinate action to be about.

The evidence classes are asymmetric in the usual direction. B's anatomical gradient is measured, replicated across labs, and has a converging connectivity signature; A's level-generality is a *theoretical* commitment plus the absence of a level effect in studies that never manipulated level. But B's gradient studies uniformly confound abstraction with difficulty — the strategy-level conflicts were harder than the motor-level ones — and A's central mechanism (an explicit subtracted cost term) is one B has no counterpart for at all, so neither position is a superset of the other.

## Status

`LIVE` — the source holding position A concedes the point rather than arguing it: "the EVC model does not speak directly to the issue of hierarchical organization of control." That is an abstention, not evidence, so the row records a theory that predicts no level effect sitting beside data that report one, with the crossed design unrun.
