# T349 — Does structure come from predicting forward, or from being allowed to see the past?

**Status:** `LIVE`
**Level:** `L2`
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]
**Closes when:** One architecture is run at fixed input window with only the target moved — a sequence-conditioned auto-encoder (attention over `I_0…I_k`, target `I_k`) against the same network with target `I_{k+1}` — and the map/aliasing measurements are compared. Either the target carries the effect or the window does.

## Position A

**The prediction target does the work.** The wiki's structure-from-self-supervision evidence is attributed to *forward* prediction throughout: next-observation prediction yields grid, band, border and object-vector units ([[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]); a next-frame pixel objective yields a distance-preserving latent map where a same-frame objective yields only image similarity, with mutual information 0.627 vs 0.227 bits and position error >80% below 7.3 vs 13.1 lattice units (Gornet & Thomson 2024, [[wiki/entities/visual-predictive-coder.md]]); the JEPA family and [[wiki/entities/cpc.md]] are built on the premise that predicting a *future* latent is what forces abstraction. Under A, "predict forward" is a design lever.

## Position B

**The observation window does the work; the target is incidental.** Gornet & Thomson's own Theorem 1 is a statement about the input, not the loss: if `f(x₁) = f(x₂)` for `x₁ ≠ x₂`, no left inverse exists, so **no** estimator that sees one stationary observation can recover position — whatever it is trained to output. Their auto-encoder control differs from the predictive coder in two ways at once: it has no attention layer and sees one frame, and its target is the current frame. The theorem bites the first difference only. The circular-corridor result — two pixel-identical red rooms separated by the predictive coder (mean position error 0.071 lattice units) and merged by the auto-encoder (5.004) — is therefore fully explained by conditioning on history, with the target doing nothing. Under B, "predict forward" is a way of *forcing* a model to carry history, and any objective that also forces it would do.

## Where it bites

Every objective-slot decision in the wiki (`G30`), and the design of any `g`-learning experiment. If B, the lever is the **context the encoder is architecturally permitted to see**, and the choice of forward target is free — which reopens targets the wiki has ruled out for being reconstructive, and makes the pixel-vs-latent debate (`T162`) a debate about the wrong variable. If A, window size is a hyperparameter and the target is the design. It also bites `T38`: whether unsupervised next-observation prediction "produces" structural codes is only answerable once the two are separated, since every model cited there conditions on a history *and* predicts forward.

## Status

`LIVE` — no experiment in the wiki varies the two independently. The named control is cheap (one target swap on a released architecture) and has not been run by anyone. Two considerations that do not settle it. (i) Position A has one clean datapoint the theorem cannot explain away: [[wiki/entities/mae.md]] conditions on 25% of an image's patches — a substantial, non-stationary context — and still learns representations that a same-target objective is supposed to be unable to learn, though nothing there involves an aliased latent state. (ii) Position B has the stronger prior from [[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]]: representations underdetermine objectives, and the wiki has repeatedly found the credited variable to be the wrong one (`T167` pair-sampler, `T310` probe depth).
