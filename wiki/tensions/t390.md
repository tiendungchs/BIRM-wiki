# T390 — Is a trained attention head performing associative retrieval, or pooling?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The operating-class measurement (median `k` = minimal number of softmax values summing to 0.90) is run per head on a model solving a task that *requires* single-item retrieval, and class-IV occupancy is shown to track retrieval accuracy — or heads are forced into the separated regime (annealed `β`, entropy floor) and the downstream cost is measured, showing the metastable regime is chosen rather than fallen into.

## Position A

**Retrieval.** `softmax(QKᵀ/√d_k)V` *is* the update rule of a continuous modern Hopfield network, so every head is an associative read with a derived capacity `N ≥ √p c^{(d_k−1)/4}` and a retrieval error `∝ exp(−βΔ_i)` (Ramsauer et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/continuous-modern-hopfield-network.md]]); the same layer with the softmax removed is an outer-product fast-weight store whose predicted breakdown at `d_dot` mutually orthogonal keys **is where the measured errors begin** — 64 / 128 / 256 / 384 associations for `ELU+1` and DPFP-1/-2/-3 at `d_key = 64` (Schlag et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/fast-weight-programming.md]]); and self-attention is *derived* as one read of a Hebbian associative memory over stored graph states ([[wiki/entities/tem-transformer.md]])

## Position B

**Pooling.** Classified by the size of their metastable state, the heads of pre-trained BERT models are predominantly **not** in the single-pattern regime: class I (averaging over `> N/2` entries) dominates the first layers, classes II and IV the middle layers, class III (`N/32 < k̄ ≤ N/8`) the last layers. The capacity theorem applies only to well-separated patterns, which is the regime these heads avoid. Lower-layer heads can be replaced outright by **input-independent** Gaussian averaging kernels at `2N` parameters per head against `2 d_k d_y` — a 95.5× reduction at BERT-base sizes — because neither the input dependence nor the per-position resolution is being used (Ramsauer et al. 2020)

## Where it bites

Whether the wiki may attach a capacity number to an attention layer. `G42` currently records softmax attention as a store whose precision falls with occupancy and whose breakdown point is predicted by the fast-weight bound; under Position B most heads are operating deliberately far from that point, so the bound describes an operating mode transformers largely decline and the relevant scarce resource is the *pooling width* `k̄`, not the item count. It also decides what an attention layer is for in a reasoning stack: a content-addressed lookup that a symbolic memory could replace, or a learned soft clustering that could not.

The two positions are measured on different objects — Position A's numbers come from a synthetic delayed key-value retrieval task and from linearised attention, Position B's from language-model pre-training with the softmax intact — so the candidate reconciliation is that the regime is **task-selected**: heads separate when the objective needs a specific item and pool otherwise. That predicts the class distribution should shift toward IV under a retrieval-heavy objective, and it has not been run.

## Status

`LIVE` — and Position B carries a second observation that makes the question urgent rather than taxonomic: heads that do enter the separated regime enter it at 9 000–10 000 pre-training steps and then stop learning, because a one-hot `p` makes `∂ξ^new/∂ξ = β X (diag(p) − ppᵀ) Xᵀ ≈ 0`. So sharpening the read vanishes the gradient that placed the keys, and "most heads pool" may be a description of what survives training rather than of what training selected.
