# T117 — Does perceiving a sequence rest mainly on top-down predictions, or on within-level priors over motion?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The two lesions are compared on a *learned* system, where the relative strength of structural and dynamical priors is trained rather than designed, and "worse" is scored rather than described.

## Position A

**Top-down.** The wiki's hierarchical stance throughout: a level's percept is constrained by the prediction descending from the level above, and abstraction is what disambiguates the stream — the structural priors of [[wiki/concepts/predictive-coding-free-energy.md]], the manifold-shaping control parameters of the hierarchical dynamical model itself, and the whole read of feedback as the carrier of context

## Position B

**Within-level.** In the same model, simulated lesions dissociate the two: cutting the top-down connections that shape the low-level manifold (structural priors) degrades recognition, but cutting the self-connections coupling orders of motion (dynamical priors, i.e. the generalised-coordinate constraint) is **worse** — frequency tracking survives while sequential structure is destroyed and the stream is no longer parsed at all (Friston & Kiebel 2009)

## Where it bites

How to spend capacity in a sequence model. Under A the payoff is depth and richer top-down channels; under B most of the work is done by each level's own prior over how its state moves, and a shallow model with strong dynamics would beat a deep one with weak dynamics. It also relocates [[wiki/concepts/event-segmentation.md]]'s open problem: if the dynamical prior parses the stream, the missing boundary detector may be missing because no boundary variable exists

## Status

`LIVE` — the evidence is one simulated lesion on one hand-built two-Lorenz model with known parameters, and "worse" is reported qualitatively (inflated prediction error, lost sequential structure), not scored. The comparison has never been run on a learned system, where the relative strength of the two priors is itself trained rather than designed
