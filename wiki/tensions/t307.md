# T307 — Does neural predictivity certify that a model implements the brain's algorithm?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A single architectural change is shown to move neural predictivity and behavioural error consistency in the same direction on the same stimuli, or a mechanism is identified that predicts which of the two a given change will move.

## Position A

**Yes — predictivity is the criterion, and it works.** Better ImageNet categorisation accuracy improves prediction of biological firing patterns; >30 architectures scored against ventral-stream representations rank in a stable order, with deep supervised networks fitting best and reproducing the *rise* of category-orthogonal (position, size) coding up the stream. Brain-Score operationalises this across neural and behavioural metrics, and the architecture search it drove produced CORnet-S — recurrent, compact, 73.1% ImageNet top-1, previously unmatched Brain-Score, and able to capture monkey object solution times — declared "the current best model of the primate ventral visual stream" (Schrimpf/Kubilius et al. 2018–2019; Yamins & DiCarlo; [[wiki/entities/ventral-visual-stream.md]], [[wiki/concepts/neuroscience-ai-transfer.md]])

## Position B

**No — the two criteria rank models differently, and the flagship case shows it.** Chance-discounted trial-by-trial agreement with human observers on the same images gives CORnet-S `κ = .066`, its ResNet-50 baseline `.068`, AlexNet (2012) `.080`, against human↔human `.331` — CORnet-S's *best* human match falls below the *worst* human–human pair, while CORnet-S↔ResNet-50 sits at `.711` with many datapoints exactly overlapping. Across sixteen ImageNet CNNs, `κ`-with-humans is unrelated to ImageNet accuracy on cue-conflict (`p = .77`) and edge (`p = .49`) stimuli and *negatively* related on clean ImageNet (`p = .008`); its relation to all six Brain-Score metrics is weak at best. So an architecture optimised for predictivity gained no behavioural strategy-match over the baseline it was built to beat (Geirhos, Meding & Wichmann 2020, [[wiki/concepts/error-consistency.md]])

## Where it bites

The whole reverse channel of [[wiki/concepts/neuroscience-ai-transfer.md]], and the *validation* argument in particular: "this mechanism is core to the brain, so redouble on it" is unscoreable until the similarity criterion is fixed in advance. It also prices `I7`/`I30` against each other in [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]] — representational fit and behavioural agreement are different ports on the same system and disagree — and it is the reason [[wiki/entities/ventral-visual-stream.md]]'s limitation 1 (*no accepted definition of success*) is load-bearing rather than housekeeping.

## Status

`LEANING` toward B on the narrow claim, and the narrow claim is all B makes: a high predictivity score does not license the inference to a matched algorithm, because a model can top the predictivity ranking while being behaviourally indistinguishable from the baseline it displaced. That does not make predictivity worthless — it is a real, replicable, monotone measurement, and B's own instrument is *necessary and not sufficient* by construction (`κ > 0` never certifies anything). What is unresolved is the mechanism: nobody knows why the two metrics decouple, and the leading suspicion is that predictivity is dominated by representational content that both texture-driven and shape-driven systems share ([[wiki/entities/stylized-imagenet.md]]'s reading — ventral-stream fits may rest on human-like *texture* representations). The dispute becomes decidable only when someone exhibits an intervention that moves both.
