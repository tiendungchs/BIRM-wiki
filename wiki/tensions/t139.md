# T139 — What defines a behavioural segment boundary — predictive failure, or clustering under a smoothness prior?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Predictive failure.** Every detector on [[wiki/concepts/event-segmentation.md]] thresholds a quantity produced by a *predictor*: accuracy (Event Segmentation Theory), epistemic variance (Baldwin & Kosie; Nguyen et al. 2025), or latent-cause posterior uncertainty. A boundary is where a model of the dynamics breaks, and the two triggers even recruit separate cortical networks

## Position B

**No predictor is needed.** [[wiki/entities/hisd.md]] segments Craftax and unmodified Minecraft with *no dynamics model at all*: frames are assigned to `K` prototypes by visual dissimilarity under an unbalanced optimal-transport plan with a temporal-smoothness penalty, and boundaries are wherever the argmax changes. It beats methods that do model the dynamics (OMPN, CompILE) on every task with more than two skills, including the perceptually aliased case where walking-toward-wood and walking-toward-stone are pixel-identical (Harvey et al. 2026)

## Where it bites

Whether a builder needs the world model *before* the discretisation or after. If clustering-plus-smoothness suffices offline, the node set can be recovered from unlabelled video and the predictive machinery only ever has to work over already-discrete states — which reverses the dependency [[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]] assumes. The two positions are not strictly opposed: the optimal-transport route needs the whole corpus, a maximum skill count, and pre-extracted features, so it cannot run online, and it is silent on the *typed* boundaries (replace vs. investigate) the predictive account delivers for free
