# T86 — Is a working memory held in persistent spiking, or in synaptic state that spiking only reads?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The task confound is removed: delayed-match-to-sample and spatial delayed-response run in the same animals, separating "holds information for later computation" from "withholds a known response".

## Position A

**Persistent activity.** Sustained delay-period spiking is the classic prefrontal correlate, and a recurrent network settling into one attractor per item reproduces it, recovers from distractors by basin recapture, and is what every attractor account of maintenance in the wiki assumes ([[wiki/concepts/attractor-dynamics.md]], [[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]])

## Position B

**Synaptic state.** In macaque dorsolateral/ventrolateral prefrontal cortex during distracted delayed-match-to-sample, sample identity is at chance in the population after ~1 s of a 4 s delay while the animal performs at ~99% — and the activity has *not* returned to baseline, it has returned to a state carrying no sample information. Trained networks with short-term plasticity reproduce exactly that profile (spike decoding falls, synaptic-weight decoding stays high for the whole delay) and are more brain-like than fixed-synapse networks across the whole hyperparameter sweep (Kozachkov et al. 2022, [[wiki/entities/stsp-working-memory-rnn.md]]; the hippocampal existence proof is [[wiki/entities/stp-flickering-cann.md]])

## Where it bites

Decides what fast **M** is made of, and what it costs: under A maintenance is continuous spiking and the item is always readable; under B maintenance is free, the item is invisible until probed, and "hold" and "use" are separate operations with separate energy budgets ([[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]). It also decides which failure mode to design against — basin escape versus decay of `τ_f`

## Status

`LEANING B` for delayed-match-to-sample, **with an explicit task confound the paper names**: spatial delayed-response tasks report much higher delay spiking, plausibly because they require withholding a *known* motor response rather than holding information for later computation. So the two positions may be reporting two different tasks, and the general claim rests on one animal. Note the positions are not exclusive — Masse et al. and Lundqvist et al. 2018 report spiking rising when a memory is manipulated, which is A's mechanism operating as B's read-out — and Lundqvist et al. add that even the *expression* is discrete, since on single trials the informative spiking occurs inside brief gamma bursts whose timing is only weakly correlated across trials, so the trial-averaged sustained signal is an averaging artefact. Related to T2 (is the fast level a separate store or recurrent activity?), one level down: this row asks the same question inside a single circuit; T87 is its sibling — the other way to deny persistent spiking while keeping the memory in activity **Adjacent row: T266** takes this row's position B as given and asks the next question — whether a memory held in synaptic state can be *operated on* or only stored. If T266 answers "only stored", position B here cannot be the whole story for any task requiring manipulation.
