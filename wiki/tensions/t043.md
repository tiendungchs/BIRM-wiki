# T43 — Is the CA3/CA1 difference in place-field shift a fixed property of their learning rules?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Yes — different rules at different synapses.** CA3 recurrent synapses potentiate symmetrically in time while CA1 Schaffer collaterals follow classical asymmetric STDP; a two-layer model with that assignment reproduces the recorded dissociation (CA1 fields drift backwards over laps, CA3 fields much less) and fails to reproduce it if CA3 is given the asymmetric rule (Keck et al. 2025, modelling Dong, Madar & Sheffield 2021)

## Position B

**Not fixed — it is environment-dependent.** The CA3-stable/CA1-shifting dissociation holds in *familiar* environments; in novel ones both populations shift, more strongly in CA1 (reported in Keck et al. 2025). A static symmetric rule in CA3 cannot produce a shift that exists early and vanishes with familiarity

## Where it bites

Decides whether temporal symmetry of plasticity is a fixed architectural constant or a *gated* variable — if gated, the natural controller is neuromodulatory (acetylcholine/dopamine already switch potentiation regimes), which would make "how much geometry vs how much policy to store" an online decision the agent makes rather than a wiring choice the designer makes ([[wiki/concepts/successor-representation.md]], [[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]])
