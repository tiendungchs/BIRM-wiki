# T33 — Is CA1 a linear relay or a second recombination-and-completion stage?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A CA1 transfer curve is measured with the CA3 input recorded simultaneously — the discipline position A demands of everyone else, and which nobody has applied to CA1.

## Position A

**A relay.** Transfer-curve measurements put CA1 on the identity line — it neither separates nor completes — and human high-resolution fMRI agrees (Yassa & Stark 2011, [[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]]). **Weakened on inspection of the primary human source:** Bakker et al. 2008 gives CA1 a lure bias score of 0.59–0.83 with lure activity statistically indistinguishable from a true repeat, so it licenses only the negative half (CA1 does not separate); the identity-line claim is not in that data

## Position B

**A recombiner.** Competitive learning in CA1 reassembles the sub-parts of an episode that CA3 had to store separately, into an efficient retrieval cue for the cortical backprojection, and the CA3→CA1 pattern association generalises to the nearest CA1 pattern when CA3 completion was imperfect (Rolls 2013)

## Where it bites

Whether the fast store's read-out is a projection or a computation. Under B there is a *second* completion stage after the attractor, which changes what a machine must implement between memory and decoder, and it is also where the wiki's `C^HBP` fan-out constraint starts

## Status

`LIVE` — the two are not strictly incompatible (a stage can be linear in similarity while recombining components), and nobody has measured a CA1 transfer curve with the CA3 input recorded, which is exactly the discipline position A demands of everyone else. **There is a third position, and it is neither.** CA1's job is to hold a **sparse, invertible** mapping of the entorhinal pattern so that the cortical activity present at encoding can be regenerated from it — a learned codec. The argument is an interference argument rather than a transfer-curve one: a direct CA3→entorhinal readout would force a *dense* entorhinal population to rapidly learn each novel pattern-separated CA3 code, re-importing exactly the interference the dentate gyrus removed, and CA1's relative sparseness is what bounds it. Under this reading CA1 sits wherever invertibility requires, the measurement that discriminates it is whether the cortical pattern can be reconstructed from CA1 (not whether CA1's own codes are similar), and the live question becomes whether the code is componential enough to invert *novel* inputs ([[wiki/concepts/compositionality.md]]) — which every CLS model installs by pretraining (McClelland & Goddard 1997; O'Reilly et al. 2011)

**The third position, with the mechanism named** ([[wiki/concepts/hippocampal-indexing-theory.md]], O'Reilly & Rudy 2001 via Teyler & Rudy 2007). The "learned codec" reading above is stated there as a specific circuit: entorhinal cortex projects *directly* to CA1 as well as through dentate/CA3, so CA1 carries a second, less separated representation of the same input; CA3↔CA1 is strengthened by coactivity; and **CA1→entorhinal** is strengthened onto exactly the entorhinal cells the experience is driving. CA1 is therefore neither relaying nor recombining but storing the **return address** — the inverse of the encoder, written in the same event as the item. This sharpens the discriminating measurement the third position already implies: not whether CA1's codes are similar to CA3's, but whether CA1→entorhinal synaptic change is detectable at encoding and whether its disruption costs retrieval *specificity* while leaving CA3 completion intact.

**A fourth reading, anatomical and untested: CA1 as many independent sub-stores** (van Strien et al. 2009, [[wiki/entities/hippocampal-formation.md]]). CA1's recurrent loops are confined to one septotemporal level. Its direct entorhinal input is stream-typed by transverse position (medial → proximal, lateral → distal), and its CA3 input comes from adjacent levels only. The authors propose that CA1 is tiled into subdomains along both axes, each storing its own combination of inputs, which would help separation and completion. That makes CA1 a computing stage, so it sits against Position A, but the computation is local and parallel rather than the global recombination of Position B. The evidence is a conference abstract (McNaughton et al. 2008: proximal CA1 cells more spatially modulated than distal ones). The discriminating measurement is recording along both axes in behaving animals, which the source names as future work. `Closes when` not met.

**A CA1 output recorded alongside its CA3 input, under a graded perturbation — the closest the row has to its `Closes when`, and it falls short** (Lee et al. 2004, via Knierim & Neunuebel 2016, [[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]]). Under local-global cue conflict at 45–180°, distal CA3 completes to the local frame while the proximal CA1 recorded with it **splits**. About as many fields follow the local cues as the global ones, and some fields split in two, half following each frame. Its other afferent, medial entorhinal cortex, follows the global frame (in other animals, Neunuebel et al. 2013). Reading for the positions. (i) It **favours A** qualitatively: the output reproduces both conflicting inputs, which is what a linear stage does. (ii) It **counts against B's clean-up role**: CA3 completion was good and CA1 still did not adopt the completed state, so nothing here completes downstream of the attractor. (iii) It is **neutral on the codec and sub-store readings**, and it fits the comparator reading the review proposes without demonstrating. Why `Closes when` is not met: no Δinput–Δoutput curve was computed, and the entorhinal half of CA1's input was recorded in different animals. The authors say themselves that a precise curve "might" be linear.

**Lesion and pharmacology evidence that CA1 is not CA3's relay, from its other input** (Kesner & Rolls 2015, [[wiki/entities/rolls-treves-hippocampal-model.md]]). Rat CA1 and CA3 lesions dissociate in both directions. CA3 alone is needed for 10 s spatial delays, within-day maze encoding and object–place learning. CA1 alone is needed for 5 min delays, next-day retrieval, object–trace–odour association and the temporal order of objects. Apomorphine infused into CA1 inhibits the direct entorhinal input and spares the Schaffer input, and it reproduces the CA1-lesion pattern exactly. So CA1's necessary contributions are carried by its **direct entorhinal input**, independent of CA3. This counts against Position A in its strong form (CA1 relays the CA3 signal). It does not support Position B's recombination, which is about the CA3 input. It fits the codec and sub-store readings, and adds a fourth job: an intermediate-term store (5 min–24 h) with its own time course. The same source restates B in full (component ensembles in CA3, conjunctive cells in CA1) with a prediction never tested: proportionally more object-or-place cells in CA3 and more object × place cells in CA1. No transfer curve is measured, so `Closes when` is not met.

**A fifth reading, and the earliest: CA1 as decoder plus match/mismatch comparator** (Lisman 1999, `raw/lisman-1999-relating-hippocampal-circuitry-to-function.md`, theory, [[wiki/entities/hippocampal-formation.md]]). Two jobs. (i) **Decoder.** CA1 converts the hippocampal code back into one cortex can read. The argument is the fanning rule: feedforward projections between regions sharing a code are point-to-point (dentate → CA3, CA1 → cortex), and those between regions with different codes fan out (cortex → dentate, cortex → CA3, CA3 → CA1). (ii) **Comparator.** CA1 compares CA3's prediction (Schaffer) with the current input (direct entorhinal), and a mismatch triggers encoding and attention. Against the positions. It shares the codec reading's decoding job but gives no write-time return address. It is the comparator the Knierim & Neunuebel data are said to fit without demonstrating. It counts against A, which has CA1 compute nothing. The source's own evidence is weak on both jobs. The code change from CA3 to CA1 is anatomical only (stream resegregation, Naber & Witter 1998), and same-size CA3 and CA1 place fields count against it. The comparator has novelty and oddball responses in the hippocampal region but no cellular account, and the source does not know whether CA1 would compute match or mismatch. Its discriminating prediction is that cutting either input abolishes the comparison. The dopamine result it cites (Otmakhova & Lisman 1999: dopamine inhibits the direct input and spares the Schaffer input) is the same manipulation as the apomorphine infusions in Kesner & Rolls 2015 (above), which scored retrieval, not a comparison. `Closes when` not met.
