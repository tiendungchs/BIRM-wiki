# T157 — When a linear probe fails, is the representation non-linear or is the label basis wrong?

**Status:** `LEANING`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**Non-linear.** The standard inference and the one the emergent-world-model literature made: a linear probe for Othello board squares {Black, White, Empty} saturates at ~75% across six layers while a 2-layer MLP probe reaches 98.7%, so the world model is encoded non-linearly and only a non-linear decoder can read it (Li et al. 2022)

## Position B

**Neither — "linear" is a property of the pair (representation, feature vocabulary).** Relabelling the *same activations* relative to whoever moves — {Mine, Yours, Empty} — puts a **linear** probe at 99.6%, above the MLP at every layer; the MLP had been spending capacity re-deriving a parity transform. Corroborated at circuit level: first-layer heads specialise by timestep parity. And the point generalises — the source's own example is a physics model carrying `√distance`, where whether distance is "non-linearly represented" depends on whether `d²` is admitted as a feature (Nanda et al. 2023)

## Where it bites

Decides what a null probe means, which is most of what an interpretability sweep produces. If B holds, every reported "non-linear representation" is unfalsified rather than established, and the missing procedure is a *search over label bases* — which no methodology in [[wiki/concepts/representation-probing.md]] contains. It also changes the practical advice: guess the frame the model's loss is invariant under (Othello legality is colour-symmetric), not the frame a human describing the domain would use

## Status

`LEANING B` for the Othello case, where B is decisive; `LIVE` in general, because B's own argument makes the question undecidable without a stated feature vocabulary, and nobody has a way to enumerate one ([[wiki/concepts/linear-representation-hypothesis.md]], [[wiki/entities/othellogpt.md]])
