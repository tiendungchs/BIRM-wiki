# T273 — What drives an internally generated content switch — the content-coding populations, or a content-free global state?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** A causal manipulation of the content-free burst regime is run — precedence rules out consequence but not a common upstream cause, and neither reading is currently causal.

## Position A

**The content populations.** Rivalry alternation is competition plus adaptation plus noise *inside* the representation layer: the incumbent's own adaptation weakens it until a noisy challenger wins the basin, which produces a gamma-distributed dwell time with no separate controller and no admission event ([[wiki/concepts/attractor-dynamics.md]]). The wiki's unit-resolution recording is consistent with it — feature-selective prefrontal units follow the percept at 95% single-trial decoding and the strong feedforward transient present at externally imposed switches is *absent* at spontaneous ones, i.e. nothing arrives from outside to trigger the flip (Kapoor et al. 2022)

## Position B

**A content-free state, and it moves first.** On the same arrays at the perisynaptic level, a 1–9 Hz burst regime suppresses the 20–40 Hz beta regime that synchronises the dominant ensemble, peaking at −60 ± 222 ms with its last burst at −198 ± 133 ms — *earlier than the end of the incumbent's own dominance period* (−97.4 ± 140 ms, `p < 10⁻⁶⁷`) — while the two ensembles' firing rates cross over only at +209 ± 295 ms, the state leading the content in 86–89% of transitions. The source's flat statement: the selective neurons *"only seem to report the active percept"* (Dwarakanath et al. 2023, [[wiki/concepts/cortical-state-bistability.md]])

## Where it bites

Whether an architecture needs a **second layer**. Under A one layer suffices: content units with adaptation and mutual inhibition, and any global signal is a downstream consequence — which is what every wiki architecture already is. Under B there is a content layer *and* a content-free permission layer with its own free-running dynamics, and the two are separately schedulable: the system can decide *that* it will switch before it has decided *to what*. It also changes where a controller intervenes — under A you bias the competitors, under B you gate the release — and it makes a step boundary predictable ~200 ms in advance from a signal carrying no content

## Status

`LIVE` — both readings come from one lab, one preparation and largely one dataset, and neither is causal. Precedence by ~300 ms rules out "the state is a consequence of the content flip" but not a common upstream cause, and B's own antagonism statistic is weak per event (`r = −0.08`, highly significant over ~10⁴ events). A reconciliation is available and untested: the low-frequency event *is* the adaptation-driven instability of A, read out at the field level one layer up, in which case the "permission layer" is a measurement of the content layer rather than a separate object. The discriminating experiment is unrun and is named on both pages — drive the 1–9 Hz rhythm directly and ask whether switch rate rises with the content competition untouched
