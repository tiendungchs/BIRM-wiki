# T118 — What does a superficial pyramidal spike mean — a candidate interpretation, or a residual?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** Superficial pyramidal spiking is measured against a manipulation that changes what the level above predicts without changing the input — a residual must vanish, a candidate interpretation need not.

## Position A

**A candidate interpretation.** The superficial layers are where the column explores possible readings of its input: L2/3 pyramids receive feedforward, lateral-patch and deep feedback input, compete through perisomatic inhibition in an iterated soft winner-take-all, and what ascends to the next area is the *selected* interpretation, with the deep layers committing it to output (Douglas & Martin 2004)

## Position B

**A residual.** The same cells encode precision-weighted prediction error on hidden causes and nothing else; expectations live on supragranular *interneurons* and on deep pyramids, inhibition *encodes* the hidden-state variables rather than arbitrating, and what ascends is only what the level above failed to predict (Bastos et al. 2012; Friston & Kiebel 2009)

## Where it bites

Everything read off superficial-layer recordings, and the design of any laminar-inspired module. Under A the forward channel carries content and the receiver gets a hypothesis; under B it carries only what is unexplained and the receiver must already hold the model. The two also disagree on inhibition's job — selection versus representation — so they predict opposite effects of silencing supragranular interneurons: loss of competition (A) versus loss of a state variable (B). Both readings fit the same wiring diagram, which is why the anatomy alone cannot decide

## Status

`LIVE`

**Keller & Mrsic-Flogel 2018 complicate Position B rather than support it.** They agree the superficial spike is a residual and deny it is *one* residual: L2/3 holds two oppositely wired error populations, and the interneurons Position B needs as *encoders of hidden states* are instead relays of the bottom-up drive a comparator subtracts ([[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]], [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] **T341**). So the closing test above — a manipulation that changes the prediction without changing the input — must be read per sign: a positive-error cell should fall silent, a negative-error cell should *increase*. A protocol that pools L2/3 pyramids will see the two cancel and report no effect, which is indistinguishable from Position A.

**The pooling objection is now answerable, and the sign types are visible without a tag.** Whole-cell recording in mouse V1 L2/3 measures each cell's sign class directly — as the sign of corr(`V_m`, visual flow) against corr(`V_m`, locomotion) — and finds the two classes intermixed within one craniotomy, with 78% of cells showing a subthreshold mismatch response against 10% spiking (Jordan & Keller 2020, [[wiki/concepts/prediction-error-neurons.md]]). So the closing test can be run per sign after all, provided the instrument sees membrane potential: the sign label is available *before* the prediction manipulation, from intrinsic properties that predict 77% of the mismatch-response variance. It also sharpens what Position A owes: under A the superficial spike is a selected interpretation, but the subthreshold variable it is selected from is anticorrelated with visual drive in most cells, which is a residual's signature and not a candidate's.
