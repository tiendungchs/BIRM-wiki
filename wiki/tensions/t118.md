# T118 — What does a superficial pyramidal spike mean — a candidate interpretation, or a residual?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Position A

**A candidate interpretation.** The superficial layers are where the column explores possible readings of its input: L2/3 pyramids receive feedforward, lateral-patch and deep feedback input, compete through perisomatic inhibition in an iterated soft winner-take-all, and what ascends to the next area is the *selected* interpretation, with the deep layers committing it to output (Douglas & Martin 2004)

## Position B

**A residual.** The same cells encode precision-weighted prediction error on hidden causes and nothing else; expectations live on supragranular *interneurons* and on deep pyramids, inhibition *encodes* the hidden-state variables rather than arbitrating, and what ascends is only what the level above failed to predict (Bastos et al. 2012; Friston & Kiebel 2009)

## Where it bites

Everything read off superficial-layer recordings, and the design of any laminar-inspired module. Under A the forward channel carries content and the receiver gets a hypothesis; under B it carries only what is unexplained and the receiver must already hold the model. The two also disagree on inhibition's job — selection versus representation — so they predict opposite effects of silencing supragranular interneurons: loss of competition (A) versus loss of a state variable (B). Both readings fit the same wiring diagram, which is why the anatomy alone cannot decide
