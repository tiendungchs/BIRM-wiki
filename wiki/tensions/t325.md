# T325 — Does capacity make a system's stated confidence worse or better?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L4`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** One model family is swept over ≥3 sizes with training data and post-training held fixed, evaluated on one benchmark with a confidence logged per item, and calibration is reported against accuracy — either as ECE at a stated bin count on a closed-response slice, or as the calibration term `C` of `PS = O + C − R` so the performance confound is differenced out.

## Position A

**Worse — monotonically, and the mechanism is named.** Guo et al. 2017 ([[wiki/concepts/confidence-calibration.md]]): on CIFAR-100, ECE grows substantially with ResNet depth (at fixed 64 filters/layer) and with width (at fixed 14 layers), while error falls; a 5-layer LeNet at 44.9% error is well calibrated and a 110-layer ResNet at 27.8% error is not. Batch Normalization worsens ECE at slightly *improved* accuracy, at every learning rate tried. The proposed mechanism is measured rather than assumed: once the training set is fit, NLL is further minimised by sharpening the softmax, so pre-calibration output entropy falls and the optimal temperature rises through training while test error keeps improving (29% → 27% across the region where test NLL is rising). Under this account overconfidence is a *consequence* of the capability-increasing part of the recipe.

## Position B

**Better — the two most accurate frontier models are the two best calibrated.** [[wiki/entities/hle.md]]: over 2,500 items with a confidence required in every answer, RMS calibration error runs 73–89%, and the two lowest values (DeepSeek-R1 at 73, o3-mini at 80) belong to the two highest-accuracy models, both reasoning models. The page offers two readings and cannot separate them: (i) a self-produced reasoning trace supplies evidence about its own reliability, or (ii) the ordering is mechanical, since a model at 13.4% accuracy has more correct answers for its confident predictions to land on.

## Where it bites

Whether an explicit calibrator is a permanent architectural component or a transitional patch — i.e. whether `G89`'s external corrector has to scale with the system or gets absorbed by it. Under **A** every capability increase buys a confidence defect that must be paid for separately, and the payment is cheap but must keep being made (one refitted scalar per model, per distribution). Under **B** the defect shrinks on its own and the engineering effort belongs elsewhere. The evidence is asymmetric in design and in regime: **A** is a controlled sweep on closed-set classification with clean labels, one factor at a time; **B** is an observational comparison across models differing in data, post-training and inference procedure, on open-ended generation where no normalised posterior exists and the confidence is a stated number rather than a softmax. **B**'s own page names reading (ii) as the `F15` performance confound, which would predict its ordering with no self-knowledge claim attached; and ~7–10 points of **B**'s column belongs to the benchmark rather than the models ([[wiki/entities/hle-verified.md]]). Neither side has been run in the other's regime, and the discriminating measurement is cheap: it is a capacity sweep, and every lab that trains a family already has the checkpoints.

## Status

`LIVE` — the two are not yet comparable enough to lean. A is better designed and confined to a regime (`P̂` a normalised softmax over a bounded label set) that B is not in; B is uncontrolled and has a named confound that alone explains it. Note that the two are not formally contradictory if what scales with capability is *resolution* while *bias* degrades — B measures a raw calibration error that Murphy's decomposition shows is contaminated by both — which is why the `Closes when` clause requires the differenced term rather than the raw one.
