# T383 — Is the egocentric↔allocentric conversion an operation localised to one region, or is "no isolated function" the correct description of that region?

**Status:** `LIVE`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]  
**Closes when:** The translation hypothesis is given an operational definition that distinguishes performing a transformation from co-representing its arguments, and a manipulation of retrosplenial cortex is shown to change a downstream frame-dependent code — or the null effect on entorhinal boundary coding is replicated under conditions where the model predicts it must appear.

## Position A

**One region, one operation.** Retrosplenial cortex translates between viewpoint-dependent (parietal, egocentric) and viewpoint-independent (medial-temporal, allocentric) representations by cancelling a rotational offset supplied as an input by the anterodorsal/postsubicular head-direction system, possibly buffering the representations while the conversion runs. The evidence is the anatomy (the only region with early sensory input *and* thalamic heading *and* reciprocal hippocampal/parietal connections), a lesion profile that spares landmark recognition and destroys landmark-to-heading conversion, dysgranular lesions leaving anterodorsal head-direction tuning drifting, and a deficit that only becomes large under cue conflict (Vann, Aggleton & Maguire 2009, [[wiki/entities/retrosplenial-cortex.md]])

## Position B

**No isolated function fits.** Retrosplenial anatomy and dynamics are consistent with roles in multiple sensorimotor and cognitive processes and with no single one; what the region supplies is conjunctive tuning across sensory, motor and spatial streams, from which two *non-exclusive* generalisations can be drawn — relating perspectives (of which egocentric↔allocentric is one special case among egocentric→egocentric, allocentric→allocentric and oblique shifts) and prediction with error correction. Three specific costs to A: retrosplenial inactivation has **little effect on boundary-anchored responses in medial entorhinal cortex**, the model's own downstream prediction; conjunctive egocentric–allocentric gain-field-like cells are found in many other structures, so the transformation is distributed and conjunctive coding here is not diagnostic; and "transformation" has no operational definition, so A is ill-posed rather than merely unsupported (Alexander, Place, Starrett, Chrastil & Nitz 2023)

## Where it bites

Whether a reasoning architecture gets a **converter module** — one block that owns the transform between two representational formats and nothing else, parameterised by an externally computed group element (`G39`'s shape) — or whether frame conversion is a property that has to be distributed across every module that holds a frame ([[wiki/concepts/distributed-reference-frames.md]]'s Prediction 4). The two differ in cost, in failure mode and in what an ablation would show: A predicts both representations survive a converter lesion and only the change of perspective is lost; B predicts no localised ablation produces that signature, which is close to what the entorhinal result reports. The row also carries a measurement problem the wiki generalises past this region: A cannot be falsified until *performing a transformation* is distinguished from *co-representing its arguments*, and the same objection applies to every verb the wiki assigns a module (`anchor`, `gate`, `bind`, `broadcast`)

## Status

`LIVE` — B is the later review with more data and holds the one failed entailment; A holds the one lesion signature (landmark recognition intact, heading derivation lost) that B never explains. Neither review is a study, and the two share most of their evidence base while differing on what it licenses, so the row cannot be settled by weighing citations — it needs the definitional repair named in `Closes when` first
