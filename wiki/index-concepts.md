**Coverage note.** Eight sources ingested. Hassabis et al. 2017 (a survey) built the skeleton, so most pages state a mechanism and its role in the framing without a quantitative result. Geirhos et al. 2020 added the first page carrying an evaluation constraint rather than a mechanism — and its consequence (the intended solution is not identifiable from data alone) now qualifies every other page. Schmidgall et al. 2023 added the *update-rule* layer — the two plasticity pages, plus the wiki's first entity page — and is the first source carrying quantitative comparisons *between* biologically-motivated methods rather than between biology and engineering. Revencu & Csibra 2023 added the *prior* layer: the first page saying what is in the meta-graph before any learning happens, and the first to raise composition-across-modules as a problem distinct from structure discovery. Hutter 2000 added the *ceiling* layer: the first page stating what an optimal learner can and cannot be guaranteed, and the wiki's first proof-backed limit (no optimality certificate exists for any agent whose actions shape its data). Spelke & Kinzler 2007 is the primary source behind the prior layer: it replaces the second-hand inventory with signature limits — the wiki's first *identification criterion* for a prior, and the first evidence that installed priors are defeasible. Butz 2016 added the *substrate* layer — the free-energy rival to the graph framing, stated in full — and with it the wiki's first mechanism for where discrete nodes come from at all. Lake et al. 2017 added the *model-building* layer — the wiki's first three-page ingest, plus its first architecture entity with a one-shot result — and with it the first two evaluation protocols that need no distribution shift (query richness, reward swap), the first quantitative human-vs-machine sample-efficiency table, and the first assignment of a *positive* role to model-free pattern recognition inside a model-building architecture. Known holes are tracked in [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]] and [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]].
