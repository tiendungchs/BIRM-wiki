# G67 — Nothing decides how many timescales a model needs, or where the boundaries between them go

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A model learns how many timescales it needs and where the band edges go, rather than being given `S` and the spectrum, and the learned allocation beats a hand-stratified bank at matched state. Timescale family with `G78` (design time) and `G94` (run time).

## Why it blocks the target

The wiki now has evidence that *allocating* memory horizon across a bank beats enlarging a single one — at fixed total state, stratifying the spectrum is worth 25 ListOps points where doubling the state is worth 4 ([[wiki/entities/ms-ssm.md]]) — which makes the allocation itself the design object. But every allocation in the wiki is authored: `S = 3` in every MS-SSM experiment, band supports fixed dyadic at `2^s·K`, eigenvalue intervals hand-partitioned into `S+2` equal blocks, and [[wiki/entities/simple-cycle-reservoir.md]]'s Def. 7 bank has one `λ` per block chosen by hand. So a model given data with four relevant timescales, or with two very unequal ones, has no way to notice. This is the temporal counterpart of G47 (nothing learns the topology of the manifold it represents on) and it is prior to G27: a segmentation detector cuts the stream at *one* grain, and nothing says which.

## Best current answer

None. The nearest candidates are all one step short: MS-SSM stratifies but does not learn `S` or the band edges; [[wiki/entities/s4.md]]'s `Δ` re-tunes every horizon by a common factor and so cannot change their *ratios*, and nothing learns it either; [[wiki/entities/ltc.md]] learns a continuous per-unit rate and therefore has no bank to allocate over, paying for it with G54. The cheapest formulation available: make the eigenvalue *measure* itself the learned object rather than the eigenvalues, and read the number of modes off it. **One existence proof that the quantity is readable off an operator rather than chosen**: for a population of neurons, the ensemble density evolves under a linear operator `Q` whose eigenvalues give both the number of modes worth keeping (those above the spectral gap — the rest dissipate) and their time constants (real parts) and ringing frequencies (imaginary parts) ([[wiki/concepts/mean-field-reduction.md]], Deco et al. 2008). It does not close the gap, because `Q` is *derived* from a hand-specified neuron model rather than estimated from data — but it shows the target object is a spectrum, which is what the eigenvalue-measure formulation above was guessing at
