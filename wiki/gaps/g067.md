# G67 — Nothing decides how many timescales a model needs, or where the boundaries between them go

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A model learns how many timescales it needs and where the band edges go, rather than being given `S` and the spectrum, and the learned allocation beats a hand-stratified bank at matched state. Timescale family with `G78` (design time) and `G94` (run time).

## Why it blocks the target

The wiki now has evidence that *allocating* memory horizon across a bank beats enlarging a single one — at fixed total state, stratifying the spectrum is worth 25 ListOps points where doubling the state is worth 4 ([[wiki/entities/ms-ssm.md]]) — which makes the allocation itself the design object. But every allocation in the wiki is authored: `S = 3` in every MS-SSM experiment, band supports fixed dyadic at `2^s·K`, eigenvalue intervals hand-partitioned into `S+2` equal blocks, and [[wiki/entities/simple-cycle-reservoir.md]]'s Def. 7 bank has one `λ` per block chosen by hand. So a model given data with four relevant timescales, or with two very unequal ones, has no way to notice. This is the temporal counterpart of G47 (nothing learns the topology of the manifold it represents on) and it is prior to G27: a segmentation detector cuts the stream at *one* grain, and nothing says which.

## Best current answer

None. The nearest candidates are all one step short: MS-SSM stratifies but does not learn `S` or the band edges; [[wiki/entities/s4.md]]'s `Δ` re-tunes every horizon by a common factor and so cannot change their *ratios*, and nothing learns it either; [[wiki/entities/ltc.md]] learns a continuous per-unit rate and therefore has no bank to allocate over, paying for it with G54. The cheapest formulation available: make the eigenvalue *measure* itself the learned object rather than the eigenvalues, and read the number of modes off it. **One existence proof that the quantity is readable off an operator rather than chosen**: for a population of neurons, the ensemble density evolves under a linear operator `Q` whose eigenvalues give both the number of modes worth keeping (those above the spectral gap — the rest dissipate) and their time constants (real parts) and ringing frequencies (imaginary parts) ([[wiki/concepts/mean-field-reduction.md]], Deco et al. 2008). It does not close the gap, because `Q` is *derived* from a hand-specified neuron model rather than estimated from data — but it shows the target object is a spectrum, which is what the eigenvalue-measure formulation above was guessing at

**Update (Orvieto et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/lru.md]]) — the gap gets a second axis, and it is the one nothing here has looked at.** LRU makes the eigenvalue measure fully explicit: `λ_j = exp(−ν_j + iθ_j)` with `ν` drawn to place `|λ|` in `[r_min, r_max]` and `θ` drawn from `[0, θ_max]`. The two are independent, and they control different things — `ν` sets **how long** the past survives, `θ` sets **what** is stored, because a channel whose kernel oscillates many times over the window is a local average of an oscillation pattern rather than a global summary. At `L = 16k`, a uniform phase draw (`θ ~ U[0,2π]`) makes the model converge to a minimiser at chance test accuracy and never escape; cutting the range to `[0, π/10]` takes Path-X to 94.2%. Every allocation this row complains about — MS-SSM's `S+2` disjoint bands, SCR's Def. 7 one-`λ`-per-block, S4's common `Δ` — partitions **magnitude only**. So the gap is wider than stated: nothing learns the number of timescales, nothing learns the band edges, *and* nothing allocates over the phase axis at all, despite one hand-set phase range being worth the difference between chance and 94.2% on the longest task available. The target object named above ("make the eigenvalue measure itself the learned object") is confirmed as the right formulation and is now concrete — it is a measure on the unit **disc**, not on an interval.

**Update (Chaudhuri et al. 2015, [[wiki/concepts/timescale-hierarchy.md]]) — an existence proof that the allocation can be *generated* rather than authored.** Scale every node's recurrent excitatory gain by `(1 + η h_i)`, with `η = 0.68` a single global scalar and `h_i` a structurally measured depth coordinate (fitted so that `h_i − h_j` predicts the supragranular fraction of projection `j → i`), drop that gradient onto the weighted directed macaque connectome, and time constants from tens of milliseconds to seconds fall out of the coupling matrix's eigenvalues. The bands are not chosen: they are the spectrum, and the corresponding eigenvectors are **spatially localised** — fast modes concentrated on sensory areas, slow modes on frontal ones — so each band is also an addressable module, which no bank in this row has. So the parameterisation of the target object shrinks from `S` band edges (or a measure on the disc) to *one scalar plus a coordinate per module*. Two ablations say the graph is as load-bearing as the gradient: remove the gradient and the spread collapses with no relation to depth surviving; scramble long-range **weights** while preserving topology and the gradient, and the separation is strongly attenuated. The row stays `OPEN` because nothing here is learned — `η` is fitted once and `h_i` comes from anatomy — but it relocates what has to be learned: not a spectrum, a **depth field over modules**, which is a far smaller object than the one this row has been asking for.

**Update (Murray et al. 2014, [[wiki/concepts/intrinsic-timescale-measurement.md]]) — the target spectrum is measured, and it is far narrower than every bank in this row assumes.** Across-trial spike-count autocorrelation over seven macaque areas, six datasets, 26 animals: the *intrinsic* constants span **`50–350 ms` — a factor of ~7 across the entire visual-prefrontal hierarchy**, ordered against laminar anatomy at `r_s = 0.97`, with mean firing rate excluded as a confound. That range fits inside a single one of [[wiki/entities/ms-ssm.md]]'s dyadic `2^s·K` bands. Yet the functional horizons this ordering predicts — decay of reward memory traces in the same areas — are **`5–10 s`, an order of magnitude longer, in the same ranking**. So the row's implicit target has been wrong in kind: what has to be provisioned is not a wide measure over the disc but a **narrow gradient plus a coupling structure that amplifies it**, which is exactly the object the Chaudhuri update above relocated the answer to. Two further specifications the row can now use: (i) the number of bands is not what cortex provisions — the fit is a *single* exponential per area plus an **offset** absorbing everything slower than the window, so the biological parameterisation is two numbers per module, `(τ, B)`, not `S` band edges; (ii) `τ` and `B` are **positively correlated** across areas (`P = 0.004`, and against the fitting procedure's own bias in 11 of 16 fits), which is the signature a gradient-generated spectrum predicts and a bank of independently-chosen decay constants does not — a one-fit discriminator between the two provisioning stories, runnable on any trained model. The row stays `OPEN`: still nothing learns the gradient, and the measurement is a population statistic that may not license a per-unit `τ` at all ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T323).
