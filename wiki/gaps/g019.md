# G19 — No local rule is selective about what it writes

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A local, online rule writes on structural coactivity and not on shortcut coactivity, with a gate whose selectivity is learned rather than supplied, measured by the shortcut rate in the resulting fast store.

## Why it blocks the target

Every rule that can write during deployment ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]) is a coactivity/covariance detector. It fires on shortcut-driven coactivity exactly as readily as on structural coactivity, so a fast **M** written by local plasticity accumulates spurious edges at the *instance* level — the same failure as G6, now inside the mechanism the wiki wants for online binding. Metaplasticity gates *how much* a synapse may change, never *whether the change is warranted*. The same is true of the one *measured* interaction between successive updates: first-spike suppression bounds write **volume** per unit time — a per-neuron efficacy recovering over 34–75 ms, so all but the leading spike of a train is discounted (Froemke & Dan 2002, [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T329) — which rate-limits shortcut writes and structural writes alike. Stated in the specification vocabulary: a rule is *learning* only if its update field has a positive projection onto some objective's gradient, and no local rule here names the objective it improves — so "unselective" may be the milder diagnosis and "orthogonal" the true one. **One configuration now names it, and relocates the question**: STDP with Poisson inputs and a stochastic winner-take-all circuit behind it is approximate online expectation-maximization on a multinomial mixture — the spike is a posterior sample (E-step), the STDP application is the M-step — so the ascended quantity is the data log-likelihood under a latent-cause model rather than retained variance (Nessler et al. 2009, 2013 via Tavanaei et al. 2019). The kernel is unchanged; the **inhibitory circuit** is what supplies the objective, which means "what does this local rule optimize" is not well-posed at the synapse. It does not close the row — a mixture has causes and no edges among them, and likelihood ascent under a mixture is exactly as happy to model a shortcut as a structural feature

## Best current answer

Meta-optimizing the rule so the outer loop's task distribution filters what the rule writes on — i.e. importing the multi-environment identifiability argument into the update rule. Named here, not built. **Biology supplies two partial selectivity mechanisms, at opposite ends of the timescale.** (i) Behavioral timescale synaptic plasticity writes only when a dendritic plateau arrives on a separate anatomical pathway, so *whether to allocate* is a licensing signal rather than a property of the coactivity — but what generates the plateau at the right moment is unknown, which relocates the selection problem rather than solving it. (ii) Offline replay culls: a stimulus that was represented online is actively suppressed from consolidation if it does not recur, and **the substrate is no longer a prediction** — symmetric spike-timing LTP at `I→E` synapses is necessary and sufficient for the suppression in a biophysical CA3 model, and pairing optogenetic stimulation with a randomly presented cue *implants* a suppressed representation in awake mice (Liao et al. 2024, [[wiki/entities/inhibitory-replay-filter.md]]). This is the row's most direct partial answer, because the selectivity is produced by **a second local write rule of opposite sign** rather than by a gate on the first: inhibition accumulates in proportion to the across-episode variance of a representation's co-active partners, so `W − αW^I` keeps edges by conditional probability rather than by count. What it does *not* close is the shortcut case — a shortcut whose co-active partners recur has low variance and therefore attracts no inhibition, the same residual [[wiki/concepts/recall-gated-consolidation.md]] leaves. So selectivity is real in biology and is implemented *outside* the excitatory write rule — once as a gate on it, once as a filter after it (Liao & Losonczy 2024). **A third, purely engineering and older than both** (Albus 1971 via Kanerva 1993, [[wiki/entities/sparse-distributed-memory.md]]): read before you write, and update only if the stored answer is already wrong — `ΔC = g(p̂_u − s_u)/K` applied to the active locations only when `|p̂_u − s_u|` exceeds tolerance. Selectivity here comes from the store's own read-out rather than from a separate licensing pathway, so it needs no plateau, no replay pass and no outer loop; what it does need is a target `p̂_u`, which is exactly what an unsupervised online writer does not have. The transferable half is the *gating quantity*: the confidence margin `|s_u|` of a read is available without labels, so "is this write redundant" and "is this region unclaimed" are answerable even when "is this write correct" is not. **A fourth, and the only one whose two signals are both internally available** (Lisman et al. 2018, [[wiki/concepts/memory-allocation-excitability.md]]): selectivity applied to the *cell* rather than to the synapse, as a conjunction of dendritic evidence that plasticity occurred (ERK from a potentiated branch, diffusing to the soma) and somatic evidence that it mattered (action-potential-driven Ca²⁺ → CaMKIV). The argument for the conjunction is the transferable part — a spike alone can be produced by already-potentiated synapses, and branch-local LTP alone need not make the cell fire, so neither signal is a reliable learning indicator by itself. It selects *which units are eligible to write for the next few hours*, so it composes with any of the rules above rather than replacing one; what it does not supply is content selectivity, since the tag cannot tell a causal pairing from a coincidental one. **Content selectivity arrives once the decision leaves the synapse** (Lindsey & Litwin-Kumar 2024, [[wiki/concepts/recall-gated-consolidation.md]]): gating the write on the *population's* recall of the proposed update separates recurring patterns from one-off ones, which no synapse-local rule can do, and the gating quantity is a single broadcast scalar. The residual is sharp — it filters noise, not shortcuts, since a stably predictive spurious feature recurs and therefore passes. **A fifth, where the selectivity is a learned action rather than a filter** ([[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]): the write is gated by a policy trained by reinforcement, so *whether this content is worth storing* is answered by its delayed consequences instead of by its statistics — the only mechanism here that could in principle reject a stably predictive shortcut, since a shortcut that does not improve reward is not reinforced. The price is that it inherits the reward's own blind spots and needs random writes to have anything to evaluate. **A sixth entry that is a refusal, and it is the deployed one** ([[wiki/entities/neural-episodic-control.md]], Pritzel et al. 2017): write every experience, gate nothing, on the explicit grounds that *learning* when to write (as the differentiable neural computer does) is slow to train and costs more than the storage it saves — the store is grown to `5×10⁵` entries per action and selectivity is deferred to eviction (overwrite whatever was least recently retrieved as a neighbour). It is the row's null hypothesis stated as a design choice by people who tried the alternative, and it holds only while capacity is cheap and every write is a genuine experience rather than a coactivity coincidence
