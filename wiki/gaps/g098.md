# G98 — Nothing decides which knowledge belongs in weights and which belongs in an addressable store

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** The micro/macrofeature test is actually applied — held-out entity predictability against base rate — and used to route content between weights and an addressable store, with the split shown to beat either alone.

## Why it blocks the target

The wiki holds both families and no criterion. One side memorises into parameters ([[wiki/entities/transformer.md]] and every language model on these pages); the other allocates explicit slots ([[wiki/entities/differentiable-neural-computer.md]], [[wiki/entities/sparse-distributed-memory.md]], [[wiki/entities/vector-hash.md]], [[wiki/entities/cn-dpm.md]]), and the choice between them is made by architectural tradition rather than by a property of the content. Two costs follow. **(i) No compression is bought where none is available.** A mapping with no task-relevant macrofeatures is incompressible ([[wiki/concepts/prediction-compression-equivalence.md]]), so putting it in weights spends parameters to store a table at table cost, with the parameters additionally entangled with everything else the network does. **(ii) The interpretability bill is paid on the wrong component.** A memorised mapping in weights is provably not circuit-decomposable ([[wiki/concepts/memorisation-vs-generalisation.md]], `T285`), and because the residual stream carries it through the whole stack it contaminates the description of the reasoning machinery downstream — five MLP layers of Pythia 2.8B resisted every instrument the DeepMind team brought, and the argument says they always will

## Best current answer

**A criterion exists and nobody has applied it.** The micro/macrofeature test is computable before any architecture is chosen: measure how much better than base rate a held-out *entity* can be predicted from the training pairs. Content that scores at base rate has nothing for weights to generalise over and should be a table; content that scores above it should be learned. Partial answers exist on both flanks — [[wiki/concepts/complementary-learning-systems.md]] splits by *timescale* (fast episodic store, slow cortical statistics) and [[wiki/concepts/generalization-optimized-consolidation.md]] by *how much a trace will be reused* — but neither splits by whether the content is compressible at all, and both assume everything eventually migrates to weights
