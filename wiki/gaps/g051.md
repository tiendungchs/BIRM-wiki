# G51 — Nothing transfers a representation out of the control layer into the store that will run it automatically

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** Practice moves a representation out of the control layer into a store that runs it without the controller, with the *operation* named and the controller's removal shown not to cost the behaviour.

## Why it blocks the target

Every account of automaticity in the wiki assumes practice moves a behaviour from the controller to a direct pathway, and each one is stated as an endpoint rather than a mechanism: guided activation says the controller's role becomes "virtually nil" but is explicitly *not* explicit about how the representation reaches the posterior regions that store well-learned behaviour, and the temporal-organization model puts habitual actions in basal ganglia and premotor cortex without saying how they got there. Without the transfer step, controller-capacity release is an assumption, skill cost accounting has no unit, and a machine architecture that freezes a controller after training has no principled successor for what it was holding

## Best current answer

None. The nearest thing is an *address*: overtraining shifts control from prelimbic to infralimbic cortex ([[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]]), and novel-vs-overlearned tasks shift prefrontal activation from anterior to medial/posterior regions — both name a destination inside the control layer rather than outside it, and neither names the operation. The contrary evidence is equally unexplained: prefrontal neurons keep responding to known actions, so whatever transfer occurs does not empty the source
