# G51 — Nothing transfers a representation out of the control layer into the store that will run it automatically

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** Practice moves a representation out of the control layer into a store that runs it without the controller, with the *operation* named and the controller's removal shown not to cost the behaviour.

## Why it blocks the target

Every account of automaticity in the wiki assumes practice moves a behaviour from the controller to a direct pathway, and each one is stated as an endpoint rather than a mechanism: guided activation says the controller's role becomes "virtually nil" but is explicitly *not* explicit about how the representation reaches the posterior regions that store well-learned behaviour, and the temporal-organization model puts habitual actions in basal ganglia and premotor cortex without saying how they got there. Without the transfer step, controller-capacity release is an assumption, skill cost accounting has no unit, and a machine architecture that freezes a controller after training has no principled successor for what it was holding

## Best current answer

None. The nearest thing is an *address*: overtraining shifts control from prelimbic to infralimbic cortex ([[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]]), and novel-vs-overlearned tasks shift prefrontal activation from anterior to medial/posterior regions — both name a destination inside the control layer rather than outside it, and neither names the operation. The contrary evidence is equally unexplained: prefrontal neurons keep responding to known actions, so whatever transfer occurs does not empty the source

**A named transfer operation, in a restricted setting** (Greenstreet et al. 2025, [[wiki/concepts/action-prediction-error.md]]). Two striatal learners, mouse auditory two-alternative choice:

| Requirement | What the source supplies |
|---|---|
| The operation, named | Online behaviour cloning. The store's update is `W_tail += β·(a − p_a)·s·a`, where `a` is an efference copy of the action the controller took and `p_a` is the store's own low-passed prediction. The source's own weights relax to baseline when its reward error is small (`τ dW_actor/dt = −W_actor + 1`) |
| The store runs it without the source | In the model, control passes to the store and the actor decays. In mice, tail-of-striatum (TS) silencing has an effect only once choices are consistent, and grows into expertise |
| Removing the source does not cost the behaviour | Dorsomedial-striatum muscimol spares expert performance, and TS muscimol impairs it |
| Biological carrier of the operation | Tail-of-striatum dopamine carries `a − A(s)`, value-free and causally sufficient to bias state→action choice |

Why this is only partial: the source of the transferred policy is a reward-trained striatal actor, not the control layer this row is about. The task has 2 states × 2 actions. Removing the source is tested once, by DMS muscimol, and the source does not assign its actor to DMS in the text. **(brainstorm)** The operation needs nothing striatal: any controller that emits an efference copy, paired with any store trained on `a − p`, transfers by the same rule. The open part is whether prefrontal control emits the copy.
