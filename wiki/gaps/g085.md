# G85 — No architecture in the wiki reads its own network topology, and none treats it as a demand-graded control variable

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `arrangement`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An architecture reads its own topology and grades it against demand, moving along an integration↔segregation axis by its own control signal — with the direction of causation established, which fMRI cannot do.

## Why it blocks the target

The human brain moves along a one-dimensional integration↔segregation axis in ~10 s: modularity `Q` drops from 0.55 to 0.42 and global efficiency rises from 0.18 to 0.24 (Cohen's `d = 0.9` / `1.5`) at matched graph density, all 375 parcels shift on the *between*-module statistic and **none** on the within-module one, the position is graded monotonically by task difficulty, and it predicts evidence-accumulation rate (Shine et al. 2016, [[wiki/concepts/integration-segregation-balance.md]]). Every modular system in this wiki holds its routing dispersion fixed at a design-time constant — a mixture-of-experts top-`k`, a hand-specified inter-module wiring, an attention pattern trained to a stationary average — so a cheap query and a query that must combine two specialisations are routed identically. Two things are missing, not one: the **measurement** (no architecture here computes a participation coefficient over its own modules, so it cannot report where on the axis it currently sits) and the **actuator** (no architecture has a single scalar that raises cross-module coupling without touching module internals). Distinct from `G56`, which asks for a per-connection gain register: the controlled variable here is a *graph statistic of the whole system*, and the biological signature — `B` moves, `W` does not — says the actuator must be one broadcast gain on the inter-module fraction, not a learned per-edge gate

## Best current answer

Only the biology, and only as a correlate. The proposed carrier is ascending noradrenergic gain indexed by pupil diameter (`r = 0.241 ± 0.06`, `R² = 0.06`), and the direction of causation is unidentified — fMRI cannot separate integration enabling communication from integration being its by-product. On the measurement half the recipe is complete and cheap: Louvain per window on multiplication-of-temporal-derivatives coupling, then `B_i = 1 − Σ_s (κ_is/κ_i)²`, needing no weights, labels or task identity. On the set-point half, [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]]'s closed-loop template applies unchanged — an internal difficulty estimate such as [[wiki/concepts/evidence-accumulation.md]]'s conflict term `S(T)` sets the gain — but nobody has run it

**The actuator half now has a specification, though still no instance** ([[wiki/concepts/network-control-theory.md]], Bassett & Sporns 2017). Control theory states the missing object exactly: a driver set `B`, an input trajectory `u(t)`, a target state, and a scalar cost — the minimum control energy `E* = (x_T − A^T x_0)ᵀ W_c^{−1}(x_T − A^T x_0)` — with `W_c = Σ_τ A^τ BBᵀ(Aᵀ)^τ` the controllability Gramian. It also supplies a warning aimed squarely at this row's proposed carrier: the broadcast-neuromodulatory-gain candidate is a *distributed* control strategy, and in Wilson–Cowan simulations distributed control quiets a **random** structural graph while being less effective on a **small-world** one, where the input must instead be aimed at specific driver nodes. So the one-broadcast-scalar answer is the weaker option on precisely the topology the substrate has, and the row needs a *targeting* mechanism it does not currently ask for. Nothing in the wiki has computed `E*` on a trained network

**And the actuator half now has a *targeted* candidate, which is what the warning above demands** (Seguin, Sporns & Zalesky 2023, [[wiki/concepts/network-communication-models.md]]). The **biased random walk** parametrises exactly the axis this row wants: a single scalar `λ` controls how much global topological information biases each node's local transition probabilities, with `λ = 0` giving unbiased diffusion and `λ → ∞` converging to shortest-path routing. That is one knob, it slides the system continuously between segregated diffusion and selective routing, and — unlike a broadcast gain — it acts *per transition* rather than uniformly, so it is a targeting mechanism rather than a distributed one. It is also already present and untuned in the wiki's own architectures: `λ` is the attention temperature in a `Transformer` and the router's logit scale in a mixture of experts. The unrun experiment is to make one of them a function of an internal difficulty estimate and check whether the resulting position on the integration↔segregation axis tracks demand the way `B` does. The review's own synthesis supplies the expected answer's shape — neither extreme of the axis is what wins empirically
