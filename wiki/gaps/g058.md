# G58 — No control layer is instantiated by the existence of a choice at its own level

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A control level's population is instantiated and dissolved by the presence of a choice at that level, with the collapse driven by a statistic the system computes over its own recent output.

## Why it blocks the target

Every controller in the wiki is a permanent module: it runs each step, and what varies is its output or a gate on it. Biology varies the *population itself* — monkey posterior medial prefrontal neurons carry a task only while more than one response tactic is live, the population shrinking under single-tactic retraining (`p < 0.01`) and returning when both are restored, with the action still varying, accuracy at 99–100% and neighbouring areas unchanged. The engagement variable is therefore the entropy of that level's own output, not error, difficulty, conflict or novelty — all of which are equated here or run backwards. Without it, a `k`-level parallel stack ([[wiki/concepts/policy-abstraction-hierarchy.md]]) pays `O(k)` forever and no architecture can report which of its levels is currently doing anything

## Best current answer

A computable candidate exists and has never been run: gate level `k`'s cost on a running estimate of the entropy of level `k`'s output over recent trials, so the stack collapses toward `O(1)` as the task's structure is pinned down. Its two open ends are the timescale (biology takes ~2 weeks of retraining, i.e. a consolidated statistic rather than a per-trial register) and the confound that the same manipulation also permits habit formation
