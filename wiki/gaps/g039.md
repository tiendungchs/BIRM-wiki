# G39 — Nothing anchors a retrieved structure to the present situation

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Spec:** `D65`, `D143` ([[_brainstorm/birm-spec.md]] §11)  
**Closes when:** A retrieved non-spatial schema is oriented — the agent's current slot on it identified — by a stability ranking the model computes, generalising the boundary-over-feature rule outside space.

## Why it blocks the target

Retrieval (G37) delivers *which* stored structure applies; it does not deliver *where on it the agent currently is*. A schema with no orientation cannot be read: coordinates, heading, and which slot the current observation occupies are all undefined, so binding, planning and prediction have no origin. Navigation makes the split visible because it has two distinct failure modes — wrong map vs. right map misaligned — and the second is the one every schema mechanism in the wiki silently assumes away

## Best current answer

**Rich in the spatial case, empty outside it.** Biology anchors by *stability ranking*: after disorientation, enclosure geometry overrides featural cues (180° errors in a rectangle; place and head-direction realignment predicts the behaviour), boundaries rather than objects set displacement, and objects anchor only once learned to be spatially fixed. Retrosplenial complex carries the mechanism: heading in a *local* frame that generalises across geometrically similar subspaces, plus a global frame in Brodmann area 29/30 — with rodent *bidirectional* cells firing opposite ways in two oppositely-polarised halves of one environment (Jacob et al. 2017). Composing the two gives a reusable local estimate plus a small per-instance local→global transform. **The negative is the gap:** no cell has been reported coding the boundary of a concept or a social milieu, and the source states it is unclear what landmark, boundary or geometry mean outside space — so the one element of map-based navigation with no machine analogue is also the one with no biological template to import. **The operation now has a general shape, in one worked case:** anchoring is `argmax` over the symmetry group relating a stored frame to the present one, scored by the stored state's posterior predictive, computed *separately per candidate structure* (Sanders et al. 2020's reference-direction inference for cue-rotation experiments, where the group is rotation and the offset `φ_k` reproduces the observed place-field rotations of 175°, −2° and 22° across three protocols). This makes the per-instance transform a small explicit parameter against a large reusable code, exactly the shape the retrosplenial local/global composition suggested. **What is still missing is the group**: outside space nothing says what transformations relate a stored abstract structure to a present one, so the argmax has no domain to range over. **One abstract-domain data point on the *origin* half of the operation**: in Park et al. 2021 the grid-like code is carried only by vectors anchored at the entity whose perspective the instructions assigned, and *not* by the vector between the two options actually on screen — so displacement in an abstract space is measured from a task-assigned origin rather than from the salient stimulus, which is what an anchoring mechanism outside space would have to select **The `argmax`-over-a-group shape now has a machine implementation** ([[wiki/entities/gcq.md]]): the group is the torus's own translation group, the candidate offsets are the `K` codewords, and the score is the L2 fit of an entire initialisation observation sequence to the action-integrated trajectory starting at each. Two qualifications and one lesson. It is **batch anchoring** — all `n` observations vote once and the rollout never re-anchors, the opposite of the fly's discrete landmark reset and of TEM's retrieve-to-correct loop. And it is available *because the topology was installed*: installing a manifold installs its symmetry group, which is exactly the object this row says is missing outside space. So the gap is not "no anchoring algorithm" — the algorithm is a least-squares fit over group elements — it is that abstract structures arrive without a group to range over (Peng et al. 2025).

 **One case where the anchor is free, and it confirms the diagnosis.** In the A24b/M2 → V1 projection the retrieved structure is a learned action→visual-flow map, and it is anchored to the present situation by *wiring*: the projection is topographically ordered, so a prediction arrives at the retinotopic sector it applies to and the argmax over the group is performed once, at development time, by the axon. The learning result makes the point sharper — inverting the visuomotor coupling reverses which movement drives a given sector, i.e. the **contents** are rewritten while the anchor is not, because the group (retinotopy) was installed in the substrate (Leinweber et al. 2017, [[wiki/entities/a24b-m2-v1-projection.md]]). Same lesson as GCQ from the opposite direction: anchoring is cheap exactly where a topology has been installed, and the gap is the absence of an installed group for abstract structures.

**And one case where the anchor is half-free, which sharpens the diagnosis further.** In the M2 → auditory-cortex filter the group is again installed in the substrate — tonotopy — but unlike retinotopy in the visual case, the *point* on the axis is not given by the wiring: which frequency gets suppressed is learned over 5–9 days, by aligning a long-range projection with the tuning of the interneurons that already tile the axis ([[wiki/entities/m2-auditory-cortex-filter.md]], Schneider et al. 2018). So installing a topology gives you the group to range over, but not the argmax; here the argmax was solved by slow synaptic alignment against a population whose receptive fields *are* the candidate offsets. The transferable shape: an anchoring mechanism needs both an installed group **and** a population that enumerates its elements, and abstract structures in the wiki have neither.

**And one case where the anchor is a *past event* rather than a cue, which is the cheapest version yet.** In a hairpin maze the structural code is re-zeroed at each compartment boundary and every position inside a compartment is coded as displacement from the **preceding** turning point (Derdikman et al. 2009, [[wiki/concepts/path-integration.md]], `T347`). Read as an answer to this row: the orientation problem is dissolved rather than solved — the agent never identifies where it is on a global map, it only knows how far it has come since the last cut, and the group to range over shrinks from "all poses of a stored frame" to "nothing, the origin is wherever the last boundary was". The price is exactly the wiki's de-aliasing requirement: geometrically identical segments then receive identical codes, and CA3/CA1 repeat with them. The transferable shape for an abstract structure: **if you can detect segment boundaries, you get anchoring for free and lose global identity** — which is a trade a reasoning model may want to make deliberately, since a schema retrieved and read as "step `k` since entry" needs no pose estimate at all. What it still does not supply is the boundary detector outside space ([[wiki/concepts/event-segmentation.md]]).

**And the one case where both halves are already installed — which is the closest thing this row has to a worked template.** Object-vector cells in medial entorhinal cortex code allocentric (distance, direction) from a discrete object (Høydal et al. 2019, [[wiki/concepts/vector-coding.md]]). Both ingredients this row has been assembling are present at once: the **group is installed** (allocentric direction × distance, held rigid across rooms — pairwise orientation offsets preserved against other object-vector cells, head-direction cells *and* grid cells, `P = 2.6 × 10⁻⁶`), and a **population enumerates its elements** (162/1,100 cells tiling the full azimuth and a wide distance range). The consequence is that the anchoring cost collapses to selecting an **origin**: the cells are sharply tuned on the *first* exposure to a novel object in a novel room, with the distance metric preserved, so nothing about the frame has to be learned per instance. The residual is exactly the identity half: because the code generalises over object identity (51/52 object–cell pairs, near-identical vectors), a reader given only this population cannot recover *which* referent it is anchored to — orientation without retrieval, the mirror image of `G37`. And the boundary case sharpens the "boundaries rather than objects set displacement" clause above: border-cell firing turns out to track **path obstruction**, not distance from a surface (a wall suspended 15 cm above the floor evokes no border field; lowered, it does), whereas object-vector fields are indifferent to obstruction (32/32 cells fire to a suspended object) — so the two anchor classes are separated by whether the referent constrains action, not by what it is.
