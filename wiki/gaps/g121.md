# G121 — Devaluation-insensitive responding is the wiki's definition of a habit, and it does not identify one

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** One design separates persistence-after-devaluation produced by stimulus–response strength from persistence produced by cue-triggered motivational gain, in the same animals. The minimal version crosses **devaluation** (devalued / not) with **cue presence** at test (the discrete predictive cue present / absent) and with a **gain manipulation** (mesolimbic amplification or its blockade): an S–R account predicts persistence that is cue-independent and unmoved by the gain manipulation; a sensitized-'wanting' account predicts persistence that appears when the cue is present and scales with the gain manipulation. Alternatively, the confound is shown not to apply to a stated class of habitisation claims, with the class stated.

## Why it blocks the target

The wiki's habitual/goal-directed division is read off one assay. [[wiki/entities/basal-ganglia.md]] (Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005) identifies the cache system as dorsolateral striatum on the evidence that lesions or dopamine depletion there "keep overtrained lever-pressing devaluation-sensitive — the animal never becomes habitual"; the same assay underwrites the two-controller arbitration on [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]] and the devaluation rows on [[wiki/entities/amygdala.md]] and [[wiki/concepts/valuation-system-decomposition.md]].

Robinson & Berridge 2025 ([[wiki/concepts/incentive-salience.md]]) attack the inference, not the data:

- The modern definition of habit is **negative** — it says what habits lack (sensitivity to the current value of the goal) rather than what they are — and at least three other processes produce the same persistence: **sensitized incentive salience** (a cue-triggered motivational multiplier that the devaluation never touched, because it is bound to the cue and not to the outcome representation), Tolman's own reading of his own test (overtraining "narrows the rat's cognitive maps" — a distortion of expectation, which he explicitly declined to call a habit), and an **urge to perform the action** rather than to obtain the outcome.
- The anatomical criterion is equally ambiguous. Dorsolateral striatum is *also* a site of cue-triggered 'wanting': opioid or dopamine microinjection there raises the ability of reward cues to trigger pursuit (DiFeliceantonio & Berridge 2016), lesions lower it (Corbit & Janak 2007), rodents work for optogenetic stimulation of its neurons (Vicente et al. 2016), and in humans cue-triggered dorsal-striatal dopamine release correlates with **craving** (Volkow et al. 2006). So "control has moved to dorsolateral striatum" does not discriminate the two accounts either.
- A behavioural counter-observation the S–R reading cannot absorb: people with addiction show "considerable ingenuity and flexibility" in procuring drugs — acquiring money, locating a source, negotiating a purchase — which is flexible goal-directed behaviour at every step except the choice of goal (Singer et al. 2018).

What the confound denies a builder is the ability to say what automatisation *is*. `T131` asks whether it is relocation of a policy or distillation into a different substrate; both readings assume the behaviour being relocated or distilled is an S–R mapping. If devaluation-insensitivity instead marks an amplified valuation attached to a cue, then the "habit" system is not a cache of actions at all, and the arbitration architecture the wiki builds on top of it is arbitrating between the wrong two things.

## Best current answer

**None run.** The two accounts have never been crossed. Partial separations that exist and do not close it:

- The one crossed design in the wiki is Killcross, Robbins & Everitt via Cardinal et al. 2002 ([[wiki/entities/amygdala.md]]), which separates outcome-specific from outcome-blind control by lesion — but the outcome-blind side there is a Pavlovian response, not overtrained instrumental responding, so it does not speak to what an overtrained lever-press is.
- Cardinal et al.'s **incentive-learning** protocol ([[wiki/concepts/valuation-system-decomposition.md]]) already shows that devaluation-insensitivity can be *transient and repairable* — responding is unchanged in extinction after devaluation and drops after a single re-exposure to the devalued outcome. A stimulus–response chain has no reason to be repaired by one re-exposure that involves no responding, so this result is already evidence that at least some devaluation-insensitivity is a stale valuation rather than an S–R habit. It is not decisive because it was run on moderately-trained animals, and the habit claim is about overtraining.
- The gain manipulation the closing design needs is standard in the incentive-salience literature and standard in the habit literature; nobody has put both in one experiment.

**The machine version is free and sharper than the animal one**, because both quantities are inspectable. Train an agent with (i) a cached action-value table and (ii) a separate cue-keyed multiplier on action values; overtrain; devalue the outcome by editing the reward function; test with and without the cue. Persistence in the no-cue condition is S–R strength; persistence that disappears without the cue is gain. If a standard model-free agent's post-devaluation persistence is *entirely* of the first kind, that is a positive result worth stating — it would mean the artificial case is unambiguous even though the biological one is not, and that the wiki's habit vocabulary is safe for machines and unsafe for the animal literature it was imported from.
