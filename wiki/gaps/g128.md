# G128 — Every cost term in the wiki is a fixed function of load; none has a write port, so no agent can become industrious

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** One agent's *elicited* effort cost curve is shown to move as a function of a training history in which reward was contingent on high demand, and to move on a **held-out** task. Minimally: elicit `SV(load)` by titration ([[wiki/concepts/effort-based-decision-making.md]]) on task `B`; train on task `A` under a high-demand reward contingency, against a **yoked** control that receives the identical reward stream and success rate without the demand contingency; re-elicit `SV(load)` on `B`. The row closes when the trained agent's curve is flatter than the yoked agent's, and stays open if the effect is absent or is carried by the reward stream alone.

## Why it blocks the target

Every quantity that prices computation or exertion in the wiki is written by the designer and never by experience:

| Mechanism | Cost term | Written by? |
|---|---|---|
| [[wiki/concepts/expected-value-of-control.md]] | `Cost(signal)`, asserted monotonic in control intensity | No — a stipulated function, never fitted |
| [[wiki/concepts/adaptive-computation-time.md]] | ponder penalty `τ` per extra step | No — a hyperparameter |
| [[wiki/concepts/default-policy-regularization.md]] | `KL(π ‖ π₀)` | **Partly** — `π₀` is distilled and therefore moves, so the *distance* changes; but the price per nat is fixed |
| [[wiki/concepts/effort-based-decision-making.md]] | `SV(load)` elicited per agent | It is *measured*, not learned — the procedure returns a snapshot with no account of where the curve came from |
| [[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]] | rollout depth / budget | No — a schedule or a constant |

Biology denies the invariance directly ([[wiki/concepts/learned-industriousness.md]], Eisenberger 1992). Reinforcement contingent on high performance conditions secondary reward value onto the response-produced sensation of effort, which lowers its aversiveness — and because the conditioned stimulus is a property of *exerting* rather than of the task, the reduction transfers. The transfer is not a within-task skill effect: it appears across apparatus (runway ratio training → lever-press extinction), across response dimension with response count and time yoked (high lever force → faster runway shuttling), across modality (rewarded pad pressure → faster anagram solving with a new experimenter in a new room), and in choice rather than only in rate (ratio training shifts rats toward a high-force/large-reward option and holds the shift for 12 sessions, with both options fully sampled at baseline).

What the missing write port denies a builder is any way for an architecture to **get better at being expensive**. An agent whose deeper rollouts have reliably paid off cannot come to charge itself less for depth; its ponder penalty is the same on episode 10⁷ as on episode 1. Every adaptive-compute result in the wiki is therefore a policy over a fixed price, where the biological system adjusts the price. The two are not equivalent: a policy learns *when* to pay, a conditioned price changes *what is affordable everywhere at once*, including on tasks never trained.

Three properties come with the biological version and no architecture here has any of them:

1. **Dimension indexing.** Rewarding reading accuracy transfers to drawing and story *accuracy*; rewarding reading speed transfers to story *speed*; each beats the other in its own dimension (Eisenberger et al. 1984). So the learned quantity is a vector over partially-substitutable performance dimensions, not a scalar budget.
2. **Context conditioning.** Two experimenters alternating high- and low-ratio requirements come to cue different effort levels on a *third*, later task (Eisenberger et al. 1983). The price is keyed by cues, which is why one agent can be industrious in one regime and lazy in another without holding two policies.
3. **Label-set indexing.** Children taught a broad self-attribution ("when I try hard I do well in all my school work") transferred the conditioned value further than children taught a narrow one, with the reinforcement history identical (Eisenberger 1990). The support set of the learned cost is set by a symbolic index — which in a language-conditioned agent is directly implementable and has never been tried.

## Best current answer

**None writes to the price.** The nearest constructs move adjacent quantities:

- [[wiki/concepts/effort-based-decision-making.md]] *reads* the curve per agent and explicitly leaves its origin as an open fork (intrinsic value for exertion vs value on effort's extrinsic products). This row is the constructive half: not what the curve is, but what writes it.
- [[wiki/concepts/default-policy-regularization.md]] is the one mechanism whose cost changes with experience, and it changes for a different reason — the *default policy* moves toward what the agent does often, so a repeated high-load task becomes cheap. That predicts familiarity-dependence; this row's evidence is **transfer to unfamiliar tasks**, which a distance-from-default penalty cannot produce. The two are discriminable by exactly the designs above and they come out on the conditioning side for biological agents.
- [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]] broadcasts learning-rule parameters from running statistics, which is the right *shape* of mechanism (a slow channel writing a scalar that reprices everything downstream) pointed at the wrong variable — learning rate and horizon, never cost.
- [[wiki/concepts/meta-learning.md]] could in principle learn a cost schedule in the outer loop, but the outer loop optimises return under a fixed inner cost; nothing in the wiki puts the cost term itself in the meta-parameter set.

The cheap machine version is small. Expose a scalar exertion signal `e_t` the agent already emits (ponder steps, rollout depth, control KL). Hold a multiplier `m ∈ (0,1]` on the cost term, and update it by a Pavlovian rule on the pairing between high `e_t` and subsequent reward — raise the discount when high exertion was followed by reward, decay it otherwise. Then check (i) whether the elicited `SV(load)` curve flattens, (ii) whether it flattens on a **held-out** task, which is the claim a yoked reward-matched control isolates, and (iii) whether per-dimension multipliers (`m_depth`, `m_width`, `m_precision`) reproduce the intradimensional specificity a single `m` cannot.

The caveat that keeps this `L1` rather than settled: the biological account predicts, and does not cleanly demonstrate, the symmetric extinction — that rewarding *low* performance drives an already-trained high-effort value back down. The evidence is that low-effort groups fail to shift, not that trained groups shift back, so the update rule's negative half is unmeasured in the source too.
