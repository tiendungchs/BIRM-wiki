# G123 — Every store in the wiki reads once per query, so no retrieval can be revised

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An architecture re-runs its read at a fixed rate on unchanged input, and reports what the repetition buys against a single-read control of matched compute — i.e. the benefit is shown to come from *re-deciding*, not from spending more FLOPs.

## Why it blocks the target

Retrieval in every store here is one event per query. Softmax attention recomputes selection once per layer from the current query and then hands the result downstream ([[wiki/concepts/attention.md]]); a Hopfield net settles once and stops at the fixed point; the continuous modern Hopfield store retrieves in **one** update by design ([[wiki/entities/continuous-modern-hopfield-network.md]]); fast-weight and key-value stores read on demand ([[wiki/concepts/fast-weight-programming.md]], [[wiki/concepts/key-value-memory.md]]). In all of them a commitment made on a weak or ambiguous cue is final for that query: there is no later moment at which the same cue is re-presented to the same store and a different answer allowed.

CA3 does the opposite. Under an instantaneous cue switch between two orthogonally-coded environments, the population commits to one chart per theta cycle and re-decides on a free-running ~8 Hz clock for seconds afterwards — flicker rate 1–3% at baseline, 10–15% for ~5 s after the switch, single-cycle full replacement in 30.8% of events, with the chart complete at onset and no growth within a flicker (Jezek, Henriksen, Treves, Moser & Moser 2011, `raw/jezek-2011-theta-paced-flickering-between-place-cell-maps.md`). The authors' functional proposal is that the repetition is **error correction**: their stated contrast is inferior temporal cortex, where completion runs once per stimulus — fast enough for perception, with no opportunity to self-correct — against a store that gets several attempts per second and can converge under cues too weak or ambiguous for any single pass.

The gap is the whole claim, on both sides. Nothing in the wiki re-reads, and nothing — including the source — measures whether re-reading helps.

## Best current answer

Partial, and none of the partial answers is a re-read. [[wiki/entities/universal-transformer.md]] applies one shared block `T` times, but each application revises the *representation*, so the query changes between passes and no decision is re-taken on the same evidence. Iterating a Hopfield update to convergence is a single descent, not a re-decision: the trajectory is monotone in energy and cannot leave the basin it entered. [[wiki/concepts/encoding-retrieval-alternation.md]] supplies the clock and half the schedule — the store reads every trough whether or not anything changed — but the read there is consumed as the minus phase of a learning rule, so the repetition is used for *writing* and its value as retrieval is never scored. The mechanism side is not empty either: [[wiki/entities/stp-flickering-cann.md]] shows a periodic collapse of global inhibition is enough to re-open a settled competition, at the cost of one gain variable per stored state, which is the cheapest known implementation of a re-read and has never been evaluated as one.

What is missing is the evaluation. The natural instrument already exists — the shuffle-null exclusivity statistic on [[wiki/concepts/attractor-identification.md]] — which lets a re-reading store be scored on whether successive reads are *independent samples* of the ambiguity or repetitions of the first commitment. A store whose re-reads all return the first answer has bought nothing.
