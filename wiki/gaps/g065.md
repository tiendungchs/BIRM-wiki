# G65 — Nothing protects a pretrained representation from a downstream module's initialisation transient except switching its learning off

**Status:** `CONTESTED`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Why it blocks the target

Every layered design in the wiki eventually attaches a new, randomly initialised module below a trained one — an action head under a backbone, a readout under a world model, a fast store under a controller. The transient from the untrained module degrades the trained one with no task sequence and no distribution shift, and the field's remedy removes exactly the level-to-level credit assignment a hierarchy is supposed to have (G59, and [[wiki/concepts/policy-abstraction-hierarchy.md]]'s unnamed per-level credit signal)

## Best current answer

**Gradient insulation** — block the head's gradients at the backbone boundary — recommended to practitioners and reported to improve both training stability and final performance; full freezing (GR00T N1.5); Low-Rank Adaptation, reported competitive with full fine-tuning on OpenVLA; staged unfreezing; RevLA's gradual reversal of backbone weights toward their pretrained values (repair, not prevention); and the limit case, **DSRL**, which updates no weight at all and learns a distribution over the diffusion policy's initial noise, taking `π₀` from ~20% to ~100% success on 10K samples. Every entry buys protection by giving up adaptation of the protected level
