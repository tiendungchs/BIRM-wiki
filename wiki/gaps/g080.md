# G80 — The wiki's most expressive per-parameter variable is the one nothing optimises

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A learning rule *creates* delays rather than selecting from a pre-drawn bank, and realises the `Θ(n log n)` capacity advantage over weights on a task where weights alone provably cannot.

## Why it blocks the target

Maass & Schmitt's bound makes `n` programmable **delays** carry VC dimension `Θ(n log n)` against `n` weights' `Θ(n)`, and the one worked construction in the wiki that computes a coincidence function at spiking cost uses **delays only, with every weight fixed at 1** ([[wiki/concepts/circuit-size-separation.md]]). Every trainable parameter in every architecture in this wiki is nonetheless a weight, a gain or a time constant. This blocks the target in the same place `G76`–`G78` do and one step earlier: if the execution half of latent-graph discovery emits a timing code, the variable that *carries* structural position in that code is arrival time, and nothing adjusts it toward a task. It also sits under `G77` — a delay is a *relative* quantity and needs no time origin, so a learnable-delay architecture is the one place where the missing-clock objection does not bite

## Best current answer

One mechanism, and it cannot create a delay. [[wiki/concepts/temporal-coding.md]] holds Gerstner et al. 1996's Hebbian **selection** over a pre-existing random bank: 600 synapses with delays drawn `N(2.5 ms, 0.3 ms)`, a multiplicative rule that saturates a coherent subset and prunes the rest, leaving survivors spaced by exact multiples of the stimulus period and vector strength 0.97 at 20 µs. Three limits, all structural. (i) It **selects rather than sets**: a delay outside the initial bank is unreachable, so the bank's mean and spread are the design-time hyperparameters the mechanism was supposed to remove. (ii) It is **unsupervised and single-variable** — driven by input coherence, not by task error, and it discovers the one latent (source azimuth) the physics happens to encode in coincidence. (iii) It is **self-reinforcing by construction** (the increment scales with the weight), which is why it converges and also why nothing arbitrates between two competing coherent subsets. **A later source supplies the missing optimiser** (Mészáros, Knight & Nowotny 2025, [[wiki/concepts/learnable-synaptic-delays.md]]): the EventProp adjoint formalism extended so that spike *arrival* times join spike emission times in the event set, giving an **exact** gradient `dL/dd_ji = −w_ji Σ_k (λ_I,j − λ_V,j)|_{t_k + d_ji}` read off the *same* backward trajectories as the weight gradient — the adjoint dynamics are unchanged, so delay learning costs one extra sample of variables already being computed. It **sets** rather than selects, is driven by task loss rather than input coherence, handles multiple spikes per neuron and — first in any method — **recurrent** connections, and is cheap in the way that matters for deployment: the delay range is a *per-neuron ring buffer* rather than a per-synapse convolution kernel, giving <½ the memory and up to **26×** the speed of dilated-convolution delay learning, on hardware (SpiNNaker 2, Loihi) that already implements per-synapse delays. Witness: two LIF neurons, two leaky integrators, **every weight fixed at 1**, delays initialised at the exact worst case — 100% on a sequence-detection task after 6 presentations of each example. **Residue, which is why this is `PARTIAL` and not closed.** (i) `d_max` and the timestep grid are new design-time hyperparameters standing exactly where the delay bank's mean and spread used to (`G78` again). (ii) Recurrent delay *initialisation* has no principle — the feedforward shift-symmetry argument (`[0,d_max]` ≡ `[x,d_max+x]`) fails inside a loop, and all recurrent delays were set to 0. (iii) `dL/dd ∝ w`, so a synapse must already matter before its timing is learnable. (iv) The gradient is exact but **offline and non-local** — an adjoint system run backwards over saved spike times — so the biologically plausible delay rule is still Hebbian selection. (v) The measured value of a delay contradicts the naive reading of the theorem: delays helped *only under a size constraint* and gave no benefit to large networks ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T237)
