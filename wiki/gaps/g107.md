# G107 — The exchange rate between computation and accuracy is a hand-set constant in every architecture that varies its own compute

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An architecture sets its own price on computation from a quantity it measures or computes at run time, and its per-input compute tracks task difficulty across at least an order of magnitude without a per-task sweep of that price.

## Why it blocks the target

Every mechanism in the wiki that spends a variable amount of computation per input needs to know what a step of thinking is worth relative to a point of accuracy, and none of them can find out. Adaptive computation time makes the budget differentiable and then hands the trade-off to a scalar `τ` that its own author reports the network is "quite sensitive" to, requiring a 20-seed logarithmic grid per task ([[wiki/concepts/adaptive-computation-time.md]]). The sampling-based half of the literature has the same hole under a different name — [[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]] names adaptive allocation as its open control problem and no source supplies a policy — and the biological stopping rules push the constant one level down rather than removing it, since a decision threshold fixes the error rate and nothing sets the error rate ([[wiki/concepts/evidence-accumulation.md]]). A reasoning system that cannot price its own deliberation cannot be deployed on a task distribution of mixed difficulty, which is every interesting one.

## Best current answer

None that removes the constant. The one substitution attempted since makes the point: [[wiki/entities/universal-transformer.md]] drops the ponder penalty `τ` entirely and stops on a hand-set **threshold** plus a step cap, with no sweep and no sensitivity analysis reported — the price changed form and stayed external. What it does add is the first evidence for the row's *second* clause independently of its first: per-position compute tracks a counted ground-truth quantity (mean ponder 2.3/3.1/3.8 steps for bAbI questions requiring 1/2/3 supporting facts) without the price being learned, so difficulty-tracking and price-setting are separable and only the latter is still open. Against that, the same source's depth-matched controls raise the possibility that the gain is regularisation rather than allocation ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T321), which would make the tracking evidence weaker than it looks.

Three partial routes: `τ` is at least *interpretable* (a cost per state transition), so it could in principle be set from a real budget if computation and error were commensurable — the source's own suggestion, and it requires an external price rather than an internal one. Higher `τ` was found to improve sample efficiency on addition, so the constant is not purely a cost knob and a naive "set it as low as affordable" is wrong. And the one-sided-penalty result for the accumulator gain `g*` ([[wiki/concepts/evidence-accumulation.md]]) shows what the target property looks like: a parameter that only needs saturating, not estimating. No compute-price parameter in the wiki has been shown to be one-sided. **The same constant on the width axis, in an architecture that already computes the test it would need to drop it** (Goyal et al. 2019, [[wiki/entities/rims.md]]): `k_A` of `k_T` recurrent modules update per step, with a stated trade-off in both directions — too few active modules hurts optimisation, too many attenuates the out-of-distribution gain — and the authors name "dynamic ways of controlling how many RIMs to activate" as future work. The unexploited part is that the selection already runs a **per-module threshold test**: each module attends over the input with a null row appended and is ranked by how little mass it puts on that null row. Activating every module that beats its *own* null row, instead of taking a fixed-size top-`k_A` over the ranking, would make the active-set size an input-dependent quantity computed at run time with **no price scalar at all** — this row's `Closes when` on the width axis, one line of code away, and unrun. **The same constant on the retrieval axis, with the per-task dependence measured** (Asai et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/self-rag.md]]). A trained token ranks when another read is needed. The exchange rate is a hand-set threshold `δ` on that token's probability (0.2 by default, 0 for ALCE), plus hand-set critique weights `w = 1.0/1.0/0.5`. Fig. 3(c) shows that retrieval frequency swings widely with `δ`, and that the accuracy cost of reading less is small on PubHealth and large on PopQA. So the right price differs by task, and the architecture cannot find it. The source presents the external `δ` as a customisation feature.

**The oldest argument that the price should not exist** (Simon 1955, [[wiki/concepts/satisficing.md]]). If payoff and the cost of discovering alternatives shared one currency, a weighted sum would fix an optimal amount of search. Simon's claim is that the searcher knows neither, and he replaces the price with an aspiration level. Search stops at the first alternative that clears it; the level rises after easy success and falls after failure. Per-input compute then tracks difficulty, because a hard input needs more alternatives examined before one clears the bar, and no exchange rate appears anywhere. It does not close the row: the up/down step sizes are a new pair of constants, unformalised in the source, and nothing has built or measured the rule in an architecture.

**A proof that the price cannot be removed by program design alone, only replaced** (Russell & Subramanian 1995, [[wiki/concepts/resource-rationality.md]]). With a known constant time cost `c`, the bounded-optimal program is `argmax_i(q_i − c t_i)`, so `c` is a primitive input to the design. A universal program exists that is asymptotically bounded-optimal for *every* time-dependence in a family (all `c`, all deadline distributions): procedures of doubling runtime, each followed by a termination test. But it "cannot terminate at an appropriate time without access to information concerning the time dependence of the utility function". It needs either an **aspiration level** per value function, supplied from outside, or a **herald** percept announcing the deadline. So the row's `Closes when` has two formal routes: compute the aspiration level, or generate the herald, at run time. The source treats neither, and leaves the unheralded case explicitly untreated. Its model also assumes quality independent of the input, so the row's per-input clause is outside it.

**The constant given units and a per-state address, but still not set** (Ortega & Braun 2013, [[wiki/concepts/thermodynamic-bounded-rationality.md]]). Three axioms on refinement cost force the exchange rate into the form `max E[U] − (1/β)·KL(p‖p₀)`. Theorem 4 then shows that the Boltzmann choice `p₀e^{βU}/Z` is within `e^{−(β−ξ)δ}` of the distribution of the maximum of `β` random samples. So `β` *is* a sample budget, and a price `c` per sample fixes a finite optimum. The sequential form lets every node of a decision tree carry its own `β(x_<t)`, which is the per-input allocation this row's second clause asks for. It does not close the row: `c` and every `β(x_<t)` are inputs, nothing in the source computes them at run time, and the sample reading is proved for random search only.
