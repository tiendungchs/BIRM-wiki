# G54 — No architecture distinguishes a channel that carries content from one that only carries timing

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** Content transport and temporal coordination are carried on separate channels, and the two are shown to be separately ablatable in a machine — cutting timing without cutting content, as the biology does.

## Why it blocks the target

Every inter-module link in the wiki does both jobs at once: it transports a representation *and* it is the only thing aligning the two modules in time. The biology separates them and they survive different lesions — cutting the direct hippocampus→prefrontal axons abolishes gamma coupling (content) and leaves theta coupling (coordination) intact, the same result holding for entorhinal→CA1 — so an architecture with one link per module pair cannot express the case where two modules are coordinated by a third and exchange nothing, or exchange content while free-running. It also means a measured correlation between two modules is not evidence they are connected.

## Best current answer

Split each inter-module connection into a content channel whose gain is the afferent drive and a coordination channel broadcast by a third module (medial septum, midline thalamus, or the collateralising relay of [[wiki/entities/nucleus-reuniens.md]]); no wiki architecture implements the second; **the nearest thing built, and it is a partial pass** — [[wiki/entities/ms-ssm.md]] holds a frozen bank of `S+2` memory horizons fixed at initialisation and lets a content-derived row vector `E_t = Linear_E(x_t)` pick a *mixture over* them, so the signal that sets the horizon is not the signal being stored and cannot alter any horizon, which is the case [[wiki/entities/cfc.md]] named as its missing test and [[wiki/entities/ltc.md]] cannot express. Its ablation states the design rule: the selector must read the **raw** stream, not a band's own representation, because a band-limited channel cannot judge its own relevance. What is still missing is the *inter-module* version — MS-SSM's timing channel is internal to one layer and there is no third module broadcasting it
