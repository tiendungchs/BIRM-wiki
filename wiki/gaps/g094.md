# G94 — Nothing in the wiki adapts a unit's *time constant*, and nothing pools its gain locally — input normalization is a global, offline, once-per-dataset operation

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A unit adapts its own time constant and pools its gain locally, within an episode, from input statistics rather than from prediction error — and holds performance under a within-episode statistics shift that defeats offline normalisation. Design-time form is `G78`.

## Why it blocks the target

Any agent whose input statistics shift *within* an episode — a moving camera, a scrolling context, a changing task regime — is in the situation the retina and thalamus are in after every saccade: the receptive field lands on a region of different mean and different variance every ~300 ms, and the unit's dynamic range is small. The biological answer is not a normalization layer. It is a **two-factor controller that sets both a gain and an integration time, from locally pooled statistics, in under 100 ms, per unit** — and the two factors (mean luminance, root-mean-square contrast) are measured to act **independently**, so their filters compose multiplicatively and an unseen (mean, variance) combination is predictable rather than requiring a lookup table. The pools are content-defined and small: the luminance pool no larger than the receptive-field surround, the contrast pool computed from nonlinear subunits coextensive with the classical receptive field. Machine practice keeps the arithmetic (divide by a pooled activity) and drops every one of these properties — the pool is a layer or a batch, the statistics are the dataset's, the time constant is fixed at construction, and no architecture in this wiki adapts an integration window online at all. **Without it, an adaptive front end is a hyperparameter rather than a control loop**

## Best current answer

None. The closest things the wiki holds are one level too coarse or one level too slow: [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]] varies gain-like quantities but broadcasts one global scalar (and G93 records that it has no addressing), [[wiki/concepts/precision-weighting.md]] has the right *form* — a multiplicative confidence on a channel — but is estimated from prediction error rather than from input statistics and carries no time-constant term, and [[wiki/concepts/spike-frequency-adaptation.md]] adapts a threshold per unit without pooling and without touching the integration window. **The composability test is the cheap part and nobody runs it**: fit each factor alone, freeze both, and check the product against the joint condition — exactly the protocol that made this result count in the thalamus ([[wiki/concepts/predictive-adequacy.md]])
