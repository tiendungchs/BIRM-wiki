# G15 — No control policy over simulation

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A policy sets rollout depth and branch choice from a quantity the agent computes for itself, and beats both always-on and hand-scheduled rollout on a task where the affordable depth varies across states.

## Why it blocks the target

Planning is path search, but nothing decides when to roll out, how deep, which branch, or when the answer is good enough. Without it, model-based reasoning is either always-on (unaffordable) or hand-scheduled (not general)

## Best current answer

Prefrontal control of hippocampal rollout, proposed biologically; controller-queries-model architectures implement the interface but not the policy. **Active inference supplies the *when* and the *switch*, not the *how deep*:** homeostatic divergence read as free energy both initiates the rollout and sets its target, the epistemic-vs-goal trade-off arbitrates exploration against exploitation on the same quantity, and dropping bottom-up gain (`γ → 0`) with strong biased competition (`β ≪ 0`) is the operational switch from perception into simulation. **A second candidate for the same half:** a rational *arbitration* between model-based and model-free control on a flexibility-vs-speed trade-off, supervised metacognitively, with habitization as the observed shift — which prices planning against its own cached output rather than against a homeostatic drive ([[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]]). **A third supplies the *interface* in closed form and still no policy:** in an adaptive continuous attractor one scalar — adaptation gain `m` relative to input strength `α` — moves the same circuit continuously from locked-to-input, through oscillating around the current estimate, to running free through the state space, with both switching thresholds analytic (`m = τ/τ_v` and `m = τ/τ_v + α/A_u`), and a noisy gain parked at the boundary makes exploration breadth itself a formula (`α_Lévy = 1 + 2μ/γ²`) ([[wiki/entities/adaptive-cann.md]]). So "when to roll out" and "how broadly" become two continuous knobs rather than mode switches — but what *sets* the gain is exogenous in that model, so the row's missing piece is now sharply localised: a controller for one scalar. No computational account of the metacognitive estimator is given, and neither candidate answers *how deep*. **A fourth candidate dissolves the row instead of filling it**: if the planner is a spacetime attractor, there is no rollout to schedule — the whole trajectory is a fixed point, depth is the number of delay-subspaces (an anatomical constant), and the plan is done when the dynamics converge, so *when*, *which branch* and *when to stop* all disappear and only *how many subspaces to build* remains ([[wiki/entities/spacetime-attractor.md]], Jensen et al. 2026). The cost is that this works only where the structure is already embedded in the weights; novel environments still need the search this row is about. **The first candidate to remove a free parameter rather than add a knob:** written over occupancies the active-inference planning objective is `⟨ℓ,μ⟩ + Φ(μ)`, a linear preference cost plus one convex negative-entropy term, so the epistemic-vs-goal arbitration named above has **no mixing coefficient** — the balance is the objective's geometry (Milosevic et al. 2026). The same result sharpens what is still missing: the nonlinearity means no globally Bellman-optimal value function exists, so "which branch" cannot be read off a cached `V` and the planner must relinearise between iterations; and *how deep* is still unanswered, though now priced, since the mirror-descent smoothness constant is `L = ½T(T+1)` **The first candidate with a *stopping* rule rather than a starting one:** rewriting the same objective as a ρ-POMDP belief utility (`ρ_EFE(b,a) = I_a(b)` for information-gathering actions, `0` for terminal ones) makes *keep looking vs. commit* one scalar comparison — observe while `−c_k + I_k(b) + E_o[max_a V]` exceeds `max_i E_b[R_i]` — and the crossover appears without a tuned threshold, a confidence criterion or a metacognitive estimator (Cooper & Velasquez 2026). Measured against tuned additive bonuses this is exactly where the difference lives: at `w = 100` on Diagnosis a bonus agent takes 13.21 tests for 99.3% success and `−3.63` reward, the derived weight takes 9.73 for 97.1% and `−1.50`, and *planning depth amplifies the miscalibration* rather than damping it. Two limits on the row's progress: the rule holds only where information-gathering leaves the hidden state fixed (`Δ_T = 0`), i.e. for measurement and not for intervention; and it optimises expected reward, not accuracy — the success-maximising weight is 20–200× larger, so a controller that must be *right* is back where the row started  **The estimator the row has been calling missing now exists, and it is cheap:** run both controllers as approximate Bayesian learners and adopt, *per action*, the value estimate of whichever has the smaller posterior variance — no metacognitive module, no value-of-computation calculation, and the criterion is a quantity each learner already maintains (Daw et al. 2005, [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]]). The crossing points are derived rather than tuned: caching wins with overtraining, task simplicity and distance from reward; search wins early, near reward, under task complexity and under frequent contingency change; and fan-out topology favours the cache while fan-in or linear topology favours search, which is the row's only *structural* routing signal. What is still missing is entirely on the machine side — no deep planner reports the uncertainty its own truncation injects (`computational noise`), so the comparison cannot be instantiated even though it is specified. **The first *optimal* stopping rule, valid only inside a strong restriction:** where the candidates are already enumerated, commit when the normalised log-posterior over them, `y_i − ln Σ_j exp(y_j)`, crosses a threshold — asymptotically optimal for any `N`, decision time growing only as `ln N`, its one free gain parameter provably one-sided in cost, and its normaliser computed by a dedicated recurrent subcircuit rather than as a free arithmetic step (Bogacz & Gurney 2007, [[wiki/concepts/evidence-accumulation.md]]). This closes the *when is the answer good enough* clause for a fixed candidate set and sharpens what remains: the rule says nothing about depth or branch, and its enumeration requirement is precisely what an open-ended planner lacks — so the row's residue is now *constructing* the alternatives (G33), not scoring them. **The row's *which branch* clause has a deployed answer and its *how deep* clause has a constant** ([[wiki/entities/alphazero.md]], Silver et al. 2017): selection by low visit count / high learned prior / high mean value is a branch policy computed entirely from quantities the agent already maintains, and it buys equal-or-better play than the strongest alpha-beta engines while evaluating ~875× fewer positions per second — so the missing piece is demonstrably not branch choice. The budget is 800 simulations per move, in every position, in all three games; and the search reports visit counts and a mean value but never the uncertainty its own truncation injected, which is the one quantity the Daw et al. 2005 crossing rule above needs. The row is therefore now sharply split: *which branch* is answered on a known model, *how deep* is unaddressed by the field's strongest planner. **The first branch policy derived from expected return rather than proposed as a heuristic** (Mattar & Daw 2018, [[wiki/concepts/replay-prioritisation.md]]): the utility of running any one Bellman backup is `EVB(s,a) = Gain(s,a) × Need(s)` — the improvement the induced policy change buys at the target state, times its discounted expected future occupancy — so *which branch* is `argmax EVB` over stored transitions, and *whether to look forward or backward* is not a mode switch but a readout of which term is larger. Both halves are quantities an agent already holds: `Need` is the successor-representation row of the current state (the stationary distribution when the agent is offline), `Gain` is a function of the current `Q`. Ordering by it reproduces forward replay before a run, reverse replay at reward, and remote sleep replay, unfitted. Three residues, and they are the row's: (i) `Gain` presupposes knowing the backup's effect on the policy *before* deciding to run it, so this is a normative bound and not a controller — the same shape of hole as the metacognitive estimator above; (ii) the rule is myopic, never pricing a backup whose value is that it enables a later one, which is precisely the *depth over computations* the row asks for, one meta-level up; (iii) the simulations buy a hand-set 20 backups per episode boundary, so *how deep* remains unaddressed by this candidate as it is by AlphaZero's fixed 800.

**What turns the simulation on, from the only biological case where the rollout is a rendered percept** (Pearson 2019, [[wiki/concepts/mental-imagery.md]]): a taxonomy, not a policy — voluntary imagery is frontally triggered, associative imagery is elicited by a learned cue with no known frontal contribution, and local perceptual filling-in needs no trigger at all. Two constraints it does place on any controller written for this row. The trigger is **content-blind** (frontal activity during imagery is largely independent of what is imagined), so the *when* and the *what* are supplied by different sources and the review has no account of how the trigger selects which memory is read. And the gain is **not a trait**: vividness fluctuates moment to moment within an individual, predicted by whole-brain activity rather than by a set point — so a fixed simulation gain misfits the one system where the quantity has been measured.

**The first *trained* when-to-consult signal, and it needs a hand-set criterion to work** (Asai et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/self-rag.md]]). The operation is a read from an external store, not a rollout. Self-RAG's generator emits a retrieve token per segment, trained on distilled GPT-4 judgements of whether the continuation needs grounding. On PopQA, retrieval is worth 20.8 points (24.7 → 45.5). The token's own argmax decision recovers 3.6 of them (28.3). A hand-set threshold `δ = 0.2` on the same probability recovers the rest, and `δ` is retuned per task (0 for ALCE). The *ranking* is learned. The *operating point* is hand-set. *Which branch* is answered by self-critique over `K` parallel candidates in a width-2 beam, but `K` and `B` are constants. The row's comparison is missing: learned gate against always-on at matched `K` and `B` has no number in the clip.

**The meta-level problem has a formal object and a human measurement, both second-hand** (Lieder & Griffiths 2020, [[wiki/concepts/resource-rationality.md]]). Strategy discovery casts planning as a **meta-level Markov decision process**: states are belief states, actions are planning operations with a cost each, and one terminal action commits. Solving it non-myopically gives a bounded-optimal planning strategy. Human planning reaches ~86% of that strategy's quality–time trade-off and matches it on ~55% of operations, shifting across environments as predicted (Callaway et al. 2018; Lieder et al. 2017). A cost-based *how deep*: plan a few steps, then substitute habitual values, with the depth set by the time available (Keramati et al. 2016). Neither closes the row. The meta-level problem is solved **offline, outside the agent**, so it is a target for a controller, not a controller, and nothing is compared against always-on or hand-scheduled rollout in a machine. Callaway et al. 2022 is queued in the same wave.

**The meta-level problem, first-hand and run online at the root of a search** (Hay, Russell, Tolpin & Shimony 2012, [[wiki/concepts/resource-rationality.md]]). Per decision: an MDP over computation outcomes, `−c` per computation, `max_i μ_i` on stopping. Four results bear on the row. (i) *How deep* gets a ceiling the agent can compute: `E[N] ≤ VPI/c`, though a realised `N` can be unbounded. (ii) *Which branch* has no index solution: the best computation inverts with the value of a third option. (iii) Myopic control stops too early, never late, and the **blinkered** policy fixes this at `k` one-action subproblems. (iv) On 9×9 Go, VOI selection at the root plus a VOI stopping rule beat Pachi's UCT with its hand-written stopping heuristic in up to **64%** of games (10000 samples/move, `c = 10⁻⁶`; both engines carry unused samples to the next move). The gain is largest at intermediate budgets. This is the row's first machine comparison of a self-computed *which branch* plus *when to stop* against a hand-scheduled one, and it wins. Three limits keep the row open. Interior nodes still run UCT, so *how deep* below the root is unaddressed, and the source names this as its open problem. `c` is set by a win-rate sweep, not computed. And the baseline is plain UCT, not a learned-prior planner such as [[wiki/entities/alphazero.md]].

**A provable *how deep*, in a restricted model** (Russell & Subramanian 1995, [[wiki/concepts/resource-rationality.md]]). If the deadline is uncertain but announced by a percept when it arrives, the bounded-optimal program is a sequence of procedures of increasing quality and runtime. The sequence is computable from the deadline distribution by dynamic programming (`O(n²L)` in general, polynomial for uniform and exponential deadlines). A universal doubling schedule that stops on the announcement matches the best such sequence for every deadline distribution, up to a constant-factor faster machine. With a known deadline or constant price the optimum is one fixed procedure: depth is chosen once, not per state. Three limits keep the row open. The procedures and their qualities are given, and quality does not depend on the input, so depth cannot vary across states. The design is offline. And the unannounced deadline is untreated, so the row's *how deep* reduces to *what generates the interrupt*.

**The cost-benefit *when*, first-hand, and its depth is a constant** (Keramati, Dezfouli & Piray 2011, [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]]). For each action, plan iff `VPI(s,a) > R̄·τ`. The benefit is the value of perfect information, read from the cached posterior. The price is the average reward rate times the expected search time. Everything is computed by the agent, in closed form and off the reaction-time path. It reproduces habitisation, persistent goal-directed control between equal-valued options, and falling reaction times with training. Three limits keep the row open. Depth is hand-set at 3, with cached values substituted below. Branch choice is per action, not per node. And the rule assumes the tree is exact, so it allocates planning wrongly whenever the model is wrong, which is when planning matters most for a learner. No machine comparison against always-on or hand-scheduled rollout is reported.
