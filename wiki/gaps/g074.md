# G74 — Every search, verification and refinement mechanism in the wiki assumes a wrong attempt is free

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A search or refinement mechanism prices its wrong attempts and changes behaviour under that price, evaluated where a wrong attempt costs something irreversible rather than a retry.

## Why it blocks the target

[[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]]'s ladder ranks acceptance tests by how many bits a rejection returns and never by what a rejection *costs*; [[wiki/concepts/refinement-loop.md]] needs a candidate checkable before it is committed to; program search needs unlimited trialling. All three hold on ARC because a task has one correct output and given demonstration pairs, so a weak proposer plus a cheap tester is a complete method. Where an action is irreversible — physical manipulation, a one-shot decision, an experiment that consumes its subject — the verdict arrives after iteration is no longer possible, and none of the wiki's mechanisms applies. This also means every score in the wiki prices candidates rather than *first attempts*

## Best current answer

Simulate before acting, which is the standard answer and is circular for this purpose: a simulation faithful enough to substitute for the world exists only for domains already modelled well enough to have been framed, i.e. where the skill exists. Partial and honest instruments: exact-match `pass@2` scoring, which forces some first-attempt discipline, and [[wiki/entities/arc-agi-3.md]]'s RHAE, which charges *environment-changing actions* against a human's count — the only score in the wiki where an attempt has a price, and it still exempts everything internal to the turn ([[wiki/gaps/g017.md]])
