# G12 — No routing policy between structure types

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A learned arbitrator selects the structure type at query time on inputs where a fixed heuristic such as a symmetry-score threshold provably misroutes.

## Why it blocks the target

Given a metric/transition-sampled module and a declarative/relational one, nothing decides which structure goes where. The arbitration is a prerequisite for G11's second mechanism to be usable at all **The wiki's first *running* router, and it is one line of code** ([[wiki/entities/corethink-compositional-reasoner.md]]): an input whose symmetry score exceeds 0.70 is sent to a rule-based jigsaw solver and bypasses the LLM entirely. A hand-set scalar on a hand-chosen statistic covering exactly one pattern family — but it shows the payoff a router buys is **cost** at least as much as accuracy (a symmetry task never pays for a frontier-model call), which is a dimension every framing of this row above states in accuracy terms only.

## Best current answer

— . **The row now has a biological existence proof for the *sorting* and still nothing for the *routing*** (Zheng et al. 2024, [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]]): a transition-sampled structure and a taxonomic one over the same objects end up in different, non-overlapping parts of the hippocampal formation, so the brain does place structure types in separate territory — but what reads the right one at query time is unaddressed, and the sorting itself may be a by-product of a resolution gradient (G38) rather than a routing decision. **The level-of-abstraction analogue of this gap now has an answer that dissolves it**: frontal cortex does not route between orders of policy at all — bands for 1st- and 2nd-order rules are both engaged above baseline from the first trial of a novel problem, and the band whose order is absent from the reward table *declines* over learning while the other is sustained, so the selection is done by attenuating unrewarded depths rather than by choosing one (Badre et al. 2010, [[wiki/concepts/policy-abstraction-hierarchy.md]]). Whether the same run-all-and-prune policy is affordable across structure *types* rather than depths is untested. **A measured cost for having no router, at the level of relational vocabulary** (Bortoletto et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/irene.md]]): on the Baby Intuitions tasks, local-adjacency edges alone give 98.0 on Multi-Agent and 99.4 on Irrational Agent but 31.8/37.2 on path- and time-efficiency; remote-comparison edges alone give the reverse; the union that must be handed to the model to cover both is optimal on neither (74.9 / 85.7). The right edge type is task-dependent, the selection is not learnable inside the architecture, and the price of the compromise is quantified. **The gap also shows up as a leaderboard artefact**: on ARC-AGI-1, program synthesis and test-time-trained transduction each solve ≈40% and the two sets are *significantly distinct*, so every 2024 state-of-the-art entry runs both and takes the union — paying the sum of two solvers because nothing predicts which one a task needs (Chollet et al. 2024, [[wiki/concepts/test-time-training.md]])
