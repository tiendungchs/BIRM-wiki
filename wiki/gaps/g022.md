# G22 — Nothing selects which compositions get built

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A composer builds the compositions a corpus never demonstrates and refuses the ones no energy minimum supports, with both directions measured on the same system — undergeneration and overgeneration counted, not one traded for the other.

## Why it blocks the target

A composer must be answerable in both directions at once. Driven from outside (corpus, teacher) it **undergenerates** — the primitives are what everyone assumes and therefore rarely says, and many symbols (logical operators, tense/aspect, modals) have no primitive counterpart. Driven from inside it **overgenerates** — nothing explains why a handful of the astronomically many available compositions are ever built. Granting composition at all inherits the combinatorial explosion; it is postponed, not solved. This is gap G4 (vocabulary co-discovery) restated as a *selection* problem rather than an induction problem

## Best current answer

**Selection by energy.** If a composition is an attractor, the compositions that are never built are the ones with no low-energy state — "a bowl lies in a ball" fails because a ball affords no interior. The pruner is then internal to the representation rather than external (corpus, teacher), which is what the overgeneration horn needs. Untested, and it prices selection in relaxation compute. **(brainstorm)** A machine is not forced to choose: give it an internal composer and use the external corpus as a *second* pruner over the generated space rather than as its source. **Two working pruners now exist, in one system** ([[wiki/entities/neo-neural-theorizer.md]]), and neither is a corpus: (a) an **MDL length prior** `λ_MDL^k · ℓ` selecting the shortest accurate explanation per instance — which prunes, but has a wrong side, since too much of it selects entangled composites instead of compositions (T155); (b) a **well-formedness constraint on the execution trace** rather than a prior over expressions — every intermediate state must decode to a valid observation, and removing this single term collapses the model entirely. (b) is the more interesting answer to the overgeneration horn because it is not a probability at all: most available compositions are never built not because they are improbable but because their intermediate states are not states of anything. It is also close in spirit to selection-by-energy, with a decode–encode cycle standing in for relaxation and costing one forward pass instead of a settling process
