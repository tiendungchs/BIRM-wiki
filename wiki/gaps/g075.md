# G75 — Nothing chooses how to parse the input; the object decomposition is fixed before the task is seen

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A model chooses its object decomposition per task from an open space rather than a fixed menu, and its parse choices match the human per-task convergence on the same ambiguous inputs. Special case of `G73`.

## Why it blocks the target

The symbolisation step every symbolic account presupposes is itself an inference. In ARC the parse is genuinely ambiguous — occlusion, and whether a set of cells is one object or two — and humans re-decide it *per task*, with their action traces converging on bottleneck states that are the task's own objects (Johnson et al. 2021). Machines do not: every solver in the wiki carries one parse rule, chosen at design time, and its errors then violate the objectness prior the benchmark declares (elongated shapes, wrap-around) whatever its score. The cost is that a wrong parse is indistinguishable from a wrong rule — the search reports failure, not that it was searching over the wrong objects. This is G73 arriving *inside* a format that supposedly fixes the framing

## Best current answer

Enumerate parses and search over them: [[wiki/entities/arc-vsa-solver.md]] treats the object parse as one of six hypotheses and selects jointly with the rule, which is the only instance in the wiki where the parse is a variable rather than a constant, and it is a fixed finite menu. [[wiki/entities/spelkenet.md]] computes an objectness segmentation from video rather than declaring one, but the segmentation is not task-conditioned. Human behaviour says the choice is made *after* seeing the demonstrations and is therefore a function of the task, which neither supplies. **And a third answer that splits the row in two** ([[wiki/entities/resonator-network.md]], Frady et al. 2020): encode a scene as a superposition of `colour ⊙ shape ⊙ v ⊙ h` products and decode it by factorization plus deflation — converge on one object, subtract it, reset, repeat. The **number of parts is a free variable** determined by when the loop stops finding one, with no menu and no hypothesis enumeration, which is the first mechanism here that does not fix the part count in advance. The **axes** are untouched and are worse than a menu: the four codebooks are declared before the task and cannot be re-chosen. So the row is really two questions — *how many parts* (answered, by a dynamical stopping condition) and *along which factors* (unanswered, and now clearly the harder half, since it is the same question as G4's vocabulary co-discovery). Two costs come with it: which object the network reads out first is uncontrolled, and non-convergence is silent
