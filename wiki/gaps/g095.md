# G95 — Every self-supervised system in the wiki has its invariance group *declared* by a hand-written augmentation list; nothing here discovers which directions are nuisance from the input's own statistics

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** The invariance group is discovered from the input's own statistics rather than declared by an augmentation list, and the discovered nuisance directions match or beat the hand-written list on a domain the list was not tuned for.

## Why it blocks the target

Invariance is the crux of recognition and, by extension, of any code that must treat many surface realizations of one thing as the same thing. Joint-embedding methods buy it by asserting the answer: crops, colour jitter, blur, masking — a fixed list, chosen by the designer, identical for every unit and every locus, frozen for the run. The biology gets the same product from a signal that is already in the stream. Object identity is temporally stable and retinal images are not, so the fast directions of population motion *are* the identity-preserving directions on average; a rule that makes temporally adjacent inputs produce similar outputs factorizes identity from nuisance without ever being told what an object is, and the supply is free (~10⁸ saccade-driven translation experiences per year). The difference is not efficiency, it is three properties: the group is **discovered** rather than declared, it is **local** (each cortical locus infers the nuisance directions of its own ~10K-dimensional subspace rather than inheriting one global list), and it is **revisable** — and the revisability is measured, not argued: swapping object identity across a saccade so that one object at position A is reliably followed by a different object at position B *destroys* existing position and size tolerance in inferior temporal neurons and builds new, incorrect tolerance within hours, changing perception accordingly (Li & DiCarlo 2008; Cox et al. 2005). **Without it, what a system treats as irrelevant is a hyperparameter of the training pipeline, cannot change after deployment, and cannot differ between layers** — and the same rule's failure mode is inherited whole: a system that learns its nuisance group from temporal adjacency learns the *capture process's* invariances (camera motion, editing cuts, playlist order), which is [[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]] one level down from the task

## Best current answer

Partial and split. The *objective* exists in machine form — the invariance term of [[wiki/entities/barlow-twins.md]] and [[wiki/entities/byol.md]] is exactly "make two views agree", and its biological ancestry is explicit (Földiák 1991; Wallis & Rolls 1997; slow feature analysis, Wiskott & Sejnowski 2002). The *data source* exists — [[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]] trains on 1M+ hours of video — but it spends time on **prediction across a gap** rather than on **equivalence between neighbours**, so no nuisance direction is ever explicitly factored out. Nothing in the wiki combines the two, and nothing at all has the locality or the revisability. **The declared-list route's best measured result is now in the wiki, and it sharpens rather than weakens the row** ([[wiki/entities/stylized-imagenet.md]]): a single hand-named nuisance direction — texture, removed by replacing it with random painting style — moves shape bias 22% → 81% and mCE 76.7 → 69.3, so declaration *works* when the nuisance is nameable; and the same run installs an unasked-for reliance on sharp edges (low-pass blur gets worse), which is the declared list's failure mode in miniature — what is not on the list becomes the next rule. **And the price of the omission is now measured on natural images** ([[wiki/entities/objectnet.md]]): every augmentation list here is a list of operations on an *array*, so rotation in depth, imaging viewpoint and object–context relation cannot appear on it at all — and randomising exactly those three at capture costs ImageNet-trained detectors **40–45%**, with rotation the largest single term (20 points), constant from AlexNet to PNASNet, and not recoverable by fine-tuning (64 images per class buys 21 points where 8 normally buys ~37). So the declared-list route is not merely incomplete on nameable directions; the directions it structurally *cannot* name are the expensive ones. **A third route is now in the wiki and it is neither discovery nor declaration — it is *abstention*** ([[wiki/entities/mae.md]]): a masked auto-encoder declares no invariance group at all, because a fresh random mask each iteration supplies the pair, and it loses only **7.8 linear / 0.9 fine-tuned** points when augmentation is removed *entirely*, against −13 for [[wiki/entities/byol.md]] and −28 for SimCLR under crop-only. Adding colour jitter makes it *worse*. This does not close the row — nothing is discovered, and a system with no invariance group has no tolerance to transfer — but it prices the declared list from the other side: on ImageNet classification and dense transfer, most of what the augmentation list buys is obtainable without naming a single nuisance direction. The open question it sharpens is whether an abstaining system's tolerance is merely *late* (built by the read-out rather than the encoder, consistent with its frozen-probe weakness and adapted strength, T310) or genuinely absent. The measurement that would make this concrete is cheap and unrun: train one system with augmentation-defined pairing and one with adjacency-defined pairing on the same stream, and score identity decodability on **transformation conditions the stream's time structure never paired**

**The route's earliest machine instance is now in the wiki, and it is on the discovery side of this row's split** (van den Oord et al. 2018, [[wiki/entities/cpc.md]]). CPC declares **no augmentation list at all**: positives are the observation `k` steps ahead of the context, so the equivalence relation is supplied by the stream's own time structure — the pairing rule this row says biology uses. It does not close the row, for two reasons that are worth stating precisely. (i) Nothing is factored *out*: the objective keeps everything predictive of the future, and the measurement proves it — one 256-d code carries phone content (64.6 linear) and **speaker identity (97.4 linear)** simultaneously, so temporal pairing separates fast from slow without ever declaring the slow direction a nuisance. (ii) The invariance class is set by a scalar the designer still picks — the horizon (2/4/8/12/16 steps → 28.5/57.6/63.6/64.6/63.8) — so "which nuisances are tolerated" has been moved from a list of named operations to one number, which is a smaller declaration rather than none. The row's wanted experiment (adjacency-defined pairing vs augmentation-defined pairing on the same stream, scored on transformation conditions the stream never paired) has a ready implementation on this side for the first time.
