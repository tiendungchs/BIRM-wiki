# G95 — Every self-supervised system in the wiki has its invariance group *declared* by a hand-written augmentation list; nothing here discovers which directions are nuisance from the input's own statistics

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** The invariance group is discovered from the input's own statistics rather than declared by an augmentation list, and the discovered nuisance directions match or beat the hand-written list on a domain the list was not tuned for.

## Why it blocks the target

Invariance is the crux of recognition and, by extension, of any code that must treat many surface realizations of one thing as the same thing. Joint-embedding methods buy it by asserting the answer: crops, colour jitter, blur, masking — a fixed list, chosen by the designer, identical for every unit and every locus, frozen for the run. The biology gets the same product from a signal that is already in the stream. Object identity is temporally stable and retinal images are not, so the fast directions of population motion *are* the identity-preserving directions on average; a rule that makes temporally adjacent inputs produce similar outputs factorizes identity from nuisance without ever being told what an object is, and the supply is free (~10⁸ saccade-driven translation experiences per year). The difference is not efficiency, it is three properties: the group is **discovered** rather than declared, it is **local** (each cortical locus infers the nuisance directions of its own ~10K-dimensional subspace rather than inheriting one global list), and it is **revisable** — and the revisability is measured, not argued: swapping object identity across a saccade so that one object at position A is reliably followed by a different object at position B *destroys* existing position and size tolerance in inferior temporal neurons and builds new, incorrect tolerance within hours, changing perception accordingly (Li & DiCarlo 2008; Cox et al. 2005). **Without it, what a system treats as irrelevant is a hyperparameter of the training pipeline, cannot change after deployment, and cannot differ between layers** — and the same rule's failure mode is inherited whole: a system that learns its nuisance group from temporal adjacency learns the *capture process's* invariances (camera motion, editing cuts, playlist order), which is [[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]] one level down from the task

## Best current answer

Partial and split. The *objective* exists in machine form — the invariance term of [[wiki/entities/barlow-twins.md]] and [[wiki/entities/byol.md]] is exactly "make two views agree", and its biological ancestry is explicit (Földiák 1991; Wallis & Rolls 1997; slow feature analysis, Wiskott & Sejnowski 2002). The *data source* exists — [[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]] trains on 1M+ hours of video — but it spends time on **prediction across a gap** rather than on **equivalence between neighbours**, so no nuisance direction is ever explicitly factored out. Nothing in the wiki combines the two, and nothing at all has the locality or the revisability. **The declared-list route's best measured result is now in the wiki, and it sharpens rather than weakens the row** ([[wiki/entities/stylized-imagenet.md]]): a single hand-named nuisance direction — texture, removed by replacing it with random painting style — moves shape bias 22% → 81% and mCE 76.7 → 69.3, so declaration *works* when the nuisance is nameable; and the same run installs an unasked-for reliance on sharp edges (low-pass blur gets worse), which is the declared list's failure mode in miniature — what is not on the list becomes the next rule. The measurement that would make this concrete is cheap and unrun: train one system with augmentation-defined pairing and one with adjacency-defined pairing on the same stream, and score identity decodability on **transformation conditions the stream's time structure never paired**
