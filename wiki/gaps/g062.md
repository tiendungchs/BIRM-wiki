# G62 — Nothing scores a world model by what a planner can do with it

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Why it blocks the target

Every world model in the wiki is trained and reported on a *prediction* metric — reconstruction likelihood, embedding distance, video fidelity — and the robotics literature states outright that these do not order models by downstream control performance: a model with sharper predictions may support a worse or less safe policy. Without such a metric, model selection is done by proxy and the wave's architectural comparisons are not decidable.

## Best current answer

Two workarounds exist and both fail. End-to-end task success confounds the model with the planner and the policy. World-model-based policy evaluation (WorldEval, EnerVerse-AC) scores policies *against* the model, which is the wrong direction and is circular for this purpose. **(brainstorm)** What is wanted is computed on the model alone and monotone in planning utility — rollout-error growth weighted by the *decisions it flips*, not by pixels. **One computable quantity now predicts the direction of the failure**, though it scores the *data* rather than the model: the weakest conditional action excitation `ρ_tr(π) = λ_min(E_z[Cov_π(a|z)])` of the behaviour policy bounds how much larger counterfactual error can be than on-policy excess risk (`δ → δ/ρ_tr`, attainably), and goal-conditioned planning error was measured to track it while training loss did not (Zhang et al. 2026). It is a *necessary-condition* check — high `ρ_tr` does not certify a good model — so the row stays open, but a model selected on prediction loss from low-`ρ_tr` data can now be rejected without running the planner. **And the divergence now has a measured instance.** Two world models differing only in whether their pretraining had an action condition sit 20.34 vs 23.58 PSNR apart on unseen Habitat — a gradient — and 26.00% vs 56.67% apart on Procgen goal-reaching, where 26.00% *is* the random planner's score. A ~14% perceptual gap and a total control gap are the same gap (Gao et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/adaworld.md]]). Success rate under a fixed planner is still not a metric on the model alone — it confounds model with planner — but it is the first pair in the wiki where the two metrics disagree about *kind* rather than degree. **And the term it was not tracking is not the prediction space** (Maes et al. 2026, [[wiki/entities/lewm.md]]): a 15M end-to-end JEPA whose latent is a single 192-d vector per frame plans ~48× faster than a DINO-WM built on a frozen foundation encoder under the same planner, and outperforms it on Push-T and OGBench-Cube at **matched FLOPs** — so planning cost is set by the model's token count, and a fixed-compute comparison reverses the ranking a fixed-episode comparison gives. **And the row now has a term it was not tracking: the model's own inference cost.** Under an identical cross-entropy-method planner, energy and GPU, an embedding-space world model evaluates 800 candidate actions in 16 s where a 7B latent-diffusion video model evaluates 80 in 4 min, and the video model scores 0%/20%/0%/0% on grasp and pick-and-place against 60%/20%/80%/50% (Assran et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]]). A search's quality is a function of how many candidates it can afford, so any metric that scores a world model *by what a planner can do with it* has to be a metric on the (accuracy, latency) pair — and every proposal in this row so far scores accuracy alone. **And the row's complaint reproduces itself in a paper whose central claim is about planning** ([[wiki/entities/gcq.md]]): GCQ argues that its representation makes goal-directed planning constant-cost per step, and reports FID and PSNR against two vector-quantisation baselines as its entire quantitative comparison, with planning, inverse modelling and zero-shot transfer shown by figure only. When the metric a field has is a prediction metric, the planning claim goes unmeasured even by authors who are making it. **And one escape from the row's framing, which works only because there is no planner.** Where a world model is used as a structure-extraction instrument rather than as a rollout engine, its output can be scored by a downstream task with no search in it: swapping appearance-defined masks for intervention-defined ones raises edit adherence by 0.081–0.143 across *four unrelated* editing pipelines, which attributes the gain to the model's extracted ontology rather than to any optimiser (Venkatesh et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/spelkenet.md]]). The generalisable move is to score a world model on a downstream consumer that is **held fixed and is not a search procedure**. Its limit is that the same paper reports no latency figure at all, which is the term this row added above.
