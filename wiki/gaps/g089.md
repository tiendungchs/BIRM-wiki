# G89 — No architecture in the wiki models its own competence, and nothing calibrates the estimates that come closest

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An architecture maintains a calibrated competence model across queries — amortised, not per-check — and its abstention or verification decisions beat a confidence scalar read off the same forward pass.

## Why it blocks the target

Every use half of latent-graph discovery has to decide *whether to act on its own output* — abstain, verify, re-plan, or call something external. The wiki's answers are all internal scalars derived from the same forward pass that produced the answer: a decision variable crossing a bound ([[wiki/concepts/evidence-accumulation.md]]), an expected-free-energy term ([[wiki/concepts/expected-free-energy.md]]), a refinement loop's own critique ([[wiki/concepts/refinement-loop.md]]). None is a persistent model of what this system is *good at*, and none has a calibrator with a referent outside the estimating loop. Biology says both halves exist and are anatomically separate, and that the uncorrected default is optimistically biased — so an architecture that estimates its own competence while being trained on a signal correlated with claimed competence should be expected to inflate, invisibly, with no internal signal that it has ([[wiki/concepts/default-self-model.md]])

## Best current answer

Nothing here holds either half. The nearest is [[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]], which supplies the *independence* requirement a calibrator needs but scores single outputs rather than maintaining a competence model, and pays a per-check cost rather than amortising into a stored estimate

**The measuring instrument now exists here even though the component does not** ([[wiki/concepts/metacognitive-efficiency.md]], Fleming & Lau 2014). Three consequences for this row. (i) The `Closes when` clause — *beat a confidence scalar read off the same forward pass* — is now a defined quantity rather than a wish: `meta-d′/d′` is the fraction of the decision's own evidence that reaches the confidence read-out, a same-forward-pass scalar should sit at the ideal-observer bound by construction, and `meta-d′ > d′` is exactly the signature of a monitor that is not a read-out. (ii) The wiki's three existing self-knowledge numbers are **not** partial answers to this row and are not comparable to each other: HLE's RMS calibration error is a bias term, MATH's AUROC 68.8% is an uncorrected sensitivity term, GPQA's 4% → 37% abstention is a criterion shift. (iii) A blocking obstacle is now named: type 1 `d′` is undefined for open-ended generation, so efficiency can currently only be measured on multiple-choice slices, and the three proposed workarounds are unrun

**The `Closes when` clause now has a hard baseline attached, and it points the wrong way** (Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017, [[wiki/concepts/selective-prediction.md]]). This row asks for an abstention decision that *beats a confidence scalar read off the same forward pass*. On closed-set classification that scalar — raw max-softmax, zero extra compute — supports **1% test risk at 78.6% coverage** on CIFAR-10 and **2% top-5 risk at 59.4% coverage** on ImageNet, each with a distribution-free bound at `δ = 0.001`; and the one alternative tried (MC-dropout uncertainty over repeated perturbed passes) is identical on CIFAR and a **factor of two worse** on ImageNet top-1. So the baseline this row was written to dismiss is the strongest known method in its own regime, and whether that survives into open-ended generation is `T320`. Two things the source *does* supply that this row lacked: (i) the first **guaranteed** competence claim in the wiki — an abstention policy whose error bound is proved rather than measured, via the tightest binomial tail plus a union bound over the `⌈log₂ m⌉` threshold search; (ii) the minimum viable form of the amortised competence model this row asks for — a **per-problem-class threshold vector** `{θ_c}` fitted against per-class labelled samples, which is a calibrated statement of what the system is good at, has its calibrator (the labels) genuinely outside the estimating loop, and costs one sort per class. What it does not supply is anything that survives distribution shift: the bound is over a fixed `P`, the calibrator is a labelled sample rather than a model, and `f` and `g` are trained separately (the source names joint training as its main open problem)

**The calibrator this row asks for exists, costs one float, and repairs the wrong half** (Guo et al. 2017, [[wiki/concepts/confidence-calibration.md]]). This row wants a competence estimate with a referent outside the estimating loop. A temperature `T` fitted by NLL on a held-out labelled set is exactly that, at one parameter and ~10 conjugate-gradient iterations, and it removes essentially the whole calibration defect of every modern architecture tested (CIFAR-100/ResNet-110 ECE 16.53% → 1.26%; ImageNet/ResNet-152 5.48% → 1.86%) — while *strictly more general* calibrators lose to it (vector scaling's fitted gain comes back near-constant; matrix scaling at `K=100` makes ECE worse than doing nothing and does not converge at `K=1000`), so the defect is **rank-1**. Three consequences. (i) The bias half of this row is cheap and solved for closed-set tasks; what remains is entirely the sensitivity half, which no post-hoc transform can supply because temperature scaling is rank-preserving and therefore moves `AUROC2`, the risk–coverage curve and the accuracy by *exactly zero* — the same-forward-pass baseline the `Closes when` clause must beat is not weakened at all by being miscalibrated. (ii) It sharpens `T315` from the machine side: the wiki now has a system whose self-assessment is demonstrably *inflated and fully repairable by an affine correction* (position **A**'s structure) with its discrimination untouched, but only where a normalised posterior over a bounded label set exists. (iii) It names the source of the inflation as the capability-increasing part of the recipe — capacity, Batch Normalization and low weight decay all worsen calibration while improving accuracy, and after the training set is fit NLL keeps falling by *sharpening* the softmax — which predicts that a competence model attached to a scaling architecture must be refitted rather than trained once, and is disputed at frontier scale (`T325`)
