# G108 — No label-free criterion selects a good representation out of a training run

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A quantity computable from the model and the unlabelled training data alone ranks checkpoints or runs by a *structural* property of the representation (factorization, invariance, graph fidelity) and the ranking is validated against ground truth on a dataset it was not tuned on.

## Why it blocks the target

Every unsupervised representation objective in the wiki has a large solution set, and the run that lands in the good part of it must be picked out somehow. Locatello et al. 2019 ([[wiki/concepts/disentanglement.md]]) measures the size of the hole: across >12,000 models, the objective explains 37% of the disentanglement score's variance and objective × regularization strength 59%, leaving ~41% to the **random seed**; reconstruction error, KL, ELBO and estimated total correlation have no consistent rank correlation with any disentanglement metric; and transferring good hyperparameters across datasets beats *random* model selection only 59.3% of the time (54.9% if the metric also changes). Since all six disentanglement metrics require ground-truth factors or the full generative model, tuning on them is supervision — so the unsupervised setting has no admissible selector at all, and a reported "our method disentangles" is a claim that a good run *exists*.

This is the practical residue of `G16`/`G44`: even where an inductive bias does select the right solution set, nothing says which run in it you got.

## Best current answer

**One partial, from a different corner of the literature.** LeJEPA's *training loss value* correlates with downstream linear-probe accuracy at Spearman `ρ_s ≈ 0.85` across architectures, datasets, learning rates and epoch budgets, and `≈0.99` after rescaling by `λ^0.4` ([[wiki/entities/lejepa.md]], [[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]]) — a monotone label-free ordering over checkpoints, which is exactly the instrument this row asks for. Three reasons it does not close the row: it ranks by *linear-probe accuracy*, an informativeness measure, not by any structural property; the correlation is reported for one loss family, and the whole point of Locatello's finding is that ELBO-like quantities correlate on some datasets and not others; and the ordering is unavailable for the EMA lineage at all, since a two-timescale dynamics has no loss whose value would mean anything ([[wiki/entities/byol.md]]).

The alternative currently in use everywhere is **seed averaging with the variance reported**, which measures the hole rather than filling it — and almost no source in the wiki does even that ([[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]] audit item 1).
