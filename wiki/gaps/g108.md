# G108 — No label-free criterion selects a good representation out of a training run

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A quantity computable from the model and the unlabelled training data alone ranks checkpoints or runs by a *structural* property of the representation (factorization, invariance, graph fidelity) and the ranking is validated against ground truth on a dataset it was not tuned on.

## Why it blocks the target

Every unsupervised representation objective in the wiki has a large solution set, and the run that lands in the good part of it must be picked out somehow. Locatello et al. 2019 ([[wiki/concepts/disentanglement.md]]) measures the size of the hole: across >12,000 models, the objective explains 37% of the disentanglement score's variance and objective × regularization strength 59%, leaving ~41% to the **random seed**; reconstruction error, KL, ELBO and estimated total correlation have no consistent rank correlation with any disentanglement metric; and transferring good hyperparameters across datasets beats *random* model selection only 59.3% of the time (54.9% if the metric also changes). Since all six disentanglement metrics require ground-truth factors or the full generative model, tuning on them is supervision — so the unsupervised setting has no admissible selector at all, and a reported "our method disentangles" is a claim that a good run *exists*.

This is the practical residue of `G16`/`G44`: even where an inductive bias does select the right solution set, nothing says which run in it you got.

## Best current answer

**Home page: [[wiki/concepts/label-free-model-selection.md]]**, which holds the full candidate table.

**The strongest candidate is `RankMe`** (Garrido et al. 2023): `RankMe(Z) = exp(−Σ_k p_k log p_k)` with `p_k = σ_k(Z)/‖σ(Z)‖₁ + ε`, the entropy of the ℓ¹-normalised singular values of the source dataset's **embedding** matrix. No labels, no training, no hyperparameters, one SVD on 25,600 samples. Over 110 models × 5 joint-embedding methods × 11 datasets it selects hyperparameters within **half a point on average** of a labelled ImageNet linear-probe oracle, on representations and embeddings, in distribution and out — and *beats* the labelled oracle out of distribution in three places (SimCLR temperature 56.4 vs 54.7; iNat-18-pretrained VICReg covariance weight 60.91 vs 60.70). The rival spectrum criterion `α-ReQ` has worst cases of 32.3 against a 68.3 oracle. It also survives leaving the linear regime it was derived in: `k`-NN, a 3-layer MLP head, semi-supervised fine-tuning and VOC detection fine-tuning all preserve the relation, and on VOC AP50 it matches or beats the ImageNet linear oracle (79.7 vs 78.2).

**Why the row does not close on it.** The `Closes when` above asks for a ranking by a *structural* property. RankMe ranks by **capacity**: effective rank counts how many directions survived, not how they are organised. Its theory says so explicitly — Eckart–Young–Mirsky plus `rank(ZW + 1bᵀ) ≤ min(rank Z, rank W) + 1` plus Cover's theorem bound the *training* accuracy of an arbitrary linear read-out, and a representation that is full-rank by memorisation scores exactly like one that is full-rank by factorisation. The out-of-distribution correlation is carried by an empirical hypothesis about corpora (`rank(Z_target) ∝ rank(Z_source)`, Pearson > 0.99, but weakest on StanfordCars, the dataset that barely overlaps ImageNet), not by the theory. Three further restrictions: it compares runs **within one method** only, so it selects a hyperparameter and not an objective; it **saturates** once the rank exceeds the downstream class count, which is the regime a low-dimensional structural code `g` lives in; and it is computed on the projector output, the space T314 argues is supposed to be rank-deficient.

**The other partials.** LeJEPA's *training loss value* correlates with downstream linear-probe accuracy at Spearman `ρ_s ≈ 0.85`, and `≈0.99` after rescaling by `λ^0.4` ([[wiki/entities/lejepa.md]], [[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]]) — but it ranks by linear-probe accuracy, an informativeness measure; it is reported for one loss family; and it is unavailable to the EMA lineage at all, since a two-timescale dynamics has no loss whose value would mean anything ([[wiki/entities/byol.md]]). `(L_align, L_uniform)` is parameter-free, validated over 521 encoders across two modalities and — uniquely — against a dense read-out, but it is two numbers rather than an ordering ([[wiki/concepts/alignment-uniformity.md]]).

The alternative currently in use everywhere is **seed averaging with the variance reported**, which measures the hole rather than filling it — and almost no source in the wiki does even that ([[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]] audit item 1).

**The cheap experiment that would move this row.** Compute RankMe on the codes of Locatello's >12,000-model sweep, where ground-truth factors exist. Correlation with a disentanglement metric would make rank structural and bring the row close to closing; correlation only with reconstruction quality would establish that the wiki has a capacity selector and still no structure selector.

## Status

`OPEN` — there are now two working label-free selectors and both rank the wrong quantity. RankMe ranks by capacity and is validated out of distribution; the LeJEPA loss ranks by linear-probe accuracy and does not apply to the EMA lineage. Neither ranks by a structural property, and neither is calibrated against partial collapse. The row is in better shape than it was and its `Closes when` is untouched.
