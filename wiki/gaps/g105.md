# G105 — Every graph-discovery mechanism in the wiki estimates pairs; nothing infers a relation among three or more elements from data

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A learner infers an `n`-ary relation (`n ≥ 3`) directly from observations — not by composing or thresholding pairwise scores — and is shown to recover structure that no pairwise estimator on the same data can, on a task where the higher-order relation is the latent variable.

## Why it blocks the target

The relations the wiki's own benchmarks are built from are not dyadic: an ARC transformation, a Raven's row, a PGM `(relation, object, attribute)` triple ([[wiki/entities/pgm.md]]). The wiki's *representations* hold them without difficulty — an associative binder plus superposition stores a relation with any number of role–filler pairs at fixed width, and a bound relation can itself fill a role ([[wiki/concepts/vector-symbolic-binding.md]]). Every mechanism that *discovers* structure is nonetheless a pairwise operator: attention is a query–key matrix, Hebbian co-fluctuation is a covariance ([[wiki/entities/hag-reservoir.md]]), inverse covariance is a precision matrix ([[wiki/entities/fcann.md]]), transition counting is over `(s, s')`. A regularity carried only by a triple — present in the combination and absent from all three of its projections — is therefore not merely hard to find, it is outside the hypothesis space. The same blindness covers the **cavity**: structure defined by edges being *absent* in a particular arrangement, which every weighted-adjacency statistic scores only as a small number, and which is the representation the wiki's world models are missing when they cannot express a wall or an unreachable state (`G6`)

## Best current answer

Only the analysis-side vocabulary, imported and unused: simplicial complexes and hypergraphs as the objects, filtration plus persistent homology and Betti curves as a threshold-free way to summarise a weighted network, and cliques/cavities as the features they expose — applied to cortical microcircuit connectivity, they report unexpectedly many directed all-to-all cliques and non-random cavities against a null (Bassett & Sporns 2017, [[wiki/concepts/higher-order-interactions.md]]). Every one of these *analyses* a complex obtained by thresholding an already-estimated pairwise matrix. Nothing estimates a three-way relation from data the way covariance estimates a pair, and the review cites no result in which a higher-order feature predicts anything better than a pairwise one — so the case is currently structural rather than empirical. The nearest adjacent measurement is the note that correlation-based edges see *redundant* information only and that synergistic information needs multivariate partial-entropy measures ([[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]])

**The structural case now has a measurement** ([[wiki/entities/esbn.md]], Webb, Sinha & Cohen 2021). The Relation Network is the wiki's purest pairwise estimator — an MLP over every pair of object embeddings, summed. Across four tasks under one protocol it is at **100.0** on both *binary* tasks (same/different, relational match-to-sample) at `m = 0` and at **35.6** and **41.5** on the two **ternary** tasks (distribution-of-three, identity rules) in the same easiest regime, i.e. near chance on a relation it can represent only by composing pairs. A ternary-subsampling variant (Temporal Relation Network) does not repair it; an order of magnitude more training data partially does. So the pairwise restriction is not merely a hypothesis-space argument: it shows up as a sample-complexity wall at `n = 3` on tasks whose binary analogues the same architecture solves perfectly. Note the scope — this measures a pairwise *comparator over given objects*, not a pairwise graph estimator, so it bounds the cost of composing pairs rather than proving a three-way estimator would do better.

**And the first construction that reaches `n ≥ 3` — by stacking, which is not what the row asks for** ([[wiki/concepts/relational-bottleneck.md]], Webb et al. 2024). Feeding the output of one relational bottleneck into another computes relations *between relations*; relational convolutional networks (Altabaa & Lafferty 2023) do this hierarchically and are reported to beat both a non-hierarchical relational architecture (CoRelNet) and a deep non-relational one (a transformer) on higher-order relational tasks. Read against this row's `Closes when`, it is the wrong shape twice over: the higher-order representation is **composed from pairwise inner products**, which is precisely the route the row excludes, and it is a *comparator over given objects* rather than an estimator of a latent three-way relation. What it does establish is that composing pairs is not hopeless when the composition is given an architecture rather than left to a generic decoder — the Relation Network's ternary wall above is a wall for a *flat* pairwise sum, not for a stacked one. Carried second-hand: the review reports no experiment.

**The first computable higher-order estimator, and it measures rather than learns** ([[wiki/concepts/o-information.md]], Rosas et al. 2019). `Ω(Xⁿ) = C(Xⁿ) − B(Xⁿ) = (n−2)H(Xⁿ) + Σ_j[H(X_j) − H(X⁻ʲ)]` reads the redundancy-vs-synergy balance of `n` variables off the joint distribution: symmetric (no predictor/target split, unlike every partial-information-decomposition variant), `2n+1` entropy terms instead of a super-exponentially growing lattice, zero for any pair so it scores only order ≥ 3, equal to the interaction information at `n = 3`, and uniquely extremised by the `n`-bit copy (`Ω = n−2`) and the `n`-bit xor (`Ω = 2−n`). The local form `ω_ij = I(X_i;X_j;X⁻ⁱʲ)` gives an `O(n²)` signed map whose entries may oppose the global sign — measured, on Baroque scores, as a locally synergistic violin pair inside a globally redundant quartet, with the redundancy tracing to two instruments realising one written bass line.

Two things this changes and one it does not. It **retires the "no result shows a higher-order feature doing anything a pairwise one cannot"** complaint above at the level of a *worked distribution*: an `n`-bit xor has every pairwise mutual information exactly zero while `Ω` is at its minimum, so covariance, precision-matrix, attention-score and transition-count estimators do not merely lose precision there, they return the empty graph. It also **prices the instrument** — the `2n+1`-term cost is cheap given the joint, and estimating the joint of `n` variables is the real bottleneck (the case study fits a `13⁴`-cell table from `4×10⁴` samples by plug-in frequencies). What it does **not** do is close the row: `Ω` scores a *given* set of variables and does not search for which set, `Ω = 0` is ambiguous between balanced synergy/redundancy and disjoint pairwise structure, and nothing here is a learner that recovers latent `n`-ary structure. Read against `Closes when`, this supplies the discriminator such a learner would be scored with, not the learner.

**And the first entry where the higher-order term is a *learnable parameter* rather than an analysis object** ([[wiki/entities/dense-associative-memory.md]], Krotov & Hopfield 2016). The Dense Associative Memory energy `E = −Σ_μ F(ξ^μ·σ)` with `F(x) = x^n` is literally an `n`-body interaction among the units, and the `ξ^μ` in it are fit by gradient descent. The worked case is this row's own canonical blindness: XOR on `N = 3` units with the four truth-table rows embedded as `K = 4 > N` memories has energy `C_n·xyz` for odd `n ≥ 3`, giving `z = Sign[−xy]`, while `n = 1, 2` — the pairwise case, which *is* the linear perceptron — leaves the energy independent of the output unit and cannot solve it at all. So a three-way regularity with zero pairwise signal is inside the hypothesis space here, and the model that holds it also gains capacity `α_n N^{n−1}` from the same term. Two reasons the row stays `OPEN`. The `n`-way interaction tensor is constrained to a **sum of rank-one terms** `(ξ^μ·σ)^n`, so an arbitrary hyperedge — a relation among a specific triple, independent of any direction in the space — is not representable; and the XOR memories are *hand-embedded* from the truth table rather than discovered, while the MNIST memories are fit against labels, so nothing here recovers a latent `n`-ary relation from observations. Read against `Closes when`, this supplies the missing *architecture* for holding an `n`-ary term under learning, not the estimator that finds one.
