# G101 — Nothing in the wiki scales *edge density* rather than unit count, and no result says what a higher synapse-per-unit ratio buys

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An experiment scales edge density at fixed unit count and reports what the higher synapse-per-unit ratio buys, separating it from the effect of adding units.

## Why it blocks the target

Human cerebral cortex is **2.75×** chimpanzee volume with only **1.25×** the neurons (Shariff 1953): the tripling that supposedly explains human cognition is overwhelmingly neuropil — dendrites, axons, synapses — not units. Corroborating measurements point the same way: the glia:neuron ratio is the highest of any primate and rises with brain size; the excess temporal-lobe white matter sits in **gyral** rather than core white matter, i.e. it is *local* connectivity between neighbouring fields; humans have as much eulaminate cortex as predicted and slightly **fewer** eulaminate neurons. Every scaling axis the wiki holds is a unit-count or depth axis, and the one biological example of a scale-up that produced a qualitative change is not on that axis. It blocks the target because the wiki's `G70`/`G21` failures are attributed to missing mechanisms and the comparative record says the same failures might be a connectivity-density regime the wiki has never entered. **The row's premise is now contested, and the challenge also supplies the sweep the row asks for** (Herculano-Houzel 2009, [[wiki/concepts/cellular-scaling-rules.md]]): whole-brain isotropic-fractionator counts across 18 species give the edge-density axis a measured exponent, `M ∼ N^a` with `a > 1` meaning average unit size — soma plus entire arbour — grows with unit count. Rodents scale edge density (`N^1.550` whole brain, `N^1.744` in cortex, falling neuronal density, rising glia:neuron ratio); primates do not (`N^1.056`, `N^1.077`, invariant density and invariant non-neuronal ratio). So nature *has* run this row's experiment across two orders, and the clade that bought units at constant wiring-per-unit holds **seven times** the neurons of the clade that bought wiring-per-unit, at the same 1.5 kg. Under the primate exponent, Shariff's 2.75×-volume-for-1.25×-neurons is ~2.5× the neurons instead ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T301), and the human cortex's 16 billion neurons are what a primate regression fitted without humans predicts. Two things keep the row open anyway: the primate cortical fit contains **no great apes**, so a hominid-specific departure is untested, and the comparison is between-clade rather than at fixed unit count, which is what the `Closes when` actually asks for

## Best current answer

**Nothing measured.** The wiki has the ingredients and no experiment: [[wiki/concepts/small-world-topology.md]] holds the wiring-cost statistics, [[wiki/concepts/dendritic-computation.md]] holds the per-unit-compute argument (a single neuron with active dendrites is a multilayer network), and [[wiki/concepts/emergent-modularity.md]] holds the one causal proposal that lives on this axis — that generalised systematicity is bought by afferent *diversity* into a fixed composition site, which is a fan-in claim and is directly sweepable. The source does not say what the ratio buys either: capacity, precision, mixing, or nothing beyond the metabolic price of being large. `G101` and `G100` are the two halves of the same complaint — the wiki scales the wrong variable and does so all at once
