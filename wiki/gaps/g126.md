# G126 — The procedure that manufactures a subgoal also trains its suppressor; no machine subgoal mechanism has an opponent term

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A hierarchical agent represents subgoal pull as the **sum of two separately-learned, opposite-signed quantities** with different time constants, and the decomposition is shown to matter: (a) with the opponent term ablated, extended training on a subgoal that is not followed by primary reward produces runaway pursuit that the intact version terminates, and (b) the intact agent's net subgoal value is non-monotonic in training amount, reproducing the inverted-U, while a single-quantity agent of matched initial magnitude is monotonic.

## Why it blocks the target

Second-order conditioning — the wiki's mechanism for manufacturing a want out of a pairing ([[wiki/concepts/conditioned-reinforcement.md]], `G72`) — is run as `S1 → US` on every trial **except** the `S2 → S1` trials, which end with no US. That is exactly the **feature-negative discrimination**, the standard recipe for building a conditioned inhibitor (Gewirtz & Davis 2000, [[wiki/concepts/higher-order-conditioning.md]]). So the subgoal's own construction trains a suppressor of it, on the same trials, and the observed behaviour is their sum:

| Component | Growth |
|---|---|
| second-order excitation | fast, early asymptote |
| conditioned inhibition | slower, ultimately **stronger** |
| net response to `S2` | **inverted U** in number of training trials |

This is strictly stronger than `G125`, which asks only that an intermediate reward *decay* when unbacked. Here an unbacked delivery does not merely fail to reinforce — it is a positive learning event for an antagonist that eventually wins. Every self-generated reward in the wiki is a single non-negative quantity:

| Mechanism | Subgoal quantity | Opponent term? |
|---|---|---|
| [[wiki/concepts/pseudo-reward-prediction-error.md]] | `r̃ = 1` | none |
| [[wiki/concepts/eigenoption-discovery.md]] | `rᵉ(s,s′) = eᵀ(φ(s′) − φ(s))` | none (signed, but one quantity) |
| [[wiki/concepts/hindsight-goal-relabelling.md]] | `r(s,a,g) = −[f_g(s′) = 0]` | none |
| [[wiki/concepts/learning-progress.md]] | `d/dt` prediction error | none — decay is by mastery, not by an antagonist |

**Two consequences a builder inherits and cannot currently express.** (i) There is no training amount at which a printed pseudo-reward stops pulling, so the farming failure `G125` describes has no self-limiting term even in principle. (ii) Any intervention that *reduces* measured subgoal pursuit is ambiguous between weakening the excitation and strengthening the inhibition — the source flags this as a live confound in the animal literature, where a manipulation that improved learning overall read as an impairment. Every ablation in the wiki that reports "the intrinsic reward mattered less" is exposed to the same inversion.

**The measurement half is worse than the modelling half.** In fear-potentiated startle the two components are **temporally segregated within the trial**: probing during `S2` gives second-order fear growing **monotonically** with training and no inverted U at all; probing after `S2` offset gives conditioned *inhibition* of startle. Same animals, same training, opposite sign, and the difference is the read-out window. Every subgoal-value and intrinsic-reward measurement in the wiki is a trial- or episode-averaged scalar, which returns the sum and reads neither.

## Best current answer

**None as an architecture.** Two things are on record and both are levers rather than solutions.

- **The biological escape is a design lever, and it is cheap.** Partial reinforcement during first-order training — mix `S1+` and `S1−` trials rather than reinforcing `S1` every time — badly damages conditioned inhibition while leaving second-order conditioning robust. The reason is mechanical: if the goal-cue is already unreliable on its own, the subgoal's presence stops being the feature that predicts omission. The machine transcription is direct and untested: **make the higher level's reward delivery stochastic so that the option's presence is not the informative predictor of a non-paying trial.** It costs one hyperparameter and predicts a specific trade (slower first-order learning, a subgoal that survives extended training).
- **The read-out escape.** Move the probe inside the trial rather than averaging over it. For a hierarchical agent this is measuring subgoal value at the moment the option is active versus at the moment it terminates — two numbers where every current report gives one.

The nearest existing constructs change a different quantity. [[wiki/concepts/affective-opponency.md]] has opponent pairs, but built by chemistry or by projection target and applied to *primary* outcome valence, never to a manufactured intermediate target, and with no differing time constants. `G125` supplies the extinction dynamic without the antagonist. `T367` asks where the subgoal error is routed and is orthogonal — an opponent term is needed on either routing.
