# G61 — Exploration is always an external schedule, never part of the selector

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** Exploration rate is an output of the learned selector, driven by the same signal that sets action preference, and matches or beats a tuned external schedule without that schedule being available.

## Why it blocks the target

Every architecture in the wiki that has to try something it does not already prefer gets its stochasticity from outside the learned policy — [[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]'s `Random Go` fires at a hand-set `p = .1` when a stripe's average `δ` has been negative and it has not fired in 10 trials, and the ablation shows the hardest task is unlearnable without it, so a load-bearing component of the model is a rule nothing learned and nothing anneals. A controller that cannot set its own exploration rate cannot notice that the value landscape has gone flat, cannot decide that *this* decision is worth sampling and *that* one is not, and cannot stop exploring when the environment has been solved. This is the action-side twin of G50 (the controller cannot set the gain of its own teaching signal)

## Best current answer

One concrete alternative, from outside the wiki's model set: make the exploration rate the **default output of the veto channel**. Chakravarthy's family inserts an *explore* regime between `Go` and `NoGo` whose substrate is the indirect pathway — when the value gradient is absent or too weak to learn from, that channel sets the stochasticity of action selection, and because dopamine already sets the balance between the two channels, a flat value landscape produces neither the transient nor the dip that would push the balance to an extreme, so annealing falls out of the same scalar that does selection (Magdoom et al. 2011; Krishnan et al. 2011). It is fitted only to Parkinsonian motor kinematics, it competes with the `NoGo` reading of the same anatomy ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T132), it competes with a *second* claim on the same anatomical ratio — that the balance sets the discount factor, not the temperature (T133) — and no machine architecture has been built on it. **The alternative answer keeps exploration global but gives it a computed set-point**: noradrenaline carries the inverse temperature, raised when the value estimate is very high or very low and lowered when the action values at the current state are widely spread, so the annealing schedule is replaced by two readouts the agent already has (Doya 2002; Ishii et al. 2002, [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]]). Neither route is implemented, and they differ on whether the exploration rate is per-state (Ishii) or per-situation (Doya's `V`-driven law). **A third route makes exploration a property of the substrate's operating point rather than of any selector**: park a delayed, noisy modular network near the onset of oscillatory instability and it samples its own reachable configurations continuously, with no exploration parameter, no schedule and no annealing anywhere — the free variables being the noise amplitude (which has an interior optimum, stochastic resonance) and the delay matrix, and The failure mode of the third route is *too much* stability, since far inside the stable region the oscillatory return is suppressed and the noise produces nothing structured (Deco, Jirsa & McIntosh 2011, [[wiki/concepts/dynamic-repertoire.md]]). What it explores is the space of *network configurations*, not the space of actions, so it answers this row only for an architecture whose choices are which modules to couple. That third route now has a **regulator** rather than only a description: `σ_R`, the temporal variance of the global order parameter, is measurable from module activity alone, has an interior optimum, and is moved by a single global coupling gain — so the operating point can be held in a band the way a homeostat holds a firing rate, and the criticality claim behind it survives across six incompatible node models rather than one (Cabral, Kringelbach & Deco 2014, [[wiki/concepts/metastability.md]]). Still unbuilt, and still exploration over configurations rather than over actions. **A fourth route makes the exploration trigger a *detector* rather than a value readout**: a predictive module compares stimuli against predictions and, on mismatch, emits a transient that flattens the landscape — so exploration starts at the contingency change itself, strictly earlier than any `V`- or `Var_a Q`-driven law can fire, and the schedule has a measurable target in the entropy of actions conditioned on the correct response, `H(A|C)` (Mei et al. 2025, [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]]). Also unimplemented, and it inherits the detector's own false-alarm rate as a new free parameter. **A second alternative, and this one is implemented and ablated**: take the exploration signal from the agent's *own world model* — the variance of a deep ensemble predicting the next latent state — and add it to the predicted extrinsic reward ([[wiki/entities/continual-dreamer.md]], Plan2Explore inside DreamerV2). It is the only exploration mechanism in the wiki that is not a schedule: it needs no task boundary (disagreement rises when the environment changes, which is the same event), nothing anneals it by hand, and it falls to zero where the model has become accurate. What it does *not* do is close the row. The two mixing coefficients `α_i = α_e = 0.9` are grid-searched constants, so the controller still does not set its own exploration *rate*; the sign of its contribution flips with benchmark difficulty (−0.26 average performance on 3-task Minigrid, +0.19 on 8-task Minihack, [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T284); and it fails entirely below a buffer size the plain agent still learns at.
