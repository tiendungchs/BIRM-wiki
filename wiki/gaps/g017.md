# G17 — No evaluation protocol can certify structure discovery

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An instrument certifies that a *discovered* meta-graph, not a sample correlation, was recovered — and does so on a domain it was not designed for. `I1`–`I16` at [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]] are the candidates; one must survive a negative control that fakes structure.

## Why it blocks the target

i.i.d. testing cannot separate a recovered meta-graph from a correlation holding in the sample, so every "architecture X reasons" claim is unscoreable and the hardness-source scoring table has no valid instrument. Existing o.o.d. benchmarks are domain-specific, hand-built, and must be revised as models learn to game them (the Winograd Schema Challenge was found to contain the shortcuts it was designed to remove)

## Best current answer

**Sixteen instruments, none sufficient, now inventoried at [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]** (split out of this cell at the 212-ingest lint pass, at 20,336 characters and with colliding ordinals; the canonical numbering is `I1`–`I16` there). The shape of the answer, which is what this row now carries: instruments are cheap in proportion to how little they need — the two that need nothing but the model itself are the **support/query split** (`I8`: induce structure from one pair, execute it on a second from the same mechanism; the *gap* between 0.994 self-fit and 0.000 transfer is the measurement) and the **consistency score restricted to items the model got wrong** (`I9`: 0.90 → **0.34** across three architectures) — while the only instrument that is a *quantity* rather than a genre, developer-aware generalization difficulty `GD` (`I5`), has no approximation and is computed for exactly zero tasks (G31). Ten failure modes are shared across the inventory rather than belonging to any instrument, and three of them close candidate answers rather than qualifying them: **developer-blindness is a depleting stock** (`F1`: ~10,000 reported scores against 100 private tasks over five years), **item-level blindness does not imply distributional blindness and the two clauses conflict with difficulty calibration** (`F2`, [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T207), and **`GD ≈ 0` items are unaudited because difficulty is asserted per-benchmark and never measured per-task** (`F3`: 49% of ARC-AGI-1's private set fell to blind brute-force search in 2020, [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T205). Two conclusions the inventory now supports and no single instrument stated: **an honest instrument returns a facet vector, not a number** — reached from the behaviour side (0.92 i.i.d. accuracy against 0.72/0.50/0.54 systematicity/productivity/localism) and from the task side (a concept-variation score that *rises* 10 points on one concept and falls 11 on another) — and **the certification target is the developer, not the system**, since skill on any task known in advance is purchasable outright. What this leaves the row: the honest current answer is that G17 is a **maintenance schedule rather than a protocol** — benchmark authorship run as a [[wiki/concepts/refinement-loop.md]] against the field, stopping when authoring tasks easy for humans and hard for AI becomes impossible — which is a different kind of answer from the one the row asks for, and the only one with five years of evidence behind it. **The first instrument whose output is a *dial* rather than a reading** (`I32`, [[wiki/entities/cfq.md]]): construct the train/test split by optimising a **computed** divergence over derivation subgraphs — maximise compound divergence `D_C` while holding atom divergence `D_A ≤ 0.02` as a control — and accuracy becomes monotone in `D_C` at `R² = 0.81–0.88` for three architectures on two datasets, against `R² = 0.11–0.22` for the length ratios the field splits on. This is a partial answer to the row's own complaint that existing o.o.d. benchmarks are "domain-specific, hand-built": the shift magnitude is now **comparable across split methods and across datasets**, published splits can be re-scored retrospectively (the field's default "query pattern" split is `D_C = 0.008`; SCAN's `add primitive` splits violate their own atom-matching control at `D_A` = 0.07–0.08), and the shift is certified *unobservable* — train and test samples at `D_C = 0.694` cannot be told apart by eye. What it does **not** do is close the row: it measures the size of a distribution shift, not whether the recovered object is the intended structure, and its atom granularity is the benchmark author's choice, so every divergence is relative to a chosen factorisation (`G16` reappearing inside the instrument). It also names a leakage channel `F1`/`F2` miss — **hyperparameters tuned on an o.o.d. validation set optimise for one known compound divergence**, leaving no trace in the reported score, so developer-blindness must cover the tuning protocol and no benchmark in the wiki reports where its baselines were tuned. Whether `D_C` is the difficulty or an average over facets that come apart is [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T312. **A defect the inventory exposed that is upstream of every instrument** ([[wiki/concepts/human-baseline.md]], `F10`): the reference system is unspecified. A human baseline is a protocol — pool, attempt budget, aggregation order, exclusion rule, item-pool identity — and the same raw data yields **73% against ~91%** on identical items and participants (T214) and **60% against 99%** on one benchmark (T213). Three tensions the wiki logged separately are one missing specification.
