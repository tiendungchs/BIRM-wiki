# G103 — No architecture imposes self–other equivalence: nothing requires that the description under which a model perceives another agent's act be the same description under which it produces that act itself

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Why it blocks the target

Tomasello & Rakoczy 2007 make this the human-specific addition — one description covering "what you did" and "what I do" (Evans' Generality Constraint), showing up as role-reversal imitation and as the bidirectionality of a linguistic symbol: the child acquires the symbol *as a producible act* the moment it comprehends it, and deictics (`I`/`you`, `here`/`there`) are acquired with the reversal already applied. It is the precondition the source names for perspective and derived normativity, and its absence is what it credits for every ape null — apes track intention-in-action and who-saw-what, and never treat the other's experience as their own from another angle

## Best current answer

**Nothing.** Perception and action live in separate parameter sets in every wiki architecture. Behavioural cloning maps observations to actions without any constraint tying the two codes; [[wiki/concepts/cross-embodiment-transfer.md]] shares a latent across bodies but never requires the shared latent to be the *same* term the perceptual encoder emits for an observed agent; inverse planning ([[wiki/entities/hbtom.md]], [[wiki/entities/autotom.md]]) infers others' goals in a representation the inferring system never executes from. The nearest thing in the wiki is [[wiki/concepts/cross-modal-grounding.md]]'s alignment of two encoders, which ties two *input* streams and neither of them to an output. The concrete form of the missing constraint is an equivalence class over (observed act, own act) pairs — a loss, not a module
