# G122 — Every hub in the wiki is drawn as one box with many inputs; nothing decides whether its afferents are mixed across the population or biased to projection-defined sub-populations

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `arrangement`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An architecture instantiates a multi-input, multi-output module in both arrangements — afferents distributed evenly over the population versus afferents biased toward projection-defined sub-populations with sparse local recurrence — and a task is exhibited on which they measurably differ, with the choice made by the model rather than by the designer.

## Why it blocks the target

The wiki's architectures are full of modules that take `n` streams and emit `m` outputs: a global workspace, a relational bottleneck, a router, an episodic store's output stage, an association cortex. Every one of them is drawn as a single population, which silently commits to one extreme of a fork that biology leaves open. Alexander et al. 2023 state it as two independent continua for retrosplenial cortex ([[wiki/entities/retrosplenial-cortex.md]]):

- **afferent targeting** — *mixed* (every source's terminals spread evenly, every cell sees every stream, the region is one mixed-selectivity population) versus *discrete* (each source biased toward a particular projection-defined sub-population, so the region is `k` semi-independent circuits sharing a volume);
- **intrinsic recurrence** — dense local `RSC`→`RSC` connectivity re-mixes segregated afferents; sparse local connectivity lets the segregation survive to the output.

This is not a realization detail, which is why it sits at `L1` rather than `L3`. The two arrangements differ in what is **architecturally denied to a reader**: under `discrete`, the output class projecting to secondary motor cortex cannot see the thalamic head-direction stream at all, so any capability requiring that conjunction is impossible rather than untrained. Under `mixed`, no output class can be given a private input, so nothing can be protected from interference by wiring. A model that "has a hub" has answered this question without being asked it.

## Best current answer

**None on the machine side; the biology's measurement is specified and not run.** Mixed selectivity ([[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]) is argued for on dimensionality grounds — conjunctive codes make arbitrary readouts linearly separable — but that argument is made for a *readout*, never for an afferent-targeting scheme, and it does not address what a projection-typed partition would buy. Sparse expert routing ([[wiki/concepts/sparse-expert-routing.md]]) partitions a module by *input*, learned, which is the dual of the question here: the biological fork partitions by **output destination** and asks which inputs each partition is allowed to see. The nearest thing to an answer in the wiki is an existence proof from a different structure: dorsal subiculum splits into adjacent subregions differing in long-range input, local wiring *and* projection target, which is the `discrete` extreme realised — so the arrangement is at least available to biology.

The decisive measurement is named by the review and has not been made in any species: for each projection class, the input density from every afferent source **including other neurons of the same region**. The human proxy — partition the region and measure mutual functional connectivity between the partitions — has not been attempted either; the only relevant datum is an anterior↔posterior connectivity gradient (posterior/lateral to visual networks, anterior/medial to default-network regions) that makes large-scale overlap unlikely without settling it.
