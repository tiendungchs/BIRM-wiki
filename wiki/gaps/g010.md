# G10 — Self-generated intermediate nodes are unreliable

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A search or commitment mechanism over a verifiably intact estimate drives off-graph rollouts to near zero at the fork decision specifically, out of distribution, without improving the underlying representation.

## Why it blocks the target

Multi-hop traversal requires the model to materialize its own intermediate states; when it does, they are not dependable graph positions, so errors compound along the path

## Best current answer

**Now measured, and it is not a representation problem.** In maze-solving transformers the graph is verifiably intact — linearly decodable at 0.83–0.99 per wall from one token — while 13–16% of in-distribution rollouts (62% out of distribution for the smaller model) still leave the graph by crossing a wall, and the single worst sub-task everywhere is the *fork decision*, not path-following. So unreliable intermediate nodes survive a correct, accessible map: what is missing is a search/commitment mechanism over the estimate, not a better estimate (Ivanitskiy et al. 2023)  **A second measurement, in a domain where the intermediate node is the model's own output rather than a hidden state** (Hupkes et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/pcfg-set.md]]): unroll a compositional expression against the model — ask for the innermost constituent's meaning, substitute the model's *own answer* back into the expression, recurse to the root — and the final answer differs from the one-shot answer in 41–54% of cases across three seq2seq architectures. The error analysis localises it: the substituted intermediate results are longer than any argument seen in training, and accuracy on a function applied to an over-length argument drops to **exactly zero at the training maximum** for the recurrent model (smoothly, to ~9 and ~12, for the attentional and convolutional ones). So the intermediate node is unreliable for a reason the maze result does not cover — the operator that must consume it was never learned as an operator over a free-length argument, only as a family of length-indexed behaviours (G69, G70).
