# G27 — Nothing supplies the discretisation the graph formalisation assumes

**Status:** `CONTESTED`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** Nodes and edges are carved from a continuous sensorimotor stream by a criterion the model computes, and the resulting discretisation supports a downstream task that a hand-supplied carving also supports — with T23 and T121 settled.

## Why it blocks the target

Nodes and edges are taken as given by every other row, but experience arrives as a continuous sensorimotor stream. Grid codes presuppose a metric space already carved up; core knowledge presupposes entities already individuated; attention selects among items already formed. Without a segmentation mechanism the graph is not merely unknown — it is undefined

## Best current answer

**Event segmentation**: an event is a set of predictive encodings holding over an extended period, a boundary is a lasting change in that set, an event schema ⟨precondition, transition, effect⟩ is a typed edge, and repeated schema chains compress into episodes (the coarse level jumpy planning needs). The *definition* is principled and covers motion onsets, occlusions, property changes and affordance changes with one detector; the *detector* is not — the two candidates are hand-thresholded change monitors and long-short-term-memory gates trained by backpropagation through time, and Butz 2016 states outright that deriving segmentation from free energy remains open. **The complementary half arrives with contextual inference** (Heald et al. 2021): a sticky hierarchical Dirichlet process over an unbounded context set makes allocation a probability rather than a threshold — a new memory is created with probability equal to the *novel-context responsibility* — and turns granularity into three hyperparameters (`γ` effective context count, `κ` persistence, `α` pooling across contexts). It predicts and confirms that an abruptly-introduced perturbation creates a new node while the same perturbation introduced gradually does not. Its price is the mirror image: a principled criterion over nodes whose content is a **single scalar**. **And the trigger side is worse than a missing detector — it is a missing *arbitration*** (Nguyen et al. 2025): prediction error and prediction uncertainty are separable control signals, each uniquely predicting human boundaries (t = 5.14, 14.32; r = 0.499 between them), recruiting dissociable networks — error → ventrolateral prefrontal shift then whole-brain pattern stabilisation, uncertainty → dorsal-attention shift with almost none — and differing in timecourse even inside the parcels that respond to both. So the boundary a downstream graph consumes should be **typed** (`REPLACE` vs. `INVESTIGATE`) and nothing in the wiki emits or consumes a boundary type; latent-cause inference is named as a likely third trigger for the ~16% of explainable variance left over. **A third family carves on a signal no passive observer has** (Taniguchi et al. 2023, survey): make the discretiser a binary bottleneck inside a predictor of *action effects*, and two situations share a symbol exactly when the agent's own repertoire cannot separate their consequences. DeepSym (Ahmetoglu et al.) runs this from raw pixels with no hand-coded feature and distils the decoder into probabilistic PDDL rules an off-the-shelf planner consumes; LatPlan (Asai et al.) reaches propositions the same way from a discrete-bottleneck autoencoder, and Konidaris et al. / James et al. obtain symbols usable directly as planning predicates. Two costs: the action repertoire `A` is handed to every one of them (so this is G27 traded against G33/G4), and the criterion is repertoire-limited — a distinction no available action makes is a distinction never symbolised, with no system reporting its own shortfall. Also the only family here that produces a node with a *precondition mask*, i.e. the entry test G23 asks for **A fourth family, and the cheapest yet: discreteness as an artefact of finite units on an imposed continuum** ([[wiki/entities/gcq.md]]). A toroidal continuous attractor network with `N²` neurons has exactly `N²` bump states, so the code is discrete without any threshold, allocation rule, bottleneck or detector — nothing decides *when* to make a new symbol because the symbol set exists before the data. This is the limiting case that shows what the other three families are buying: it supplies a node set with perfect stability, a metric, and a group structure, and it supplies **zero** correspondence between those nodes and anything in the world until an encoder is trained to project onto them. Segmentation is not solved here; it is defined away, and the price is G47 (Peng et al. 2025). **A fourth family carves *space* rather than time, and needs no threshold on a surprise signal** (Venkatesh et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/spelkenet.md]]): poke a generative flow model with one synthetic motion token at a named pixel, sample its response distribution, and cluster the pairwise co-response matrix — entities are the pixels that move together under an imagined force. It is the only discretiser in the wiki that runs on a **single static frame** (the temporal evidence is absorbed into the model rather than read off the stream), the only one whose threshold is *derived* rather than hand-set (Otsu on the observed histogram), and the only one that returns node content and a relation in one operation (the same probe returns the support hierarchy, and the shape of the affordance map distinguishes rigid from deformable). Its units are orthogonal to the three above — events say when a node changes, this says what the node is made of — so the row now has two axes and no mechanism spanning both. What it does *not* fix: choosing where to intervene is a threshold plus random sampling, and it is where the method's advantage over a supervised segmenter disappears (G60). **A fifth family makes the discretisation an explicit ranked hypothesis and scores it before anything downstream runs** ([[wiki/entities/arc-vsa-solver.md]]). Six candidate parses of the same grid (8-connected · 4-connected · vertical runs · horizontal runs · same-colour-anywhere · one-pixel-each) are all computed; each is scored by encoding every object as a vector and taking, for each *output* object, the max of the softmax over its similarities to the candidate *input* objects, averaged. One number penalises two things at once: parses that shatter the grid (each extra object adds mass to the denominator) and parses that make the correspondence ambiguous (two near-equal similarities split the max). Parses are then tried in descending order and the first whose implied action set is cheap enough wins. Three properties no other family here has: the criterion is **computed from the data rather than thresholded**, it is a *representation*-selection criterion that never consults whether the task is solved, and it is explicitly top-down — the paper's argument for that direction is that the parse space is smaller than the action space and that pixel-level actions are not cohesive even when object-level ones are (a rotation is well-defined on an object and undefined on a pixel). Its two limits are equally sharp: the score needs **both** grids, so it can rank a parse of a demonstration and not of a query, and the six hypotheses are a closed authored menu — this row's question is answered for *choosing among* discretisations and untouched for *generating* one.
