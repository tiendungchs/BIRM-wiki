# G7 — Non-stationary topology is tractable only under untested conditions

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** Rule-config factorization is learned rather than supplied, and generalises to held-out rule combinations in Baba Is AI or an equivalent domain where the rules are content.

## Why it blocks the target

Lifting rule-state into the node (`s' = (base_state, rule_config)`) restores stationarity in principle, but the lifted space is `(base) × (rule-configs)` — learnable only if rule-config factorizes and rewrites are sparse, legible, bounded, and meta-stationary. **A domain that meets every clause now exists, and the conditions turn out not to be sufficient** ([[wiki/entities/baba-is-ai.md]]): in Baba Is AI the rules are word tiles inside the image, so rule-config factorises into `{noun} is {property}` triples over ~15 possibilities, a rewrite is one tile push, the rule is *written in the observation* in the instruction language, and the activation predicate (horizontal alignment) never changes in any level. Three May-2024 frontier multimodal models reach 100% on the same environments when following the rules suffices and **14.7–20.0%** once winning requires two edits composed — and they fail the held-out `break; make; goto` under every rotation of which plan shape is withheld. So legibility, sparsity, boundedness and meta-stationarity make the lift *well-posed*; what is missing is any solver that searches over rewrites at all ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T229). **A second domain meets the clauses even more cheaply and the reference system fails too**: [[wiki/entities/fluxx.md]] keeps its rule-config in a handful of named slots, face up in English, one rewrite per play, with a *countable* generator (a fixed shuffled deck) — and experienced human players report losing the combined effect at ten to eleven concurrent rules, including a live win condition none of them noticed for several turns `(tentative)`. So legibility buys well-posedness, not smallness: `|rule_config|` is multiplicative in the live slots regardless. That domain also splits the row in two — where the *solver* owns the rewrites (Baba) the rule dimension is an action and this is a search problem; where *other agents* own them (Fluxx) it is a partially controlled state and this becomes a filtering problem in the sense of [[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]] **And a domain now exists that breaks the clauses on purpose, which bounds the row from the other side**: [[wiki/entities/nomic.md]] satisfies sparsity (one rule-change per turn), legibility (every proposal written down before the vote) and factorisation (numbered discrete rule-nodes), and fails **boundedness** (proposals are free text) and **meta-stationarity** (the amendment rules, the precedence rule and the win condition are all amendable) by construction — and it additionally puts the *reward function* inside `rule_config` (scoring and the winning threshold are ordinary mutable rules), which the lift `s' = (base_state, rule_config)` silently assumes it is outside. So the row's five clauses now have a domain on each side: two that meet them all and defeat every solver, and one that violates two of them deliberately and has no solver to defeat.

## Best current answer

Rule-state lifting; rewrite generator → slow W, active edge set → fast M
