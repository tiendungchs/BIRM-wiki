# G104 — No architecture holds role-filler independence *during* binding: in every structural code here the role and the filler stop being separately addressable the moment they are bound

**Status:** `PARTIAL`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** Role and filler stay separately addressable while bound, at a capacity well beyond LISA's 2–3 propositions, with a similarity operation still available on the bound code.

## Why it blocks the target

Penn, Holyoak & Povinelli 2008 decompose "symbolic" into eight properties and find four of them in every taxon examined — discrete enduring representations, single-exposure atomistic update, van Gelder's *functional* compositionality (produce and decompose by any means) and *featural* systematicity (`R(a,b)` implies `R(b,a)` where the slots are constrained by observable filler features). The four claimed human-only all rest on one operation: the compound must **preserve the identity of its constituents** while bound, so that a role can be reasoned about as a type distinct from its current filler, a relation can be tokened as an object filling a role in another relation, and systematicity can hold `∀R` by definition rather than by feature overlap. The wiki's principal structural code fails the first of these by construction: `a ⊛ b` is unlike both `a` and `b`, and which roles an object fills is "recoverable only by unbinding, i.e. by computation, not by comparison" ([[wiki/concepts/vector-symbolic-binding.md]]). Tensor products keep the parts and grow exponentially; PBWM stripes keep them and require every reader to learn every stripe's code. It blocks the target because the four properties above the line are exactly what the wiki's relational benchmarks keep failing — a relation carried by a new attribute channel ([[wiki/entities/raven.md]]), a known function on a new argument type ([[wiki/entities/pgm.md]]), a preference left unbound to its agent ([[wiki/entities/irene.md]])

## Best current answer

**One partial, and it does not scale.** [[wiki/entities/lisa.md]] delivers it with synchrony — a role and its filler fire in phase, simultaneously and separably active — at a stated capacity of **2–3 propositions**, with no dot-product similarity available on the resulting code and "does not scale to large analogs" as the reviewed verdict. The sparse-binary code-vectors of [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]] union roles in rather than multiplying them through and so keep both parts readable, but their binder has no inverse. Contextualized HRRs pre-compute *specific* structural properties into the code at encoding time, which spends the independence rather than holding it. **The strongest partial, and the one the row had been mis-scoring** ([[wiki/concepts/tensor-product-representation.md]], Smolensky 1990): the *uncompressed* tensor product does hold both constituents while bound — `Ψ(s)·u_i = f_i` is exact whenever the role vectors are merely linearly independent (not orthogonal), the read is a partial inner product that leaves `Ψ(s)` intact so any role may be probed again in any order, and the operator is **symmetric**, so probing with a filler returns the superposition of every role that filler occupies. Values serve as variables directly (§3.6), which is the "relation tokened as an object" property. Three things stop it closing the row: the binder count is `dim V_F · dim V_R` and grows multiplicatively with rank, so capacity is bought in units rather than held; both reads are still *computations* rather than comparisons, which is the form of the objection; and the annihilator obstruction (`G106`) makes fully crossed structure sets linearly dependent at every dimension, so the exactness guarantee lapses on exactly the benchmark geometry. Note what would *not* close this: a bigger dimension, a longer context, or more training — the source's own reading of LISA is that P1–P4 is the easy local minimum and no incremental step leaves it

**The first partial that clears LISA's capacity and keeps a usable read** ([[wiki/entities/esbn.md]], Webb, Sinha & Cohen 2021). The ESBN holds role-filler independence by the cheapest possible route: it never forms a compound. A key (controller-authored) and a value (encoder-authored image embedding) are appended to two aligned memory columns, and the binding *is* the shared row index. Both constituents are separately addressable throughout, at one row per timestep rather than 2–3 propositions, and the read `softmax(M_v · z_t) → M_k` is a dot product, so the similarity machinery LISA forfeits is still available *on the value column*. The evidence is the right kind: the controller has no input line from the encoder, so the rule it learns cannot be a function of the entity, and the model scores 100.0 on same/different trained on **two entities** and tested on 98 unseen ones, unchanged when the encoder is replaced by an untrained random projection. What still stops it closing the row is the third clause: **there is no bound code to run a similarity operation on**. A key/value row is not a vector — it cannot be compared with another row, superposed, or made the filler of a further binding — so the row buys P1-style preservation by giving up the algebra, where the tensor product buys it by paying in units. Two further limits: the keys are *positional* (learned functions of the timestep, per the source's own PCA), so the independence is between a filler and an arrival slot rather than between a filler and a role type; and it is tested to `T = 9`.
