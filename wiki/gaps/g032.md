# G32 — Nothing designs the experience stream

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A curriculum generator produces an ordering that raises a fixed learner's expressed capability over hand-designed ordering, with the task source not capped by the generator's own description length. `G66` is its pair-sampling special case.

## Why it blocks the target

Expressed intelligence is a function of the curriculum, not only of the learner: the same system scores differently under different task orderings, so a builder who ignores curriculum is leaving measurable capability unclaimed. Yet no procedure generates a good curriculum, and the wiki's meta-learning pages take the task distribution as given

## Best current answer

The **teacher–student loop**: a learning teacher generates tasks optimized for novelty and for difficulty just past the student's reach, in closed loop with the solver, drawing on an external incompressible source (the real world) so the task space is not capped by the teacher's own description length. Proposed, not built. **Biology's answer is internal and offline**: replay is a curriculum generator inside the agent, reweighting the already-experienced stream toward what generalises rather than proposing new tasks ([[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]) — which covers the *ordering and resampling* half of curriculum design and none of the task-generation half. **The first measured curriculum ablation in the wiki, and it is non-monotone** (Bortoletto et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/irene.md]]): retraining one model on all 15 subsets of a 4-task training set, the task *matching* the evaluation task gives chance on it (51.5), pairing it with an unrelated task gives the study's best score (80.4), adding a third unrelated task returns it to chance (51.4), and *dropping* a task raises an aggregate score by restoring a shortcut. The transferable ingredient is a behavioural statistic the task induces (does the agent traverse the space?), not the task's label — so any curriculum generator that reasons over task identity is selecting on the wrong variable. **The gap has a second, cheaper instance inside self-supervised training, and it is worth a factor of 3.5** (Assran et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/i-jepa.md]]): in a masked joint-embedding learner the experience stream is not only the image corpus but the distribution over *which sub-images are paired* as context and target, and swapping that sampler — loss, architecture, dataset and epoch budget all fixed — moves ImageNet-1% linear evaluation 15.5 → 54.2, larger than the spread across every anti-collapse objective the wiki carries. The winning setting sits between two named failure modes (targets predictable by local texture continuation, contexts uninformative about their targets) and was found by search; no statistic computable before training predicts where it is ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T167) **The first *closed-loop* curriculum generator in the wiki, and the first with a coefficient measured at matched budget** (Nie et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/anli.md]]): the two instances above are open-loop choices fixed before training, where HAMLET conditions the next example on the *current* model's failure — a paid human is shown the model's live output probabilities and retries until it is wrong. Substituting that data for i.i.d. data at identical training-set size is worth **+27.5 / +16.8 / +14.9** points on the three adversarial rounds while the i.i.d. scores do not move (SNLI 92.5 → 92.3, MNLI 89.8 → 90.3), and 163k adversarial items match a 943k i.i.d. set on MNLI. Three things this pins down for any automated version. The generator needs a **rejector as well as a proposer**, and the rejector is the load-bearing half: model-wrong-but-unverified data is worth *random* by round 3, so the asset is `model-wrong ∧ human-right`, not model-wrong (G68). The teacher does not need to know the ability being taught — writers picked numerical inference because it worked, and the transfer showed up on an independently-authored numerical stress test (+18.5). And the loop's unpaid bill is [[wiki/concepts/continual-learning.md]]: every round retrains from scratch because nothing adds a round without forgetting, which is what stands between three rounds and the never-ending version. **The row's vocabulary finally has an artefact behind it** (Bengio et al. 2009, [[wiki/concepts/curriculum-learning.md]]): a curriculum is a *sequence of training criteria* `Q_λ(z) ∝ W_λ(z)P(z)` with `H(Q_λ)` rising and `W_λ(z)` non-decreasing — a continuation method whose smoothing parameter is the data distribution — so the generator this row asks for is now a search over a **defined** object rather than an undefined one. Two things the source hands the row directly. The gain is an *optimisation* gain, so it is priced by how hard the target objective is to descend: 16.3% vs 17.1% on a convex SVM criterion, against a 3-hidden-layer network whose best test error comes from spending **128 of 256 epochs** on the easy set, with the union-of-both-sets control significantly worse than the curriculum — the effect is a choice of local minimum, not extra data. And the paper states the row's blocker in its own words: every easiness measure it uses (Bayes-correct side, margin `y w′x`, shape variability, word frequency) requires knowing something the learner is trying to learn, and the pace (switch epoch, +5,000 words per pass) is set arbitrarily. **The ledger the definition produces is the useful part**: of six schedules already running in the wiki, the two with the largest effects — i-JEPA's fixed mask sampler (15.5 → 54.2) and ANLI's closed loop (+27.5) — satisfy *neither* condition, one being a single `Q` and the other having entropy *fall* onto the model's failures. So the generator this row wants is not a curriculum generator in Bengio's sense, and the monotone easy-first family is the cheap-to-specify corner of the design space rather than the paying one. **And the row finally has a *stream* rather than a schedule, with a unit and a shortfall attached** (Orhan, Gupta & Lake 2020, [[wiki/entities/saycam-baby-vision.md]]): every instance above designs an ordering or a sampler over a corpus assembled for convenience, where SAYCam is ~500 h of head-mounted video from three children aged 6–32 months and the learner is trained on **one child at a time**, so the training distribution is a strict subset of one individual's actual sensory history. Three things this hands the row. (i) **A conversion rate.** The stream is ~**1 week** of a child's waking visual input, well-distributed over two years, and the authors price the gap to a 2.5-year-old at **two orders of magnitude** — so "design the experience stream" acquires a scale, and any curriculum claim can now be asked what fraction of a real history it is competing against. (ii) **A measured cost of realism**: a PCA analysis through a fixed trunk ranks information content ImageNet > first-person video > third-person video, i.e. a developmentally realistic stream is a *worse* i.i.d. corpus and is nonetheless sufficient for linearly decodable categories, cross-child transfer and unseen-exemplar generalisation. (iii) **The null for ordering.** There is no schedule at all — the whole 6–32-month window is trained as one i.i.d. distribution, with no capacity growth and no easy-first pass — so this is the row's control condition: how much the *content* of the stream buys when its *order* is thrown away. What it does not supply is the generator; the stream was recorded, not proposed, and the one lever the study does sweep (episode length) is a pair-sampler scalar (`T167`), not a curriculum. **And the one generator in the wiki that is *inside the agent and running*, with the stream it designs measured in fixations** (Ballard, Hayhoe, Pook & Rao 1997, [[wiki/concepts/active-vision.md]]): every instance above is a schedule imposed on a corpus by a designer or a teacher, where a human copying a block pattern *selects their own next observation* three times a second and the selection is a function of the current step of the task. Three things this hands the row. (i) **The generator has a cost function and it is not novelty, difficulty or progress** — it is the carrying cost of a working-memory item: subjects choose the zero-carry strategy (3.0 s per block) over the full-memory one (1.5 s) under an explicit speed instruction, so the stream is designed to minimise *instantaneous state*, not to maximise learning. No intrinsic-motivation formula in the wiki has that term. (ii) **It is a measurable exchange rate rather than a policy**: raising the price of a look (model and workspace separated by 70°) moves fixations from 1.3 to 1.0 per block, and making the pattern chunkable (monochrome) moves it to 0.7 — so the generator is a *pricing* mechanism whose output changes when the cost of sampling changes, which is the one property none of the open-loop schedules above has. (iii) **The stream it produces is radically impoverished and the task still completes perfectly**: what survives a saccade is the one or two values the current step binds, and colour changed mid-saccade goes unnoticed on most trials. So this is the row's existence proof that a *self-designed* experience stream can be far worse as an i.i.d. corpus than anything a designer would assemble and still be sufficient — the same lesson SAYCam teaches from the content side, here with the selection mechanism visible. What it does not supply is the row's object: the policy emitting the next look is not modelled, and the 16-bit feature set the pointers read through was hand-designed. **And the row's first *fully automatic* closed-loop generator, measured against hand-designed curricula rather than against i.i.d. data** (OpenAI et al. 2021, [[wiki/entities/asymmetric-self-play.md]]): ANLI's loop has a paid human inside it; here the proposer is a policy (Alice) that acts for `T` steps in the solver's own body and publishes whatever state she leaves behind as the goal, rewarded `+5` whenever the solver fails. The comparison arm is the strong one this row has been missing — four curricula built by the same team on Automatic Domain Randomization machinery, annealing initial→goal distance, rotation tolerance, pick-up probability and stack probability, with "a decent amount of time iterating" — and three of the four acquire **no** pick-up or stacking skill at all, while the self-play distribution yields a single policy that solves all four block holdouts and generalises zero-shot to table setting, mini chess, tangram, dominoes and 2–4 block stacking with unseen objects. Four things it hands the row. (i) **Reachability can be bought by embodiment rather than by statistics**: a proposer instantiated in the solver's action space at the solver's initial state *cannot* propose an unreachable goal, which is the guarantee every generative goal sampler has to recover by other means and the failure this row inherits from `random` relabelling. (ii) **The generated distribution may not be the asset.** With the demonstration channel removed and the generator unchanged, the solver scores **zero** on every holdout — so a curriculum generator may have to emit a *witness trajectory*, not a task (`T403`), which would foreclose the pure-sampler design family this row has been assuming. (iii) **The termination rule is free under an adversary**: a goal the solver reliably solves stops paying the proposer, so the frontier advances with no competence estimate, no progress derivative and no easiness measure computed anywhere. (iv) **The fence is where the designer returns**: three hand-written legality predicates (something moved, nothing off the table, everything inside the camera volume) plus a `−3` out-of-zone penalty are the entire defence against the degenerate optimum, so the task knowledge removed from the goal distribution reappears as a validity filter. The row does **not** close on it: generalisation is scored against holdouts one team wrote down, the goal space is fixed to per-object pose within hand-set tolerances, and the scheme requires a resettable simulator, so "not capped by the generator's own description length" is met only in the weak sense that the physics rather than Alice's parameters supplies the structure.
