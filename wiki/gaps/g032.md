## Why it blocks the target

Expressed intelligence is a function of the curriculum, not only of the learner: the same system scores differently under different task orderings, so a builder who ignores curriculum is leaving measurable capability unclaimed. Yet no procedure generates a good curriculum, and the wiki's meta-learning pages take the task distribution as given

## Best current answer

The **teacher–student loop**: a learning teacher generates tasks optimized for novelty and for difficulty just past the student's reach, in closed loop with the solver, drawing on an external incompressible source (the real world) so the task space is not capped by the teacher's own description length. Proposed, not built. **Biology's answer is internal and offline**: replay is a curriculum generator inside the agent, reweighting the already-experienced stream toward what generalises rather than proposing new tasks ([[wiki/concepts/offline-replay.md]]) — which covers the *ordering and resampling* half of curriculum design and none of the task-generation half. **The first measured curriculum ablation in the wiki, and it is non-monotone** (Bortoletto et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/irene.md]]): retraining one model on all 15 subsets of a 4-task training set, the task *matching* the evaluation task gives chance on it (51.5), pairing it with an unrelated task gives the study's best score (80.4), adding a third unrelated task returns it to chance (51.4), and *dropping* a task raises an aggregate score by restoring a shortcut. The transferable ingredient is a behavioural statistic the task induces (does the agent traverse the space?), not the task's label — so any curriculum generator that reasons over task identity is selecting on the wrong variable. **The gap has a second, cheaper instance inside self-supervised training, and it is worth a factor of 3.5** (Assran et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/i-jepa.md]]): in a masked joint-embedding learner the experience stream is not only the image corpus but the distribution over *which sub-images are paired* as context and target, and swapping that sampler — loss, architecture, dataset and epoch budget all fixed — moves ImageNet-1% linear evaluation 15.5 → 54.2, larger than the spread across every anti-collapse objective the wiki carries. The winning setting sits between two named failure modes (targets predictable by local texture continuation, contexts uninformative about their targets) and was found by search; no statistic computable before training predicts where it is ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T167) **The first *closed-loop* curriculum generator in the wiki, and the first with a coefficient measured at matched budget** (Nie et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/anli.md]]): the two instances above are open-loop choices fixed before training, where HAMLET conditions the next example on the *current* model's failure — a paid human is shown the model's live output probabilities and retries until it is wrong. Substituting that data for i.i.d. data at identical training-set size is worth **+27.5 / +16.8 / +14.9** points on the three adversarial rounds while the i.i.d. scores do not move (SNLI 92.5 → 92.3, MNLI 89.8 → 90.3), and 163k adversarial items match a 943k i.i.d. set on MNLI. Three things this pins down for any automated version. The generator needs a **rejector as well as a proposer**, and the rejector is the load-bearing half: model-wrong-but-unverified data is worth *random* by round 3, so the asset is `model-wrong ∧ human-right`, not model-wrong (G68). The teacher does not need to know the ability being taught — writers picked numerical inference because it worked, and the transfer showed up on an independently-authored numerical stress test (+18.5). And the loop's unpaid bill is [[wiki/concepts/continual-learning.md]]: every round retrains from scratch because nothing adds a round without forgetting, which is what stands between three rounds and the never-ending version.
