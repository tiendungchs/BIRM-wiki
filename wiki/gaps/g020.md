## Why it blocks the target

The two available regimes are mutually exclusive: e-prop is forward-computable but requires a real-time error at each step and is blind to errors delayed beyond individual-neuron timescales; REINFORCE / node perturbation handle arbitrarily delayed sparse reward but are high-variance and give no per-step signal. Multi-hop traversal has precisely the delayed-sparse credit structure, evaluated only at the end of a path, so neither regime fits the wiki's target task

## Best current answer

Eligibility traces plus a retroactive neuromodulatory signal — the mechanism both families share and neither exploits fully. **A fourth regime the pair above misses** (O'Reilly & Frank 2006, [[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]): make the delayed problem a *shallow* one by changing what the critic reads. A Pavlovian critic is trained at reward time on the **internal states being maintained** rather than on the sensory events that produced them, so the temporal gap is crossed once, inside the store's own contents; the striatal write action is then trained by *immediate* feedback from that critic evaluating the state it just created. Structural credit is separate and anatomical — the broadcast scalar is multiplied by the acting unit's own gating activation (`δ_j = snr_j·δ`), so non-actors receive nothing. Removing that multiplication drops success to 15/5/0% across three tasks; removing the neuromodulator entirely drops it to 0/0/0%. Two residuals: it requires an external reward (nothing here says what to maintain absent one), and it requires exploratory writes (`Random Go`) whose cost is the maintained information they destroy
