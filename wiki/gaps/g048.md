# G48 — Nothing separates a store's content register from its attentional pointer

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** One architecture carries a task-level attentional pointer as state distinct from its content register, and the separation is shown necessary by a task where pointer and content have the same type.

## Why it blocks the target

Fast **M** is specified as a place where items are held, and reads are by content similarity. But a controller also needs to carry *what it is currently pointing at* across steps — and when the pointer and the contents are the same type (a location, an entity id, a slot index), no read-out can tell them apart. The biological case is the strongest evidence that this is not a pedantic distinction: in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex the pointer signal is the *larger* one during a delay (61% of spatially tuned cells vs. 16% for the remembered item), grows as the moment of use approaches, and is cleared prospectively when its target stops being relevant

## Best current answer

No architecture in the wiki has both. Machine attention recomputes selection from the current query every step and keeps no attentional state at all; memory-augmented networks ([[wiki/entities/differentiable-neural-computer.md]]) come closest, since the temporal-link and usage vectors are read-head state that persists independently of the memory matrix — but they are addressing bookkeeping, not a task-level "what I am tracking", and no gate clears them by relevance. The population-level partial answer is anti-aligned mixed selectivity: same-type variables stay jointly decodable when units prefer *different* values for the two, which is a property of the learned read-out rather than of the store ([[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]])

**The split is now measured non-invasively, and by *which instrument*, not by which region.** Wolff et al. 2017 ([[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]) decode the same human working-memory item two ways during one delay: standard delay activity tracks the **focus of attention** — an unattended but still-required item is at chance there, and is nevertheless reported correctly later — while the response to a content-free impulse tracks the **contents**, decoding attended and unattended items alike. Trial-wise, the attention index (alpha lateralisation) does not predict impulse decodability at all. So the pointer and the content are not merely separable in principle; they are carried in signals that dissociate on single trials. This sharpens what a machine store owes: the two variables have different *read-out procedures*, not merely different addresses, and an architecture with one memory matrix read one way cannot express the difference.

**And the separation exists in one biological mechanism by *construction* rather than by decoding** ([[wiki/concepts/visual-indices.md]], Pylyshyn 2001). A FINST is a pointer with **no content register at all**: four or five of them are seized by feature clusters, each keeps referring to the same item as its properties and location change arbitrarily, and none encodes any of those properties — the probe that interrogates an indexed item is routed using no encoding of the target, not even its location. The object-file literature is then the *same* mechanism after content has been attached (Kahneman, Treisman & Gibbs 1992 concede the identity: a FINST is "the initial phase of a simple object file before any features have been attached to it"), so pointer and content are not two readings of one signal here — they are two stages of one process, and the empty stage is separately measurable. Three things this changes for the row. **(i)** It supplies the task the `Closes when` asks for, already run in humans: multiple object tracking has pointer and content of the same type (both are "an item in the display"), tracked items are reported correctly while colour and shape changes made to them behind an occluder go unnoticed, and the tracked set can even share one spatial locus and move through feature space (Blaser et al. 2000). **(ii)** It prices the separation: the pointer store's capacity is 4–5 *references*, independent of how much content each referent has, which is a capacity model no machine store in the wiki has for its pointer side (`G42`). **(iii)** It adds a clearing rule no architecture here implements — the reference is destroyed when the referent leaves the field of view, so the pointer register is cleared by the world rather than by the controller ([[wiki/concepts/memory-read-and-erase.md]]).
