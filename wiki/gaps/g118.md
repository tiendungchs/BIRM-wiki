# G118 — No architecture separates an outcome's hedonic magnitude from the motivation it commands

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** One system computes an outcome's stored worth and the pursuit it commands by two independently ablatable mechanisms, and the double dissociation is shown: remove the motivational term and pursuit collapses while learned preferences and the evaluative read-out survive; remove the evaluative term and the agent still pursues, at unchanged intensity, something it now values differently.

## Why it blocks the target

Every architecture in the wiki has one reward scalar. `r` enters `δ = r + γV(s′) − V(s)`, is what `V` estimates, is what the policy maximises, and is fitted to choice or pursuit data. That commits the design to an identity — *how good the outcome is* equals *how hard it is pursued* — which the biology falsifies directly ([[wiki/concepts/incentive-salience.md]], Berridge & Robinson 2016):

- Near-total dopamine depletion abolishes pursuit and consumption in rats while leaving affective orofacial reactions to sucrose **completely normal** (Berridge, Venier & Robinson 1989); electrode stimulation quadruples food 'wanting' without raising 'liking' (Berridge & Valenstein 1991).
- The hedonic generator is anatomically separate and chemically different: hotspots of ~1 mm³ in rat accumbens (10% of its volume) plus ventral pallidum, driven by opioid and endocannabinoid signalling, where **dopamine stimulation always fails** to enhance 'liking' (Smith, Berridge & Aldridge 2011). The remaining 90% of accumbens generates intense pursuit and no hedonic enhancement.
- ~15% of Parkinson's-disease patients on dopamine-agonist medication develop compulsive gambling, shopping or sex, and intense pleasure is **virtually never reported** (O'Sullivan, Evans & Lees 2009) — a manufactured pursuit with no hedonic counterpart, reversible on stopping the drug.
- The extreme case, and the one that makes the two quantities provably independent rather than merely separable: **'wanting what hurts'** (Warlow et al. 2020, via Robinson & Berridge 2025). Optogenetic basolateral-amygdala stimulation paired with an electrified shock rod makes rats return to the rod, touch it repeatedly and climb a barrier to reach it, while the shocks remain unpleasant and the stimulation does not enhance 'liking'. The same stimulation paired with sugar or with cocaine produces a rat narrowly addicted to whichever was paired — so the motivational term's target is set by **coincidence with the modulatory transient**, not by any property of the outcome. A single scalar cannot represent this state at all: the number driving pursuit and the number reporting the outcome would have opposite signs.

What the collapse denies a wiki architecture is the ability to express, or even detect, a divergence between the two. An agent whose motivational gain has drifted has no internal quantity that disagrees with it: the value estimate that would flag the error is the same number. The failure is therefore **silent in the loss**, which is exactly the shape of incentive sensitization — a gain that ratchets with spaced large transients, is gated by the context of induction, does not decay with disuse, and leaves the hedonic estimate untouched.

A second consequence is instrumental and applies to every source the wiki reads. Choice and pursuit are 'wanting' read-outs. If the two quantities dissociate, then an `r` fitted to choice data is the motivational term, not the hedonic one, and no source in the wiki reports the one assay that separates them — affective orofacial reactions, which have no artificial-agent analogue. This is the affect/pursuit twin of `G116`'s valence/action confound and rides on the same literature.

## Best current answer

**None built, and the pieces do not assemble.** The wiki has three partial constructs and each supplies the wrong half:

- [[wiki/concepts/affective-opponency.md]]'s movable origin re-signs an outcome against a context-set zero — **additive, applied to the outcome**, where this row needs a factor applied to the *cue* at retrieval.
- `G56` asks for a run-time gain register on a network's own connections; the biological version here is stronger, being **indexed by context** (sensitized reactivity is not expressed in an environment where the drug was never taken) and having its own slow plasticity independent of the value estimate.
- `G117` splits the *teaching signal* from one event into a signed and a rectified channel. This row splits the *quantity being taught about*, and the two are orthogonal: a system could have both channels and still have one `r` behind them.

The cheap machine version needs no drug and no neuroscience: give an agent a state- and cue-dependent multiplier on action values, separate from the learned values, let the multiplier be potentiated by large transients, and check (i) that spaced large updates grow it more than massed updates of the same total, (ii) that ablating the multiplier collapses pursuit while leaving learned preference rankings intact, and (iii) whether any standard loss detects (i) happening.

**The count is higher than two, and the separating assay already exists.** Cardinal et al. 2002 ([[wiki/concepts/valuation-system-decomposition.md]]) individuate a **third** quantity between the two above — the *instrumental incentive value* governing how hard an action is worked for — and supply the protocol that separates it from the hedonic one in a single design: devalue an outcome, test in extinction (responding is unchanged while consumption is already suppressed — hedonic value written, instrumental value not), re-expose the animal to the outcome once, retest (responding now drops — *incentive learning*). That is this row's demanded double dissociation, run, with the twist that the two quantities are separated **in time** rather than by two ablations: the same animal holds an updated hedonic value and a stale instrumental value for a measurable interval. A machine version is free — a value cache invalidated only by re-visiting the outcome, never by inference — and no architecture here has it.

**The closed form now exists, and it is one parameter.** Zhang et al. 2009 ([[wiki/concepts/incentive-salience.md]]) write the motivational term as `Ṽ(s_t) = κ·r_t + γ·V̂(s_{t+1})` with three restrictions that make it exactly this row's independently ablatable pair: `κ` is set by physiological state and **never by prediction error**; `V̂` is written by `δ` and by nothing else; and `κ` is denied access to `V` and `Q`, touching only the primary-reward term. So ablating `κ` collapses pursuit while leaving the learned rankings intact *by construction*, and the cost of having two quantities instead of one is a single multiply at retrieval and no new learning rule. Three things it still does not supply. (i) **The other half of the dissociation is not run** — no experiment there removes `κ` and shows preference survives; the salt-appetite and amphetamine experiments both manipulate `κ` upward. (ii) **No bound on `κ`**, which is this row's standing hole restated in the model's own vocabulary. (iii) **No addressing scheme**: the model requires one `κ` per appetite system, each keyed to its own outcome class and that class's cues, reports spillover across systems, and names "what to want most" as its first open problem — which is the same hole as the pairing-written index above, now with a parameter to hang it on. The operator itself is also not settled: the same paper keeps an additive sub-type `(r_t + log κ)` alongside the gain, because a valence reversal preserves the rank order of a graded family where a negative multiplier would invert it (`T362`).

The same source complicates the row's framing, which is why it opens `T359`: its evidence makes the motivational term a **separate valuation system** rather than a multiplier on the value estimate, since accumbens lesions and dopamine antagonists leave instrumental incentive value and contingency sensitivity intact while abolishing the Pavlovian influence on responding. The closing experiment above is stated in the multiplier's vocabulary and should be read as testing one of two admissible decompositions.

## Status

`OPEN` — the dissociation is well-evidenced across four manipulation classes and two species and is not itself contested; what is absent is any architecture that represents the two quantities separately. Adjacent rows, and the distinctions that keep this from duplicating them: `G117` (two *channels* carrying news about one event, versus this row's two *quantities* about one outcome), `G56` (a run-time gain register, versus this row's demand that the gain be a separate quantity from value and separately ablatable), `G64` (one scalar setting a gain and a learning rate, whose predicted instability this row's biology names and measures), `G116` (the same literature's other confound — valence against action, where this is affect against pursuit), `T122` (which reads of the broadcast signal this bears on), `T359` (whether the second quantity is a gain on one estimate or a second valuation system — this row is agnostic and its closing test is not), `G121` (the measurement confound this split rides on in the devaluation literature), `T362` (which operator the motivational term applies, once it is a separate quantity).

**One thing the new evidence does not supply**: what normally *bounds* the motivational term. If the gain can select a target of unchanging negative hedonic value, an architecture that separates the two quantities still needs a rule that keeps them coupled in the healthy case, and no source in the wiki states one.

`G125` (an *intermediate* target's single quantity with no update rule at all — an architecture could hold this row's two quantities and still print unbacked subgoal currency), `T396` (whether a gain and a loss of the same magnitude are equal and opposite, which is this row's split asked of a common currency rather than of one outcome).
