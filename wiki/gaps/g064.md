# G64 — No architecture lets one modulator set a gain and a learning rate at once

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** One broadcast scalar sets both unit gain and learning rate, with opposite signs on two subpopulations, in a single implemented system — the row is cheap to build and has never been built.

## Why it blocks the target

Striatum reads a single broadcast scalar as *both* a change in excitability and a signed change in corticostriatal plasticity, oppositely on two populations — so selection and credit assignment are one modulation read at two timescales. Every third-factor rule in the wiki multiplies `Δw` and leaves the forward pass untouched, so the wiki cannot express the mechanism at all, let alone test whether its predicted self-reinforcing selection is the origin of fast habit formation or an instability.

## Best current answer

**One sketch, nothing built.** The change is one line — scale both the update and the unit gains of the updated population by the same scalar, with opposite signs on two subpopulations — and has no implemented precedent in the wiki. The nearest constructs each supply one half: neuromodulatory metaparameter control supplies a global scalar with a feedback law but consumes it only as a metaparameter, and gated architectures supply per-population gains but derive them from content rather than from outcome. The one proposal that couples them is Mei et al. 2025's conceptual actor–critic: a surprise-triggered noradrenaline-like transient targets excitatory units in both actor and critic, *simultaneously* inducing correlated bursting (a gain change, read as flattening the energy landscape) and promoting plasticity in the same units. It differs from the striatal case in the driver — mismatch rather than outcome — and in having no opposite-sign subpopulation. Conceptual figure only: no spiking implementation, no baseline, one task.
