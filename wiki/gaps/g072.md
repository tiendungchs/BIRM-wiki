# G72 — Nothing infers what counts as success

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Why it blocks the target

Every architecture in the wiki receives its objective from outside: a reward scalar, a goal image ([[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]]), a target grid, a preference distribution `C` set by the designer ([[wiki/concepts/expected-free-energy.md]]), a query whose posterior entropy is to be minimised ([[wiki/entities/autotom.md]]). None decides *what to want* from the environment itself. This is upstream of G33 (which decomposes a goal already given) and of G28 (which composes a learned model with a reward supplied at query time), and it is the component that makes exploration terminable — without it an agent has no criterion for when a hypothesis about the environment is worth acting on rather than probing further

## Best current answer

None. [[wiki/entities/arc-agi-3.md]] makes it a scored requirement — the agent is never told the objective, and the only feedback is a terminal `WIN`/`GAME_OVER` — and frontier models score 0.1–0.5% against a human baseline the metric normalises to 100%. **A second, cheaper half of the row is now visible**: [[wiki/entities/fluxx.md]] makes the objective fully *legible* and still unusable, because any opponent replaces it — the missing quantity there is not the objective's identity but its **hazard rate**, and the game's own rulebook states the resulting policy (bank goal-general state, never commit to the live goal, "it will very likely be replaced by another long before you find the second Keeper"). Every architecture in the wiki treats the goal as fixed for the episode and so carries no such quantity. **A design that bounds the hazard without freezing the goal is now on record** ([[wiki/entities/nomic.md]], Rule 112): entrench the objective's *form* in the dominant, hardest-to-edit tier — winning is and remains "achieving `n` points" — while leaving `n` and the entire scoring map mutable. The agent then re-infers the objective's **arguments** every episode and never its type, which is the cheapest general recipe the wiki has for a non-stationary objective and the minimum patch that keeps a fully self-amending system from dissolving its own success criterion. It is a design, not a result: nobody has built or measured it. The nearest partial mechanisms are the epistemic term of expected free energy (which supplies a drive to reduce uncertainty but no stopping condition, because the pragmatic term it trades against is the missing one) and the two preview-competition entries, which replace goal inference with exhaustive informed search over the action space and reach 6.7–12.6% by encountering wins accidentally
