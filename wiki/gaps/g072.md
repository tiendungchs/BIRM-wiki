# G72 — Nothing infers what counts as success

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An agent infers what counts as success from a terminal signal alone and reaches a non-trivial score on ARC-AGI-3 or an equivalent objective-free environment.

## Why it blocks the target

Every architecture in the wiki receives its objective from outside: a reward scalar, a goal image ([[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]]), a target grid, a preference distribution `C` set by the designer ([[wiki/concepts/expected-free-energy.md]]), a query whose posterior entropy is to be minimised ([[wiki/entities/autotom.md]]). None decides *what to want* from the environment itself. This is upstream of G33 (which decomposes a goal already given) and of G28 (which composes a learned model with a reward supplied at query time), and it is the component that makes exploration terminable — without it an agent has no criterion for when a hypothesis about the environment is worth acting on rather than probing further

## Best current answer

None. [[wiki/entities/arc-agi-3.md]] makes it a scored requirement — the agent is never told the objective, and the only feedback is a terminal `WIN`/`GAME_OVER` — and frontier models score 0.1–0.5% against a human baseline the metric normalises to 100%. **A second, cheaper half of the row is now visible**: [[wiki/entities/fluxx.md]] makes the objective fully *legible* and still unusable, because any opponent replaces it — the missing quantity there is not the objective's identity but its **hazard rate**, and the game's own rulebook states the resulting policy (bank goal-general state, never commit to the live goal, "it will very likely be replaced by another long before you find the second Keeper"). Every architecture in the wiki treats the goal as fixed for the episode and so carries no such quantity. **A design that bounds the hazard without freezing the goal is now on record** ([[wiki/entities/nomic.md]], Rule 112): entrench the objective's *form* in the dominant, hardest-to-edit tier — winning is and remains "achieving `n` points" — while leaving `n` and the entire scoring map mutable. The agent then re-infers the objective's **arguments** every episode and never its type, which is the cheapest general recipe the wiki has for a non-stationary objective and the minimum patch that keeps a fully self-amending system from dissolving its own success criterion. It is a design, not a result: nobody has built or measured it. The nearest partial mechanisms are the epistemic term of expected free energy (which supplies a drive to reduce uncertainty but no stopping condition, because the pragmatic term it trades against is the missing one) and the two preview-competition entries, which replace goal inference with exhaustive informed search over the action space and reach 6.7–12.6% by encountering wins accidentally. **A whole family of self-generated success criteria is now on the page, and it answers half of the row on purpose** (Oudeyer & Kaplan 2007, [[wiki/concepts/intrinsic-motivation-typology.md]]): in the *competence-based* family a goal `g_k` is **any** set of properties of a sensorimotor configuration the agent sets for itself, an episode runs on its own abstract clock, and the reward is computed from the level of achievement — incompetence, competence progress (Flow), or mastery — so interestingness is a property of the *achievement process* and never of the goal's content. The agent therefore needs no externally supplied success criterion at all. What it does not get is this row: the goal space is exactly as arbitrary as the sensorimotor parameterisation, so a self-set goal carries no guarantee of being reachable, of being on a path to anything, or of coinciding with the environment's `WIN`. The family supplies *what to want* in the weak sense (something to get better at) and is silent in the strong sense (what this environment rewards) **The row now has a name for its own regime, and a negative result about the literature it would borrow from** (Gottlieb & Oudeyer 2018, [[wiki/concepts/information-sampling-vs-search.md]]): *information sampling* reduces uncertainty inside a task whose structure is known, so a query's value is computable as outcome value × marginal gain in `P(outcome)`; *information search* runs before the agent knows whether a pattern exists or what it would be, and has **no** normative account at all — the review's argument is that normative schemes assign value over a hypothesis space they never count ("a decision problem with two alternatives is not distinguished from one with 2¹⁰⁰"), so they do not scale to the regime this row lives in. Every epistemic-value ablation in the wiki is run in the sampling regime. What is offered for the search regime is a heuristic with one demonstration: a robot under a learning-progress reward, given hidden object interdependencies (a cylinder movable only via a stick) and uncontrollable distractors, self-generates goals, moves hand → stick → cylinder as each becomes a niche of progress, and abandons what it cannot learn from — the environment's own couplings supply the stepping stones that an externally-scored success criterion would have had to. The human counterpart is a measured set-point rather than a criterion: free play with games of varying difficulty settles at **70–80% correct**. Neither supplies what this row asks — a criterion coinciding with the environment's `WIN` — but both are existence proofs that a terminable, non-random investigation runs with no such criterion present. **A third existence proof, and the one that needs no criterion at all** ([[wiki/concepts/empowerment.md]], Salge et al. 2013): maximising the channel capacity from the agent's own actuators to its later sensors drives a pendulum to upright, a maze agent to the topologically central location, and a grid agent to the neighbourhood of a manipulable object — states a human observer would have nominated as goals, reached with no goal, no reward, no episode and no success criterion, learning only transition dynamics along the path actually taken. It is the cheapest thing in the wiki that looks like *wanting something* without being told what. The row is not closed and the reason is sharp: the authors state the contraindication themselves — a desired goal state that is not highly empowered will never be sought — so capacity maximisation does not *infer* the environment's `WIN`, it substitutes a default for it and coincides with the intended goal only in well-chosen scenarios and at well-chosen resolutions (`T363`). For this row that is still a gain: it separates *terminable self-directed behaviour* (solved, three ways over) from *goal inference* (untouched), and says the two have been conflated **And the cheapest half-answer in the wiki, with its own boundary marked** ([[wiki/concepts/hindsight-goal-relabelling.md]], Andrychowicz et al. 2017): an agent can manufacture its entire goal curriculum from its own failures — relabel each stored transition with a goal the episode actually achieved, recompute the reward for that counterfactual goal offline, and the goal distribution walks outward from whatever a random policy reaches as competence grows, with no shaping, no demonstration and no schedule. What it does *not* infer is this row: the goal *space* is designer-given as a predicate family `f_g : S → {0,1}` plus a state→goal projection `m : S → G` (`m(s) = s_object` discards the arm), so what is generated is a distribution over an objective type that was fixed in advance — the same shape as [[wiki/entities/nomic.md]]'s entrenched-form/mutable-arguments recipe, now with a working instance. Two transferable constraints come with it. (a) **Reachability is the whole content of a goal generator**: sampling relabelled goals from the trajectory's own future works, sampling them from the global training history is the *only* strategy that fails — an arbitrary goal carries no gradient, so "generate your own goals" is free and "generate goals that are reachable-but-not-yet-reached" is the entire problem. (b) **The success criterion must be a queryable module, not a channel the environment emits** — relabelling requires `r(s,a,g)` evaluable offline for goals never pursued, which is a hard interface constraint on any design in this row that intends to re-infer its objective and reuse old experience under the new one. Against Fluxx's half of the row it is also a negative result: the mechanism assumes the goal is fixed *within* an episode and that past episodes stay re-interpretable, and a goal replaced mid-episode by an opponent breaks the first assumption

**And the one mechanism in the wiki that manufactures a want with content, rather than a drive with none** ([[wiki/concepts/conditioned-reinforcement.md]], Williams 1994). Pair an arbitrary stimulus with a primary reinforcer and it becomes something the animal will work for in its own right — a specific, arbitrary, environment-supplied target that the reward function never named, whose *value* is derived rather than assumed, and which then behaves as an autonomous objective: devaluing the primary reward by satiation or by aversion leaves it standing (Holland & Rescorla 1975; Rescorla 1977), and it can beat the primary reward outright in direct competition (Cronin 1980 — pigeons chose the S− on ~90% of trials, stably, because the conditioned reinforcer signalled by that choice arrived immediately and the food did not). Against empowerment, learning progress and hindsight relabelling, all of which supply *something to get better at* with no relation to what the environment pays, this supplies a target that inherits its worth from the environment's own terminal signal. What it does not do is close the row, and the boundary is exact: conditioned value **cannot exist before a primary reinforcer has been encountered**, so it explains how a terminal `WIN` propagates outward into a structure of intermediate wants and says nothing about inferring the objective in an environment where the terminal signal has never yet been reached — which is `ARC-AGI-3`'s regime. It also arrives with a cost the row should carry forward: the manufactured want is audited by nothing except its own unbacked delivery (`G125`), and one that stops being backed decays only if the agent keeps visiting it
