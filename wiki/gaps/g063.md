# G63 — The behaviour policy is a load-bearing parameter of world-model estimation, and every mechanism the wiki has for improving an agent destroys it

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An agent maintains a floor on conditional action excitation as an explicit objective, separated from reward-seeking exploration, and its world-model estimate stays identifiable while the policy improves.

## Why it blocks the target

Identifiability of a controlled world model requires positive **conditional** action excitation — action variation that survives conditioning on the state (`ρ_tr(π) > 0`). That quantity belongs to the data-collection policy, not to the architecture or the loss, and it falls to zero exactly as the actor gets good: [[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]]'s Mode-2 → Mode-1 distillation fits a *deterministic* `A(s)` to the planner's argmin, reinforcement learners anneal exploration, and a competent policy is by definition one whose action is nearly a function of its state. At `ρ_tr = 0` the data constrain only the closed-loop map `A + BK`; two models that agree exactly on-policy give different answers to "what if I had done otherwise", and **the training loss cannot see the difference** — it keeps falling. An agent that trains its world model on its own recent experience therefore degrades the model it plans with while every number it reports improves. This is the model-estimation twin of G61 (exploration is an external schedule): there the missing controller sets exploration for reward, here it must set it for *identifiability*, and the two set-points have no reason to agree

## Best current answer

Only the classical fix, from system identification: inject dither, or hold a floor on conditional action variance. Nobody in the wiki does it, and nobody separates the exploration needed to *find* reward from the excitation needed to *estimate the model* — [[wiki/concepts/expected-free-energy.md]]'s epistemic term is the closest, but it is driven by the agent's uncertainty about states and parameters and would happily go silent on an action direction the model is confidently wrong about off-policy. **(brainstorm)** The measurable version is cheap and nobody reports it: log `λ_min(E_z[Cov_π(a|z)])` over training alongside the loss, and read the ratio of the two curves as the counterfactual amplification the model is accruing. A replay buffer that mixes old high-variance data postpones the problem and changes `P_π` in a way the theory does not cover **One system happens to sit on the right side of this row, by accident of its data source** ([[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]]): the only stage of V-JEPA 2 that has to identify controlled dynamics is post-trained on 62 hours of *human teleoperation* — many operators, failed attempts retained — which is close to the highest conditional action variance anyone has on a real robot, and nothing in the system distils its planner into a reactive policy, so the loop this row describes is never entered. Neither the paper nor the field frames it that way, and the corollary is the row's own prediction: retraining the same action model on trajectories from its own increasingly competent planner should make it worse while its training loss improves

**The loop this row describes was written down in 2018 as a feature** ([[wiki/entities/world-models-vmc.md]]). Ha & Schmidhuber's iterative training procedure — roll out in the real environment with the *current* controller, refit the world model, re-evolve the controller inside it, repeat — starts from a random policy (maximal `ρ_tr`, which is why one iteration suffices on their tasks) and hands each later iteration data from a progressively more deterministic actor. Their stated motivation for iterating is coverage: only a competent policy reaches the parts of the state space the model needs. So the two requirements are in direct opposition on the same knob — competence buys **coverage** of the state space and destroys **excitation** within it — and no proposal in the wiki separates them. Their own remedy points the same way this row does without naming it: augment the collection reward with the world model's *negated* predictive loss, i.e. make data collection an objective of model estimation rather than of return.
