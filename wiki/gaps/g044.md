# G44 — Nothing accounts for how much of an emergent phenomenon came from the training target rather than the task

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** An emergence result reports how much of the phenomenon is implied by the second-order statistics of the training target, by a control that scrambles the target's structure while holding the task fixed.

## Why it blocks the target

An emergence claim is evidence about an objective only if the phenomenon is not already implied by the second-order statistics of the supervised target. In the wiki's best-documented case it was: the readout correlation matrix plays the role of the recurrent interaction kernel, so a centre–surround target *is* the pattern-forming mechanism, and >11,000 networks show the task alone yields the phenomenon in almost no configuration (Schaeffer et al. 2022). The wiki has a six-item audit checklist ([[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]]) and no measure. This is G16/G17's identifiability failure moved one level up — from *which graph* to *which objective*.

## Best current answer

The closest thing to a partial answer is *mechanism* ablation rather than target accounting: reporting the emergence rate with each architectural term removed, four seeds per condition, as [[wiki/entities/spiking-tem.md]] does — which localises the cause among the model's own components but still says nothing about how much came from the target

## From

Schaeffer et al. 2022; [[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]], [[wiki/entities/spiking-tem.md]]
