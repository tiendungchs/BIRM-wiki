# G47 — Nothing learns the topology of the state-space manifold it represents on

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L3`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A model infers the topology of its own state-space manifold from experience — ring versus torus versus plane discovered, not built in — and the inferred topology matches the domain's on a case the designer did not label.

## Why it blocks the target

Every continuous attractor in the wiki has its topology *built in* by translation-invariant recurrence: a ring for heading, a torus for grid modules, a plane for a 2-D concept space. The manifold is therefore a statement the designer makes about the domain before any data arrive — and it is exactly the statement G3's path-consistency argument, G41's action-closure precondition and the whole `g` programme depend on. Where the domain's true structure is not the assumed one (a branching graph, a tree, a space of unknown dimension, a space whose dimension changes), the code silently represents the wrong thing, and no page in the wiki reports a mechanism that would notice. **The cost of getting it wrong is now measured rather than assumed**: in M1 the manifold a population has settled on predicts which new readouts can be learned at all, and readouts requiring activity patterns off it are not learned within a session, produce no aftereffect, and do not become easier over ~2 years of exposure — so a wrongly imposed manifold is not a representational inaccuracy but a region of behaviour the system cannot reach ([[wiki/concepts/manifold-constrained-learning.md]], Sadtler et al. 2014; and even readouts *inside* the manifold are learned only by re-associating an unchanged repertoire of activity patterns, so the reachable set is narrower still than the imposed topology suggests, Golub et al. 2018)

## Best current answer

**None, and the two nearest things point away from each other.** Prestructured stores make the scaffold *more* rigid on purpose, because that is what buys convex spurious-free basins and exponential capacity ([[wiki/entities/vector-hash.md]]); population-geometry measurement can *read off* the intrinsic dimensionality of a manifold a circuit has settled on, and finds it varies with task complexity ([[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]), but reading a dimension is not building one. **(brainstorm)** The measurable version is cheap and unrun: train the same integrator on families of domains with different true topology (ring / line / tree / product space) and score whether anything in the learned dynamics distinguishes them, or whether the imposed manifold simply absorbs the mismatch. Whether the manifold can be *grown* at all, on any timescale, is itself unsettled ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T77). **A partial answer for the discrete case, and it is not learning**: train one network across many environments and it builds a *superset* scaffold — units coding future transitions that are possible in at least one environment — then lets sensory input inhibit the transitions absent in the current one, so the operative topology is carved out of a learned superset within a trial and with no plasticity ([[wiki/entities/spacetime-attractor.md]], Jensen et al. 2026). It shifts the problem rather than solving it: the superset's own extent is still authored by the training distribution, and nothing infers a mask that is not observable as input **And the one experiment that unfreezes an installed manifold points away from learning it**: making the CANN bump codewords trainable, by adding the standard vector-quantisation codebook loss to an otherwise identical model, degrades every metric (FID_p 43.41 → 47.76, PSNR_p 27.77 → 24.48). The stated cause is that codewords in a scaffold stand in *dynamical* relations to one another — `e_i + a` must still be a codeword — which a per-code gradient does not respect. The result bounds nothing about topology (it perturbs code content within a fixed torus and never tests a wrong topology), but it is the wiki's first measurement that adaptivity in a prestructured code has a cost rather than merely no benefit ([[wiki/entities/gcq.md]], [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T150, Peng et al. 2025).

## The estimator now exists, so the missing experiment is fully specified and cheap

This row's `Closes when` requires a model to *infer* its own manifold's topology. The reading half of that — determining what topology a population has actually settled on, without labels and without assuming the answer — was the unstated blocker, and it is now supplied: persistent homology plus an on-manifold spline parameterization, which recovers a ring from a mouse thalamic population and needs only ~**35** simulated grid cells to recover a 2-torus (Chaudhuri et al. 2019, [[wiki/concepts/topological-latent-decoding.md]]).

Two consequences for this row.

- **The brainstormed experiment above is now runnable as written.** Train the same integrator on domain families with different true topology (ring / line / tree / product space), run the estimator on the hidden states, and ask whether the recovered barcode tracks the domain or the architecture. Cell counts are not a barrier at model scale, and the instrument returns a topology rather than a number, which is what the comparison needs.
- **It also supplies the negative control the row has been missing.** Every global embedding method — PCA, Isomap, t-SNE, UMAP — *assumes* trivial topology, so a model whose imposed manifold is the wrong shape would be reported by all of them as a higher-dimensional trivial manifold. The wiki's existing dimension estimates therefore cannot have detected a topology mismatch even where one existed, which is why "nothing would notice if it were wrong" was true for instrumental as well as architectural reasons.
