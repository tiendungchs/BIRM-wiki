# G125 — Every internally-generated reward in the wiki is immune to its own delivery; the biological one extinguishes each time it is claimed

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** One agent holds a *learned, separately-stored* value for its own intermediate targets, updated by the primary reward's arrival or non-arrival after the target is reached, and the resulting dynamics are shown to matter: a subgoal that stops being backed by primary reward loses its pull without the designer touching it, and an ablation replacing that store with a constant pseudo-reward of the same initial magnitude produces measurably worse behaviour — subgoal farming, or persistence on an intermediate target after the path through it has stopped paying.

## Why it blocks the target

Every self-generated reward here is a fixed function of state, immune to how often it is collected:

| Mechanism | Intermediate reward | Changes with use? |
|---|---|---|
| [[wiki/concepts/pseudo-reward-prediction-error.md]] | `r̃ = 1` at subgoal attainment | No — a constant, `1 : 1` with primary reward by fiat |
| [[wiki/concepts/eigenoption-discovery.md]] | `rᵉ(s,s′) = eᵀ(φ(s′) − φ(s))` | No — a fixed function of the Laplacian eigenvector |
| [[wiki/concepts/hindsight-goal-relabelling.md]] | `r(s,a,g) = −[f_g(s′) = 0]` | No — a designer-given predicate, recomputed identically forever |
| [[wiki/concepts/empowerment.md]] | channel capacity actuators → sensors | No — a property of the dynamics |
| [[wiki/concepts/learning-progress.md]] | rate of change of prediction error | **Yes, but for the wrong reason** — it falls because the activity is *mastered*, not because the primary reward stopped arriving |

Biology denies the invariance directly ([[wiki/concepts/conditioned-reinforcement.md]], Williams 1994). A conditioned reinforcer's value is a Pavlovian association, so **every delivery not followed by the primary reinforcer is an extinction trial on the reinforcer itself**. The source states the resulting trade-off as inherent and unresolved: presenting the intermediate reward more often strengthens the behaviour it maintains *and* devalues the currency, with opposite sign and no theory of which dominates. The exchange rate is measured, not assumed — a food-paired tone substitutes for food at **0.7** of a tone+food pairing when 50% of its own presentations end in food, **0.4** at 30%, and **0** when it is never paired (Williams & Dunn 1991b).

What the missing dynamic denies a builder is any self-correcting bound on subgoal pursuit. A pseudo-reward is a currency the agent prints; a conditioned reinforcer is a currency backed by a reserve that is audited on every transaction. The failure the backing prevents is on record in both literatures: Ribas-Fernandes et al. 2011 name paying-yourself as the framework's open problem, and Cronin 1980 *observes* it — pigeons chose the stimulus signalled S− on ~90% of trials because the immediate conditioned reinforcer outweighed the delayed primary reward, stably, for 30 sessions. With an unbacked `r̃` there is no mechanism by which that preference could ever correct itself.

Two further properties come with the backed version and no architecture here has either. (i) **Autonomy by default is wrong in one direction and right in the other**: conditioned reinforcers *resist devaluation of the primary* (Holland & Rescorla 1975 — devaluing the US cuts `S1` responding and leaves `S2` untouched; Rescorla 1977 — satiated rats' maze errors appear near the goalbox first), so the store is not a cache recomputed from the current primary value, but it *is* revised by direct non-reinforcement of the intermediate target itself. That is a specific and copyable invalidation policy: **invalidate on the subgoal's own unbacked delivery, never by inference from the goal's revaluation** — the same re-visit-only rule [[wiki/concepts/valuation-system-decomposition.md]] reports for instrumental incentive value, applied one level up. (ii) The value is **context-relative**: delay-reduction theory makes it the fractional reduction in expected time-to-reward relative to the situation's average, so the same subgoal is worth a great deal in a lean environment and nothing in a rich one, and can be **negative** (Fantino 1969: delay reductions of `.92`/`.75`/`.63` and `.75`/`.25`/`−.11` for two fixed terminal links as the ambient rate is varied, with preference swinging from indifference to exclusivity).

## Best current answer

**None, and the nearest constructs change a different quantity.**

- `G33` flags the `r̃` : `r` ratio as "set by fiat" and asks what fixes it. This row is the next question: not what the ratio is at initialisation, but that it is a *constant at all*. The two are complementary and Williams 1994 answers `G33`'s half — the ratio is the intermediate target's own pairing probability, which is measured and learned.
- `G118` splits an outcome's hedonic magnitude from the pursuit it commands. That split is about *one* outcome's two quantities; this row is about whether an *intermediate* target's single quantity has an update rule. An architecture could have `G118`'s pair and still print unbacked subgoal currency.
- [[wiki/concepts/multi-horizon-value-learning.md]] and every critic here learn `V` from `δ`, so a *state's* value certainly extinguishes. What does not is the **pseudo-reward term itself** — `r̃` sits where `r` sits in the update, and nothing writes to it.
- [[wiki/concepts/learning-progress.md]] is the one mechanism whose intrinsic reward decays, and the decay is driven by mastery rather than by the primary reward's absence, so an activity that is still hard and pays nothing keeps its pull. That is the opposite policy.

The cheap machine version needs no neuroscience and is one line. Give a hierarchical agent a scalar `w_o ∈ [0,1]` per option, deliver `r̃ = w_o` on termination, and update `w_o` by the same rule as any Pavlovian association: raise it when the primary reward follows termination within the trace, lower it when it does not. Then check (i) whether the farming failure a constant `r̃` permits disappears, (ii) whether `w_o` converges to the option's empirical probability of leading to reward, which is the quantity the exchange-rate measurements imply, and (iii) whether a devaluation of the primary reward leaves `w_o` standing until the option is actually run unbacked — the biologically-observed autonomy, and the property that makes this a store rather than a cached product.

The single caveat the source itself supplies, and the reason this row is `L1` rather than settled: in both of Williams' new experiments the advantage conferred by pairing **disappears at asymptote**, with paired and unpaired signal conditions converging after ~10 sessions. The demonstrated function of conditioned value is on *acquisition rate*. A builder therefore has a strong argument that a backed subgoal currency learns faster and no evidence that it performs better once learned, which is exactly what `Closes when` above demands be measured.
