# G55 — Every machine controller is either one resource or *n* independent ones; none has a shared component plus operation-specific parameters

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Why it blocks the target

The biological control layer factors into a common component that all control tasks load on, plus updating-specific and shifting-specific components with their *own* genetic influences, receptor dependences and lesion sites — and no inhibition-specific component at all. Wiki architectures sit at the two extremes: a single monolithic controller ([[wiki/entities/h-jepa.md]]'s configurator, any task-embedding conditioning) has no diversity, and independent per-stripe gating ([[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]) has no unity. The two extremes make opposite, testable predictions about a population of trained instances, and neither matches. The sharpest evidence against a single shared budget is *anticorrelation*: dopamine depletion in marmoset prefrontal cortex impairs delayed response while **improving** extra-dimensional set-shifting, so at least one control parameter has opposite signs on two components — a stability–flexibility axis no architecture here exposes, and one that an inverted-U dose–response makes a *tuned* quantity rather than a gain to be maximised

## Best current answer

None. The nearest thing is per-module gain, which no wiki model varies independently. The measurement protocol is cheap and unused: train a population of instances, run ≥3 tasks per control construct, fit a bifactor model over the population, and report factor correlations — which would also fix the task-impurity confound under every single-task control claim in the wiki (biological latent correlations are 0.42–0.63 where the raw task correlations are −0.05–0.34)
