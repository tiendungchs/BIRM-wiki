# G124 — A manifold code's drift is set by the alignment of its noise, and every robustness number in the wiki is reported against the one perturbation that cannot move it

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L2`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A model with a continuous state manifold reports the drift of its own state under zeroed task input, decomposed into the part driven by independent per-unit noise and the part driven by noise entering through the update channel — and a change to that channel is shown to move the measured drift while a change in per-unit noise does not.

## Why it blocks the target

A state on a `d`-dimensional manifold in an `N`-dimensional space can only be corrupted along the manifold; motion off it is restored. A unit of **isotropic high-dimensional** noise therefore has variance `1/N` along the manifold, so per-unit noise — Poisson spiking, dropout, weight jitter, any input projecting in a spatially uncorrelated way — is close to impotent, and over-dispersing it does not help. A **low-dimensional perturbation aligned to the manifold** is not restored at all.

This is measured, not argued. In mouse anterodorsal thalamus during REM sleep the head-direction ring diffuses at **1.1 ± 0.04 rad² s⁻¹** (0.52 ± 0.03 and 1.3 ± 0.06 in two other animals), which is **20–50× larger** than a matched network model predicts from independent per-neuron noise, and is accounted for by manifold-aligned input noise of SD 8.5 rad² s⁻¹ with ≤ 20 ms correlation time — an amplitude *comparable to the waking head-velocity drive itself* (Chaudhuri et al. 2019, `raw/chaudhuri-2019-intrinsic-attractor-manifold-head-direction-circuit.md`, [[wiki/concepts/topological-latent-decoding.md]]). The channel that updates the code and the channel that corrupts it are the same wire, and fidelity is therefore limited by **input** noise rather than internal noise — a conclusion previously available only for sensory and sensorimotor pathways.

Two things this blocks.

- **Every robustness claim in the wiki for a low-dimensional code is measured against the impotent perturbation.** Cell-dropout and rate-noise survival numbers ([[wiki/entities/bb-model.md]]: 20% cell loss + 20% rate noise), unit ablation, synapse ablation ([[wiki/entities/stsp-working-memory-rnn.md]]) and capacity-under-noise bounds are all high-dimensional and all fall as `1/N`. A model can pass all of them and drift arbitrarily under a small correlated perturbation of its velocity input. No page reports the second number.
- **The design lever is on the wrong side.** Every drift-control knob in the wiki is internal — basin depth, adaptation strength, inverse temperature, gain ([[wiki/concepts/attractor-dynamics.md]] `G38`, `G15`). If drift is set by the drive's alignment, none of them is the operative parameter, and the operative one — how much of the update channel's variance is signal — is neither measured nor regulated anywhere.

## Best current answer

**None, and the two nearest things are on the biological side.** The measurement exists exactly once, in tissue, and required an unsupervised manifold decoder plus a matched network model to make the comparison ([[wiki/concepts/topological-latent-decoding.md]]). The circuit's own apparent answer is `N`: thousands of cells for a single circular variable, which is what makes the `1/N` suppression large — a functional account of population size that the source raises and does not commit to.

On the machine side the ingredients are all present and never combined. [[wiki/entities/adaptive-cann.md]] has analytic thresholds for the stability-versus-mobility trade but parameterises it by adaptation strength, i.e. internally. [[wiki/entities/stp-flickering-cann.md]] is the wiki's one model that injects *coloured* input noise (`τ_N dη/dt = −η + A_n ξ(t)`) rather than white per-unit noise — the right class of perturbation — but uses it to drive state switching, not to measure drift, and never compares it against an independent-noise control of matched variance. [[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]] reports bump diffusion with variance growing linearly in delay, and that the drift predicts behavioural error; nothing decomposes it.

**(brainstorm) The cheap version is a two-line ablation.** Zero the task input, run the recurrence, and measure `⟨(α(t+τ) − α(t))²⟩` against `τ` under (i) per-unit Gaussian noise and (ii) the same total variance injected through the model's own velocity/update projection. The ratio is the number this row asks for, and the prediction is that it scales as `N`. The design consequence, if it holds, is that **noise budgets should be allocated per channel, not per unit** — and that a filter on the update channel buys more fidelity than any amount of internal regularisation.

## Status

`OPEN` — the physics is settled and the measurement has been made once, in one biological circuit. Nothing in the wiki's machine half reports the decomposition, and no architecture treats the update channel's noise as a designable quantity.
