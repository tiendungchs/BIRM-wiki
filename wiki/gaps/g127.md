# G127 — Every self-generated reward in the wiki is spent the instant it is earned; nothing holds a balance, and behaviour is controlled by the redemption schedule

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L1`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A hierarchical agent represents its manufactured reward as a **held inventory with its own redemption contingency**, and the split is shown to matter on both halves: (a) with the redemption delay equalised across options, the agent prefers the larger-later option, and with it left unequal-and-shorter for the sooner option, the same agent prefers the smaller-sooner one — the preference reversal produced with the subgoal-production contingency untouched (Jackson & Hackenberg 1996, three of three subjects); and (b) the agent's **accumulation** policy moves with the two prices in opposite directions — batching more when redemption is made costlier, less when each unit of manufactured reward is made costlier (Yankelevitz et al. 2008).

## Why it blocks the target

Every internally generated reward in the wiki is produced and consumed in the same step:

| Mechanism | When the manufactured reward is credited | Balance? | Redemption schedule? |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[wiki/concepts/pseudo-reward-prediction-error.md]] | at subgoal completion | none | none |
| [[wiki/concepts/eigenoption-discovery.md]] | per transition | none | none |
| [[wiki/concepts/hindsight-goal-relabelling.md]] | when the relabelled predicate holds | none | none |
| [[wiki/concepts/learning-progress.md]] | per update | none | none |

Token-reinforcement procedures ([[wiki/concepts/token-reinforcement.md]]) split that one event into three separately-priced contingencies — **token production** (responses per unit of manufactured reward), **exchange production** (units per redemption opportunity) and **token exchange** (units per primary reward) — and the behaviour is not a function of their product:

- **Choice is set by the redemption delay, not by the production delay.** Pigeons given 1 token now vs 3 tokens later reversed preference completely between equal- and unequal-exchange-delay arrangements, with the token contingency identical. Humans do the same (Hyten et al. 1994). The "species difference" in self-control is, in this dataset, a difference in where the cash-out sits.
- **Amortised cost is the wrong variable.** Holding unit price constant (responses per reinforcer) while raising the procurement cost lowered response rate (Bullock & Hackenberg 2006). Behaviour tracked work-and-delay per *redemption event*.
- **The balance is an observation.** Removing the token display while leaving every contingency intact collapsed accumulation (Yankelevitz et al. 2008), so the stored quantity is read by the policy and not merely accrued by the learner.

**What a builder inherits.** An agent that credits `r̃` at subgoal completion has silently chosen the arrangement that produces subgoal capture — payment immediate, and unequal across options — without the choice ever being posed. `T367` (does the agent prefer its own subgoal payment to the goal?) is therefore not an inherent defect of self-payment but a **schedule parameter**, and no architecture here exposes it. Three further capabilities follow from the split and exist nowhere: deciding *when to cash in* (a control problem over one's own intrinsic reward), pricing a decomposition by redemption episodes rather than by total work ([[wiki/concepts/optimal-hierarchy-criterion.md]]), and withdrawing manufactured reward already earned, which is a punisher in its own right beyond the reinforcement-density change it causes (Raiff et al. 2008).

## Best current answer

**None.** The nearest constructions each supply one piece and none supplies the separation.

- **Multi-step returns and `n`-step credit** delay *when the learner is updated*, not when the agent is paid; the reward is still consumed at generation and there is no quantity the policy can observe or choose to spend.
- **Options with termination-time bonuses** ([[wiki/concepts/temporal-abstraction-options.md]]) put the payment at `β`, which is exactly the unequal-exchange arrangement.
- **Behavioural-economic fits** of the animal data (unit price plus temporal discounting; delay-reduction theory read in time) describe the choice but are not agents.

The cheapest transcription is a two-line change to any hierarchical learner: accumulate `r̃` in a register, expose the register to the policy, and deliver it to the critic on a separate schedule. The experiment that would close the row is then the ED/UD reversal run on the agent.
