# G31 — Generalization difficulty is uncomputable and unapproximated

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** A computable approximation to `GD` is validated against per-task human success rate on a benchmark with solvers, closing the loop Chollet 2019 proposed and nobody has run.

## Why it blocks the target

The one quantity that would make G17 measurable — `GD`, `P` (priors) and `E` (experience) — is defined through Kolmogorov complexity. Without an approximation, "this benchmark measures abstraction" stays a claim about design intent, and no architecture can be scored on *how much* structure it recovered rather than whether it passed

## Best current answer

Empirical proxy only: use per-task human success rate as a stand-in for `GD` and check, once solvers exist, whether it correlates with an AIT approximation (Chollet 2019's own proposed validation, never run). Every downscaling of `K` in the wiki — minimum description length, resource-bounded complexity — gives up the property that made the quantity meaningful, exactly as in [[wiki/concepts/universal-induction.md]] **The proxy has now been run, once, at scale** ([[wiki/entities/arc-agi-2.md]]): ARC-AGI-2 computes a per-task **empirical difficulty index** — the proportion of human participants achieving full correctness, from 407 participants over 13,405 test-pair attempts — and uses it to assemble subsets whose mean human accuracy matches to ≤1 percentage point. That is Chollet 2019's own proposed stand-in for `GD`, executed. Two things it still does not deliver: the correlation with an AIT approximation (no approximation exists to correlate against, so the validation half of the proposal is untouched), and per-task reliability — with 2–10 testers per task the *subset means* are calibrated while any individual task's index has enormous variance. So `GD` is now empirically ordinal at the level of task populations and still unquantified at the level of a task. **And a second axis the proxy does not index: per-*concept* coverage** ([[wiki/entities/raven.md]]). Human success rate orders *tasks*; it says nothing about which concepts a solver's successes are spread over, and the same program can be above its benchmark average on one concept and at less than half of it on another (ARC-Kaggle2: 19% overall, 29% on *top/bottom*, 8% on *boundary*). A difficulty index per task and a coverage profile per concept are different quantities, and only the first has ever been collected. **The coverage profile has now been collected, at 16 concepts** ([[wiki/entities/conceptarc.md]]): 10 hand-authored instantiations per concept, three test inputs each, scored for 415 humans and three solvers. What it shows about the quantity: concept difficulty **does not transfer across solvers** — the first-place ARC-Kaggle program's best concept (*Extend To Boundary*, 0.77) is GPT-4's worst (0.07) — so a coverage profile is a property of the (solver, concept) pair and cannot be collapsed into a per-concept difficulty index the way human success rate collapses per-task difficulty. `GD` per task and coverage per concept are therefore not two views of one scalar; the first is now empirically ordinal over task populations and the second is only measurable relative to a solver.
