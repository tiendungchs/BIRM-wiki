# G114 — Every timescale the wiki cites is a biased point estimate, and the estimator that prices the bias has never been run on any of them

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Level:** `L0-INSTR`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** At least one load-bearing timescale in the wiki is re-estimated with a bias-aware estimator at the data's actual trial duration and reported as a **posterior** — the seven macaque areal `τ` of [[wiki/concepts/intrinsic-timescale-measurement.md]], or the per-module `τ` of a trained model measured by the same protocol — and the result states how much of the quoted spread survives. A cheaper sufficient version: run the parametric-bootstrap pre-check ([[wiki/concepts/timescale-estimator-bias.md]]) at the relevant trace lengths and show the bias is small, which licenses the existing point estimates as they stand.

## Why it blocks the target

The timescale family (`G67` number and boundaries, `G78` design-time provisioning, `G94` run-time adaptation) is argued with numbers: a `7×` intrinsic spread, a `10×` functional spread, a `50–350 ms` band that fits inside one of [[wiki/entities/ms-ssm.md]]'s dyadic blocks, a `τ`–`B` correlation offered as a one-fit discriminator between gradient-generated and hand-provisioned banks. Every one of those numbers comes from a **direct exponential fit to a sample autocorrelation**, which is negatively biased by finite sample size, by an amount that grows with the true `τ` and with the shortness of the trial and that cannot be subtracted because it depends on the unknown answer ([[wiki/concepts/timescale-estimator-bias.md]]). So the quantity the design arguments turn on is systematically under-reported, non-uniformly across the very axis being compared, and is not comparable across datasets with different trial durations — which is exactly how the wiki's figures were assembled.

What survives and what does not is sharply divided. The **orderings** are safe as long as the bias is monotone in `τ`: rank correlations (`r_s = 0.97` against anatomy, `r_s = 0.89` across six datasets) are the robust part. The **magnitudes and the ratios between them** are the fragile part, and they are what `G67` was re-aimed on.

## Best current answer

**The estimator exists, is published with code, and is cheap on a model.** Fit a mixture of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes by adaptive approximate Bayesian computation: simulate synthetic data matched to the observed data in duration, trial count, mean and variance, so the synthetic summary statistic carries the same bias, and accept parameters whose synthetic autocorrelation (or PSD) is close to the observed one. Returns a posterior, plus a Bayes factor `B₂₁(ε) = CDF_{M₂}(ε)/CDF_{M₁}(ε)` that decides the *number* of timescales. On macaque V4 it selects two timescales over one and beats the direct double-exponential fit by `3×` in residual distance (`P < 10⁻¹⁰`); the direct fit's estimates are systematically short, as in every synthetic case with known ground truth (Zeraati, Engel & Levina 2022, `abcTau`).

Why the row is still open: the method is a *reading* in the wiki, not a measurement. Nobody has re-estimated Murray's areas (the raw spike trains are six labs' property), and no wiki model has had its module timescales measured at all — so the bias is priced in principle and unpriced in fact. The one thing that is free and not done: a trained network is its own generator, trials cost a forward pass, and the pre-check prices the bias at a given trace length before any posterior is computed.

## Status

`OPEN` — and it is an instrument row rather than a mechanism row: nothing about the architecture changes if the bias turns out small, but several `L1` arguments lose their quantitative basis if it turns out large. **Timescale family:** this row gates the numeric premises of `G67`, `G78` and `G94`, and is distinct from `T323` (which asks at what *level of description* a timescale exists) and `T319` (which asks whether it is fixed or input-dependent) — both of those presuppose that a reported `τ` is an estimate of the quantity it names, which is the assumption this row suspends. `T323`'s per-neuron test is the case where the suspension bites hardest: a single neuron's trial-wise autocorrelation is the shortest, noisiest sample in the problem, so a direct per-neuron fit would be the most biased measurement anyone could make of it.
