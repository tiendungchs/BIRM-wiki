# G59 — Every hierarchical controller lets its lower level see the same observation as its upper level

**Status:** `OPEN`  
**Kind:** `part`  
**Registry:** [[wiki/architectural-gaps.md]]  
**Closes when:** _unset — see [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]_

## Why it blocks the target

Options, feudal manager/worker, PBWM stripes and every two-level policy in the wiki hand the lower level a goal or context vector *alongside* the raw observation, so the lower level can in principle learn a bypass mapping observation→action and leave the goal channel unused — the standard failure mode of feudal architectures. Biology withholds the argument: with tactic and spatial cue separated in time, monkey pmPFC carries tactic, cue position and action, while pre-SMA one station caudal carries tactic and action with **no significant cue-position signal**, despite being spatially tuned in tasks with no tactic choice; pre-SMA receives few direct parietal projections and is fed by prefrontal cortex, so the sensory argument is gated upstream. The binding `action = f(tactic, cue)` therefore has a unique address, and what descends is the resolved output plus the protocol identity

## Best current answer

Build the inter-level interface as a *narrowing*: level `k−1` receives level `k`'s abstract variable and its resolved output, and is architecturally denied the observation that produced them. Non-bypassability is then enforced by wiring rather than by an auxiliary loss. Two open ends: the downstream stage still codes the abstract variable through execution, so the variable is a persistent mode setting and not a consumed intermediate, and nothing says what an implementation stage does with a mode setting it cannot re-derive; and the withholding is inferred from projection densities plus a between-study discrepancy, never measured directionally
