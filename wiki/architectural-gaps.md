# Architectural Gaps

What a brain-inspired reasoning model needs and no current architecture supplies. Updated after every ingest that opens or closes a gap, and rewritten at each lint pass.

**Status key:** `OPEN` — no known solution · `PARTIAL` — solved in a restricted setting · `CONTESTED` — candidate solutions exist and disagree.

**Two kinds of gap, identified at the 284-source lint pass** ([[wiki/overview.md]] section 15). Almost every row below is a request for a **part** — a mechanism, a loss, a representation — and could in principle be closed by building one better component. **Six are not.** `G84` (no module knows its topological position), `G85` (none reads its topology as a control variable), `G88` (none derives its direction of information flow from its own weights), `G90` (none has an internally generated mode or an arbitrator for it), `G91` (none has a discrete commit, or a learned gate on one) and `G93` (none has an *addressed* context channel) are properties of the **arrangement** of components, and there is no component one can add to a wiki architecture that closes them. They are marked here rather than in a column because sorting the table on the distinction is an open task (`P2` at [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]]).

> **Note (duplicate audit, 284 sources).** **All 95 rows are open** — 64 `OPEN`, 27 `PARTIAL`, 4 `CONTESTED`, none closed. `G54` carried a bare `PARTIAL` and is normalised; the 284-source pass normalised three bare tokens but matched on `OPEN` only, so the file's one bare `PARTIAL` survived it. **No gap is a duplicate of another, but four families were stating one question in several rows without citing each other** and are now cross-linked in both directions: `G52`/`G56` (a connection's four state registers, and one of them), `G67`/`G78`/`G94` (how many time constants, who sets one at design time, who adapts one at run time), `G32`/`G66` (the experience stream, and its pair-sampling case) and `G73`/`G75` (the problem representation, and its parse). None was findable by title overlap — see `S12` at [[wiki/priority-tasks.md]].

The closest things the wiki holds are one level too coarse or one level too slow: [[wiki/concepts/neuromodulatory-metaparameters.md]] varies gain-like quantities but broadcasts one global scalar (and G93 records that it has no addressing), [[wiki/concepts/precision-weighting.md]] has the right *form* — a multiplicative confidence on a channel — but is estimated from prediction error rather than from input statistics and carries no time-constant term, and [[wiki/concepts/spike-frequency-adaptation.md]] adapts a threshold per unit without pooling and without touching the integration window. **The composability test is the cheap part and nobody runs it**: fit each factor alone, freeze both, and check the product against the joint condition — exactly the protocol that made this result count in the thalamus ([[wiki/concepts/predictive-adequacy.md]]) | Carandini et al. 2005 (Mante et al. 2005), [[wiki/entities/early-visual-system.md]] | `OPEN` **Timescale family, linked at the 284-source duplicate audit: `G78` is this row's design-time form** (nothing sets a slow variable's `τ`, and memory span is that `τ`), and `G67` asks how many `τ` there should be. | G95 | **Every self-supervised system in the wiki has its invariance group *declared* by a hand-written augmentation list; nothing here discovers which directions are nuisance from the input's own statistics** | Invariance is the crux of recognition and, by extension, of any code that must treat many surface realizations of one thing as the same thing. Joint-embedding methods buy it by asserting the answer: crops, colour jitter, blur, masking — a fixed list, chosen by the designer, identical for every unit and every locus, frozen for the run. The biology gets the same product from a signal that is already in the stream. Object identity is temporally stable and retinal images are not, so the fast directions of population motion *are* the identity-preserving directions on average; a rule that makes temporally adjacent inputs produce similar outputs factorizes identity from nuisance without ever being told what an object is, and the supply is free (~10⁸ saccade-driven translation experiences per year). The difference is not efficiency, it is three properties: the group is **discovered** rather than declared, it is **local** (each cortical locus infers the nuisance directions of its own ~10K-dimensional subspace rather than inheriting one global list), and it is **revisable** — and the revisability is measured, not argued: swapping object identity across a saccade so that one object at position A is reliably followed by a different object at position B *destroys* existing position and size tolerance in inferior temporal neurons and builds new, incorrect tolerance within hours, changing perception accordingly (Li & DiCarlo 2008; Cox et al. 2005). **Without it, what a system treats as irrelevant is a hyperparameter of the training pipeline, cannot change after deployment, and cannot differ between layers** — and the same rule's failure mode is inherited whole: a system that learns its nuisance group from temporal adjacency learns the *capture process's* invariances (camera motion, editing cuts, playlist order), which is [[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]] one level down from the task | Partial and split. The *objective* exists in machine form — the invariance term of [[wiki/entities/barlow-twins.md]] and [[wiki/entities/byol.md]] is exactly "make two views agree", and its biological ancestry is explicit (Földiák 1991; Wallis & Rolls 1997; slow feature analysis, Wiskott & Sejnowski 2002). The *data source* exists — [[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]] trains on 1M+ hours of video — but it spends time on **prediction across a gap** rather than on **equivalence between neighbours**, so no nuisance direction is ever explicitly factored out. Nothing in the wiki combines the two, and nothing at all has the locality or the revisability. The measurement that would make this concrete is cheap and unrun: train one system with augmentation-defined pairing and one with adjacency-defined pairing on the same stream, and score identity decodability on **transformation conditions the stream's time structure never paired** | [[wiki/concepts/manifold-untangling.md]], [[wiki/entities/ventral-visual-stream.md]] (DiCarlo, Zoccolan & Rust 2012) | `PARTIAL` |
| G96 | **Top-down control needs an address inside the control layer, and every model gets it by reading the ground truth** | A controller can only modulate what it can aim at. | G98 | **Nothing decides which knowledge belongs in weights and which belongs in an addressable store** | The wiki holds both families and no criterion. ## How gaps are used

A gap is worth recording only if it is *actionable*: it names a mechanism the wiki would adopt if it existed. Gaps that reduce to "the problem is hard" are folded into the open-problems section of the relevant concept page instead.

**Dependency structure.** These are not independent. G16 sits *under* G1: it says the factorization G1 wants is not the kind of thing data can supply, so G1 is a design decision to be paid for rather than a result to be waited for. G17 is the instrument problem for the whole table — without an out-of-distribution test, no gap can be shown closed, because an architecture that assumed the latent variable away is indistinguishable from one that recovered it. G1 is the root — G2 and G3 are the conditions under which its factorization is meaningful, and G9 is what happens when it is applied to itself. G7→G8 and G11→G12 are each a problem followed by the mechanism that would dissolve it. G13 is the accounting frame for all of them: an architecture is scored by which hardness sources it reaches, not by whether it "works". G14 and G15 are the two halves of the write/read loop around the factorization: G14 is how the meta-graph gets updated from experience, G15 is how the graph gets used once it exists. G19 and G20 are the corresponding pair one level down, on the *write rule for fast M*: G19 is what gets written, G20 is when the signal that authorizes the write arrives. G18 is the tax on G9's new candidate answer — self-reference buys the third tier, and generalization is the price. G21–G23 open a column the table did not have: what is in the graph *before* learning. G23 is the condition under which installed priors can be stacked at all; G21 is what they cannot do once installed; G22 is the reason G21's candidate answers do not obviously work. G21 also inverts G4 — where G4 asks how a vocabulary is induced, G21 assumes several are given and asks how they are joined.

**What would close one.** A gap closes when an ingest supplies an architecture that (a) instantiates the named mechanism and (b) is tested on a task where the corresponding latent variable is actually hidden. An architecture that assumes away the latent variable does not count as evidence. Per G17, condition (b) means an **out-of-distribution** test with a well-defined intended solution — an i.i.d. result never closes a gap here.
