# Shortcut Learning

**A shortcut is a decision rule that solves the training and i.i.d. test data but fails under distribution shift — the learner recovered a sufficient correlation instead of the intended structure (Geirhos et al. 2020).**

This is the failure mode the whole wiki is organized against. In the core framing ([[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]]) a shortcut is a **spurious edge that survives training because nothing in the objective distinguishes it from a causal one** — hardness source 5, gap G6. Geirhos et al. 2020 supply what that framing lacked: evidence that this is the *default* outcome across every subfield, an account of why, and the only measurement that detects it.

---

## Taxonomy of decision rules

Nested sets, each stricter than the last (Geirhos et al. 2020, Fig. 3):

| Rule class | Solves training set | Solves i.i.d. test | Solves o.o.d. test | Feature type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-solution | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | uninformative |
| Overfitting solution | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | overfitting |
| **Shortcut** | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | shortcut |
| Intended solution | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | intended |

**The decisive consequence: the top two rows are indistinguishable under i.i.d. evaluation.** Toy case — stars vs. moons separable by (a) shape, (b) white-pixel count, (c) location; a fully connected network learns (c). "Which rule is intended is in the eye of the beholder": *the intended solution is not a function of the training data.* It enters only through inductive bias.

For the wiki this is a statement about the meta-graph. The intended solution is the one whose edges hold across the whole environment family; a shortcut is an edge that holds only in the sampled instances. **A learner given one environment cannot tell them apart in principle** — which is why the two-level hierarchy is not merely a sample-complexity convenience but the *identifiability condition* for the intended rule.

## Where shortcuts come from

| Origin | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Shortcut opportunities in data** | Systematic object↔context relations are natural, not artefactual (cows on grass; a hospital-specific metal token co-varying with pneumonia prevalence) | Persist at scale — "Big Data" does not dilute systematic bias; data alone rarely constrains a model, and cannot replace assumptions |
| **Discriminative feature selection** | Any feature sufficient to discriminate is taken; no notion of how features *combine* to define an object. Texture suffices for ImageNet, so global shape is largely ignored | Decision rules resting on a *single predictive pixel*; **excessive invariance** — models invariant to nearly every feature humans use |
| **Sub-symbolic subtlety** | High-frequency patterns invisible to humans are highly predictive; adversarial examples are perturbations of exactly these | Adversarial vulnerability read as a *symptom* of shortcut learning, not a separate defect |

**A fourth origin, and the only one the practitioner *installs* deliberately: the choice of negatives.** In a contrastive objective the negatives define what has to be discriminated, and therefore what the cheapest sufficient feature is. Crops of one image share a colour histogram while histograms differ across images, so a contrastive task on crop-only augmentations is solved by colour histogram alone and the representation is never charged for anything else — which is why SimCLR needs colour jitter and loses 27.6 points without it. BYOL, whose target is a *regression* onto a representation that already contains more rather than a discrimination, loses 13.1 points on the same ablation and still reaches 59.4% (Grill et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/byol.md]]). The generalisable statement: **an objective's shortcut is fixed by its noise distribution, so augmentation design in a contrastive method is shortcut design** — the same free parameter [[wiki/concepts/cross-modal-grounding.md]] identifies in CLIP's in-batch captions, seen from the data side. **The generalisation is now contested, and the counter-example is the strongest kind — a method with no noise distribution at all.** Barlow Twins scores its two views by a cross-correlation matrix, has no negatives and no discrimination anywhere, and degrades on the same progressive-removal sweep like SimCLR (≈ −25 at crop-only) rather than like BYOL (Zbontar et al. 2021, [[wiki/entities/barlow-twins.md]]). Its authors read fragility as *fidelity* — the invariances a two-view loss learns are the ones its distortions demand, and BYOL's being "generic and intriguingly independent" of the distortions is the anomaly. On that reading the shortcut is not authored by the negatives but by the *augmentation set being the only signal*, which is true of every joint-embedding loss whose target is another view ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T165).

**A fifth origin, and the one with the largest measured coefficient: the *pair sampler* in a self-supervised objective** (Assran et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/i-jepa.md]]). Where the fourth origin is which negatives are drawn, this is which *positives* are formed — and it applies to methods with no negatives at all, so it survives the counter-example above. In an image JEPA the two views are two blocks of one image, and the distribution over blocks is the entire remaining degree of freedom: random small patches or one large block (the masked-image-modelling defaults) make the target predictable by continuing local texture, which is a shortcut in this page's exact sense, and the representation is never charged for object-level content. Holding loss, architecture and data fixed, moving from those samplers to four semantically-sized targets from a spatially distributed context is worth **15.5–20.2 → 54.2** on ImageNet-1% linear evaluation. **The generalisable statement is that a joint-embedding method's shortcut is authored by whatever supplies its pairs** — negatives where there are negatives, augmentations where there are augmentations, the mask sampler where there is neither — which unifies the fourth origin and its counter-example under the data lever rather than under the objective ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T167).

**Biological parallel (the paper's central move): shortcut learning is a property of learning systems, not of deep networks.**

| System | Phenomenon | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Rats in a maze | *Unintended cue learning* — apparent colour discrimination was odour discrimination of the paint | Ability vanishes when smell is controlled |
| Students | *Surface learning* — rote reproduction | Scores *well* on multiple-choice, fails on essay questions |
| Animal conditioning | *Blocking effect* — an already-predictive cue prevents association of a new, equally predictive one | The behavioural analogue of excessive invariance |
| Language change | *Principle of least effort* — form erodes toward minimal speaker effort, shaped by anatomy (architecture) and prior experience (data) | — |

**Implication for the brain-as-prior argument.** The brain does not escape this failure mode; it is *subject* to it. So the biology cannot be mined for a shortcut-proof learning rule — what differs is which solutions are cheap, i.e. the priors ([[wiki/concepts/neuroscience-ai-transfer.md]]).

## Generalisation is misdirected, not absent

Deep networks generalise o.o.d. *enormously* — an abstract pattern with a curved body and strings is classified "guitar" with high certainty; huge sets of images humans see as noise map confidently to object categories. Conversely, rotation, blur, a few pixels of translation, or a texture swap on intact shape derail predictions.

> **Generalisation failure is neither a failure to learn nor a failure to generalise — it is a failure to generalise in the *intended direction*. Using a feature set creates insensitivity to everything else; the model generalises o.o.d. exactly along the axes its chosen features ignore.**

This cuts against reading o.o.d. failure purely as capacity or as mixture-fitting — recorded as T6 in [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]]. Operationally the two readings differ in prescription: *fit the right object* (add hierarchy) vs. *choose the right invariances* (add bias).

## The four levers: inductive bias

Whether a solution is easy to learn — and shortcuts are learned because they are easy — is fixed by four components jointly, not by data alone (Geirhos et al. 2020, Box II). This is the wiki's **control surface**: the complete list of places a bias toward graph-structured solutions can be inserted.

| Lever | How it biases | Known effects |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure — architecture** | Convolution makes *location* hard to use, a prior strong enough that untrained networks support inpainting and denoising; transformer attention layers model inter-word relations | Implicit priors are mostly opaque; even ReLU produces unwarranted far-from-data confidence |
| **Experience — training data** | Blocking a specific shortcut by construction | Works for adversarial vulnerability and texture bias; adding *more* data does not. **The wiki's largest measured instance sits in this row**: in a self-supervised objective the *pair sampler* is training data, and swapping it is worth a factor of 3.5 with the loss held fixed ([[wiki/entities/i-jepa.md]]) |
| **Goal — loss function** | **Cross-entropy stops learning once a simple sufficient predictor is found**; modifications force use of all available information; regularizers using extra dataset information disentangle intended from shortcut features | The most direct lever on "how much evidence must the rule use" |
| **Learning — optimisation** | Stochastic gradient descent is biased toward simple functions; *large* learning rates learn simple patterns shared across examples, *small* ones enable complex-pattern learning and memorisation | Architecture×optimiser interaction poorly understood; strong claims only in simple cases |

**Three levers or four?** Richards et al. 2019 give the same control surface with **training data removed**: objective function, learning rule, architecture are the three things a designer specifies, and the environment is "a crucial consideration to anchor the core components, but not one of the components itself" — on the grounds that the components must pass through the genome while the environment supplies the unbounded information. Geirhos et al. 2020's data lever is the one that blocks a shortcut *by construction*, which is precisely what this page shows is needed and what the three-component reading demotes. Recorded as [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T15; full statement of the rival framing on [[wiki/concepts/three-component-framework.md]].

**(brainstorm)** Read against the wiki's target: the factorization `p = f(g, x)` is a *shortcut-avoidance device*. A shortcut is by construction a rule that reads `x` where the intended rule reads `g`; nothing in the data distinguishes them, so the split must be imposed on one of these four levers. The wiki has so far treated `g`/`x` separation as something an architecture would *discover* (G1). This source says it cannot be — it must be *paid for* by bias, and the four rows above are the exhaustive set of accounts to pay from. The cross-entropy row is the sharpest candidate: a loss that terminates at the first sufficient predictor will never search for the structural one when a content one is available.

## Diagnosis and measurement

| Practice | Statement |
|---|---|
| **Dataset ≠ ability** | ImageNet was meant to measure object recognition; models largely count texture patches. A dataset is useful only while it remains a proxy for the ability. Reproducing labels per se is uninteresting — a lookup table does that |
| **Morgan's Canon for machine learning** | *Never attribute to high-level abilities that which can be adequately explained by shortcut learning.* Blocks the **same-strategy assumption**: human-level performance does not license inferring a human-like algorithm (Marr's algorithmic level is unconstrained by matched behaviour) |
| **Surprisingly strong baselines** | Run a model that provably lacks the intended feature — local features only, single cue words, answering movie questions without the movie. If it scores well, the benchmark admits a shortcut |
| **o.o.d. testing as default** | The i.i.d. assumption has been called "the big lie in machine learning"; i.i.d. validation is structurally incapable of separating rows 3 and 4 of the taxonomy |

**The strong baseline built into the *trial set* rather than run as a separate model** (Qu et al. 2026). On a 2-D rank map, every comparison is either **congruent** (one item dominates on both dimensions, so "which one usually wins" answers it) or **incongruent** (each item dominates on a different dimension, so only the joint structure answers it). The two trial types are visually and procedurally identical and are matched in frequency across all subjects, so the contrast holds everything fixed and varies only whether the shortcut is available.

What it buys: 8-year-olds score well above chance overall (0.64) — and that aggregate is entirely carried by congruent trials (0.68 vs **0.53, chance**, difference `p` < 0.001). A single accuracy number would have reported partial competence where there is none. The split also has a neural moderator: the congruency gap shrinks with age *only* in subjects with strong entorhinal grid codes (three-way age × grid-strength × congruency interaction, `β` = 0.024, `p` = 0.002), which is the wiki's only case of a structural-code measurement predicting *which of two strategies* a subject uses rather than how well they perform ([[wiki/concepts/abstract-structural-codes.md]]).

**The same partition, run the other way: a shortcut that *penalises* the model refusing it** (Bortoletto et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/irene.md]]). On the Baby Intuitions Benchmark's Instrumental Action set, two of three subtasks (No Barrier, Inconsequential Barrier) are solved by the heuristic "head straight for the goal object"; the third (Blocking Barrier) is not. A model that never learned barriers scores 97.9–99.7 and 78.2–91.9 on the first two and 47.1–64.2 on the third; a model that did learn them scores 78.4 and 52.4 on the first two and 83.5 on the third. **The reported average over subtasks then ranks the heuristic model higher (84.7 vs 71.5).** Worse, the effect is reachable by curriculum alone: dropping the barrier training task raises the same model's Instrumental average from 71.5 to 80.4. Two consequences — (i) an unbalanced subtask mix prices reasoning negatively, so aggregate benchmark scores must be reported with the shortcut-solvable subset broken out; (ii) a *drop* in aggregate score is admissible evidence *for* structure learning, which no standard reporting convention allows.

**(brainstorm) This generalises to any benchmark whose items live in a product space, ARC included** ([[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]]): partition the item set by whether a marginal statistic suffices, and report the two accuracies separately. It is cheaper than every instrument below it — no second model, no distribution shift, no new data — and it converts Morgan's Canon from a warning into a number, because the shortcut-solvable subset *is* the surprisingly-strong-baseline score. Its limit is that it needs the intended structure known in advance to define the partition, which is exactly what the identifiability problem denies in the general case (G16).

**Two instruments that need no distribution shift** (Lake et al. 2017). Both hold the trained model fixed and vary the *query* instead of the data:

- **The richness protocol.** A concept, if it is a model, supports classification, generation, parsing, creation of new concepts, explanation and communication. A shortcut rule is by construction a mapping to one output type, so it answers row 1 and fails the rest ([[wiki/concepts/causal-model-building.md]]).
- **The re-goaling protocol.** Fix the transition structure, swap the reward function, and see how much retraining is needed. Humans re-goal a video game to any of a dozen novel objectives with little or no relearning; a model-free network must be retrained ([[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]]).

Their limitation is the mirror of their cheapness: a system trained multi-task on all the queries could pass without any of the abilities being consequences of one underlying model.

**Where the relation slot fails.** Caption networks return the key objects of a scene and the wrong relations between them — a man being thrown off a horse read as "a woman riding a horse on a dirt road", a crashed airplane as "an airplane is parked on the tarmac". This is a shortcut with an unusually clean localization: the parts are recovered, the arrangement is not represented at all, so the model emits the arrangement most frequent in training ([[wiki/concepts/compositionality.md]]).

**Three shortcuts built into a benchmark rather than into a model** (Bordes et al. 2024, [[wiki/concepts/cross-modal-grounding.md]]). The vision-language compositionality benchmarks are the wiki's clearest case of the "surprisingly strong baseline" being *the benchmark's own default*:

| Channel | Mechanism | Cost of the fix |
|---|---|---|
| **The tie-break** | These are binary discriminations (correct caption vs perturbed caption) resolved by `argmax`, which returns element 0 on a tie — and the correct caption is conventionally element 0. A model with **all parameters zero** collapses both captions to one representation, ties every item, and scores 100% | One line: add an epsilon of noise, or count ties |
| **The missing negative image** | ARO-style negatives are generated procedurally with no image to match them, and real corpora are biased (a coffee cup is always photographed *on* a table), so caption plausibility alone answers the item without consulting the image | Winoground finds a real negative image (expensive); PUG *renders* the paired scene with one element moved (cheap, synthetic) |
| **Ungrammatical negatives** | Word-swapped hard negatives are often incoherent or ungrammatical, so a fluency judgement with no visual input solves them; SUGARCREPE regenerates them with an LLM | Regeneration |

The first row is worth stating on its own terms: **the field's principal instrument for compositional structure is maximally fooled by representational collapse**, which is the exact failure the objectives being measured exist to prevent (gap G34). It is also the cheapest instrument bug in the wiki and, per the source, standard practice does not check for it.

**"Zero-shot" is a coverage measurement.** VLM zero-shot performance depends mostly on how much the downstream concept was present in pretraining, and every CLIP variant — including MetaCLIP, which deliberately caps each of 500k Wikipedia/WordNet queries at 20k samples — remains imbalanced across concepts because web data is long-tailed (Udandarao et al., cited in Bordes et al. 2024). A zero-shot number is therefore a *purchase* priced in corpus frequency, not evidence of an intended rule ([[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]]). **And the purchase has now been priced against a matched control**: 142M images retrieved as nearest neighbours of curated seed sets beat 142M images sampled from the same crawl, at the same model, iterations and compute, by 14.5 points on ImageNet-A and 14.3 on iNat2018 — with the uncurated corpus *winning* on ADE20k segmentation, so the effect is a redistribution across read-outs rather than a uniform gain (Oquab et al. 2023, [[wiki/entities/dinov2.md]]). The uncomfortable half is that the curated distribution is defined by retrieval against the evaluation benchmarks' own train splits.

**The shortcut that survives a dataset built to exclude it** (Hupkes et al. 2020, [[wiki/entities/pcfg-set.md]]). PCFG SET is generated by recursive application of string-edit operations, contains no non-compositional phenomena, and never repeats a string argument — memorisation is priced out by construction and the intended rule *is* the data-generating process. A transformer still reaches 0.92 sequence accuracy while scoring 0.72 on held-out function pairs and 0.50 on longer sequences. The shortcut is named: the model learns *adjacent function pairs as single units* rather than functions, which is invisible to i.i.d. accuracy because every pair is well-attested, and which is confirmed independently by the localism test (feeding the model its own intermediate results changes the answer 41–54% of the time). Two consequences: (i) "design the data so only the intended rule works" fails even in the limit where the data are generated by the intended rule — this is the strongest form of G16 available; (ii) the diagnostic that caught it needs no distribution shift and no targets, only a meaning-preserving transform of the input and a comparison of the model against itself.

**The strong baseline reported as a column, and the o.o.d. axis declared in the generator** (Barrett et al. 2018, [[wiki/entities/pgm.md]]). Two practices from this page, run together on one benchmark. (i) A **context-blind ResNet** — the eight candidate panels, never the matrix — is trained and reported alongside every model: **22.4%** against 12.5% chance. Ten points of the benchmark sit in the answer set even though the distractors were *not* generated by perturbing the answer, which is the quantitative version of RAVEN's >90% and says the floor of a multiple-choice benchmark must be measured rather than assumed. (ii) Because the matrix's abstract content is generated from an explicit triple set `[relation, object, attribute]`, the *shift* can be specified over the abstraction rather than over pixels — eight regimes, each a different declared held-out abstraction, all scored against that same floor. Three of the eight (held-out triples 19.0%, held-out line-type 14.4%, held-out shape-colour 12.5%) fall **at or below the blind baseline**, so a model at 62.6% on the i.i.d. split retains nothing at all when the relation must reach a new attribute.

Its interest for this page is the design lesson rather than the scores: the third condition below ("a well-defined intended solution") is normally argued informally, and here it is *constructed* — the intended rule is the generator's own triple set, so a regime is admissible by construction and the drop is attributable to a named abstraction. The price is that this needs a symbolic generator, which is exactly what the hand-authored benchmarks the instrument is most needed on do not have.

**Three conditions for a good o.o.d. test:** (1) a *clear distribution shift*, human-perceptible or not; (2) a *well-defined intended solution* (training on natural images and testing on white noise is a shift with no solution); (3) *current models struggle* — most conceivable shifts are uninteresting. Benchmarks must co-evolve with models: the Winograd Schema Challenge, designed to close Turing-test shortcuts, was later found to contain more shortcut opportunities than intended.

**o.o.d. benchmark families named** (no wiki pages yet — see [[wiki/index-entities.md]]): adversarial attacks (model-specific worst case) · ARCT with shortcuts removed · cue-conflict stimuli (texture vs. shape, directly human-comparable) · ImageNet-A (natural worst case) · ImageNet-C (15 corruptions) · ObjectNet (scientific controls over background, rotation, viewpoint) · PACS (domain generalisation by design) · Shift-MNIST / biased CelebA / unfair dSprites (injected correlations; measures how prone an architecture+loss pair is to taking a shortcut).

The last family is the instrument the wiki actually needs: a *controlled* shortcut with a known intended rule turns "does this architecture recover structure?" into a measurable quantity.

**Concept-based evaluation — the cheapest o.o.d. test in the wiki, and the one that abandons both i.i.d. *and* independence** (Odouard & Mitchell 2022, [[wiki/entities/raven.md]]). Procedure: (1) name the concepts the system is supposed to hold; (2) pick ones it demonstrably answers *correctly* on the i.i.d. test set; (3) hand-author items instantiating the **same concept through different attributes, layouts and roles**; (4) report the drop. The evaluation items are by construction not drawn from the training distribution and not independent of each other — they are generated by varying one factor — which is the train/test contract of machine learning deliberately broken, on the grounds that a concept is *"a competence or disposition for generating infinite conceptualizations of a category"* (Barsalou) and can therefore only be measured across instantiations.

| System | i.i.d. test | Concept variations |
|---|---|---|
| MRNet on RAVEN | 73% | 49% (Sameness, 210 items) · 44% (Progression, 80) |
| SCL on RAVEN | 89% | 62% · 68% |
| ARC-Kaggle2 on ARC | 19% | **29%** (top/bottom, 14 tasks) · 8% (boundary, 12) |

**The instrument at benchmark scale, with the missing baseline collected** (Moskvichev, Odouard & Mitchell 2023, [[wiki/entities/conceptarc.md]]): 16 concept groups × 10 variations × 3 test inputs in the ARC format, authored to be *easy*, plus 415 human participants.

| System | Mean per-concept accuracy | Range across the 16 concepts |
|---|---|---|
| Humans (415 participants, 8–14 per test input) | ~0.91 | 0.83 – 0.97 |
| ARC-Kaggle 1st place (21% on ARC's hidden set) | ~0.51 | 0.23 – 0.77 |
| ARC-Kaggle 2nd place (19% on ARC's hidden set) | ~0.28 | 0.03 – 0.57 |
| GPT-4, zero-shot, grids as text | ~0.19 | 0.03 – 0.33 |

Three further findings, each of which is about the *instrument* rather than the solvers. **(a)** Two programs indistinguishable on the parent benchmark (21% vs 19%) are 23 points apart here — a benchmark hard enough to floor everything destroys the rank order it exists to produce, so the diagnostic regime is the one where humans are at ceiling and machines are not. **(b)** Concept difficulty does not transfer across solvers (the first-place program's best concept, *Extend To Boundary* at 0.77, is GPT-4's worst at 0.07), so a per-concept profile is a property of the (solver, concept) pair and cannot be summarised by an ordering of concepts. **(c)** The clause "(iii)" below is now half-closed: the human baseline exists, and the exclusion protocol needed to get it (67 of 482 participants dropped for failed attention checks or nonsensical explanations, because faking failure was faster than solving) is itself a warning that unfiltered crowd baselines on constructed-answer benchmarks are biased downward.

Three things this adds that no other row here supplies. **(i)** It is *not* a harder test set: the ARC program went up on one concept and down by more than half on the other, so a benchmark score is a mean over a concept mixture whose per-concept variance is unmeasured, and the per-concept number is the one that says which structure is held. **(ii)** It needs no distribution-shift design, no generator, and no labels beyond the items themselves — the concept inventory does all the work, which is why it runs on a hand-authored benchmark like ARC where every automated shift procedure is unavailable. **(iii)** Its weakness is the mirror of its cheapness and is the same one this page charges elsewhere: the variations are authored by hand, no human baseline was collected on them, and 12–210 items per concept is small — so the score is against the variation-author's notion of the concept, and the developer-blindness problem is inherited whole.

**The tenth instrument, and the only one that asks the solver what it did** (Beger et al. 2025, [[wiki/concepts/rule-level-evaluation.md]]). Require the rule *and* the answer in one output, hand-classify the rule against the task's author-declared abstraction as **correct-intended / correct-unintended / incorrect**, and check rule–grid alignment (>90% for all three frontier models) so the report is not taken on trust. On ConceptARC's 480 items this gives the wiki's first direct measurement of shortcut *propensity* on identical items for humans and machines:

| Solver (textual, medium + tools) | Accuracy | Share of correct grids resting on a non-intended rule | Correct-unintended share of all correct rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| o3 | 75.6% | **27%** | **29%** |
| Gemini 2.5 Pro | 60.4% | 27% | 22% |
| Claude Sonnet 4 | 55.0% | 19% | 15% |
| Humans (same items) | 73% (`pass@1`) | **~8%** | **2.7%** |

Three consequences for this page. **(i)** It needs no distribution shift, no second model, no controlled dataset and no partition of the item set — every earlier instrument here needs at least one of those. Its cost is a human rater and a benchmark whose intended rule is declared, so it is the *dual* of the concept-variation instrument above rather than a competitor: that one varies the item and reads the drop, this one holds the item and reads the rule. **(ii)** The named shortcut channels are architecturally diagnostic: colours serialised as the ordered integers 0–9 are treated as ordinal (rules sorting objects "by colour value ascending"), and rules operate on pixels, lines, bounding boxes and 4-/8-connectivity rather than on objects — on a corpus built around objectness. **(iii)** Shortcut use is concentrated where the intended abstraction is unavailable: over correct rules the correct-unintended rate is 29.8% (SD 16.4) in text, reaching 70.6% on *Top/Bottom 3D*, which is also among the lowest-accuracy concepts — a prior deficit predicts this and a capacity deficit does not.

**The same instrument run backwards: accuracy can *under*-report abstraction too.** In the visual modality o3 states the intended rule on 40.0% of tasks and produces 29.2% correct grids, Gemini on 23.6% at 5.8%. Every instrument on this page assumes a score is an upper bound on understanding; where the bottleneck is downstream of the rule (perception, or output construction), it is a lower bound. This is the wiki's only measured case of the second kind ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T215).

## Two cheap o.o.d. instruments, from the math-word-problem family

The instruments above need a human rater, a declared intended rule, or a controlled dataset. Two from [[wiki/entities/gsm8k.md]] need none of them, because the intended rule is *the reasoning graph of an item the model already solved*.

| Instrument | Procedure | Returns | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Named-perturbation sweep** (GSM-Plus, Li et al. 2024) | Rewrite each solved item along 8 declared axes — numerical substitution, digit expansion, integer/decimal/fraction conversion, added operation, **reversed operation**, rephrasing, **distractor insertion**, **statement deletion** | Per-axis accuracy drop; the axis ranking *is* the shortcut diagnosis | One rewrite pass per item (GPT-4 draft + human revision; **18.85%** of the draft variants needed correction) |
| **Template re-instantiation** (GSM-Symbolic, Mirzadeh et al. 2024) | Variabilise the item (typed variables, domains, satisfiability conditions), draw `n` instantiations, evaluate all of them | `(mean, SD)` over the same reasoning graph, **plus the published item's z-score inside its own distribution** | One templating pass; no rater, no second model, no ontology |

**The second returns two measurements for one price, and the wiki's G17 list had neither.** The SD is robustness — spread >12% (Gemma2-9B) and ~15% (Phi-3.5-mini) across draws that differ only in names and numbers. The z-score is a **contamination test that needs no access to the training corpus**: for 21 of 25 models the original GSM8K item scores in the right tail, often beyond one SD, and the only distinguishing property of that instantiation is that it was published.

**What the sweep localises.** Sensitivity is ordered *names < numbers < both*, so surface identity is nearly free and **quantity binding is not** — the failure is at the point where a token is attached to a role in the computation, which is the wiki's recurring relation-slot failure ([[wiki/concepts/compositionality.md]], and the caption networks above) appearing in arithmetic. Difficulty scaling is worse than the linear step count: as clauses are added (M1 → Symb → P1 → P2) the mean falls, the variance rises, and the *rate* of fall increases.

**And the largest drop is on relevance, not difficulty.** GSM-NoOp adds a clause that looks pertinent and changes nothing; accuracy falls by up to **65%**, with models converting statements to operations by keyword (a "discount" becomes a multiplication regardless of context). This is a shortcut in the taxonomy's strict sense — a rule that solves every i.i.d. item and fails the moment an unused quantity is present — and the repair experiments say where it does *not* live: supplying 8 in-context demonstrations of the identical question with its correct reasoning chain leaves the drop within one SD, and 8 demonstrations of other NoOp items (all requiring the clause be ignored) do not help either. Whatever selects relevant statements is below the in-context-learning layer.

## Routes beyond shortcuts

| Route | Mechanism | Relation to the framing |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain-specific prior knowledge** | Architectural invariance or data augmentation for known-irrelevant transformations; auto-augment as its general form; extreme augmentation is the core of current semi- and self-supervised methods | Hand-specified `g`/`x` split for one known nuisance axis; does not scale to unknown structure |
| **Adversarial robustness** | Adversarial examples as *counterfactual explanations* — the smallest input change producing a given output; robust models are somewhat more human-aligned and generalise better | Ties robustness to causality: aligning counterfactuals is aligning edges |
| **Domain adaptation / generalisation / randomisation** | Multiple training distributions; under assumptions the intended (or causal) rule is identifiable from several environments. Domain randomisation closes the simulation-to-real gap | **The environment family of the two-level hierarchy is exactly this multi-environment signal** — the meta-graph is what is invariant across instances |
| **Meta-learning** | Representations that adapt quickly to new conditions; fast adaptation is connected to *identification of causal graphs*, since causal features require small changes when the environment changes | Direct: [[wiki/concepts/meta-learning.md]] gets a second justification — not just sample efficiency but shortcut resistance |
| **Generative modelling and disentanglement** | Modelling every variation forces the network to account for all of it; disentanglement seeks the true generating factors via independent causal mechanisms | Generation alone does not give useful or o.o.d.-robust representations; the structure has to be demanded of the latent space |

---

## Open problems

- **Identifiability.** No procedure recovers the intended rule from data alone; the assumptions must come from outside. What is the minimal set for graph-structured solutions?
- **Which lever, and how much?** The four levers interact and the interactions are not understood. There is no theory saying which one to spend on for a given shortcut.
- **Is the multi-environment signal sufficient?** Invariance across environments identifies causal edges *under assumptions*. Whether an environment family generated by real task variation meets them is untested.
- **Cheap o.o.d. tests.** No simple, general procedure exists to replace i.i.d. benchmarking; every listed benchmark is domain-specific and hand-designed. **Partly answered by template re-instantiation** (above): variabilising an item and reporting `(mean, SD, seed z-score)` is general wherever an item has a symbolically expressible structure — which covers word problems, generated grids and program-induction tasks, and does not cover perception.
- **Can shortcut learning be eliminated at all?** The paper's own position is that full elimination may be impossible — only better alignment between learned and intended solution.

- **[[wiki/entities/h-jepa.md]]** — an architecture-lever bet against shortcuts: predict in a space trained to be simultaneously maximally informative about its input and maximally predictable from the previous state, so a rule reading only appearance is not expressible in it. Untested.
- **[[wiki/entities/irene.md]]** — the inverse instance: the model that learned the role of barriers loses 20–40 points on the two subtasks a straight-line heuristic solves, so the subtask average rewards the shortcut and a score *drop* is the evidence of structure.

- **[[wiki/entities/maze-solving-transformers.md]]** — a shortcut taken with the correct map in hand: models reach the target in ≈100% of rollouts while crossing walls in 13–62% of them, because goal-direction is cheaper than path validity and next-token loss never separates the two (Ivanitskiy et al. 2023).
- **[[wiki/concepts/representation-probing.md]]** — the inside-out complement to shifted test sets: a probe says what the network holds, a shift says whether the executed rule is wrong, and neither alone identifies which rule is running.
- **[[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]]** — a second site for the same failure: a model can recover the right structure and still bind it to the least stable cue available, where the biological policy is stability-ranked (boundaries over objects, objects only once learned to be fixed).
- **[[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]]** — Morgan's Canon turned on the modeller: never attribute to an objective what is adequately explained by the second-order statistics of the supervised target, and never read a linear-regression fit to neural data as similarity when representational dimensionality predicts the score.
- **[[wiki/concepts/recall-gated-consolidation.md]]** — bounds what a recurrence-based consolidation filter buys: it keeps one-off noise out of long-term storage, but a shortcut that is stably predictive across encounters passes the gate exactly as readily as a causal edge, so reliability filtering is orthogonal to causal validity.
- **[[wiki/concepts/generalization-optimized-consolidation.md]]** — the complementary failure mode of the same slow learner: there the fitted structure is *unpredictable* noise and stopping early fixes it, here it is reliably predictive but non-causal and no amount of early stopping touches it — so predictability filtering and causal validity are orthogonal, exactly as recurrence filtering is.
- **[[wiki/concepts/arbitrary-sensorimotor-mapping.md]]** — a curriculum lever for choosing which mechanism a task recruits: visual and configural discriminations are solvable by arbitrary mapping but are instead solved by approach-to-high-valence, and the proposed determinant is spatiotemporal contiguity of cue and choice — separate them and the binding pathway is recruited, which makes contiguity a designable knob rather than an incidental task property (Murray et al. 2000).
- **[[wiki/entities/aixi.md]]** — the identifiability problem restated where the architecture is optimal by construction: the passive/active asymmetry means the failure cannot be blamed on the learner.
- **[[wiki/entities/basal-ganglia.md]]** — a filter no model in the wiki has, and the limit of what it buys: inwardly-rectifying Kir2 channels shunt cortical input that lacks spatial and temporal convergence to *nothing* rather than attenuating it, so a spurious feature driving few afferents never reaches the selector — selection on evidence convergence rather than magnitude. - **[[wiki/concepts/learned-world-models.md]]** — the same failure with the roles reversed: when a planner optimises action sequences through a deterministic learned model, the *search* finds the model's spurious structure and acts on it, so model error becomes a reward channel — and the reported defence is stochastic transitions, which wash exploitable ridges out under expectation (Long et al. 2025).
- **[[wiki/entities/v-jepa-2.md]]** — the appearance/motion trade as a measurement of the architecture lever: an objective that forces prediction of masked *spatiotemporal* patches beats every language-supervised image encoder by 20+ points on Something-Something v2 (77.3 vs 55.4) while losing to all of them on ImageNet, COIN and Kinetics — so making single-frame shortcuts unavailable has a price, and it is paid in static recognition (Assran et al. 2025).
- **[[wiki/entities/spelkenet.md]]** — the clearest case in the wiki of an objective's shortcut becoming a *permanent* representational commitment: a contrastive objective is defined to pull same-category instances together, so DINO merges two chairs into one segment and no readout downstream can individuate them — the failure is in the loss, not in the probe.
- **[[wiki/concepts/violation-of-expectation.md]]** — supplies a shortcut control this page has been asking for in general form: run untrained networks of the same architecture through the identical scoring pipeline, and the score they get is how much of the benchmark is solvable from low-level statistics before any learning.
- **[[wiki/concepts/cross-modal-grounding.md]]** — three shortcuts located in the *benchmark* rather than the model (an `argmax` tie-break a zero-parameter model wins, negatives with no paired image so language priors suffice, and ungrammatical procedural negatives), plus the finding that zero-shot scores track pretraining concept frequency.

- **[[wiki/entities/dinov2.md]]** — the coverage argument with the control it usually lacks: 142M *curated* images beat 142M uncurated ones drawn from the same crawl, at matched compute, by 14 points on ImageNet-A and on iNat2018 — so what a “zero-shot” number purchases is set by the pretraining *distribution* rather than its size. - **[[wiki/entities/arc-vsa-solver.md]]** — the remedy applied to the input set rather than the loss: which of an object's three properties the rule-condition predictor may see is chosen by leave-one-demonstration-out cross-validation, so a spurious property is rejected before it can be fit — at the price of needing a demonstration the task may not be able to spare.
- **[[wiki/entities/mlc.md]]** — the shortcut destroyed by the sampler rather than penalised by the loss: permuting word→meaning assignments every episode makes any stored surface association worthless, so the only solution that pays is inferring the mapping in context — and the residue names what was left alone, since functional words with stable meanings across episodes are free to be memorised in the weights.

- **[[wiki/concepts/rule-level-evaluation.md]]** — this page's tenth instrument and its first human/machine calibration of shortcut *propensity* on identical items (2.7% vs 15–29% correct-unintended rules), obtained by asking the solver to state its rule and classifying it rather than by shifting the distribution — and the case that inverts the page's standing assumption, since in the visual modality accuracy under-reports the abstraction the model demonstrably found.
- **[[wiki/entities/pgm.md]]** — two of this page's practices run together and reported as columns: a context-blind baseline at 22.4% (chance 12.5) that three of eight generalisation regimes fail to clear, and a distribution shift declared over the *generator's own* symbolic structure so that each regime withholds a named abstraction rather than a pixel distribution.
- **[[wiki/entities/anli.md]]** — the surprisingly-strong-baseline instrument used twice in one paper, with opposite verdicts: a hypothesis-only model is at chance on ANLI while scoring 71.4 on SNLI (the collection procedure blocked the artefact), and on rounds 2–3 the *full* state-of-the-art model beats hypothesis-only by only 2.6 and 1.6 points — so a benchmark authored adversarially against the current model drives toward the regime where the shortcut baseline and the intended solver are indistinguishable, and the instrument stops discriminating (Nie et al. 2020).
- **[[wiki/entities/agent-benchmark.md]]** — Morgan's Canon with the numbers to enforce it: a graph-encoder ToMnet reaches the single-human aggregate (.90 vs .91) while its per-trial-type profile correlates **0.06** with the human profile, scores 1.0 on the two types where "walk away when a costly obstacle appears" suffices and .57 on the matched type where it does not, and drops to **.05 — anti-correlated — on the type authored to catch "the straightest path is least surprising"**; the benchmark's 13 types are built one-per-heuristic, which is what makes the shortcut legible instead of averaged away.
- **[[wiki/entities/bib.md]]** — a benchmark whose contrast pairs are deliberately **anti-aligned** with perceptual similarity, which converts a shortcut into a below-chance score; the shortcut is named and quantified there (nearest-object prediction in ~70% of trials, and a goal-location rather than goal-object preference), which is what turns 26.3% from an anomaly into a diagnosis.
- **[[wiki/entities/math-dataset.md]]** — a format-level defence with its price stated: free generation with a delimited, normalised answer removes the candidate-set channel that floors [[wiki/entities/raven.md]], but only the answer is graded, so a correct answer reached through an invalid derivation is invisible to the metric — the paper prints one, and it is the shortcut this benchmark cannot see.
- **[[wiki/entities/gpqa.md]]** — a hygiene control this page's instruments cannot run because they need the intended ability known in advance: hand every item to a skilled non-specialist with a search engine and a stopwatch, and the resulting accuracy bounds the "the answer was findable" shortcut per item (34% at a 37-minute mean, against 65% in-domain and 25% chance).
- **[[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]** — this page states the problem the whole inventory exists to detect (shortcut and intended rule equally consistent with the observations) and supplies two of its instruments, `I1` (controlled-shortcut datasets) and `I10` (concept-based evaluation).
