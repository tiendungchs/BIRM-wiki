# Emergent Modularity

**Domain-specific competence need not be a domain-specific part: small heritable shifts in *domain-general* parameters — attention flow, executive control, working memory, inhibition, perceptual bias, and above all the growth schedule — redirect what an organism explores, and the modules fall out of the resulting ontogeny as products rather than inputs.**

> **Provenance.** Sherwood, Subiaul & Zawidzki 2008, *Journal of Anatomy* 212:426–454 (`raw/sherwood-2008-natural-history-human-mind.md`). A multidisciplinary review with an explicit model in its final section; its empirical rows are citations to others' work, and the authors state the synthesis as a hypothesis ("a preliminary model"). The wiki takes this source for its **subtraction inventory** — what changed and what did not between the last common ancestor and *Homo sapiens* — and for the **architectural claim that follows from the null half of that inventory**.

The page exists because the source's central result is *negative* and the wiki has no page for the position it forces. Human cognition diverges from the great apes about as far as any two mammalian minds diverge; the platypus bill and the bat auditory cortex show what a divergence of that size usually looks like in neuroanatomy. **The corresponding neuroanatomical specialisation is not there.** Almost every human neocortical area has a macaque homologue; total frontal cortex is exactly at the ape scaling prediction; the extra cortical volume is mostly not extra neurons (contested — [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T301). Whatever built the difference did it without adding parts.

## The subtraction, run in two directions

The source reconstructs the hominin/panin last common ancestor (LCA) by cladistic parsimony — a trait shared by all extant great apes but absent in monkeys is inferred present in the LCA — and then subtracts that reconstruction from modern humans.

| Trait | LCA (great-ape stem, ~14 Ma) | *Homo sapiens*-specific | Builder's reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mirror self-recognition | **Present** (chimp, orangutan; gorillas mostly fail) — also convergent in dolphins, one elephant | — | A self-model is not the human-specific part ([[wiki/concepts/default-self-model.md]]) |
| Gaze following, incl. through occluders and from eyes alone | **Present** | — | Attention-direction inference is shared |
| **Triadic joint attention** (infant ↔ adult ↔ object, with look-back) | absent | **Present**, onset ~9 months | The first genuinely human item, and it is a *routing* capability, not a representation |
| Behavioural traditions transmitted horizontally | **Present** — 39 documented in chimpanzees, 19 in orangutans, ≤ 2–3 in any monkey | — | Cultural accumulation has a pre-human substrate |
| Approximate + small-*N* number, addition, subtraction, **modality-independent** (matched across heard calls and seen faces) | **Present** | — | Matches [[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]]'s shared/unique split exactly |
| **Understanding of physical causality** | **Present, and no better than monkeys** — chimpanzees choose by superficial contact rather than force transfer across pulling/pushing/poking tasks (Povinelli 2000) | — | **The load-bearing negative.** Ape tool use exceeds monkey tool use while ape *causal* competence does not; the difference is dexterity plus social learning |
| Inhibitory control / delay of gratification | **Present**, exceeding monkeys | Extended further | The domain-general parameter the model's whole argument runs through |
| **Novel imitation** (copying a response not in the repertoire) | Partial — outcome copied, action fidelity poor; orofacial neonatal imitation shared with macaques and fading at ~9 weeks in both | **High-fidelity, and functionally linked to other faculties** | Imitation is not one mechanism; 10 direct comparisons split, 2 finding no human advantage |
| **Learning in the "ghost condition"** (object moves and outcome occurs with no agent present) | absent | **Present** | Humans extract a transition model from *unagented* dynamics; no nonhuman does ([[wiki/concepts/learned-world-models.md]]) |
| **Learning from others' mistakes** (counterfactual: the observed action was wrong) | absent | **Present** | Requires a correct/incorrect label on an observed transition — a signed demonstration, not a demonstration |
| Language: the five design features below | absent | **Present** | See table |

**(brainstorm) The ghost-condition result inverts the naive expectation and is the single most useful row for a builder.** One would predict that a *social* species learns better *with* the demonstrator; the finding is that only humans learn when the demonstrator is removed. Read as machine learning: nonhuman social learning is conditioned on an agent token in the input, and human social learning is not — it operates on the state-transition sequence with the agent factored out. That is precisely the abstraction that lets the same learner ingest observation, demonstration and self-generated experience through one channel, and it is a **type-erasure**, not an extra module. It is also what the wiki's imitation-free world-model learners already do by construction, which means the wiki's architectures are on the human side of this particular boundary and on the tamarin side of the [[wiki/concepts/discrete-infinity.md]] one.

### The five design features, as a checklist

The source's operational decomposition of language, more granular than the FLB/FLN cut at [[wiki/concepts/discrete-infinity.md]]:

| Feature | Nonhuman best case | What the wiki would have to exhibit |
|---|---|---|
| **Modality / stimulus independence** | Ontogenetically ritualised ape gestures (play-hit → raised arm); vervet alarm calls, audience-tailored. Both narrow: apes cannot do vocally what they do gesturally | The same content emitted through an arbitrary channel, and emitted absent its eliciting stimulus — voluntary control ([[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]) |
| **Duality of patterning** | Birdsong/whalesong have the *first* level only (meaningless units → larger sequences); the sequences are not words and do not compose further | Two nested composition levels with the lower one meaningless — the wiki's tokenisers have level 1 and no level 2 ([[wiki/concepts/multi-token-embedding.md]]) |
| **Shared arbitrary symbols with displaced reference** | Honeybee dance is displaced but *iconic*, not arbitrary; Kanzi is arbitrary and displaced but heavily scaffolded, absent in the wild | Arbitrariness *plus* shared-ness, which the source grounds in the consumer inferring the producer's intent — a theory-of-mind term inside the symbol's semantics ([[wiki/concepts/nameability.md]]) |
| **Generalised systematicity / task-domain neutrality** | Essentially none | Any term predicable of any term; and information represented *independently of any task it is put to*. See below — this is the feature the source gives a mechanism for |
| **Hierarchical / recursive syntax** | None (Fitch & Hauser 2004); Kanzi conspicuously fails | `G70`, unchanged |

**Task-domain neutrality is the underrated one.** "There are fruit trees beyond the hill" imposes no constraint on what the hearer does with it. Every representation in the wiki that is learned by an objective is, by construction, shaped to a task; a representation with no immediate implication for action is the thing [[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]] says nothing selects for. The source's archaeological dating for it — symbolic decoration on tools appears at the *Homo sapiens* speciation and not with *Homo erectus* — makes it late and separable from tool-making competence itself.

## The null half: no new modules

| Claim | Measurement | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Human cortical areas are novel | **False, mostly.** Functional/structural homologues in macaque exist for the majority of human neocortical areas, including multimodal and language-related ones | The evolutionary-psychology picture (accumulated genetically specified modules) has no anatomical support at the areal level |
| Frontal cortex is disproportionately enlarged | **False.** Total frontal cortex in humans is no greater than ape scaling predicts, and occupies a similar fraction of cortex as in great apes (36% is already the *hominid* value; gibbons 29%, macaque/capuchin 31%, and the 36% is itself an allometric consequence) | The most-cited "seat of human cognition" claim fails at the level of gross volume |
| Higher-order cortex grew at the expense of primary | **True, locally.** V1 is only ~1.5× the great-ape value while the rest of neocortex is ~3×, and V1 is *below* allometric prediction; the lunate sulcus had already shifted to a human-like position in *Australopithecus* endocasts (4–2.5 Ma) — **before** the size expansion | Reorganisation preceded scaling. The order matters: this is a counterexample to "scale first, structure follows" |
| Prefrontal cortex specifically enlarged | **Unresolved.** Gyrification exceeds prediction and motor/premotor occupy a smaller fraction, both circumstantial; direct volumetric studies contradict each other on `n = 1–2` per species | The wiki's most-cited anatomical anchor for executive control has no settled comparative measurement |
| Candidate genuinely new areas | Posterior parietal / dorsal intraparietal regions supplying extra central-visual-field representation and 3-D-form-from-motion (Orban et al. 2006) — the same regions activated when humans learn Oldowan knapping (Stout & Chaminade 2007) | **No ape data exist**, so even this is undetermined |

## What *did* change: the quantities

| Variable | Human | Comparison | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brain mass | ~1400 g | ~3× great apes; LCA reconstructed at 300–400 g | Expansion accelerates from ~1.8 Ma (*Homo erectus*), not gradually throughout |
| **Cortical volume vs. neuron count** | **2.75× chimpanzee volume, 1.25× chimpanzee neurons** (Shariff 1953) | Direct whole-brain counts across 18 rodent, primate and insectivore species give primate cortical mass `∼ N_CX^1.077` at invariant neuronal density, under which 2.75× volume is ~2.5× neurons (Herculano-Houzel 2009, [[wiki/concepts/cellular-scaling-rules.md]]) | **The scale-up is neuropil — dendrites, axons, synapses — not units.** The parameter that grew is *connections per unit*, and the extra units are a minority term — but the ratio comes from one 1953 regional-density study, and the exponent it implies (`a ≈ 4.5`) is steeper than any measured clade including rodents. **Contested: [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T301** |
| Glia : neuron ratio | Highest of any primate; rises with brain size across primates | — | Metabolic support scales super-linearly with the connectivity, not with the unit count |
| Temporal-lobe white matter | Exceeds hominoid allometric prediction; the excess sits in **gyral** white matter, not the central core | — | The added wiring is **local** — neighbouring-field connectivity — not long-haul. Gyrification itself is a tension-based consequence of dense local wiring |
| Arcuate fasciculus | Markedly stronger projection to **middle temporal gyrus semantic areas** than in chimpanzee or macaque (Rilling et al. 2007) | — | The one long-range change with a named functional payoff — see below |
| Long-range association neurons | Proportion of neocortical neurons with molecular adaptations for long associational projections rises with brain size in anthropoids | — | A *free* consequence of scaling; also why these cells are the ones that degenerate first in Alzheimer's |
| Dorsal thalamus | More neurons in anterior principal, mediodorsal, pulvinar; **sensory relay nuclei unchanged**. Human-unique migration of GABAergic interneurons from the telencephalic ganglionic eminence into dorsal thalamus (not seen in macaque); bimodal pulvinar soma-size distribution vs. unimodal in apes | — | The higher-order thalamus was rebuilt while the relay thalamus was not — the same asymmetry [[wiki/concepts/transthalamic-context-routing.md]] rests on |
| Cerebellum | Large relative to body size; **ventral dentate nucleus** enlarged, projecting via ventrolateral thalamus to non-motor frontal cortex | Lateral cerebellar hemisphere already enlarged in the ape stem | Cortex and cerebellum evolve as one coupled system |
| Corticomotoneuronal control of larynx | Neocortical axons synapse directly on nucleus ambiguus vocal-fold motoneurons **only in humans**; expanded laryngeal representation in dorsal precentral gyrus adjacent to lips | Macaque/squirrel-monkey laryngeal area is anterior, premotor, and does not contribute to vocalisation | Voluntary control is bought by a *direct* path replacing a mediated one — an architectural change with zero new modules |
| Lateralisation | Population right-handedness ~90% (chimp 67%, `n` = 467; gorilla n.s.; orangutan 79% **left**, `n` = 19); left-occipital/right-frontal petalia torque, absent before *Homo erectus*; **hemispheric asymmetry in neuropil fraction** in Broca's, Wernicke's, M1-hand and V1 — found in humans, not in chimpanzees | — | Lateralisation is argued to be a *consequence* of size: conduction delay makes bilateral duplication expensive, so large brains specialise ([[wiki/concepts/small-world-topology.md]]). **Now supported by a cross-species regression**: structural connectivity asymmetry rises with brain size across 14 primates (β = +0.73), and the callosal cross-section per unit cortex falls 90 → 132 → 211 cm² of cortex per cm² of callosum from galago to chimpanzee to human ([[wiki/concepts/connectivity-scaling-bottleneck.md]]) |
| Adult synaptic plasticity | Thrombospondins THBS2/THBS4 — astrocyte-secreted synaptogenic factors — elevated in adult human neocortex and striatum; up-regulation of neuronal-signalling, plasticity and metabolic genes vs. chimpanzee | — | The plasticity window is not merely long, it is *molecularly maintained* into adulthood |
| Von Economo neurons | Present in great apes and humans (and convergently in cetaceans); in humans **larger, more numerous, clustered** | — | A pre-existing ape cell type amplified, not invented — and selectively destroyed in frontotemporal dementia |

**Genes named, and the honest status of each:** *FOXP2* (two fixed amino-acid substitutions, also present in Neandertals; no known link to any human neuroanatomical difference), *AHI1* (axonal pathfinding/decussation), *DUF1220* (copy number amplified, function unknown), *HAR1* (118 bp, fastest-evolving human accelerated region, expressed in Cajal–Retzius cells at gestational weeks 7–9, i.e. **cortical layer specification**), *ASPM*/*microcephalin* (selected on the lineage *to* the LCA), *GLUD2* (hominoid glutamate-dehydrogenase duplication enabling high excitatory flux), electron-transport-chain subunits. Of these, only *HAR1* and *AHI1* name a developmental process, and neither has a phenotype attached. **The most transferable finding is the last one in the source's gene section:** accelerated *cis*-regulatory evolution near cell-adhesion and neurogenesis genes occurred in **both** human and chimpanzee lineages, with **little overlap in the specific genes** — the two lineages tuned the same knob independently and arrived at different minds. Selection acted on the regulatory schedule, not on new components.

## The mechanism the source offers for generalised systematicity

This is the only place in the source where a named human cognitive property gets a named anatomical cause, and it is worth isolating:

> Positively selected changes in **axon guidance and cell adhesion**, plus the generic tendency of larger brains to form long association projections, give human inferior frontal cortex access to a **greater diversity of afferents** — particularly temporoparietal regions holding multimodal semantic and lexical representations (the arcuate-fasciculus/middle-temporal result). Generalised systematicity — combining concepts from arbitrary domains, and therefore analogy — is what afferent diversity into one composition site buys.

| Element | Statement | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Composition site | Inferior frontal cortex / Broca's area — which in **both** humans and macaques handles object manipulation, motion imagery, movement preparation and imitation, not only language | A general sequencing/planning operator that acquired new arguments, not a new operator ([[wiki/concepts/policy-abstraction-hierarchy.md]]) |
| The change | **Fan-in diversity at one site**, delivered by a white-matter tract | Not a new part, not a new algorithm — a wiring-degree change |
| The predicted capability | Systematic cross-domain combination; analogy ("light as a wave") | Exactly [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]]'s target, and exactly what [[wiki/concepts/compositionality.md]] measures as the recombination facet |

**(brainstorm) This is a testable and cheap architectural prescription, and the wiki has not tried it.** The claim reduces to: *systematicity is a monotone function of the number of distinct representational sources routed into a single composition operator, holding the operator fixed*. That is a sweepable axis in any transformer or slot architecture — vary the number of distinct pretrained encoders whose outputs share one composition head, measure the recombination facet on [[wiki/entities/pcfg-set.md]] or [[wiki/entities/mlc.md]]. If the curve is flat, the source's only causal proposal is wrong and the remaining candidate is the operator itself ([[wiki/concepts/discrete-infinity.md]] H3). If it rises, `G21` and `G99` are partly a *degree* problem rather than a *mechanism* problem. Nothing in the wiki has run this, and it does not need new machinery to run.

## The developmental lever

The source's model puts the greatest weight here, and it is the part with the sharpest numbers.

| Species | Neonatal brain as % of adult | Postnatal growth rate |
|---|---|---|
| Macaque | ~70% | Drops shortly after birth |
| Chimpanzee | ~36% | Drops shortly after birth |
| **Human** | **~27%** (and already ~2× great-ape neonatal absolute size) | **Continues at the fetal rate through the whole first year** |

Consequences the source draws, and the one it under-draws:

- **Most human wiring is laid down inside a social environment,** not inside a uterus. The onset of triadic joint attention at ~9 months falls inside the window of fastest postnatal growth and synaptogenesis — the human-unique behaviour appears exactly when the human-unique growth schedule is running.
- The schedule is forced by obstetrics (bipedal pelvis) — i.e. **the developmental architecture is a workaround for a constraint from a different organ system**, and it happens to be the thing that pays.
- **Enculturated apes** (Tomasello & Carpenter 2005) demonstrate the lever's reality from the other side: change the early environment and the behavioural repertoire changes, in an animal whose genome did not.
- Prolonged life history (delayed skeletal/dental/sexual maturation, extended lifespan) is already an ape trait, present in Miocene *Sivapithecus* and *Dryopithecus*. The human change is a schedule shift on top of an already-slow schedule.

**The lever is two levers, and the source states only one.** Somel et al. 2009 attributes human brain size to *rapid early postnatal growth* rather than an extended growth period, and measures the human-specific *delay* as sparse (~4% of the prefrontal transcriptome, a global shift positively excluded), gray-matter-specific, and peaking at **~10 years** — a different variable, a different tissue compartment and a decade later than the 9-month window this section reads the payoff into. Volume accelerates early; gray-matter expression is delayed late. Logged as [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T294; the measured shape is at [[wiki/concepts/developmental-heterochrony.md]].

**No architecture in the wiki has a growth schedule at all** (`G100`): capacity is fully available at step 0, so nothing in the wiki was ever formed under a capacity constraint it later escaped. `G32` is the complementary half — nothing designs the *experience* stream — and the two are separately open.

**The model, stated as an algorithm.** Selection modifies heritable parameters (growth-factor gradients, cell-adhesion, cell-cycle dynamics in neural precursors) → these set **biases** in the developing connectivity → biases steer **attention, motivation, arousal, working memory, inhibition** → biased domain-general processes steer **exploration** → exploration determines **what is learned** → domain-specific skills crystallise, some of which (reading, writing, music, games) are demonstrably cultural products of the last few millennia and therefore cannot be genetically specified modules at all. Selection scores only the last stage. **Modules are the output of the loop, and the genome addresses only its first stage.**

## Open problems

- **The model is unfalsified because it is unquantified.** "Shifts in the weight of attention, executive control, working memory and inhibition are sufficient to yield domain-specific skills" names no magnitude, no bias, and no skill it predicts. It is compatible with every observation in the source, including the ones that contradict each other.
- **Nothing distinguishes "no new modules" from "new modules not yet found".** The source concedes it: comparative cytoarchitecture between humans and great apes is thin, most volumetric studies run at `n = 1–2` per species, and the one candidate novel area has no ape data. The null result is an absence of evidence with a stated cause.
- **The neuropil-vs-neuron ratio has no computational interpretation** (`G101`). 2.75× volume on 1.25× neurons is a striking number and the source does not say what a higher synapse-per-neuron count buys — capacity, precision, mixing, or nothing beyond the metabolic price of being large. The wiki has no architecture that scales edge density instead of unit count, so the one biological scale-up that produced a qualitative change is on an axis the wiki has never swept.
- **Lateralisation-as-consequence-of-size and lateralisation-as-adaptation-for-language are not separated**, and the human-unique neuropil asymmetry is offered under both readings on the same page ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T290). **Half of the discriminating measurement now exists** (Ardesch et al. 2022, [[wiki/concepts/connectivity-scaling-bottleneck.md]]): *structural* connectivity asymmetry between left/right homologous regions rises with cerebral volume across 14 primates spanning 350× in size (standardised β = +0.73, `p = 5 × 10⁻³`, against a weight-permutation null at `p < 2 × 10⁻¹⁶`), with the corpus callosum's cross-section scaling sub-isometrically on cortical surface (`b = 0.88`) as the mechanism. What is still missing is the other half — degree of *functional* lateralisation against size **with the function held fixed** — so the size term is now measured and the function term is not.
- **The `Homo erectus` window is unexplained.** Rapid encephalisation begins 1.8 Ma; symbolic/domain-neutral cognition appears archaeologically only at the *Homo sapiens* speciation. A million-plus years separate the anatomical event from the cognitive one, and the model's causal chain runs the wrong way round for that ordering.
- **Small-brained tool use is a standing counterexample.** *Australopithecus garhi*, 450 cm³, 2.5 Ma, associated with butchery. The size variable is doing less work than the framing assumes.

## Connections

- **[[wiki/concepts/discrete-infinity.md]]** — the direct architectural opponent, from the source ingested immediately before this one: Hauser et al.'s H3 says one small new operator plus rich interfaces, this source says no new part at all and afferent diversity into an old operator. Both are answers to `G70`, they prescribe opposite builds, and the dispute is logged as [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T289.
- **[[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]]** — the same shared/unique subtraction reaching the same verdict on number: approximate magnitude, small-*N* tracking, addition and subtraction are all present in the LCA reconstruction and modality-independent there (heard calls matched to seen faces), so the installed content-invariant code long predates the species that reasons with it.
- **[[wiki/concepts/causal-model-building.md]]** — supplies this page's most useful negative datum and takes a hit from it: chimpanzees choose tools by superficial contact rather than force transfer, no better than monkeys, so the great-ape tool-use advantage is not a causal-model advantage, and behavioural tool competence is not evidence of a learned causal model in any agent.
- **[[wiki/concepts/control-unity-and-diversity.md]]** — supplies the parameters this page's model varies: attention, updating, shifting and inhibition are exactly the domain-general components the source proposes as the evolutionary knobs, and that page's finding that they are separable in genetics and pharmacology is what makes "shift their relative weights" a mechanically coherent proposal rather than a metaphor.
- **[[wiki/concepts/compositionality.md]]** — receives the source's only causal proposal: generalised systematicity is bought by routing a greater diversity of afferents into one composition site (the arcuate-fasciculus/middle-temporal-gyrus result), which converts the recombination facet from an architectural mystery into a sweepable fan-in parameter.
- **[[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]]** — the same proposal read as its named payoff: the source treats analogical thought ("light as a wave") as the direct consequence of one operator gaining arguments from multiple semantic domains, which is a wiring account of analogy rather than a matching-algorithm account.
- **[[wiki/concepts/learned-world-models.md]]** — the ghost-condition result is a comparative boundary on what a world model may be conditioned on: only humans learn a transition from an outcome with the agent removed, so nonhuman social learning carries an agent token that human learning has factored out.
- **[[wiki/concepts/transthalamic-context-routing.md]]** — the anatomical asymmetry that page needs, measured across species: human dorsal thalamus gained neurons in the anterior principal, mediodorsal and pulvinar nuclei while sensory relay nuclei did not change, and only the human dorsal thalamus receives migrating GABAergic interneurons from the ganglionic eminence.
- **[[wiki/concepts/small-world-topology.md]]** — supplies the mechanism for this page's lateralisation row: conduction delay makes bilateral duplication of a computationally demanding function expensive as a network grows, so hemispheric specialisation is predicted as a *consequence* of scale rather than as an adaptation for the function that ends up lateralised.
- **[[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]]** — this page supplies the conversion rate's missing term: the source's model makes priors, experience and skill causally sequential through a growth schedule, so two organisms with the same priors and the same environment can differ in acquired skill purely by *when* their capacity arrived.
- **[[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]** — the voluntary-control axis, with an anatomical price tag: human-unique direct corticomotoneuronal synapses onto vocal-fold motoneurons show that voluntary access to an effector was bought by replacing a mediated path with a direct one, no new controller involved.
- **[[wiki/concepts/nameability.md]]** — sharpens what a shared symbol requires: the source grounds shared-ness in the consumer inferring the producer's communicative intent, putting a theory-of-mind term inside the semantics of a name rather than downstream of it.
- **[[wiki/concepts/objective-identifiability.md]]** — task-domain neutrality is that page's problem stated as a target rather than a defect: a representation with no immediate implication for action is precisely what no objective selects for, and the source dates its archaeological appearance to the *Homo sapiens* speciation, later than tool-making competence.
- **[[wiki/concepts/motivation-representation-synergy.md]]** — the same rejection of a new module reached through a different variable: MacLean 2016 locates the human change in a motivational parameter (reduced aggression, cooperative-breeding payoffs) that releases representations the great apes already hold, where this page locates it in a growth schedule — two re-weighting accounts that jointly form the third position on `T289`, and that page supplies the biasing variable with a *sign* for this page's unquantified model.
- **[[wiki/concepts/shared-intentionality.md]]** — what this page's first human-unique row is a symptom of: triadic joint attention at ~9 months is the behavioural marker of self–other equivalence, and the emulation/imitation distinction there is the mechanism behind this page's imitation-fidelity and ghost-condition rows — nonhuman social learning learns *from* the demonstrator, human social learning *through* it.
- **[[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]]** — the argument for one supermodule rather than many modules, made from a behavioural regularity instead of anatomy: the same first-order-present / higher-order-absent profile recurs across same-different, space, transitivity, hierarchy, tools and mindreading, which independent per-domain adaptations would not produce.
- **[[wiki/concepts/developmental-heterochrony.md]]** — measures this page's developmental lever at the molecular level and narrows it sharply: the human delay is carried by ~4% of the prefrontal transcriptome, is specific to gray-matter genes rather than white-matter ones, and peaks at ~10 years, so "the human growth schedule is slower" is false as stated and true of a sparse, tissue-selective, age-banded subset on a curve whose shape is conserved from mouse to human (Somel et al. 2009).
- **[[wiki/concepts/sparse-expert-routing.md]]** — the one architecture in which this page's central claim is directly measurable rather than argued: mixture-of-experts modules are enumerated by construction and the assignment is a readable integer per input, so "did a module for X emerge?" is a contingency table — and every specialisation reported to date (quantities, punctuation, verbs, textures, wheels) is lexical or perceptual, never relational, which is a negative result for the hypothesis that relational structure falls out of scale plus a routing bottleneck.
- **[[wiki/entities/sparsely-gated-moe.md]]** — the first large-scale test of this page's claim, and the cleanest negative: 2048 modules emerged in a translation encoder from scale plus a routing bottleneck and nothing else, and what they encode is a lexical field (*innovation/research*), a function word (the article "a" introducing a direct object), and a synonym set (*speed*) — modularity without relational content.
- **[[wiki/entities/switch-transformer.md]]** — the scale at which this page's question stopped being asked: 2048 experts were trained to 1.6T parameters and shipped with **no specialisation analysis at all** in the primary source, so the largest emergent module library in the literature was built without anyone checking what the modules encode — every specialisation table the wiki carries for this family comes from surveys and follow-up work, never from the system's own paper.
- **[[wiki/concepts/cellular-scaling-rules.md]]** — the direct measurement against this page's central quantitative row: whole-brain neuron counts across three mammalian orders give an exponent for how cortical mass tracks cortical units, and the primate value (`N^1.077`, invariant density) contradicts the neuropil-not-neurons reading this page takes from Shariff 1953 ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T301) — while supplying the same page's null half with a stronger form, since the human brain's *whole* cellular composition is predicted to within 7% by a primate regression fitted without it.
- **[[wiki/concepts/connectivity-scaling-bottleneck.md]]** — supplies the size term this page's lateralisation row was missing: connectivity asymmetry between homologous left/right regions rises with cerebral volume across 14 primates, and the corpus callosum scales sub-isometrically on cortical surface, so hemispheric divergence is a measured consequence of scale — which also means specialisation appearing in a scaled-up model is weak evidence that a *function* was discovered.
- **[[wiki/entities/rims.md]]** — a new failure mode for this page's central claim, from an architecture designed to exhibit it: the one module-to-object correspondence ever measured there was visible only after each module was hard-masked to 1/6 of the image, and it **decayed over training** as prediction improved ("the predictable dynamics of the system do not necessitate constant attention") — so an emergent module can be a transient of the learning curve, invisible at convergence, and every specialisation reported is temporal or spatial rather than relational (Goyal et al. 2019).
- **[[wiki/concepts/independent-causal-mechanisms.md]]** — the normative counterweight to this page: ICM argues modules should mirror the world's mechanisms, this page shows modules can instead be *products* of a growth schedule over domain-general parameters, which would make modular structure evidence about ontogeny rather than about the world.
