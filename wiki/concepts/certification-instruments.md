# Certification Instruments

**The inventory of protocols proposed for answering "did this system discover the structure, or fit a correlation?" — nineteen of them, none sufficient, and the record of *why* each fails is more informative than any of them.**

This page is the answer half of gap **G17** (*no evaluation protocol can certify structure discovery*), split out of the gap table at the lint pass at 212 ingests. G17 states the problem; this page holds the accumulated attempts. The split was forced by size — the row had reached 20,336 characters and its instrument ordinals had silently collided (two protocols numbered "eighth", two "seventh") — but it earns its place independently: the failure modes below are shared across instruments and were not statable while each was a clause in one cell.

**The numbering here (I1–I19) is canonical and replaces the ordinals previously used inside G17.** They are ordered by what the instrument needs, cheapest first — the ordering is the page's main claim, because an instrument's cost determines whether it gets run.

---

## What certification would have to mean

The target is not accuracy. i.i.d. testing cannot separate a recovered meta-graph from a correlation that holds in the sample, so a score is evidence about the sample and nothing else. Certification means licensing a claim of the form *the system's behaviour is generated by the intended structure*, which requires an instrument that can come back **negative on a system with a high score**.

Three requirements every instrument is measured against here:

1. **A well-defined intended solution** — something the score can be attributable *to*. Generated benchmarks satisfy this by construction (the generator's own rules); hand-authored ones satisfy it by author declaration; natural data does not satisfy it at all.
2. **Developer-blindness** — the system's builder must not have known the test. And this is a *depleting stock*, not a property (F1 below).
3. **Non-gameability by the obvious training move** — every instrument that presents a fixed query set can be trained on.

**Requirement 3 has a quantitative form, and one benchmark states it at authoring time.** [[wiki/entities/frontiermath.md]]'s *guessproofness* criterion: no strategy may exceed a **1% chance of the correct answer without doing most of the work the intended solution requires**, enforced per item by a reviewer whose job includes trying to break it, and implemented by making the answer space large (`367707`, `1876572071974094803391179`) rather than by hiding it. Every instrument below measures this quantity *after* the benchmark exists; this bounds it *before*. Its value here is that it is audited and reported failed — 2 of 35 second-reviewed items admitted a cheap guess, and one of the four problems any model ever solved fell to "a few simple simulations".

## The inventory

| **I1** | **Controlled-shortcut datasets** — Shift-MNIST, biased CelebA, unfair dSprites: the intended rule is known by construction and the shortcut is inserted deliberately | A synthetic generator with a planted confound | Accuracy drop when the confound is removed | [[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]] |
| **I2** | **Signature limits + resource-stress probe** — certify an installed prior by a quantitative constant (capacity, Weber ratio, entry threshold) that stays put across scale, retraining and domain shift; then shrink the budget, since in biology learned distinctions degrade first and core ones last | Nothing but repeated runs | A constant, or its absence. **Diagnostic evidence is in the failure profile, not the success rate** | [[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]] |
| **I3** | **Richness protocol** — one representation must answer classify / generate / parse / create-new-concepts / explain; a shortcut rule is by construction a mapping to *one* output type | Multiple task heads on one model | Which output types the representation supports | [[wiki/concepts/causal-model-building.md]] |
| **I4** | **Re-goaling protocol** — hold the transition structure fixed, swap the reward function, measure the retraining needed | An environment with separable dynamics and reward | Retraining cost as a proxy for model reuse | [[wiki/concepts/simulation-based-planning.md]] |

| **I6** | **Paired violation of expectation** — one history, two continuations differing only in the property under test; score whether the intended one is rated higher | Hand-authored contrast pairs | A *relative* judgement needing no labels, no shift, no absolute calibration. **The only instrument that can register a score below chance**, i.e. the opposite rule acquired (26.3% on Baby Intuitions Preference) | [[wiki/entities/hbtom.md]], [[wiki/concepts/violation-of-expectation.md]] |
| **I7** | **Linear probing** — decode a hypothesised structure from internal activations | Ground-truth structure labels | Whether the structure is *there*, and by which layer: a whole maze at 0.83–0.99 per wall from one token at layer 2 | [[wiki/concepts/representation-probing.md]], [[wiki/entities/maze-solving-transformers.md]] |
| **I8** | **Support/query split** — induce whatever structure the model infers from one observation pair, then **execute it on a second pair from the same latent mechanism** | A generator producing two instances of one mechanism | **The gap between self-fit and transfer**: 0.994 / 0.000 means the answer was encoded, not the mechanism. Paired with **action primitiveness** (does some single learned code reproduce each atomic transformation) it splits vocabulary coverage from search competence (T156) | [[wiki/entities/neo-neural-theorizer.md]] |
| **I9** | **Consistency score** — apply a transform the task's semantics says is meaning-preserving (synonym substitution; replace a sub-expression by its computed value) and compare the model's output to *its own* output, never to a target | A known invariance | No labels, so none of I7's circularity. **Its refinement is what makes it certification rather than robustness: restrict the score to items the model got *wrong*** — identical *incorrect* outputs cannot both be explained by being correct — which takes the best of three architectures from 0.90 to **0.34** | [[wiki/entities/pcfg-set.md]] |
| **I10** | **Concept-based evaluation** — enumerate the concepts the system should hold, select those it answers *correctly* i.i.d., hand-author items instantiating each through different attributes, layouts and roles, report the drop | A concept inventory and an author | MRNet 73% → 49%/44%; SCL 89% → 62%/68%; ARC-Kaggle2 19% → **29%** on *top/bottom*, **8%** on *boundary*. The **rise** is the methodological finding: a concept-variation set is a *decomposition*, not a harder test set | [[wiki/entities/raven.md]] |
| **I11** | **Declared held-out abstraction + context-blind baseline** — generate items from an explicit symbolic structure, then *declare* what is withheld (a `[relation, object, attribute]` triple, a pair of triples, an attribute pair, half a value range), and report every regime against a trained context-blind baseline (22.4% vs 12.5% chance) | A symbolic generator | Requirement 1 satisfied **by construction**, and a *dissociation* rather than a score: recombining seen triples costs ~20 points while a relation carried onto an unseen attribute lands at or below the blind baseline — one architecture passes and fails depending on which axis is withheld | [[wiki/entities/pgm.md]] |
| **I12** | **Adversarial per-item authoring** — pay a human to write an item the deployed model misclassifies, have two others verify the label, retrain, repeat | ~5 min annotator time per accepted item, forever | (i) A score that **cannot saturate** (18.33% → 8.07% → 6.92%); (ii) a **try count and a clock** per item (3.4 → 6.4 tries, 199 → 355 s), so F3's audit is a by-product of collection; (iii) the **item composition as a read-out** of which weaknesses closed (numerical inference 38% → 10% of items, and +18.5 points on an independent stress test) | [[wiki/entities/anli.md]] |
| **I13** | **Accuracy-profile correlation** — correlate the model's accuracy *profile across item types* with the human profile instead of comparing aggregates | A per-item human baseline | The dissociation that makes it necessary: a graph-encoder ToMnet at the human aggregate (.90 vs .91) correlates **0.06**, and is *below chance* (.05) on the one type authored to catch its heuristic; an inverse planner at .96 correlates 0.55. Valid only if item types are authored one-per-heuristic, not as difficulty tiers | [[wiki/entities/agent-benchmark.md]], [[wiki/concepts/human-baseline.md]] |
| **I14** | **Named-perturbation sweep + template re-instantiation** — rewrite each solved item along declared axes (value substitution, digit expansion, type change, added operation, *reversed* operation, rephrasing, distractor insertion, statement deletion) and read the per-axis drop; separately, variabilise the item and draw `n` instances of the identical reasoning graph | A parseable item format | Per-axis drops that **rank the shortcut channels**, and `(mean, SD)`. Free second measurement: **the published item's z-score inside its own distribution is a contamination test needing no corpus access** — right-tail, often beyond 1 SD, for 21 of 25 models. **The axis's limit case is translation** — a parallel-language item pair changes every token and no relation, and the EN>ZH gap is significant across 14 models (Wilcoxon, 582k trajectories) — which also exposes the instrument's unstated precondition: a meaning-preserving perturbation must preserve the **grader's** competence too, and answer-extraction failures cluster in the incorrect Chinese responses (T223) | [[wiki/entities/gsm8k.md]]; [[wiki/entities/olymmath.md]] |
| **I15** | **Rule-level evaluation** — require the rule *and* the answer in one output, hand-classify the stated rule against the task's author-declared abstraction | An author-declared abstraction per task, and a hand-classifier | The only instrument that asks the solver *what it did*: a solver at or above human accuracy reaches ~27% of its correct answers by a rule the concept group is not about, against ~8% for humans | [[wiki/concepts/rule-level-evaluation.md]] |
| **I16** | **Paired method-preserving / method-invalidating perturbation** — rewrite each solved item twice at *matched* lexical distance: one rewrite that the original solution still solves, one small edit that destroys the property the original solution exploited (symmetry, reducibility, linearity, a feasible search space). Both must change the answer. Report the drop on each arm | Two expert rewrites per item, and a benchmark that shipped a train/test split | The **difference between the arms**, which is the first quantity that isolates *applicability judgement* from difficulty and from surface novelty: ~0 on the preserving arm against **−13 to −16** at the frontier and −25 to −32 below it. Its design target is a number — **retrieval MRR of the original from the perturbation, 0.986** — i.e. a nearest-solved-problem policy provably cannot separate the arms. Two free measurements: the preserving arm's drop restricted to **train-split seeds** is a contamination test needing no corpus and no template, and one-shot ICL with the original solution splits into a repair rate (24–40%) and a **misleading rate (18–40%) that grows with model capability** | [[wiki/entities/math-perturb.md]] |

| **I17** | **Automated pre-submission ensemble gate + model-difference searchability screen** — run every candidate item against a *fixed* frontier-model ensemble before any human sees it and admit only what the ensemble fails (exact-match must stump all; multiple-choice must stump all but one, as a guess correction applied at authoring time); separately, flag an item as searchable if a model **with** search answers it correctly and the **same** model without search does not, then hand-audit the flags | An API budget and a frozen ensemble — no annotator time per item | A **bit** per item, and an aggregate yield (>70,000 attempts → ~13,000 stumped → 2,500 accepted). The cheapest admission filter here: I12's cost per item drops from ~5 min of paid annotation to one API call, and what is lost is exactly I12's by-product, the per-item try count and clock, so `F3` returns in full. The searchability half is the cheap substitute for a paid human-with-a-search-engine baseline, and it returned a **null** — removing the flagged items barely changed frontier accuracy | [[wiki/entities/hle.md]] |

| **I18** | **Post-release component-wise audit and repair** — decompose every *shipped* item into problem / answer / rationale, validate each component independently on a three-valued scale (valid / invalid / **indeterminate**), repair what is flawed-but-fixable under an *evaluation-intent-preservation* constraint (two independent expert teams, fixed order Problem → Solution → Answer, minimal necessary edits), and publish the residue **as** indeterminate with a required-expertise tag rather than discarding it | Two expert repair teams per item plus adjudication — the most expensive instrument here, and it runs *after* the benchmark is already in use | A **defect map** (19 categories: 5 problem, 10 rationale, 4 answer) and, uniquely, **the effect of the defects on the scores already reported**: 668/2,500 items usable unmodified, 1,143 repaired, 689 indeterminate, and a scoring-relevant defect rate of **~26%** derived identically from seven independent models. It is the only instrument that can *correct* a benchmark instead of retiring it | [[wiki/entities/hle-verified.md]] |

| **I19** | **Reference-paraphrase differential** — synthesise three surface-paraphrased copies of each split (structure, numbers and difficulty preserved), measure perplexity on the answer part and exact-match `n`-gram accuracy over the whole item on original and copies, normalise `δ = (M_ori − M_ref)/M_ori`, and report `δ_train − δ_test`. Aimed at **contamination**, not at structure discovery | A benchmark that shipped a train/test split, a paraphrasing model, and either logits **or** (for `n`-gram accuracy alone) plain API access | A ranking of models by train-split familiarity (Qwen-7B **44.29**, Aquila2-34B 26.98, Llama-3-8B **0.00**), a per-item verbatim-recall flag (Qwen-1.8B reproduces all 5 sampled 5-grams in **223** GSM8K-train and **25 MATH-test** items), and a regime read-out — `n`-gram `δ` dominates under pre-training, ppl `δ` under SFT. Validated by a real meta-experiment rather than asserted | [[wiki/concepts/benchmark-contamination.md]] |

**Numbering note.** I17 is appended rather than inserted: by cost it belongs beside I1, but the ordinals are load-bearing elsewhere in the wiki and renumbering is worse than a footnote. I18 is appended for the same reason, and it does not belong on the cost axis at all — it is the only instrument here applied to a benchmark rather than to a system. I19 is appended and is a third kind again: it is applied to a **model's corpus**, and it answers the question upstream of every other instrument here — was the item withheld at all?

**I18's own defect is structural and worth stating with the instrument.** Its flagging stage (pass@8 frontier solvers) and its repair stage (multi-model proposals, multi-model adjudication) are model-in-the-loop, while its headline output is *model accuracy on the repaired set*. An item where the frontier consensus is confidently wrong and the key was right is a candidate for being repaired into agreement — and that is exactly the population an I17-style ensemble gate selects for, so the two instruments compose badly. The control is cheap and unrun: repair a blind sample with no model outputs visible and report key agreement.

**Two scaling notes.** I10 scaled and given its missing half is [[wiki/entities/conceptarc.md]] (16 concept groups × 10 variations × 3 test inputs, 415 participants) — which closed the instrument's standing "no human baseline" weakness and measured a 40-point gap on tasks authored to be *easy*. I5 built to spec is [[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]] (private evaluation set, ~3 examples per task, no data generator, enumerated priors).

## Failure modes — shared across instruments, and the reason none is sufficient

| # | Failure mode | Evidence | Which instruments it hits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **Developer-blindness is a depleting stock, not a property** | ARC-AGI-1's 100 private tasks absorbed ~10,000 reported scores across four competitions, each leaking a little. The authors' fix is structural: separate the leaderboard set from the final-scoring set, enlarge both | Every instrument with a fixed held-out set — I1, I5, I10, I11 |
| **F2** | **Item-level blindness does not imply *distributional* blindness, and the two clauses conflict** | If the public training set is i.i.d. with the private test set, a model trained on the public data is already adapted to the private one — and the difficulty calibration that makes cross-subset scores comparable is precisely what guarantees it. Evidenced: an ARC harness never mentioning ARC elicits correct ARC colour naming from Gemini 3 Deep Think over raw integer arrays (T207) | I5, I10, I11 — and it is unfixable jointly with calibration |
| **F3** | **`GD ≈ 0` items are unaudited; difficulty is asserted per-*benchmark* and never measured per-*task*** | 49% of ARC-AGI-1's private set fell to blind brute-force DSL search in 2020, before LLMs, at low compute (T205) — invisible to the protocol. **And rating it per task is not enough**: [[wiki/entities/frontiermath.md]] rates every item on three axes (background 1–5, hours to find the key idea, hours to execute it) and reports that first- and second-reviewer ratings "rarely matched and often showed substantial differences" | All of them except I12, where the try count makes it a by-product — an *estimated* per-item difficulty inherits its raters' disagreement, a *measured* one (tries, seconds) does not |
| **F4** | **Probe labels are two guesses deep** | The experimenter must know the answer *and* know it in the model's own coordinates: an Othello world model reads out at 75% under human colour labels and **99.6%** under mover-relative ones — same activations, same decoder class (T157) | I7, decisively; I2 partially |
| **F5** | **The framing axis** — an instrument supplying the same problem *representation* to every task measures search within that representation and cannot detect abstraction upstream of it | ARC-AGI-1's generalization difficulty is ≈0 on this axis by construction (G73) | All of them; it is upstream of leakage |
| **F6** | **Adversarial authoring drives toward the regime where the strong-baseline check stops discriminating** | By ANLI round 2 a context-blind hypothesis-only baseline is within **2.6 points** of the full state-of-the-art model (T216) | I12 specifically, and it is the price of F1-immunity |
| **F7** | **A benchmark hard enough to floor every solver destroys the ordering it exists to produce** | Two programs 2 points apart on ARC (21% / 19%) are **23 points** apart on ConceptARC. The diagnostic regime is **humans-at-ceiling**. The opposite corner, occupied deliberately: [[wiki/entities/frontiermath.md]] floors six frontier models below 2%, **four problems total** are solved even once, and the authors themselves say the resulting ordering should not be read | Every instrument; it is a design constraint, not a defect |
| **F8** | **Constancy-under-perturbation is necessary and not sufficient** | I2 assumes an invariant failure profile indicts an installed prior — and a model with *no* prior exhibits one: V-JEPA's intuitive-physics partition is constant across 115M→ViT-H, three objectives, three corpora and a 100× data sweep, because what fixes it is a memory window and a framerate none of those levers move (T161). Repair: perturb the *resource* the failure could otherwise be attributed to | I2 |
| **F9** | **The scored statistic is a free parameter fitted on the results** | For I6, *which moment of the error trace* counts as surprise is unspecified: `max_t` vs `mean_t` swings the same model by 16 points one way on one subtask and 11 the other way on another, and rival groups justify opposite choices (T146). Repairs: pre-register the statistic; report the shortcut-solvable subtask subset separately | I6, and by extension any instrument reducing a trace to a scalar |
| **F11** | **The item count is a resolution limit, and scores are reported past it** | AIME as *evaluated by the field* pools both administrations at **30** problems, so one item is worth **3.33 points** and the binomial standard error is ≈**2.6×** that of a 200-item benchmark — and the instrument **as administered is 15 items** ([[wiki/entities/aime.md]]), i.e. 6.67 points each and ≈**3.7×**, which is the figure that applies to any single-contest score — yet the wiki carries AIME numbers quoted to a tenth of a point and compares models 2–3 points apart on them (Sun et al. 2025, [[wiki/entities/olymmath.md]]). Distinct from F7: F7 is a benchmark placed where every solver floors, this is a benchmark whose `n` cannot resolve the differences it is used to claim. The fix is arithmetic and nobody applies it — report the item count with every score, and refuse comparisons below `1/n`. | Every instrument with a fixed item set; acutely I10, I11, I15, where per-concept or per-axis subsets shrink `n` again inside an already-small benchmark |
| **F10** | **The reference system is unspecified** | The human denominator is a protocol, not a number: 73% vs ~91% on the same items and participants; 60% vs 99% on the same benchmark (T213, T214, T210) | I10, I13, I15 — every instrument scored against a human |

| **F12** | **A benchmark's measured ground-truth error rate is a property of its audit protocol, not of the dataset — and it can exceed every score reported on the benchmark** | HLE audits itself at **15.4%** expert disagreement (two rounds of 200 sampled items, solved in full, disputes routed to consensus) and **~18%** on a biology/chemistry/health subset — which becomes **25%** on the *same items* if a question counts as flagged on one dissenting expert with no author rebuttal. Every score on the leaderboard (2.7–13.4%) is smaller than the floor. Unlike `F11` this is a bias term of unknown sign that does not shrink with `n` (HLE has 2,500 items; its binomial error is ~0.7pp) | Every instrument scored against a fixed labelled set; and it makes `F3`-style self-reported error rates ([[wiki/entities/frontiermath.md]] ~10%, [[wiki/entities/gpqa.md]] 23.6–26.4% non-objectivity, this 15.4%) **non-comparable**, since each used a different reviewer count and rebuttal rule |
| **F13** | **A renewed benchmark has no fixed scale, and renewal is the only structural fix for `F1`** | AIME re-authors 15 fresh items twice a year, so a 2026 item set cannot sit in a 2025 training corpus — developer-blindness restored annually and for free, without a private split to deplete. The price is that nothing is anchored: the human mean over 25 administrations spans **4.28 to 6.63 of 15** (15.7 percentage points), and the two contests *within* 2016 differ by 1.4 points — while "AIME 2024" and "AIME 2025" model scores are compared as one instrument ([[wiki/entities/aime.md]]). Contamination-immunity and cross-year comparability are the same property read from either end, and no instrument in this inventory has both. The repair is cheap and unused: retain a small anchor set of retired items, or report the concurrent human mean with every machine score, which turns the drift into a measured covariate | Every instrument that renews rather than withholds — the answer `F1` demands, and the reason `F1`'s answer is not free |
| **F14** | **The detector's null and its worst case have the same signature** | Every contamination statistic that differences a train split against a test split returns the same reading for a clean model and for one that ingested *both* splits. Measured: ChatGLM3-6B reads `δ_train-test = 0.55` — near the clean end of a 31-model ranking — on `δ_train = 36.85` and `δ_test = 36.30`, roughly 3× LLaMA-7B's absolute familiarity. Compounding it, `I19` returns a flat **zero** suspicious items for Aquila2-34B on the GSM8K test split it is *known* to have ingested, because the model learned the content under a different answer delimiter (`The answer is` for `\n#### 12`) | `I19` directly; and every instrument on this page that assumes a withheld set — i.e. all of them except `I12` and `I18` |

**F12 demonstrated a second time, by a second party, on the same 2,500 items.** [[wiki/entities/hle-verified.md]] re-audits HLE **component-wise and three-valued** instead of item-wise and binary, and gets a different number from the same dataset: 26.7% usable unmodified, 45.7% repairable, 27.6% indeterminate, and a **~26% scoring-relevant defect rate** (derived from the ratio of full-set to repaired-subset accuracy gain, identical to ±0.03 across seven models) against the benchmark's own 15.4%. Two refinements F12 did not have. (i) **The rate and the *kind* both depend on the domain, and they trade off**: mathematics and chemistry produce decidable errors (65.9% / 70.3% invalid rationales), engineering and the humanities produce indeterminacy (73–77% *uncertain*, near-zero explicit invalidation) — so an aggregate error rate averages two categorically different failures and hides which one it is made of. (ii) **The consequence is now measured rather than argued.** Re-scoring after repair leaves the full-set ordering intact for all seven models — F12 does not automatically invalidate a ranking — but the correction is *non-uniform* (+7.78 to +11.50), halves adjacent gaps, and on the defective quarter alone compresses the field from a 2.5× to a 1.22× spread while changing the top rank. The correct statement of F12's cost is therefore not "the ordering is unreadable" but **"the gaps are inflated by an unknown, non-constant amount, and any sub-leaderboard restricted to hard items is the part most likely to be an artefact"**.

**The control F8 forces, and it is cheap:** run `n = 20` **randomly-initialised** networks of the same architecture through the identical scoring pipeline and report the per-property effect size over that null rather than an accuracy — the machine analogue of habituation trials, one forward pass per seed, and the only thing that makes a benchmark's low-level bias subtractable. *Every VoE number in this wiki predating it quotes an unknown chance level.*

## What the record adds up to

Three conclusions the inventory supports that no single instrument states.

**1. An honest instrument returns a vector, not a number.** Reached independently from two sides. From the behaviour side (I9): a dataset *generated by* the intended compositional rule, with memorisation designed out, on which 0.92 i.i.d. accuracy coexists with 0.72 systematicity, 0.50 productivity and 0.54 localism. From the task side (I10): a concept-variation score that *rises* on one concept and falls 11 points on another, so every single-number benchmark score in this wiki is a mean over a concept mixture with unmeasured per-concept variance. Any single number, including the good ones, is under-determined.

**2. The certification target is the developer, not the system.** I5's developer-aware conditioning is not a refinement — it is the whole point, because skill on any task known in advance is purchasable outright with priors or with data. This is why F1 and F2 are the inventory's hardest failures: they are both statements about what the builder knew, and neither is measurable from the model.

**3. G17 may be a maintenance schedule rather than a protocol.** The only response anyone offers to F1 is to treat benchmark authorship as a [[wiki/concepts/refinement-loop.md]] run against the field, with the stopping criterion *"authoring tasks easy for humans and hard for AI becomes impossible"*. I12 is that loop with the period shortened to one item. If this is right, the instrument is not a thing you build once, and its cost is a standing expense rather than a capital one — which is a different kind of answer from the one G17 asks for, and the only one with five years of evidence behind it.
