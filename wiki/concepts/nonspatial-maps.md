# Non-Spatial Cognitive Maps

**The claim that the hippocampal–entorhinal map is a general relational substrate rather than a navigation system — fourteen domains in which its signatures appear outside physical space, one matched control in which they do not, and the single element of the map that has no non-spatial version at all.**

Split out of [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]] at the 244-source lint. That page holds the map's three spatial elements (coding, anchoring, route planning) and the models that build them; this one holds the question the whole framing of this wiki depends on — **does any of it apply when the space is not a space?** — because the answer is not uniform across the three elements, and the wiki had been reading it as though it were.

**The short version, and it is the reason for the split.** Of the map's three elements, two generalise and one does not. A metric-like *code* over a non-spatial structure is well evidenced across fourteen domains, including cellular-resolution and causal entries. *Search* over such a code is evidenced by inference on never-traversed vectors. **Anchoring has no non-spatial instance anywhere** — no cell codes the "boundary" of a concept or a social milieu, and nothing says what a landmark or a local geometry would even mean off a plane. The two elements a machine can already copy are the two that transfer; the one that is genuinely missing from every architecture is the one with no biological template to copy. That is the real content of gap `G39`.

## The evidence

The review's reason for taking the map seriously as a general reasoning substrate, and the wiki's second and third instances of grid-like coding outside navigation:

The review's reason for taking the map seriously as a general reasoning substrate, and the wiki's second and third instances of grid-like coding outside navigation:

| Domain | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Concept space** | Hexadirectional entorhinal modulation while viewing sequences of morphed birds (neck × leg lengths) aligned vs. misaligned to a sixfold axis; also in ventromedial prefrontal cortex, orbitofrontal, posterior cingulate, retrosplenial, lateral parietal and temporoparietal cortex — the spatial-navigation network and the default-mode network — with modulation strength and grid-angle stability both correlating with task accuracy, and entorhinal and prefrontal grid angles aligned to *each other* (Constantinescu et al. 2016) |
| **Social space** | Hippocampal response scales with the *angle* of the vector to a character in a power × affiliation space, posterior cingulate with its *magnitude* (Tavares et al. 2015) — a polar decomposition across two regions |
| **Discrete spaces** | Hippocampal-entorhinal coding of spaces defined by transitions between discrete items, with no underlying continuum. The strongest instance: entorhinal fMRI adaptation scaling with distance on a 12-node object graph learned from random walks, with the graph recoverable by MDS on the adaptation matrix, the metric symmetric and **non-Euclidean** (best predicted by communicability / successor representation), and 0 / 23 subjects aware any structure existed (Garvert et al. 2017, [[wiki/concepts/successor-representation.md]]) |
| **Latent context in a reversal task** | The table's only **human single-unit** entry, and the only one where the map is watched *forming*: hippocampal populations code an uncued latent context and stimulus identity in disentangled, cross-generalizing format, but only in sessions where participants actually perform one-shot inference — and the same geometry appears within minutes of a four-minute verbal description of the task's latent structure (Courellis et al. 2024, [[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]) |
| **Latent context in a bandit** | The table's only entry that is **causal and cellular at once**, and the only one with no spatial task wrapped around it: mice in an operant chamber infer which of two levers is currently high-probability; ~30% of ventral-CA1 pyramidal cells code that contingency irrespective of choice or expected outcome, the code cross-generalizes across trial types, and ablating the population shifts behaviour from state inference to Q-learning while stripping the state-inference signature from accumbens dopamine (Mishchanchuk et al. 2024, [[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]]) |
| **Time / context** | Episodic memory is cut by temporal boundaries the way it is cut by spatial ones — [[wiki/concepts/event-segmentation.md]] |
| **Non-spatial cells** | Odour, elapsed-time and sound-frequency cells when those are the task's ordering dimension; human concept cells firing for a person independent of the evoking stimulus |
| **Word space** | Entorhinal grid-like code over the relative *angular* positions of newly learned audiovisual-object words, with a distance code in prefrontal/orbitofrontal/cingulate cortex (Viganò et al. 2021, via Chen et al. 2022) |
| **Discrete decision space** | Hexadirectional entorhinal and medial-prefrontal modulation for **inferred, never-traversed** vectors between people in a 4×4 social hierarchy that was itself reconstructed from two 1-D rank orders learned on separate days; the same code appears in temporoparietal junction and superior temporal sulcus, and decision value read off the map is stronger for grid-aligned vectors (Park et al. 2021) |
| **Two-step sensory chains** | Hippocampus is *necessary* for a non-spatial inferred choice — optogenetic dorsal-CA1 silencing during the cue abolishes it while sparing first-order conditioning — and represents the chain's intermediary at choice, with the inferred outcome appearing instead in medial prefrontal cortex and midbrain (Barron et al. 2020). The wiki's only causal, non-spatial test of the map's involvement in inference |
| **Rank space, in developing humans** | The largest instance in this table by an order of magnitude: 203 subjects aged 8–25 on a 5×5 attack-rank × defence-rank map assembled from 1-rank neighbour pairs never shown as a plane. Six-fold entorhinal modulation (six-fold only), a medial-prefrontal 2-D Euclidean **distance** code that beats either 1-D rank, and both codes strengthening with age; grid strength mediates the age→inference gain, and both codes predict Raven's matrix-reasoning score (Qu et al. 2026) |
| **Gaze** | Grid-like coding of eye position in monkey entorhinal cortex during free viewing, with no locomotion at all (Killian et al. 2012) |
| **Accumulated evidence** | The table's only **rodent single-unit** entry for a learned, non-perceptual variable: mouse dorsal-CA1 cells have firing fields in a 2-D accumulated-evidence × position space (917 cells, 1.7 ± 0.3 fields each, 53% multi-field), ~90% of them genuinely conjunctive rather than position- or evidence-only, and the population lies on a ~5-dimensional manifold on which position and evidence are two independent gradients (Nieh et al. 2021, [[wiki/concepts/population-geometry.md]]) |

**What the concept-space row actually required of the subject.** Constantinescu et al. 2016 is the wiki's only case where the map is demonstrably *built* rather than shown, and the construction is the interesting part for a model:

| Element | Detail | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **Dimensions were never displayed as a space** | Stimuli were single birds morphing on screen; the two dimensions (neck length, leg length) had to be extracted from one-dimensional visual features and assembled into a plane | The map is inferred from experience, not perceived — the discovery half of LGD, in a human, with the code as the readout |
| **"Movement" was a rate ratio** | Trajectory direction `θ` = ratio of the rates of change of legs to neck; the same stimulus lies on trajectories of different orientations and the same orientation is formed by different stimuli | Displacement in a concept space is defined by *relative rates of attribute change* — a concrete `a_t` for [[wiki/concepts/path-integration.md]] where the wiki previously only had "movement along attribute dimensions" |
| **Training was association, not mapping** | Subjects learned stimulus→outcome pairs by freely morphing until an outcome appeared, then were tested by ballistic morphs to a remembered target | The plane was never the task; it fell out of navigating it |
| **The map was not reportable** | 0 of 28 subjects conceived of the bird or outcome relationships as lying in a spatial map | A structural code can be present and behaviourally effective while unavailable to report — so the absence of an explicit map in a model is not evidence against one |

**The discrete-decision row is the wiki's only case where the map is assembled from *marginals*, and the only one where a value is computed off it.** Park et al. 2021, n = 21, two fMRI sessions more than a week apart:

| Element | Detail | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **The 2-D space was never observed** | 16 faces ranked 4×4 on competence × popularity. Training was feedback-based binary choices between *adjacent* ranks, **one dimension per day**, the two days ~2 apart, dimensions never co-presented, the hierarchy never displayed, and no task ever required combining them | The joint structure is assembled from two 1-D marginals with **no joint sample**. Neither [[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]-style traversal learning nor [[wiki/concepts/successor-representation.md]] estimation performs this operation — both consume transitions *in* the space they build |
| **What is built is a product space, not two number lines** | Mahalanobis pattern dissimilarity in hippocampal and entorhinal ROIs scales with 2-D Euclidean rank distance and does so significantly better than either 1-D rank distance alone (per-dimension `τ_A` = 0.04–0.07, all `p < 0.001` FWE); MDS on hippocampal patterns recovers the 4×4 layout; searchlight adds medial prefrontal/orbitofrontal and posterior cingulate/precuneus/retrosplenial cortex | Two independently maintained orderings would pass every behavioural test in this task, so the 2-D-beats-1-D contrast is the whole claim. It is the wiki's cleanest evidence that integration across separately learned dimensions happens *without being asked for* |
| **The vectors carrying the grid code were never taken** | Hexadirectional modulation of the F0→F1 and F0→F2 angles, aligned to each subject's entorhinal grid orientation `φ`, in bilateral entorhinal and medial prefrontal cortex; **six-fold only** (four-, five-, seven-, eight-fold null and each significantly weaker); survives restriction to the 88 pairs presented for the first time | Constantinescu's trajectories were morphs the subject actually executed. Here the displacement exists only as an inference composed on the fly, so the periodic code is over a *hypothesised* move — the closest thing the wiki has to a grid code used for planning rather than for tracking |
| **The origin is set by the task, not by the display** | **No** hexadirectional coding of the F1→F2 vector — the pair actually being compared — only of vectors anchored at F0, the entrepreneur whose perspective subjects were instructed to adopt | Which point a vector is measured *from* is selected by task role rather than by stimulus salience: an instance of anchoring (G39) in an abstract space, and the wiki's only case where the origin is a *perspective* |
| **Value is read off the map and inherits its frame** | "Growth potential" (the area spanned by the pair's better rank in each dimension) — never trained with feedback — is encoded in medial prefrontal cortex, bilateral temporoparietal junction and entorhinal cortex, essentially uncorrelated with the hexadirectional regressor (`r` = 0.04 ± 0.03) and surviving its inclusion. The value effect is **significantly stronger for grid-aligned than misaligned vectors** in all three regions, and across subjects entorhinal grid modulation predicts temporoparietal value coding (`r` = 0.58 left, 0.44 right, controlling for accuracy). The comparison term \|GP1 − GP2\| appears in ventromedial prefrontal and entorhinal/hippocampal cortex | The wiki's only biological coupling between a structural code and a *value* computation, and a working existence proof for gap G28: a novel objective is evaluated on the fly by vector operations over an already-built map, with no relearning of the map and no feedback on the objective |
| **The frame is stable and shared across regions** | `φ` estimated in one session predicts hexadirectional modulation in a session 8.95 ± 0.81 days later, in entorhinal, medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate cortex; temporoparietal junction, superior temporal sulcus and dorsal medial prefrontal cortex are modulated in alignment with the *entorhinal* orientation | Replicates concept-space cross-week stability in a discrete domain, and extends shared-orientation coordination from two regions to a network ([[wiki/concepts/distributed-reference-frames.md]], G43) |

**Limits.** Training deliberately encouraged treating ranks as equidistant intervals and filtered at > 95% accuracy, so subjects who built a non-metric or differently shaped representation would have been excluded; linear and monotone-nonlinear (e.g. sigmoidal) dimension geometries are not separable in this design; and unlike the concept-space study, grid modulation predicts **no** behavioural measure here — no reaction-time or accuracy effect of alignment, and no correlation between a subject's grid modulation and how much value difference explains their reaction time. With accuracy at 98.68 ± 0.27% that is a ceiling rather than a clean null, but it means the code's behavioural relevance rests on the neural value coupling alone.

**The bandit row is what the Kumaran & Maguire null and the BOLD caveat below jointly demand, and it is worth stating what it does and does not settle.** It is non-spatial (the animal is stationary at one wall of a small chamber), cellular, causal, in a rodent, over a variable that has no sensory correlate whatsoever — the context exists only as an inference over past outcomes. What it does **not** supply is any metric or periodic structure: two contexts is not a space, so it evidences "the hippocampus differentiates non-spatial latent contexts the way it differentiates rooms" and nothing about grids ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T45's amodal side, again without T37's detector problem). It is also **ventral** CA1, whereas Barron et al. 2020's causal test is dorsal — so the wiki now has two non-spatial causal results from opposite ends of the long axis, and no account of why the division falls where it does.

**The latent-context row is the second cellular-resolution entry, and it is in humans.** It buys three things no fMRI row can: the variable is *latent* (never presented, inferable only from feedback), the contrast is within-subject and behavioural rather than task-vs-baseline, and the format degrades on error trials at single-trial resolution. What it does not buy is any claim about a *metric* or a grid — the space is four stimuli × two contexts, so it evidences "the hippocampus builds a relational arrangement over a non-spatial variable" and says nothing about periodicity ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T45's amodal side, without the T37 detector problem).

**A caveat that travels with all of these rows.** Every entry above except the single-unit ones is fMRI, and hexadirectional BOLD modulation is not a measurement of a grid population; the detection procedure for grid patterns is threshold-arbitrary and its standard autocorrelation form produces false positives without spike-shuffle controls; and no single-unit grid code in a conceptual space has ever been recorded in any species (Chen et al. 2022, [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T37).

**The evidence-accumulation row is the one that partly answers that caveat, and it is worth being precise about how far.** Nieh et al. 2021 is cellular-resolution, in a rodent, with a variable that is *learned and not present in the input* — so "the map takes non-perceptual variables as dimensions" now has single-unit support rather than only BOLD. What it does **not** supply is a periodic code: no grid analysis is run, and the abstract dimension is coded by localised fields, like a place dimension. It is also inseparable from space — the abstract variable was accumulated *while navigating*, and the two are carried by the same cells — so it is silent on whether a map forms for an abstract variable with no spatial task wrapped around it ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T45).

**And the one matched control that exists returns a null.** Every row above compares a non-spatial task against a low-level baseline; none of them holds relational demand fixed and swaps only the domain. Kumaran & Maguire 2005 do exactly that, and the hippocampus drops out.

| Design element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Task** | Pass a crate of wine along a chain of the subject's own 14 friends, minimising hops. **Spatial**: each recipient must live physically closer to the target. **Social**: each recipient must *know* the next. Same 8 names on screen in both |
| **Non-relational controls** | Count friends who wear glasses (social) / who live in a given building type (spatial) — same stimuli, no chain to search |
| **Matching** | Spatial and social relational tasks did not differ on performance (80% vs 74%), difficulty (6.3 vs 6.0/10), reaction time (16.3 vs 15.8 s), episodic-memory, emotional, self-related or imagery ratings. The *only* significant differences were the intended ones: frequency of "where they live" vs "how they know each other" thoughts |
| **Result** | Spatial relational > social relational: bilateral posterior hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, retrosplenial, posterior parietal. Social relational > spatial relational: dorsomedial/ventromedial prefrontal, orbitofrontal, anterior insula, superior temporal sulcus, temporoparietal junction, temporal poles — **no medial temporal activation at all**, not even against the vowel-counting baseline at p < 0.01 uncorrected |
| **Interaction** | Domain × task interaction significant in **exactly one region**, right posterior hippocampus (30, −36, −3; z = 4.28). Parahippocampal/retrosplenial/parietal came out on the spatial *main effect* (they prefer spatial content regardless of task); the hippocampus needed spatial **and** relational together |
| **Main effect of task** | Relational > non-relational engaged a distributed working-memory/attention network with **no hippocampal voxels** — so relational processing per se does not drive it |

The graph was matched too: both domains are node-and-edge networks the subject has navigated for years (mean 16.6 years' London residence; mean social-network clustering coefficient 0.66). So this is not "space is harder" or "space is a graph and social is not" — it is a domain preference surviving after graph structure, difficulty and search depth are equated. Read strictly, the finding is that **an amodal relational engine is not what the human hippocampus is**, at least for over-learned frameworks (Kumaran & Maguire 2005). Held against the rows above, the wiki now carries a positive social-space result (Tavares et al. 2015) and a social-space null from a better-controlled design ([[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T45).

**The strongest counterweight to the null is non-spatial, discrete and *newly learned*.** Garvert et al. 2017 ran a graph the subjects had known for one day, in a domain with no continuum and no task use, and got a bilateral entorhinal distance code plus a behavioural signature — so a non-spatial relational map is recoverable from the human hippocampal formation when the structure is new. This is exactly reconciliation (i) of [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T45: the two studies differ in *age of structure* as much as in domain.

### Extending a map without rebuilding it: assimilation

The wiki's map operations have been discovery, retrieval (G37), anchoring (G39) and search. Qu et al. 2026 isolate a fifth that none of them covers: **insert new nodes into a map already in use, and keep the frame**.

| Step | What was done | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Local learning** | After the main inference task, four new objects are placed at the *centre* of the 5×5 map; subjects learn only their rank differences to immediate neighbours, and are told existing relations are unchanged | The new nodes arrive with local edges only — no global coordinates |
| **Non-local inference** | Subjects must infer relations between the new objects and *distant* existing ones, across both dimensions | Accuracy rises with age (`r` = 0.56) and the age effect **survives controlling for performance on the original map** (`r` = 0.16) — so assimilation is a separable ability, not a rescaling of map quality |
| **The frame is reused, not re-fit** | The entorhinal grid angle `φ` estimated on the *original* map is used to test trajectories on the *new* map; a conjunction analysis finds overlapping grid-like coding in right entorhinal cortex across both | Map-alignment strength predicts non-local inference accuracy (`r` = 0.21) — and is **uncorrelated with age** (`r` = 0.01) |
| **A second, age-dependent mechanism sits on top** | The medial-prefrontal distance code in the new map correlates with age (`r` = 0.21) and with non-local inference (`r` = 0.16) after controlling for the original map, and mediates the age effect (`β_ab` = 0.02) — the grid code does not | Two dissociable contributions: a frame that must be *held constant*, and a metric readout that must be *rebuilt* onto it |

**Why this matters architecturally.** It is the cleanest available statement of what a fast store should and should not touch when new structure arrives: the structural code `g` is the thing that must **not** move (its constancy is what makes the new items reachable from far away without traversal), while the content-to-position binding and the distance readout are what get written. That is [[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]'s rebind-don't-retrain claim tested on a *partial* update to a map that is otherwise unchanged, rather than on a whole new world — a case no model in the wiki is evaluated on ([[wiki/concepts/continual-learning.md]], G14).

**(brainstorm) It also makes G39 falsifiable in the cheapest possible way.** Anchoring says a retrieved frame must be registered to the present situation. Here the registration is *inherited*: new nodes take their coordinates from the frame the old nodes already sit in, and the measured quantity — how well the new trajectories align to the old `φ` — is a per-subject scalar that predicts behaviour. A model with a persistent `g` can be scored the same way: freeze the structural code, insert nodes with local edges only, and regress non-local query accuracy on the angular agreement between the new nodes' inferred displacements and the pre-existing basis. Failure modes are distinguishable — a model that re-fits its frame will show high accuracy with low alignment, one that cannot assimilate will show the reverse.

**The dissociation between the two mechanisms is the load-bearing part** and it is exactly the part that is only correlational: alignment predicts assimilation but not age, prefrontal distance coding predicts both, and the design is cross-sectional, so "the frame stops developing before the readout does" is a hypothesis the data are consistent with rather than one they establish.

### The same objects carry two maps at once, and the brain does not merge them

Zheng et al. 2024 re-ran the Garvert et al. 2017 fMRI (n = 23) with **three** parametric adaptation regressors per trial instead of one — link distance on the newly learned graph, **semantic** distance between the same objects, and a **residual** distance — entered together, unorthogonalised. The objects were embedded in two relational structures simultaneously: a transition graph one day old and acquired implicitly, and a taxonomy acquired over a lifetime and fully reportable.

| Element | Detail | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **The second structure was measured, not assumed** | Semantic similarity from a triplet odd-one-out task (128 raters, 31 objects), then linearly regressed onto an independent rating of *matched photographs of the same objects* from THINGS (5301 raters). The explained part is the object relation that survives a change of depiction — semantics; the residual is the rest, including perceptual similarity. Multidimensional scaling on the semantic matrix recovers animate/inanimate and natural/man-made clusters | Two graphs over one node set, made near-orthogonal by *measurement* rather than by design: semantic and link distance are uncorrelated (Spearman `ρ` = 0.03 ± 0.12, `p` = 0.31), so neither effect can be a re-description of the other |
| **Both are in the hippocampal formation** | Link distance: bilateral **entorhinal** (peak `t₂₂` = 4.44, `p` = 0.042 small-volume-corrected). Semantic distance: bilateral **hippocampus** (`t₂₂` = 4.69, `p` = 0.028). Residual distance: **nothing**, anywhere (all `p` > 0.99) | Lifetime-old taxonomic structure gets the same map-like treatment as a one-day-old transition structure — the geometric coding principle does not care about the timescale of acquisition. The residual null is the control that makes the semantic effect a *relational* rather than a stimulus-similarity effect |
| **They do not overlap** | Non-overlapping clusters; the semantic effect extracted from the entorhinal (link-defined) ROI is indistinguishable from zero (`t₂₂` = −0.25), and the link effect from the hippocampal (semantic-defined) ROI likewise (`t₂₂` = 0.54). Along the long axis the semantic peak sits **posterior** to the link peak in both hemispheres, cleanly separated on the right | The headline: **no conjunctive map**. A single embedding in which distance mixes both structures is ruled out at the level the measurement reaches; what exists is two parallel maps over the same items |
| **The semantic map is hippocampus-only *here*** | No effect in left anterior temporal lobe or left angular gyrus, the two standard cortical semantic regions (all `p` > 0.73) | Not evidence that cortex lacks the code: the cover task was "was there a grey patch on the previous object", so semantics was task-irrelevant and unattended. The right reading is that the hippocampal semantic map is built **without task demand**, the same incidental profile as the graph map |

**What a builder takes from it.** The wiki's default has been one map per agent, selected by [[wiki/concepts/contextual-inference.md]]. This says a single set of items can be simultaneously embedded in several relational structures held in **separate address space**, with a *selection* step deciding which one a query reads — Zheng et al.'s own argument for the design is that relevance of a stimulus dimension shifts faster than a map can be rebuilt, so keeping the dimensions apart is what makes flexible re-weighting cheap. That is gap G40 answered in one measured case, and answered *against* entanglement: two structures over the same nodes, acquired in different modes and at different timescales, are kept factored even though nothing in the task ever required either.

**Three reasons to hold it loosely.** (i) The design confounds everything that could drive the split — recency of learning, implicit vs explicit access, associative/temporal vs taxonomic relation type, and stage of consolidation all differ between the two structures, so the source cannot say *which* feature the anatomy is sorting on. (ii) A posterior→anterior **gradient** and **two discrete clusters** are not separable given fMRI spatial autocorrelation; the authors flag this and it changes the architectural reading (a gradient says one map read at several resolutions, clusters say two modules). (iii) `p` = 0.028 and 0.042 after small-volume correction on n = 23, in a re-analysis of the dataset that supplied the first effect.

**(brainstorm) The gradient reading has a cheap machine test.** Anterior hippocampus carries coarser spatial codes than posterior ([[wiki/concepts/pattern-separation-completion.md]]'s `α` gradient, G38), and here the *newer, coarser* structure is anterior while the *older, finer* one is posterior. If that is one principle rather than two coincidences, then a store running the allocate-vs-reuse decision at several resolutions in parallel would sort structures along its own resolution axis with no separate routing rule — placement falls out of granularity, and G12's missing routing policy becomes a by-product of G38's missing controller rather than a second mechanism.

---

**(brainstorm) The map-vs-memory schedule predicts the null, but only half of it.** Section "The model side" below holds that the hippocampus builds a relational map while structure is *novel* and reverts to being an index once cortex has learned that world's structure. Tavares et al. 2015 had subjects learn a social space inside the experiment; Kumaran & Maguire used friendships years old. That is the schedule, and it would dissolve the domain reading entirely — except the spatial task was equally over-familiar and still drove the hippocampus. To survive, the schedule would need the consolidation rate to be domain-dependent (social structure offloads to the social-cognition network, spatial structure does not fully offload). Testable: run this exact paradigm on a *newly learned* social network and a *newly learned* city; the schedule predicts hippocampus in both, the domain account predicts spatial only. **Half of that test is now on the record and the schedule wins it**: Park et al. 2021 built a social hierarchy inside the experiment over four days and recovered hippocampal *and* entorhinal distance coding plus a grid-like code, in the same subjects whose mentalising network (temporoparietal junction, superior temporal sulcus, dorsal medial prefrontal cortex) was also engaged — so the social-cognition network and the hippocampal formation are not alternatives, they share a grid orientation. What remains untested is the other arm, a newly learned city.

**And the anchoring element does not transfer.** There are no reports of cells coding the "boundary" of a concept or a social milieu, and the review states it is not clear what landmark, boundary or local geometry even mean in a non-spatial domain. So the two elements a machine can copy today (a metric-ish code, a search over it) are the two that already have machine analogues, and the one that is genuinely missing is also the one with no biological template outside space. This is gap G39's real content.

## Open problems

- **The anchoring asymmetry is unexplained, not just unfilled.** Every non-spatial domain here supplies a code and none supplies an anchor. Two readings are live and nothing separates them: either anchoring is genuinely spatial machinery that the general map borrows only when a physical frame is available, or it exists off-plane and no experiment has been designed that could see it (`G39`).
- **Almost all of it is BOLD.** Every row except the four single-unit entries is fMRI, and hexadirectional modulation is not a measurement of a grid population — the standard detection procedure is threshold-arbitrary and its autocorrelation form produces false positives on non-grid data. The cellular entries (mouse ventral CA1 contingency cells, mouse dorsal CA1 evidence fields, human latent-context populations, monkey entorhinal gaze grids) are what the claim actually rests on, and there are four of them.
- **The only matched control returns a null.** Kumaran & Maguire 2005 hold relational demand, difficulty, reaction time and graph structure fixed and swap only the domain, and the hippocampus drops out — a domain preference surviving full relational matching. Every other row compares a non-spatial task against a low-level baseline. Nothing in the wiki reconciles this with Garvert et al. 2017's positive result on a one-day-old discrete graph, and the obvious candidate difference (**newly learned** versus known for sixteen years) is untested as such.
- **Nothing says what "distance" is in a domain with no continuum.** The discrete-graph rows recover a metric by multidimensional scaling *after the fact*; no source specifies what the learner was minimising such that a metric appeared, and a graph admits many.
- **The frame's origin is selected by instruction.** In the social-hierarchy study the grid code appears only for vectors anchored at the perspective character the subjects were *told* to adopt, and not for the pair actually being compared. What sets that origin in an agent nobody instructs is unaddressed, and it is the same unowned choice as `G60`'s unchosen query.
- **Two maps over the same objects do not merge, and nothing says when they should.** Link distance and semantic distance over identical stimuli load on separate regressors entered together. A builder gets no rule for when a newly learned structure should overwrite, coexist with, or be composed against the one already there.
