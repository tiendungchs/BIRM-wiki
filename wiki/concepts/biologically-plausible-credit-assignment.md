# Biologically Plausible Credit Assignment

**How can a deep, multi-layer network assign credit for a global error to individual synapses using only signals locally available at each synapse?**

Backpropagation solves credit assignment and is the reason deep representation learning works. If no biological realization exists, the algorithmic level was never substrate-neutral and the transfer argument of [[wiki/concepts/neuroscience-ai-transfer.md]] has a hole in it. If one does, the wiki's slow-**W** meta-graph learner is implementable in neural hardware.

---

## The objections

| # | Objection | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weight transport** | Backpropagation requires the backward pass to use the *transpose of the forward weights* — perfectly symmetric feedforward/feedback connectivity, which mammalian brains do not show |
| 2 | **Non-locality** | A weight update needs error signals generated by units many layers downstream; biological synaptic plasticity depends primarily on locally available quantities (pre- and post-synaptic activity) |
| 3 | **Unrealistic neurons** | Artificial units emit a continuous rate and sum their inputs linearly; real neurons emit spikes, and the derivative term `f'(·)` of Equation 1.5 has no obvious definition on a discrete spike train (Whittington & Bogacz 2019) |

**The longer list.** Bengio et al. 2015 enumerate *six*; taken together with the trio above the count is seven, and the extra four are not covered by objections 1–3:

| # | Extra objection | Why it bites |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | The backward computation is **purely linear**, whereas biological neurons interleave linear and non-linear operations | A feedback path built from real neurons cannot be a linear operator without extra machinery |
| 5 | The feedback path would need **precise knowledge of the derivative of each forward non-linearity at its operating point** — for neurons that are not all identical | Even granting symmetric weights, matching `f'(·)` per cell per instant is a second transport problem, on the derivatives |
| 6 | **Precise clocking** — feedforward and backward phases must alternate, since the latter consumes the former's results | Requires a global controller that sequences the network; nothing local supplies it |
| 7 | **Where do the output targets come from?** | Supervised framing presupposes a teacher; the unsupervised/generative framings below are the attempt to remove it |

Objection 6 is the one Bengio et al. 2015 explicitly **fail** to address (they claim all the others), and it is the same phase-control signal that the temporal-error class below requires — so across two independent sources the residue of implausibility lands on the same thing: *who tells the network which phase it is in*.

All three of the original objections are constraints on *implementation*, and 1–2 have been substantially weakened (Hassabis et al. 2017). Objection 2 is the historically decisive one — Crick 1989 and Grossberg 1987 are why the field treated backpropagation as off the table for 30 years — and it is the one the models below are built to answer: a network that **represents its own errors locally** turns Equation 1.3 (`ΔW_l ∝ δ_{l+1} x_l^T`) into a product of a presynaptic activity and a postsynaptic quantity, which is a Hebbian rule by definition. Objection 3 is addressed on [[wiki/entities/spiking-neural-networks.md]].

## Solution families

| Family | Mechanism | Resolves | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feedback alignment (FA)** | Use **fixed random** backward weights `B` in place of `(w^out)ᵀ`; forward weights adapt until the random backward projections carry useful teaching signals — alignment is learned, not assumed. Update: `Δw^in = −η·x·z`, `z` the error propagated through `B` | 1 | Demonstrated on MNIST and CIFAR; **suboptimal on ImageNet-scale data** (Schmidgall et al. 2023) |
| **Direct feedback alignment (DFA)** | Connect the output-layer error *directly* to every hidden layer, shortening the weight-transport chain further | 1 | Same family, fewer assumptions |
| **Sign-symmetry (SS)** | Feedback weights are random in magnitude but **share signs** with the forward weights | 1 (partially — sign information is still transported) | Comparable to backpropagation *even on large-scale datasets* — the strongest result in the family |
| **Eligibility propagation (e-prop)** | Extends FA to spiking networks. Maintain a forward-computable eligibility trace `e_ji(t) = dz_j(t)/dW_ji` (this synapse's total contribution to the neuron's output over all past inputs), then multiply by an error estimate `L_j(t) = dE(t)/dz_j(t)` obtained from output error via symmetric or fixed random feedback | 1, 2 | Purely forward — no backward pass. **Blind to the future:** needs a real-time error at each step, so it cannot learn from delayed errors beyond individual-neuron timescales, unlike REINFORCE / node perturbation ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]) |
| **Cell-type-specific local neuromodulation** | Neurons broadcast their contribution to the learning outcome to *nearby* neurons via neuron-type-specific modulation; a computational model combines dopamine-like temporal-difference signalling with neuropeptide-like local modulation | 1, 2 | Built on e-prop; **improves over both e-prop and feedback alignment**. Proposes neuron-type diversity as a missing piece of the credit-assignment puzzle |
| **Predictive-coding networks** | Hierarchy minimizing prediction error locally at each level; the local updates **approximate backpropagation** | 1, 2 | Equivalence results; ties credit assignment to a normative theory of cortex |
| **Energy-based nets** (continuous Hopfield, equilibrium propagation) | Settle to an energy minimum, nudge the output, and use the change in local activity between phases as the gradient | 1, 2 | Local updates approximating backpropagation — and, at finite nudging strength, *exact* gradient descent on a free-energy difference rather than an approximation of anything (Litman 2025, below) |
| **Target propagation (targetprop)** | Replace the backward gradient with a **target** for each layer, computed by the *inverse* mapping: layer `k` is told what activity would have satisfied layer `k−1`, and moves toward it. Backprop through one layer is estimated by `∂log p(x\|h)/∂h ≈ (f(x) − f(g(h)))/σ_h²` — encoder `f`, decoder `g`, ordinary forward passes only, no derivatives computed anywhere (Bengio et al. 2015; Lee et al. 2014) | 1, 2, 4, 5 | MNIST generative model; the update provably climbs `log p(x,h)`. Removes the derivative-transport objection outright, since no `f'(·)` is ever evaluated |
| **Local rules producing invariance** | Hebbian-family rules that yield high-level invariances characteristic of biological systems — e.g. mirror-symmetric tuning to physically symmetric stimuli such as faces | 2 | Shows structure attributed to end-to-end optimization is partly reachable locally |

Rows 1–5 are what Schmidgall et al. 2023 call **backpropagation-derived local learning**: they compute an explicit approximation of the backpropagation gradient using locally available quantities, and they are the class the generalization result below is measured on. Rows 6–7 also land near backpropagation but arrive from a *local objective* (prediction error, energy) rather than by approximating the global one; rows 8–9 do not approximate it at all — targetprop is the interesting case, since it targets the same *fixed point* as backpropagation while computing nothing resembling a gradient. The rules that were never trying to approximate a gradient are on [[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]].

## Where the error lives: temporal vs. explicit (Whittington & Bogacz 2019)

The families above are sorted by *what they do about weight transport*. The sharper cut for a builder is **how the network represents `δ` at all**, and it has exactly two answers.

| Class | Error is | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporal-error** | A **difference in the same neurons' activity across time** — between a phase without the target and a phase with it, or as the rate of change `ẋ` | Simple architecture (no extra cells, unconstrained connectivity), but the network must be *told* which phase it is in |
| **Explicit-error** | The activity of **dedicated units** (predictive coding error nodes) or of a **dedicated compartment** (apical dendrites of pyramidal cells) | Runs autonomously with one rule and no phase signal, paid for with constrained, partly one-to-one connectivity |

Four concrete models, all reaching backpropagation-level accuracy on MNIST with purely local updates:

| | Contrastive learning (O'Reilly 1996) | Continuous update (Bengio et al. 2015, 2017) | Predictive coding (Whittington & Bogacz 2017) | Dendritic error (Sacramento et al. 2018) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class | Temporal | Temporal | Explicit | Explicit |
| Error encoded in | Activity difference between two phases | Rate of change of postsynaptic activity | Activity of specialised error neurons | Apical dendrite of the pyramidal neuron |
| Update | Anti-Hebbian in the prediction phase, Hebbian once the target is clamped: `ΔW ∝ x_L\|_t x_{L−1}^T − x_L\|_{¬t} x_{L−1}^T` | `ΔW ∝ ẋ_post · x_pre` — output is dragged from prediction toward target, so `ẋ ∝ δ` | Hebbian on error × value node, at equilibrium | Hebbian, with `δ_l` read off the apical compartment |
| Control signal | **Required** (which phase) | **Required** (learn vs. predict) | Not required | Not required |
| Connectivity | Unconstrained | Unconstrained | Constrained (one-to-one error↔value) | Constrained (one-to-one pyramidal→interneuron) |
| Forward propagation delay | `L−1` synapses | `L−1` | **`2L−1`** — every hop routes through an error node | `L−1` |
| Pre-training | No | No | No | **Yes** — interneurons must first learn to predict the feedback they cancel |
| MNIST error (%) | ~2–3 | — | **~1.7** | ~1.96 |

Three things this buys the wiki:

- **The "two phases" complaint is not generic — it is a property of the temporal class only.** [[wiki/entities/boltzmann-machine.md]] and [[wiki/entities/fcann.md]] frame single-phase vs. two-phase as the biological weak point of contrastive rules. Predictive coding *already is* the single-phase answer, and for a stated reason (below), not by luck.
- **A cost axis the wiki was not tracking: inference latency.** Predictive coding pays `2L−1` synaptic delays per forward pass *forever*, in exchange for training fast; the dendritic model runs at `L−1` but must pre-train its interneurons. Neither cost shows up in an accuracy table.
- **The two explicit models are the same equations.** Substituting the error-node definition `ε_l = x_l − W_{l−1}x_{l−1}` into the value-node dynamics yields the dendritic model's pyramidal dynamics directly: decay + feedforward + feedback + a *negative* within-layer recurrent term, which — since pyramidal cells are excitatory — must be supplied by interneurons. **Predictive coding's error node and the pyramidal apical dendrite are one mechanism at two levels of description**; the interneuron is what the wiring costs when you refuse to spend a separate cell on the error.

### Equilibrium propagation is the frame that contains all four

Every model here has dynamics that minimise a scalar energy, and every learning rule here is the gradient of that same energy w.r.t. the weights (Scellier & Bengio 2017):

| Model class | Energy minimised | Phases needed |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal-error (recurrent excitatory nets) | **Hopfield energy** — dissimilarity in activity between strongly connected nodes | Two: raise energy at the free equilibrium, lower it at the clamped one |
| Explicit-error (predictive coding, dendritic) | **Free energy** — whose negative lower-bounds `log p(target \| input)` | **One** |

The reason the explicit models need one phase is precise and worth carrying: the free-energy landscape's **global** minimum is already reached in the first (unclamped) phase, so the prescribed anti-Hebbian update is exactly zero and only the clamped phase does work. This is the mechanism behind the wiki's standing observation that the free-energy rule is "the only known local, incremental, single-phase rule" ([[wiki/entities/fcann.md]]) — it is not a different rule, it is the same two-phase rule with one term identically zero.

The framework is also a *recipe*, which is its real value here: **for any network whose dynamics minimise a definable energy, it outputs the local plasticity rule required for efficient learning.** Read against [[wiki/concepts/three-component-framework.md]], that collapses two slots into one — pick an architecture and an energy, and the learning rule is derived rather than designed.

Whittington & Bogacz further note that the best-performing biologically plausible networks are the ones minimising free energy rather than Hopfield energy, and attribute it to free energy's probabilistic interpretation (it bounds the log-likelihood of the target). Weak evidence — one benchmark, non-comparable simulations — but it points the same way as [[wiki/concepts/divergence-objectives.md]].

**The infinitesimal-nudge assumption is gone, and it was the wrong way round (Litman 2025).** The frame above is usually stated with a caveat — the two-phase update equals the gradient only as the nudge `β → 0`, and the finite `β` needed in practice introduces *bias* relative to backpropagation. Modelling the state as a Gibbs distribution rather than a minimum removes both the caveat and the bias:

| Claim the wiki carried | Replacement |
|---|---|
| The contrastive update approximates a gradient, exactly only at `β → 0` | It is the **exact** gradient of `J(θ) = A(θ,1) − A(θ,0)`, the Helmholtz free-energy difference between nudged and free phases, at **any** finite `β`, without convexity, unique minima or symmetric weights |
| Finite nudging introduces bias to be minimised | The extra term is `T·KL(ρ_1‖ρ_0)`: `J(θ) = E_{ρ_1}[ℓ] + T·KL(ρ_1‖ρ_0)`. A regulariser that drags the *free* dynamics toward the nudged phase — supervision distilled into spontaneous behaviour, not an error |
| Small `β` is the safe regime | Small `β` is the **unusable** regime. Fashion-MNIST, one `tanh` hidden layer: `β = 0.01` stalls at 20–30% (chance), `β = 1.0` and a path-integral variant both reach ~80%, tracking backpropagation. The signal-to-noise ratio of `Δs = s_β − s_0` is below the sampling-noise floor for `β ≲ 10⁻²`, and update–gradient cosine similarity rises monotonically to ~0.5 at `β = 1` |

This lands directly on the bias/variance framing below. The contrastive Hebbian rule's position was listed as *unknown*; it is now **zero-bias for its own objective**, with all the error in the sampler's variance — but the objective is `J`, not `L_sup`. The rule is therefore not a member of the "backpropagation-derived local rule" family at all, and the generalization deficit measured on that family does not automatically transfer to it. See [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T69.

**(brainstorm)** If the finite-nudge story is right, the wiki's standing complaint against two-phase rules narrows to *one* objection. Weight transport: gone. Gradient bias: gone. Convexity and equilibrium uniqueness: gone. What remains is objection 6 — the phase control signal — plus the sampling cost of estimating expectations under `ρ_β`. That is the same residue every temporal-error model on this page leaves, so the whole class now fails or succeeds on a single question: *who tells the network which phase it is in, and how expensive is reaching equilibrium in each*.

### STDP is the bridge, and its *shape* selects the rule

The correspondence is specific, and it makes the plasticity kernel a design parameter rather than a biological detail:

| Learning rule | Requires plasticity that depends on | Matching STDP form |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous update (`ΔW ∝ ẋ_post · x_pre`) | The **gradient** of postsynaptic activity around a presynaptic spike | **Classic asymmetric STDP** (Bi & Poo 1998): pre-before-post potentiates, post-before-pre depresses. If the postsynaptic rate is *rising*, more post spikes fall in the potentiating window than the depressing one, so `ΔW > 0` automatically |
| Predictive coding (`ΔW ∝ ε_post · x_pre` at equilibrium) | The **level** of postsynaptic activity relative to a baseline `x₀` | **Symmetric STDP** (Vogels et al. 2011): near-coincident spikes potentiate regardless of order, plus a small depression per presynaptic spike — which subtracts the baseline |

So the same substrate implements a *derivative* rule or a *level* rule depending only on whether its window is asymmetric or symmetric. See [[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]], where the same symmetric/asymmetric dial selects successor vs. reversible transition structure (Keck et al. 2025) — two independent results in which STDP kernel symmetry is the parameter that changes *what is computed*, not how fast.

### Do not pick one — the models are motifs

The review's own conclusion, and the most useful stance for a builder: cortical circuitry is richer than any of these architectures, so treat them as **learning motifs that co-exist in one network** rather than as competing hypotheses.

- Temporal-error motifs suit primary sensory areas predicting the next input, where **the target always arrives** so no control signal is needed (Singer et al. 2018).
- Explicit-error motifs suit tasks where target timing is uncertain.
- Predictive coding is fast to train and slow to run; dendritic error is slow to train and fast to run. **(brainstorm)** A network could beat the trade-off by running predictive-coding motifs early and letting dendritic motifs take over as the interneurons converge — a learned handoff from a slow-inference/fast-learning circuit to a fast-inference/slow-learning one. This is a curriculum over *mechanisms* rather than over data, and the wiki has no other instance of one.
- Predictive coding may also cover circuits with no pyramidal cells at all — cerebellum (Friston & Herreros 2016) and basal ganglia, where dopaminergic reward prediction error is an explicit error node in the literal sense (Schultz et al. 1997).

**Evidence that errors are explicit at all:** V1 responses increase in the brief intervals where visual input mismatches the expectation generated by self-motion (Attinger et al. 2017), and expectation violation raises the fMRI signal (Summerfield et al. 2008). Whether *separate populations* carry errors and values is unsettled — Kok & de Lange 2015 find "no direct unequivocal evidence", with the suggestive datum that inferotemporal inhibitory neurons respond most to novel stimuli while excitatory neurons respond most to preferred familiar ones.

## Inference *is* the error signal (Bengio et al. 2015)

The families above all keep backpropagation's shape — a global objective whose derivative must reach every synapse somehow. This route drops the shape. Its claim: **the neural dynamics that settle a network into a consistent state are already the credit-assignment computation**, and plasticity only has to read them off locally.

Three steps, each independently reusable:

**1. STDP is stochastic gradient descent, if the dynamics are doing inference.** Posit the update

`ΔW_ij ∝ V̇_j · S_i`

(`S_i` presynaptic spike, `V_j` postsynaptic voltage). The observed STDP curve *falls out of this* by a threshold-crossing argument rather than being assumed: if `V̇_j > 0` the postsynaptic cell reaches threshold after the presynaptic spike, and sooner for larger `V̇_j` — so positive `ΔT` gives positive `ΔW`, decaying with `ΔT`; if `V̇_j < 0` the cell was above threshold *before* the presynaptic spike, giving negative `ΔW` at negative `ΔT`, shrinking in magnitude as `ΔT` grows. Simulation reproduces the Bi & Poo 1998 window. The consequence is conditional and is the whole argument: **if the change `ΔV_j` improves some objective `J`, then STDP is approximate SGD on `J`.** Xie & Seung 2000 had already shown STDP ≡ differential anti-Hebbian plasticity; what is added is a machine-learning `J`.

**2. `J` is a variational bound, so the dynamics are variational E-steps.** With observed `x` and latent `h` (the voltages), take the regularized variational MAP-EM criterion

`J = log p(x|h) + log p(h) + α log q(h|x)`

`h` is a **free variable**, initialized from the feedforward `q(h|x)` and then iteratively updated to increase `J`; parameters of both `p` and `q` follow after. Splitting `h` into layers with `q` running up and `p` running down makes every `h`-update depend only on layers `k−1` and `k+1` — **local by construction**, no weight symmetry required, with all layers still coupled through the iteration. Add noise to the `h` update and the E-step is MCMC rather than MAP. Notably a rejection step is ruled out as implausible on two independent grounds: returning to a previous state, and evaluating a global joint likelihood.

**3. Targetprop removes the last derivative.** Step 2 still needs `∂log p(h^{(k−1)}|h^{(k)})/∂h^{(k)}` — backprop through one layer. Using the denoising-autoencoder score theorem (Alain & Bengio 2013, `r(x) − x ≈ σ² ∂log p(x)/∂x`) twice, once on the `p(h)` autoencoder and once on a **joint autoencoder over `(x,h)`**, gives estimators built only from forward passes: `(f(g(h)) − h)/σ_h²` for `∂log p(h)/∂h`, and `(f(x) − f(g(h)))/σ_h²` for the input's pressure on `h`. Geometrically: the ideal `h̃` satisfies `g(h̃) = x`; since `f` and `g` are approximate inverses, `f∘g` moves by the same small amount near `x`, so the encoder difference measures the needed move in `h`-space.

| What it buys | Detail |
|---|---|
| Objections addressed | All of 1–7 above **except 6 (clocking)** — no symmetric weights, no `f'(·)`, no linear backward path, spikes are the corruption noise, targets are the data |
| Spikes become useful | The spike train is a *quantization* of `V`, i.e. injected noise — so every encoder/decoder pair is automatically a **denoising** autoencoder, hence contractive. Noise level is set by integration time or ensemble size |
| A different generative procedure | Sampling the directed model `p(x\|h)p(h)` ancestrally scores Parzen LL = 126; running the joint denoising-autoencoder Markov chain (alternate `q(h\|x)`, `p(x\|h)`) scores **236** — comparable to or better than contractive autoencoders (121), GSNs (214) and GANs (225) of the day, on the same trained weights. Iterating encode/decode drags `h` toward the empirical `q*(h)` that the decoder was actually trained on |
| Inference and generation are the same circuit | Generation = inference with `x` unclamped. Clamping *part* of `x` gives in-painting for free, with `f(x^v, g^m(h)) − f(g(h))` substituted for the visible-only pressure |
| Empirical check on the mechanism | 20 targetprop iterations monotonically increase `log p(x,h)` on held-out MNIST — the update is verified to climb the objective it claims to, which is the check most local-rule papers omit |

**Why this matters beyond plausibility.** It relocates credit assignment from the weight update to the **activity relaxation**: bottom-up and top-down pressures are reconciled by iterating on `h`, and the synapse then only has to be Hebbian in `(V̇_j, S_i)`. That is the same architectural move predictive coding makes, but derived from a likelihood bound instead of a prediction-error hierarchy, and it inherits the same cost — depth is paid in iterations ([[wiki/concepts/predictive-coding-free-energy.md]], and the "iteration count does not scale" open problem below).

**Where it sits relative to the two-phase problem.** This scheme has no wake/sleep switch, but it does need to know when `x` is clamped, which is objection 6 wearing different clothes. The paper's own residue list is honest about it: operations are backprop-free but "may still require some kinds of synchronizations (or control mechanism)".

**(brainstorm)** The load-bearing assumption is `f ≈ g⁻¹` — targetprop's estimator is a first-order statement that the encoder inverts the decoder locally. That is exactly the condition an autoencoder trained on the data manifold satisfies *on the manifold and nowhere else*, which predicts a specific failure: targetprop should degrade sharply on out-of-distribution inputs, precisely where a reasoning model needs credit assignment most. No result here tests that, and it is a cheap experiment — measure `‖f(g(h)) − h‖` as a function of distance from the training manifold and check whether it tracks the gap to the true gradient.

## The generalization deficit of backpropagation-derived local rules

The yardstick: **flat minima generalize better** — for a perturbation `ε` in weight space, performance degrades more sharply around *narrow* minima, so a rule that finds flatter minima generalizes better.

Measured against backpropagation through time, backpropagation-derived local rules show **worse and more variable generalization**, and the deficit **does not close by scaling the step size**, because the gradient approximation is poorly aligned with the true gradient (Schmidgall et al. 2023). The review draws the uncomfortable conclusion itself: it is unsurprising that a *local approximation* of an optimization process generalizes worse than the complete process, which raises the question of whether backpropagation-derived local rules are worth pursuing at all given that they are *fundamentally* sub-par.

**Consequence for this wiki.** "Biologically plausible" and "approximates backpropagation" are pulling in opposite directions. A rule earns its place either by being a better optimizer or by doing something backpropagation cannot (learning during deployment, from delayed sparse reward, on a spiking substrate) — not by being a cheaper version of the same computation. See [[wiki/empirical-tensions.md]] T7.

**The deficit has two possible sources, and the wiki has been conflating them.** Treat every rule here as an *estimator* of `∇_W F` and score it on bias and variance separately (Richards et al. 2019, Fig. 2): node/weight perturbation — which reinforces random weight changes through reward — is **low-bias, high-variance**; feedback alignment, which pushes error through random feedback weights, is **low-variance, high-bias**; backpropagation is the near-zero reference for both. The bias/variance positions of contrastive Hebbian learning, predictive coding, dendritic-error learning, regression-discontinuity design and attention-gated reinforcement learning are *unknown* — the same source places them by conjecture only. **Contrastive Hebbian learning is now placed** (Litman 2025): unbiased with respect to `∇J`, all error in sampling variance, and therefore repairable by averaging — but the bias with respect to `∇L_sup` is not zero, it is a *deliberate* `T·KL(ρ_1‖ρ_0)` regulariser. The right way to score a local rule is thus against the objective it actually descends, and only then to ask whether that objective is the one wanted. This matters because the repairs differ: a high-variance rule is fixed by averaging (more samples, eligibility traces, smaller steps), a high-biased one is not fixable by any amount of data. The Schmidgall et al. 2023 result that the deficit "does not close by scaling the step size" is exactly the signature of the **bias** branch, which is why it lands on the family that estimates the gradient by substituting a wrong backward operator. See [[wiki/concepts/three-component-framework.md]].

---

**Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP)** is the connective tissue: concrete correspondences have been drawn between updates in energy-based / predictive-coding networks and STDP, a Hebbian mechanism instantiated widely across the brain. Credit assignment does not require a new biological mechanism — it requires the known one to be *interpretable* as gradient-following.

## Why the question cuts both ways

The motivation is not only biological fidelity. Very deep networks (>20 layers) compound successive nonlinearities in ways that make backpropagation-based optimization itself difficult, so the search for biologically plausible alternatives doubles as a search for **better** learning algorithms — the same virtuous-circle logic that produced temporal-difference learning.

---

## Relevance to a reasoning model

- **Slow W must be trainable in the target substrate.** Any spiking or neuromorphic instantiation of the meta-graph learner ([[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]]) inherits this problem directly.
- **Locality constrains the architecture, not just the optimizer.** If credit must be local, the factorized code `p = f(g, x)` is an advantage: conjunctive binding is a local operation, and errors in fast **M** never need to propagate through slow **W**.
- **(brainstorm)** That predictive coding both resolves credit assignment *and* is a normative theory of cortical function is suspicious in a productive way: the brain's answer may not be "backpropagation implemented awkwardly" but a different objective whose gradient happens to coincide. Treat the equivalence results as a bridge to cross in both directions, not as a vindication of backpropagation.

## Open problems

- Do the approximations hold at scale, or only in small networks and shallow hierarchies? (Partly answered: feedback alignment does not reach ImageNet; sign-symmetry does — so **what sign-symmetry transports is apparently the load-bearing part**, and it is the part that is least biologically defensible.)
- How far does feedback alignment actually reach in deep and convolutional settings?
- Is the generalization deficit intrinsic to gradient *approximation*, or an artefact of the specific approximations tested? No result separates these.
- None of these families addresses credit assignment across *time* at behavioural timescales (seconds to days) — where a reasoning agent actually needs it.
- Does local credit assignment survive when the same synapses must also support fast one-shot binding?
- **All four models of the section above assume symmetric feedforward/feedback weights** and rely on their plasticity rules to *maintain* the symmetry. Whether they survive the asymmetry the cortex actually has is the review's own first outstanding question — so objection 1 is unresolved for exactly the models that resolve objection 2.
- **Does targetprop's inverse assumption survive depth?** The estimator `(f(x) − f(g(h)))/σ_h²` is exact only if encoder and decoder are mutual inverses and the moves are small; the demonstration is two hidden layers on MNIST, and no result characterises how the approximation error compounds with depth or off-manifold distance (Bengio et al. 2015).
- **Nothing says what fraction of the work the relaxation does.** In the variational-EM scheme, quality depends jointly on the number of inference steps `T` and on how good the amortized initializer `q(h|x)` is; the trade-off between the two is unmeasured, and it is the same knob as the wiki's fast-inference/slow-learning axis.
- **The iteration count does not scale.** Explicit- and temporal-error models both reach their answer by relaxing network dynamics to equilibrium, and the number of iterations required grows with network size. Depth is bought in wall-clock, which is why the demonstrations stop at MNIST (see also Bartunov et al. 2018).
- **Every model is stated for static input patterns.** No local scheme here handles sequences; the recurrent-hidden-state extension that backpropagation got is missing, and a reasoning agent's credit problem is temporal.
- **There is no evidence for the one-to-one connectivity** both explicit-error models require — error node ↔ value node, and higher pyramidal cell → the interneuron that must mimic it. This is the price paid for dropping the control signal, and it is currently unpaid.

- **[[wiki/concepts/continual-learning.md]]** — both concern *which* synapses change: credit assignment sets direction and magnitude, plasticity gating sets eligibility.
- **[[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]** — the complementary family: rules that never approximated a gradient, and therefore do not inherit the generalization deficit measured here; node perturbation reaches the delayed-reward case e-prop cannot.
- **[[wiki/entities/spiking-neural-networks.md]]** — the substrate where local credit assignment stops being optional, because backpropagation fails outright on the discrete spiking nonlinearity rather than merely lacking a biological story.
- **[[wiki/concepts/meta-optimized-plasticity.md]]** — the alternative to approximating backpropagation: instead of deriving a local rule from the gradient, search rule-space directly and let the outer loop decide what the local rule should compute.
- **[[wiki/concepts/predictive-coding-free-energy.md]]** — the local rule taken as the substrate rather than as an approximation target: the error `e^{S_i} = y^{S_i} − (W^{S_{i+1}})^T y^{S_{i+1}}` is computed inside a layer from quantities present there, so activity relaxation and weight update are the same descent with no weight transport (Butz 2016).
- **[[wiki/concepts/three-component-framework.md]]** — places every family on this page in the *learning-rule* slot and scores them on a second axis, bias vs. variance of the gradient estimate (perturbation high-variance, feedback alignment high-bias), which splits this page's generalization deficit into two failure modes with different repairs.
- **[[wiki/entities/spiking-tem.md]]** — where the biological-plausibility argument currently stops: neuron model, memory write and oscillatory gating are all biological, while the weights are still set by surrogate-gradient backpropagation through time, which the paper names as its outstanding problem and points at replay-based one-shot alternatives.
- **[[wiki/entities/sparse-distributed-memory.md]]** — a supervised local rule whose teacher is delivered by anatomy: the climbing fibre carries the target bit to every synapse of a single Purkinje cell's column, so the delta rule of CMAC needs no backward pass and no weight transport. The cost is that the target must exist as a wired-in signal per output unit, which bounds how many outputs can be taught this way.
- **[[wiki/entities/boltzmann-machine.md]]** — the canonical contrastive Hebbian rule this page lists with unknown bias/variance, written out: `Δw_ij ∝ ⟨s_i s_j⟩_data − ⟨s_i s_j⟩_free`, with no weight transport and no backward pass, bought with a global wake/sleep phase switch and an uncertified equilibrium assumption — the assumption Litman 2025 narrows to sampler mixing time alone.
- **[[wiki/concepts/divergence-objectives.md]]** — supplies the reading of what a two-phase rule's finite-nudge "bias" is: `T·KL(ρ_1‖ρ_0)`, a divergence between the network's supervised and unsupervised operating states, paid rather than fitted.
- **[[wiki/concepts/energy-based-models.md]]** — the machine-learning statement of the equilibrium-propagation frame: if the dynamics minimise a scalar `F`, the plasticity rule is `∂F/∂W` evaluated at two equilibria, so the learning rule stops being an independent design choice.
- **[[wiki/entities/fcann.md]]** — the empirical face of the one-phase/two-phase split derived here: the free-energy rule's second term is the network's own instantaneous prediction of its correlations, which is why it needs no sleep phase, and the connectome data test the orthogonality that follows.
- **[[wiki/concepts/amortized-inference.md]]** — the variational-EM route makes credit assignment *depend* on amortisation: `q(h|x)` is a learned initializer for an iterative relaxation rather than a final answer, and the training criterion contains a term (`α log q(h|x)`) whose only job is to make the compiled guess a good starting point for the expensive process (Bengio et al. 2015).
- **[[wiki/concepts/energy-based-models.md]]** — the same shape with a different scalar: variational EM relaxes `h` to maximise `log p(x,h) + α log q(h|x)` where equilibrium propagation relaxes to minimise `F`, and in both the plasticity rule is read off the relaxed state rather than transported into it.
- **[[wiki/concepts/dendritic-computation.md]]** — sharpens the target of any local rule: if the computational unit is a dendritic segment with its own threshold rather than the cell, credit must be delivered to a *branch*, which is what dendritic-error schemes assume and what makes the apical/basal split a computational commitment rather than an anatomical detail.
