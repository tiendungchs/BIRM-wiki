# Biologically Plausible Credit Assignment

**How can a deep, multi-layer network assign credit for a global error to individual synapses using only signals locally available at each synapse?**

Backpropagation solves credit assignment and is the reason deep representation learning works. If no biological realization exists, then the algorithmic level was never substrate-neutral and the transfer argument of [[wiki/concepts/neuroscience-ai-transfer.md]] has a hole in it. If one does, the wiki's slow-**W** meta-graph learner is implementable in neural (and neuromorphic) hardware.

---

## The two objections

| # | Objection | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weight transport** | Backprop requires the backward pass to use the *transpose of the forward weights* — perfectly symmetric feedforward/feedback connectivity. Cortex has feedback projections, but not weight-symmetric ones |
| 2 | **Non-locality** | A weight update needs the error signal generated by units many layers downstream. Biological synaptic plasticity depends primarily on locally available quantities — pre- and post-synaptic activity |

Both are constraints on *implementation*, and both have been substantially weakened.

---

## Solution families

| Family | Mechanism | Resolves | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feedback alignment** | Use **fixed random** backward weights. The forward weights adapt so that the random backward projections come to carry useful teaching signals — alignment is learned, not assumed | Objection 1 | Demonstrated: random fixed backward connections suffice for effective learning |
| **Predictive coding networks** | A hierarchy that minimizes prediction error locally at each level; the resulting local updates **approximate backprop** | Objections 1 and 2 | Explicit equivalence results; connects credit assignment to predictive coding as a unified account |
| **Energy-based / continuous Hopfield nets, equilibrium propagation** | Settle to an energy minimum, then nudge the output; the change in local activity between the two phases carries the gradient | Objections 1 and 2 | Local weight updates approximating backprop |
| **Target propagation** | Each layer learns to invert the next, so it receives a *target activity* rather than a gradient | Objection 2 | Weaker guarantees, no weight transport |
| **Local rules producing invariance** | Hebbian-family rules that yield high-level invariances characteristic of biological systems (e.g. mirror-symmetric tuning to physically symmetric stimuli such as faces) | Objection 2 | Shows some structure attributed to end-to-end optimization is reachable locally |

**Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP)** is the connective tissue: concrete correspondences have been drawn between the updates in energy-based/predictive-coding networks and STDP, a Hebbian mechanism found widely across the brain. Credit assignment does not need a new biological mechanism — it needs the known one to be *interpretable* as gradient-following.

---

## Why the question cuts both ways

The motivation is not purely biological fidelity. Very deep networks (>20 layers) accumulate successive nonlinearities that make backprop-based optimization itself difficult. The search for biologically plausible alternatives doubles as a search for **better** learning algorithms — the same virtuous circle logic that produced TD learning.

---

## Relevance to a reasoning model

- **Slow W must be trainable in the target substrate.** Any spiking or neuromorphic instantiation of the meta-graph learner ([[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]]) inherits this problem directly.
- **Locality constrains the architecture, not just the optimizer.** If credit must be local, the factorized code `p = f(g, x)` is an advantage: conjunctive binding is a local operation, and errors in the fast **M** level never need to propagate through the slow **W** level.
- **(brainstorm)** Predictive coding resolving credit assignment *and* being a normative theory of cortical function is suspicious in a productive way — it suggests the brain's answer is not "backprop implemented awkwardly" but a different objective whose gradient happens to coincide. The wiki should treat the equivalence results as a bridge to be crossed in both directions, not as a vindication of backprop.

---

## Open problems

- Do the approximations hold at scale, or only in small networks and shallow hierarchies?
- Feedback alignment degrades in deep and convolutional settings; how far does alignment actually reach?
- None of these families addresses credit assignment across *time* at behavioural timescales (seconds to days) — the eligibility-trace problem, which is where a reasoning agent actually needs it.
- Does local credit assignment survive when the network must also support fast, one-shot binding in the same synapses?
