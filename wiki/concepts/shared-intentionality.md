# Shared Intentionality

**The human-specific operation is not belief attribution but the earlier and simpler capacity to hold *the same* intentional content with another agent — one referent, two agents, each aware the other is attending to it — which imposes three constraints no wiki architecture carries: self–other equivalence (the same description covers my act and yours), perspectivity (one referent, many admissible descriptions, one chosen for the addressee), and derived normativity (a "correct use" that comes from collective practice rather than from the world).**

> **Provenance.** Tomasello & Rakoczy 2007, *Intellectica* 46–47:25–48, "What makes human cognition unique? From individual to shared to collective intentionality" (`raw/tomasello-2007-shared-collective-intentionality.md`). A position paper: the argument is the authors', every experiment cited is a primary study by the Leipzig group or others. The 4-year-transition mechanism is offered explicitly as a proposal ("our proposal for the moment"), and the authors concede "there simply is not enough empirical research for anyone to feel confident".

The page exists because the wiki's theory-of-mind machinery ([[wiki/entities/autotom.md]], [[wiki/entities/hbtom.md]]) models the *later* and, on this source's argument, *derived* capacity — recovering another agent's belief as a latent — while the operation the comparative record marks as human-specific arrives three years earlier, is not belief-shaped at all, and is the precondition for symbols, artefacts and cumulative culture.

## The two transitions, and why only one of them is an adaptation

| | **~1 year: intentional agents** | **~4 years: mental agents** |
|---|---|---|
| Content understood | Others act *toward goals* and *attend* selectively; means are separable from ends | Others hold beliefs that may be **false**, i.e. a perspective that can mismatch reality |
| Capability unlocked | Joint attention, imitative learning, linguistic symbols, pretense — "shared intentionality" | Institutional facts (money, marriage, government) — "collective intentionality" |
| Reference frame internalised | Mead's **significant other** — this partner, this activity, now | Mead's **generalized other** — the culture's perspective, applied in all situations |
| Cross-cultural variance in onset | **None known**; no environmental variable speeds or retards it | **Substantial**; family constellation and linguistic experience both move it |
| Selective deficit | Autism strikes here — the joint-attentional skills, not a later belief module | **No known deficit exclusive to this transition** |
| Status claimed | The biological adaptation | An **ontogenetic construction** with "no specific biological underpinnings of its own" |

**The architectural claim is the asymmetry in the last row.** One installed component plus ~3 years of a *specific kind of input stream* yields the second capacity; nothing is added to the genome between them. This is the wiki's cleanest biological instance of a capability that is a property of the **curriculum**, not of the architecture — the data-schedule gap `G32` with a worked example attached, and the complement of `G100`'s growth schedule.

## The three signatures that make the 1-year capacity "the real thing"

The paper's burden is to show the early competence is mental, not a behavioural precursor. Three properties, each with an experimental marker:

| Signature | Statement | Marker | Builder's reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(i) Sharedness** (self–other equivalence) | The same description covers the other's act and mine — "I do what you did" (Evans' Generality Constraint) | Role-reversal imitation; deictic terms (`I`/`you`, `here`/`there`) acquired with the reversal already in place | An **equivalence constraint tying the perception encoder to the action decoder**; nothing in the wiki imposes it (`G103`) |
| **(ii) Perspectivity** | One referent admits many simultaneously-available descriptions; the speaker *chooses* one for the addressee, and both know which were not chosen | Self-correction mid-utterance ("the man… the policeman", Clark 1997); Tomasello & Haberl 2002 — 12-month-olds identify which toy is new **for the adult**, not for themselves | A node in the graph carries a *set* of admissible labels plus a selection policy conditioned on the addressee's state — the wiki's node-definition problem with a communicative selection rule attached |
| **(iii) Normativity** | Uses can be **correct or incorrect**, and the standard comes from collective practice, not from outcome | Word play and deliberate misnaming (calling an elephant a giraffe) with shared mirth at ~2 years; pretense with a look-back to the adult | An evaluation signal that is neither reward nor ground truth: *"this is how **we** use it"* |

**Original vs derived normativity.** *Original*: an intentional action can be judged successful or unsuccessful against its own goal — available to any goal-directed system, plausibly to apes. *Derived*: symbols, tools and toys are invested with a correct use by the attitudes of their users. Only the second requires shared intentionality, and it is the one that gives cultural artefacts a specification a lone learner cannot recover from the object.

## Two social-learning algorithms, and what each can transmit

The source's sharpest engineering distinction (Tomasello 1990, 1996):

| | **Emulation learning** | **Imitative learning** |
|---|---|---|
| What is observed | The *object's* state change | The demonstrator's **goal-directed strategy** |
| What is learned | "That object affords X" — learn **from** the demonstrator | "This is what one does with it, and why" — learn **through** the demonstrator |
| Representation required | World model over object dynamics | Model of the other as an intentional agent, plus self–other equivalence |
| Distribution | Great apes' main channel; also human infants' first channel | Human, and the channel that carries **conventions** |
| Failure it explains | Cannot transmit an arbitrary convention: nothing in the object says which use is correct | — |

**Evidence that imitative learning is doing the goal inference and not motor mimicry:** 12-month-olds re-enact an action while looking in anticipation to its goal object (Carpenter et al. 1998a); 18-month-olds reproduce what an adult was *trying* to do and never completed (Meltzoff 1995); 16-month-olds preferentially copy intentional over accidental acts (Carpenter et al. 1998b); 24-month-olds read the same terminal behaviour differently depending on a *prior* intention cue seen seconds earlier (Carpenter, Call & Tomasello). Gergely, Bekkering & Király 2002: children evaluate *why* an unusual means was chosen (was the normal means blocked?) before copying it.

**(brainstorm) This is a boundary on imitation learning in the machine sense.** Behavioural cloning is emulation with extra steps: it copies the observed trajectory, so it can only transmit what is recoverable from the demonstration's surface. The transmissible-convention channel requires the learner to invert the demonstration into a *goal plus a means chosen under constraints*, and then to re-execute from its own body toward that goal. The wiki's nearest existing machinery is [[wiki/concepts/cross-embodiment-transfer.md]] (latent actions from video, body-specific decoder below a shared level) — which has the re-execution half and no goal-inference half — and inverse planning ([[wiki/entities/hbtom.md]]), which has the goal-inference half and never acts.

## Status functions: a binding maintained by joint declaration

Pretend play is the source's test case for an assignment that **overrides causal affordance**:

| | **Physical function** ("tool") | **Status function** ("toy") |
|---|---|---|
| Grounding | Intrinsic causal properties — "this object can be used to do X in context C" | Collective treatment alone: "this pencil **counts as** a toothbrush in our pretense" |
| How acquired | **Socially mediated** — the child could in principle discover it alone | **Socially constituted** — adult and child create the function on the spot |
| Learnability result (Rakoczy, Tomasello & Striano 2002, novel objects, 3 encounters) | Easier to learn, **generalised readily to other objects** | Learned imitatively too, but **not generalised creatively until 24 months** |
| Spontaneity (Striano, Tomasello & Rochat 2001, 18–30 months) | — | Children **under 24 months almost never** produce a pretense act with an object they had not first seen used symbolically; corroborated informally in cultures with few toys and no adult pretense modelling |

**Two consequences a builder should take.** (a) The romantic reading of pretense as individual creativity is **not supported**: symbolic use is transmitted before it is generated, so the creative-recombination capacity is downstream of a copied vocabulary, not upstream of it. (b) A status function is an **arbitrary symbol grounded by nothing but joint commitment** — precisely the binding [[wiki/concepts/affordance-grounded-symbols.md]] cannot supply and [[wiki/concepts/vector-symbolic-binding.md]] supplies without any mechanism for who agreed to it or how long it holds.

## What builds the 4-year transition: two independent linguistic factors

The wiki's usual position is that language is either the target capability or an interface. Here it is the **training signal for a non-linguistic representation**.

Correlational base: language scores predict false-belief performance 1–2 years later at `r ≈ 0.60–0.70` (Dunn 1991; Astington & Jenkins 1995, 1999; de Villiers 2000; Farrar & Maag 2002). Deaf children of signing deaf parents outperform deaf children of hearing parents with poor sign — the manipulation is linguistic *access*, with the deficit held fixed (Peterson & Siegel 2000).

Causal base — Lohmann & Tomasello, 3-year-olds, four training conditions, 3 sessions, false-belief outcome:

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Deceptive objects, **no substantive language** | **Insufficient.** Experiencing the appearance/reality mismatch does not by itself facilitate false-belief understanding |
| Adult labels the **two perspectives** on one object in discourse (`apple` / `candle`) | Facilitative |
| Adult uses **sentential complements** with propositional-attitude verbs — *even with no deceptive objects* | Facilitative |
| Both | **Strongest**; the two effects are approximately **independent** |

| Factor | What it supplies | Machine analogue |
|---|---|---|
| **Perspective-shifting discourse** — disagreements, misunderstandings, clarification requests, repairs, and above all *reflective discourse* (an interlocutor evaluating the child's expressed thought) | Repeated evidence that another agent's take on the same situation differs from mine, plus a **normative evaluation** that Vygotskian internalisation converts into self-regulation | A training stream of *disagreements about a shared referent*, with the correction attributed to a source — not a label |
| **Sentential complement syntax** (`X thinks that p`) | A **representational format** that packages a proposition inside an attitude, so `p` and the commitment to `p` are separately addressable, with referential opacity carried by the construction | An explicit typed embedding operator: `attitude(agent, proposition)` as a first-class term |

**The bootstrapping order is formal-before-conceptual.** Diessel & Tomasello 2001 (5 children, longitudinal): the construction enters as frozen formulae (`I think`, `I bet`, `You know`) — no past tense, no negation, no third-person subject, one invariant verb per child — and only from ~2;6 to ~3;6 diversifies into varied verbs, third-person subjects, modals and tenses. The child says `I believe p` correctly long before having a concept of belief, then acquires the concept by using the form across ever wider discourse contexts, especially reason-giving ones. **Karmiloff-Smith's representational redescription is the summary: express the competence in language, then redescribe the expression in that same language.**

**(brainstorm) This is the strongest developmental argument in the wiki for a *syntactic scaffold that is not about language*.** The claim is not that the child needs words for mental states; it is that a construction with the right *shape* — one slot for a content, one for an attitude toward it — makes a distinction cognitively addressable that was previously only enacted. A model that never has a term separating a proposition from its endorsement cannot represent its own uncertainty as an object, which is the same missing type the wiki's confidence and verification machinery keeps needing ([[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]], [[wiki/concepts/precision-weighting.md]]).

## The logical net: why belief cannot be learned piecewise

The source's answer to "why does it take three more years": objective reality, subjective belief and intersubjective perspective "form a logical net that can only fully be grasped as a whole". Reality here is not the child's own current perspective, nor an intersubjectively shared one, but **the view from nowhere — the frame in which no perspective is privileged**.

| Term | Definition requires |
|---|---|
| Belief | A perspective that can mismatch **reality** |
| Reality | The frame no perspective is privileged in — definable only once there are several perspectives to not privilege |
| Perspective | Something held by an agent, against a shared background of what is not in dispute |

**(brainstorm) A mutually-defining triple is a fixed point, not a curriculum item.** No ordering of the three can be learned first, which predicts exactly the empirical profile observed: a long plateau (meta-analysis: 4–5 years, and making the tasks child-friendly buys only minor improvements — Wellman, Cross & Watson 2001; the nonverbal version passes at the same age, Call & Tomasello 1999) followed by a fast transition. The wiki's usual assumption — that competences are acquired in dependency order — fails on any such set; what is needed is a joint estimation over all three, and the source's proposed driver is the *only* signal that presents all three at once: discourse in which two agents disagree about one referent, in a shared conventional code.

## Where the apes are, precisely

The source concedes more to apes than its own earlier position and the concession sharpens the boundary:

| Ape result | What it shows | What it does **not** show |
|---|---|---|
| Call, Hare, Carpenter & Tomasello 2002 — chimps more impatient with an *unwilling* than an *unable* human, with body movement and gaze matched; effect strongest when the experimenter **acts on the food** | Reading of **intention-in-action** (Searle) — the cue is perceptible in the behaviour | No inference to an intention not consummated in perceptible movement |
| Hare, Call, Agnetta & Tomasello 2000 — subordinate preferentially takes food the dominant **cannot see**; transparent-barrier control rules out behaviour-reading | Tracking of another's **visual access** | Nothing about the other's experience being *like mine from another angle* |
| Hare, Call & Tomasello 2001 — subordinate prefers food the dominant did not *see hidden*, and discriminates a **swapped** dominant from a non-swapped one | Memory of **who witnessed what**, individuated by identity | — |

**What is missing is uniformly the shared dimension:** no declarative pointing or showing (captive apes point imperatively, to humans only); no pretense; no evidence of self–other equivalence or of perspective; no shared symbols used to take a perspective on anything. The proposed factorisation: apes have a **social-cognitive schema** that sees below the surface of behaviour to its intentional structure and to how perception drives it; humans weave identification with others into that schema early in ontogeny, yielding self–other equivalence and, from it, perspective and derived normativity.

**The framing correction the paper makes and the wiki should adopt:** treating collective intentionality (money, marriage, government) as the human-unique thing "is like saying that only human beings build skyscrapers, when the fact is that only human beings, among primates, build any shelters at all". The diagnostic target is **shared** intentionality, because that is what language, cultural learning and pretense all sit on.

## Reading in the core framing

| This page | Latent-graph reading |
|---|---|
| Joint attention | Two agents provably indexing the **same node**, each with evidence that the other is indexing it — a synchronisation primitive, not a representation |
| Self–other equivalence | One description covers the edge traversed by me and the edge traversed by you: the graph is **agent-indexed and the index is quotiented out** |
| Perspective | A node carries a set of admissible labels; which is emitted is a function of the *addressee's* estimated state, not of the node |
| Derived normativity | An edge label that is *correct* by collective stipulation — no environmental signal distinguishes it from an incorrect one |
| Status function | An edge asserted into the graph by joint declaration and maintained only while the declaration holds — the wiki's only **revocable, socially scoped** binding |
| Cumulative culture | The graph is discovered across generations; the per-lifetime learner inherits a partially built map and its labels, so its search problem is not the species' search problem |
| The 4-year transition | Belief, reality and perspective as a **jointly identified** triple rather than three learnable variables |

## Open problems

- **Nothing here specifies the mechanism of self–other equivalence.** "Identification with others" is named as the human addition and given no computational content in the source. It is the single load-bearing term and it is a placeholder.
- **The two-transition story is a claim about biological basis resting on one asymmetry** — cross-cultural invariance and the autism deficit at 1 year vs. environmental sensitivity at 4 years. Both are compatible with a second maturational component whose timing is simply more environmentally elastic; nothing in the source excludes it.
- **The training-study effects are 3 sessions on 3-year-olds with a false-belief outcome measure.** The inference from "facilitates by 3 sessions" to "constructs over 3 years" is a large extrapolation, and the authors do not defend the scaling.
- **No account of how a normative signal is represented.** "This is how we use it" must be stored, retrieved and weighed against outcome; the source describes its acquisition (Vygotskian internalisation of an evaluative voice) and nothing about its format. The wiki's evaluation machinery ([[wiki/concepts/rule-level-evaluation.md]], [[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]]) all grounds in a checkable outcome, which is precisely the grounding derived normativity does not have.
- **The ape boundary is a set of nulls in a cooperative frame**, which is exactly the reading [[wiki/concepts/motivation-representation-synergy.md]] argues has been wrong before at this boundary (`T291`, `T292`).
- **Nothing distinguishes shared intentionality from a seventh core-knowledge system.** [[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]] lists it as an unresolved candidate; this source treats it as a single adaptation with a synchronous onset (Carpenter, Nagell & Tomasello 1998a: 9 joint-attention tasks emerging in developmental synchrony, correlated, with a consistent ordering across children) — which is *evidence for one underlying skill* and not a criterion for whether it is primitive or composite.

- **[[wiki/concepts/cross-embodiment-transfer.md]]** — holds half of what imitative learning needs: a body-specific decoder under a shared latent gives re-execution from a different body, and the missing half is inverting the demonstration into a goal plus a constrained means before re-executing.
- **[[wiki/concepts/nameability.md]]** — the perspectivity signature stated as that page's variable: one referent has many admissible descriptions (`dog`/`animal`/`pet`/`pest`), the speaker selects by the addressee's state, so description length is not a property of the referent but of a communicative choice.
- **[[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]** — perspectivity is that problem with a social answer attached: what counts as one node is settled per-utterance by the descriptive choice two agents converge on, not by a partition of the world.
- **[[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]]** — the desert-island argument's home: this source is where it originates, and it is that page's decomposition at its limit — hold priors at the great-ape value and nearly all human skill sits in the experience term, because the experience stream is culturally assembled.
- **[[wiki/concepts/language-of-thought.md]]** — the sentential-complement result read against it: `attitude(agent, p)` is exactly the embedding a language of thought supplies for free, and here it is *acquired from the ambient language* and dated (frozen formulae at ~2;0, productive at ~3;6), which makes the installed-vs-acquired dilemma (`T8`) empirically addressable for at least one construction.
- **[[wiki/concepts/external-verification.md]]** — the contrast that isolates derived normativity: every verifier in the wiki grounds correctness in a checkable outcome, while "this is how we use this symbol" is correct by collective practice and has no outcome to check against.
- **[[wiki/concepts/vector-symbolic-binding.md]]** — supplies a binding operation and not a binding *authority*: status functions are bindings created by joint declaration, scoped to a context and revoked when the pretense ends, and nothing in the algebra represents who asserted a binding or when it lapses.
- **[[wiki/concepts/learned-world-models.md]]** — the transmission counterpart: emulation learns object dynamics from watching (a world-model update), imitation learns a convention that no amount of observing the object could supply, so a world model is sufficient for one social-learning channel and not for the other.
- **[[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]]** — the rival reading of the same comparative record, differing in what it calls primitive: a domain-general relational supermodule that theory of mind is one consumer of, against a social operation that relational reasoning is downstream of — and the source explicitly argues the theory-of-mind-only route cannot explain the physical-causal and spatial nulls.
