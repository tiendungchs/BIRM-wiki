# Lateral Frontoparietal Network — Where the Relational Supermodule Sits

**Three cortical regions — rostrolateral prefrontal cortex (RLPFC), dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) and inferior parietal lobule/sulcus (IPL/IPS) — plus the long-range white matter joining the first and third. Proposed as the anatomy of higher-order relational thinking: the same set is recruited whenever a relation between relations must be evaluated, across memory, reasoning, decision-making and perception. Its RLPFC↔IPL link is the one connection in the set with no macaque analogue.**

## The three nodes and their division of labour

| Node | Proposed function | The measurement that assigns it |
|---|---|---|
| **IPL / IPS** | Represents the *first-order* relation itself | Activation scales with the **number** of relations to be considered (Crone et al. 2009; Hampshire et al. 2011; Watson & Chatterjee 2012); higher for **specific** relations ("the green ball is heavier than the orange ball") than for general association ("the green and orange balls are associated") — *corrected from the primary:* that effect peaks in **superior** parietal lobule (BA 7) and extends into IPL, so it is a dorsal-parietal result rather than an IPL one, and posterior hippocampus shows it too (Wendelken & Bunge 2010, **Transitive inference with the premises on screen** below). **Causal evidence is split:** focal BA 7/40 lesions take verbal transitive inference to chance (Waechter et al. 2013), while 74 posterior-lobe patients are unimpaired on a verbal relational-syllogism task (Mole et al. 2025); `T412` |
| **RLPFC** (frontopolar, ≈ Brodmann area 10) | Compares / integrates **several sets** of relational representations — the second-order operation | Selectively engaged by second-order > first-order contrasts at *identical stimulus displays*, with relation count held at two and the **response** count run against the effect (Wendelken et al. 2012, primary — see below); the only region of five with a main effect of integration; **not** driven by difficulty (see below); top cluster for chaining two on-screen relations in transitive inference, with hippocampal activation unchanged (Wendelken & Bunge 2010) |
| **DLPFC** | Working-memory manipulation, interference suppression, response selection — a general support tier | Activation scales with **task difficulty** across paradigms, including relational ones (Kroger et al. 2002; Cho et al. 2010; Krawczyk et al. 2010a) |
| **VLPFC** (≈ BA 44/45/47) — *the fourth node, added by Parsons & Davies 2022* | **Controlled access to semantic content**: anterior (BA 47) retrieves stored semantic knowledge, posterior (BA 45) *selects* among what retrieval returns | Tracks the **associative strength** between the words of a verbal analogy while RLPFC tracks integration demand in the same subjects and trials (Bunge et al. 2005); activates more for verbal-semantic than for visuospatial analogies in meta-analysis (Hobeika et al. 2016); has direct tracts to inferior/lateral temporal cortex and medial temporal lobe (Petrides & Pandya 2002; Croxson et al. 2005; Barredo et al. 2015) |

The VLPFC row is not a fourth function bolted on — it is the node that makes the network's *inputs* explicit. RLPFC integrates relations, IPL holds them, DLPFC pays for the effort, and VLPFC decides **which stored content the other three operate on**. Vendetti & Bunge's three-node account has no term for that, and every wiki architecture that "retrieves then maps" is asserting this edge without naming it ([[wiki/entities/macfac.md]]).

**The convergence result.** Neurosynth reverse inference over the term *relational* (46 studies spanning memory, reasoning, decision-making and higher-level perception) returns RLPFC, DLPFC and IPL/IPS as the voxels reported more selectively with *relational* than with any of 524 other terms; forward inference additionally returns hippocampus, insula and posterior cingulate. This is the wiki's first **anatomical** evidence for the supermodule claim in [[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]], which had argued for a shared relational competence purely from the fact that the same first-order/higher-order behavioural profile recurs in every domain. Convergence across task domains in a term-based meta-analysis is weak evidence — the term is applied by the original authors, not by the analysis — but it is evidence of the right *type*, and there was none before.

## The intrinsic system: one seed, three resting networks, and a parietal tripartition

Vincent et al. 2008. Spontaneous BOLD (blood-oxygen-level-dependent) fluctuations during passive fixation, correlated against a seed. `n = 105` in three datasets: 10 subjects at 4 mm voxels, then 50 + 45 at 2 mm. Regions of interest were defined on dataset 2 and tested on the held-out dataset 3. Preprocessing regressed out motion, white matter, ventricles **and the global signal**, so `T264` applies to every sign below.

| Seed | Network (authors' name) | Members recovered |
|---|---|---|
| Left/right aPFC (anterior prefrontal cortex, MNI −36/57/9, taken from a cognitive-control task study: Koechlin et al. 1999) | **Frontoparietal control system (FPCS)** | aPFC along the middle frontal gyrus into DLPFC; dorsomedial superior frontal / anterior cingulate; **anterior insula**; **anterior IPL (aIPL)** extending into lateral IPS; caudate head; lateral cerebellum |
| MT+ (middle temporal motion complex) | Dorsal attention system (DAS) | Frontal eye fields, medial IPS, SPL, premotor, pre-supplementary motor area |
| HF (hippocampal formation) | Hippocampal-cortical memory system (HCMS), a component of the default network | vMPFC, posterior cingulate/retrosplenial, lateral temporal, **posterior IPL (pIPL)** |

**The formal test is parietal.** Inter-regional correlations in held-out dataset 3:

| Seed ↓ / parietal ROI → | SPL (±24–27/−52…−56/55–57) | aIPL (±52/−46…−49/46–47) | pIPL (−47/−71/29; 50/−64/27) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MT+ | **positive**, all `P < 0.001` | none | none |
| aPFC | none | **positive**, all `P ≤ 0.01` | none |
| HF | none | none | **positive**, all `P < 0.005` |

"None" = no positive correlation, all `P > 0.05`. Seeding the three parietal ROIs back recovers maps "highly similar" to the three original seeds' maps. All three maps converge across the three datasets at a false-discovery-rate threshold of `P < 0.05`. The same DAS → FPCS → HCMS ordering repeats in lateral and medial frontal cortex. Overlap between systems is small: FPCS∩HCMS in posterior cingulate, lateral parietal, dorsal frontal and lateral temporal cortex; FPCS∩DAS in IPS, lateral temporal and lateral PFC. aPFC→aIPL coupling is stronger within the same hemisphere (both seeds, `P < 0.001`), so the system is lateralised. Right MT+→SPL shows the same effect, although the authors' earlier 25-subject study had found no lateralisation in the other two systems.

**What it settles or sharpens on this page.**

| Point | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **The seed is the integrator's tissue** | The aPFC seed lies 14–15 mm from this page's RLPFC peaks (Wendelken et al. 2012 −42/54/−3; Bunge et al. 2005 −45/45/6; Urbanski et al. 2016 −31/51/−3), and Wendelken et al. 2012's RLPFC-seeded β-series map is labelled with this network. So the resting partner set of the relational integrator is aIPL, DLPFC, anterior cingulate and anterior insula |
| **The IPL is two regions, not one node with two names** | aIPL (FPCS) and pIPL (HCMS) are adjacent, non-overlapping, and dissociate in held-out data. Task contrasts merge them because both deactivate under demanding tasks (Shulman et al. 1997), and the default-network deactivation meta-analysis extends from pIPL into aIPL (Buckner et al. 2008). This partly resolves the IPL collision with [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]] recorded in Connections. Not yet checked: which subregion the relational IPL activations on this page (relation-count scaling; Wendelken et al. 2012's IPS BA 40/7) fall in |
| **The salience nodes are inside this system** | At this seed and grain, anterior insula and anterior cingulate are FPCS members. Seeley et al. 2007, using independent component analysis, split them off as "salience"; Dosenbach et al. 2007 separate a cingulo-opercular network. So whether the triple-network arbitrator is a separate system from the network it engages depends on the estimator ([[wiki/entities/salience-network.md]], [[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]) |
| **Interposition** | FPCS lies *between* DAS (externally directed attention) and HCMS (memory reconstruction) in parietal, frontal and lateral temporal cortex. The authors' reading is that it is "anatomically positioned to integrate information" from both and to "adjudicate between potentially competing inner- versus outer-directed processes". That is inferred from adjacency; nothing in the study measures integration or adjudication |
| **Evolution** | Anterior lateral PFC and aIPL, the two FPCS anchors, are among the most expanded regions from macaque to human (Van Essen & Dierker 2007, 23 landmark constraints). They are also the two endpoints of the RLPFC↔IPL edge in **Evolution** below. Buckner et al. 2008 attribute the IPL expansion to the default network ([[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]); the tripartition assigns the expanded anterior part to FPCS |

**Three readings of aIPL from the authors' discussion** (the task literature is second-hand):

| Reading | Evidence cited | Load variable it predicts |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary buffer | Up under articulatory suppression (Gruber 2001); holds retrieved content "in a form accessible to decision-making processes" (Wagner et al. 2005) | Hold demand |
| **Conflict / uncertainty detector** | Higher for low- than high-confidence recognition (Kim & Cabeza 2007), for rare old items (Herron et al. 2004), and for items studied once rather than 20 times (Velanova et al. 2003). **Kroger et al. 2002's relational-complexity increase in bilateral aIPL on Raven-like matrices is read as stimulus conflict** | Decision conflict, not relation count |
| Attentional-set maintenance | Sustained activity across task blocks (Dosenbach et al. 2006) | Flat within a block |

**For a model.**

- The conflict reading competes with this page's "first-order relation store" assignment for the IPL node, and it fits Mole et al. 2025's parietal lesion null better. A conflict signal triggers recruitment and is not content, so damaging it need not impair the relational decision `(brainstorm)`. Every IPL design on this page confounds relation count with conflict, because more relations means more competing mappings. The discriminating item holds relation count fixed and varies how similar the competing mappings are, which is `I36`'s crossed shape.
- `(brainstorm)` An L1 decomposition read off the topology. There are three systems: an external selector (DAS), an episodic store/constructor (HCMS) and a controller (FPCS). The controller's posterior node sits physically between the other two posterior nodes, and its frontal node is the integrator. The architectural claim is that the controller reads an external-attention stream and a retrieved-memory stream through an adjacent buffer, while the two streams are anticorrelated and have no direct coupling. That is the "shared bus" of [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]'s subsystem decomposition, one level up. It is untested, because adjacency is not connectivity.

**Limits (mostly the authors' own).** Correlation of slow (< 0.08 Hz) fluctuations gives no direction; the authors name Granger causality and structural equation modelling as the needed follow-up. Direct coupling and shared input from a third source cannot be separated. The global signal is regressed. The authors call the "control" label tentative. Because the seed was placed from a task study, the network is conditional on that seed.

## The task test: one control network, two partners chosen by content source

Spreng, Stevens, Chamberlain, Gilmore & Schacter 2010. The first task test of the interposed system above. `n = 20` young adults, event-related fMRI with three conditions:

| Condition | What is given | What the subject does |
|---|---|---|
| **Autobiographical planning** | A personal goal ("freedom from debt"), then two steps and one obstacle printed in Tower-of-London discs | Integrates them into a personal plan within ≤ 15 s; rates plan detail |
| **Visuospatial planning** (Tower of London, 3–7 moves) | Goal and start configurations of three discs on three rods | Determines the minimum number of moves |
| Counting (baseline) | Random letters in the same discs | Counts vowels |

Both planning tasks give the goal state, require ordering intermediate steps online, and share one display. They differ in where the content comes from: retrieved personal knowledge vs the screen. Task analysis by spatiotemporal partial least squares (PLS; multivariate, no global-signal regression). The three networks were replicated from a separate resting run with Vincent et al. 2008's seeds, global signal regressed, and used as a priori regions of interest.

| Measure | Default | Dorsal attention | **Frontoparietal control** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latent variable 1 (72.7% of covariance): autobiographical vs visuospatial | Autobiographical only (mean bootstrap ratio 4.54) | Visuospatial only (3.99) | **Not reliable for either task's unique component** (1.04 / 1.54) |
| Latent variable 2 (27.3%): both planning tasks vs counting | — | — | **Reliable** (3.00) |
| % BOLD (blood-oxygen-level-dependent) vs counting, autobiographical | **Up** (`t = 6.49`) | **Down** (`t = −15.14`) | Up (`t = 4.75`) |
| % BOLD vs counting, visuospatial | **Down** (`t = −3.57`) | Up (`t = 7.36`) | Up (`t = 5.32`) |
| Control-network difference between the two planning tasks | | | **None** (`t = −1.65`, uncorrected `p > .10`) |

Planning > counting peaks include bilateral RLPFC (−32/58/4; 28/56/0), DLPFC, bilateral aIPL (−40/−56/44; 44/−50/44), precuneus, dorsal anterior cingulate and bilateral anterior insula/frontal operculum. The left RLPFC peak is ≈ 13 mm from Wendelken et al. 2012's integrator peak (−42/54/−3).

**Coupling: the partner follows the content source.** Correlation with the control network's time course over the 25 s trial (Fisher `z'`):

| Pair | Autobiographical | Visuospatial |
|---|---|---|
| Control ↔ default (vs counting) | **1.10** (`t = 8.4`) | 0.06 (n.s.) |
| Control ↔ dorsal attention (vs counting) | −0.23 (n.s.) | **0.91** (`t = 8.0`) |
| Control ↔ default (vs trial onset) | **0.83** | −0.34 (n.s.) |
| Control ↔ dorsal attention (vs trial onset) | 0.76 | 0.71 |

Task × network interaction `F(1,19) = 42.49` vs counting; `6.25` vs trial onset. Seed PLS across subjects agrees. The default seed's pattern under autobiographical planning covers default and control networks (bootstrap ratios 3.32 / 2.44) but not dorsal attention (1.48). The dorsal-attention seed's pattern under visuospatial planning covers dorsal attention and control (2.28 / 2.23) but not default (1.12).

**Difficulty does not move the control network.** Easy vs hard Tower of London (3–4 vs 5–7 moves, accuracy falls stepwise with moves) and median-split self-rated autobiographical difficulty show no brain-score difference. The block analysis ends each block at the button press, which removes the response-time confound.

**For a model.**

- **The controller's partner is a per-trial routing variable, not a fixed wire.** The control network is engaged equally by both tasks. It couples to the default network when content is retrieved and to dorsal attention when content is on screen, and the two partners still move in opposite directions. This is `G21`'s switchable fan-in at network scale. Wendelken et al. 2012 showed one RLPFC seed switching between left middle temporal gyrus and right DLPFC by content domain. Here the whole control system switches between the internal and external systems by content source. `(brainstorm)` A machine version is one domain-general planner over two encoders, a memory-retrieval stream and a perception stream that inhibit each other. The read gate is set by where the task's content lives, not by a mode scheduler. This is the router reading of `T262`. Its ablation: cut the planner and both planning types should fail; cut either stream and only its own planning type should fail.
- **What the controller is denied is partial.** Against counting, the control network is uncorrelated with dorsal attention during autobiographical planning (−0.23, n.s.). Against trial onset, which keeps the shared display, it is coupled to both (0.83 / 0.76). So the external stream is down-weighted, not cut off, while the controller reads memory. An `L2` design consequence `(brainstorm)`: a hard gate that removes the external stream from the controller's inputs is stronger than what biology measures. A relative weighting is the matched version.
- **The internal mode can be goal-directed.** The default network carries a task with a goal state and a demand for ordered steps. So its suppression under externally directed tasks tracks the content source, not goal-direction. The authors' point is that the deactivation literature on [[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]] confounded external attention with goal-directed cognition.
- **Difficulty null, at network grain.** It agrees with this page's DLPFC/RLPFC split only weakly. The network ROI contains both nodes, so a DLPFC difficulty effect could be diluted. The hard bin (5–7 moves) also adds counter-intuitive moves.

**Limits.**

| Limit | Consequence |
|---|---|
| "Coupling" is the correlation of **trial-averaged** time courses over 10 scans of 2.5 s (25 s), plus one across-subject seed analysis | Two regions that both follow the evoked response correlate whether or not they exchange information. The brain-score correlation of `r = .94` is mostly shared response shape. The measure is co-activation over time, not trial-to-trial interaction. The authors' argument against sequential operation only rules out alternation slower than 2.5 s |
| The two planning tasks differ in more than content source | Autobiographical planning has no correct answer and longer response times (14.2 vs 12.4 s for counting). It is checked by verbal protocols and a post-scan interview |
| `n = 20`, one task pair | The partner switch is shown for one internal/external pair. Whether the control network's partner set includes the salience network or the medial-temporal subsystem alone is not tested |
| Network regions of interest come from a resting map with the global signal regressed | `T264` applies to the region definitions, not to the task signs, which are computed without regression. `T264` is resolved: the default↔task-positive sign survives without GSR, at reduced magnitude (Chai et al. 2012) |
| Autobiographical difficulty is self-reported after the scan | The difficulty null is strong for the Tower of London and weak for autobiographical planning |

## The interposed system's wiring: three port types

Spreng et al. 2013. Same lab and same task contrasts as **The task test**, now read at node grain at rest.

| Step | Choice |
|---|---|
| Nodes | 43 spheres (5 mm) at peaks of the default, dorsal attention and control components of the autobiographical / Tower-of-London / counting PLS, kept only where significant in **all three** independent task samples (`n = 20 + 18 + 25`) |
| Rest data | `n = 105` (43 overlap with the node samples), 6 min 12 s eyes open, 3 T |
| Nuisance | Motion, ventricle, white matter **and global signal** regressed. Only **positive** edges analysed, to avoid GSR-induced negatives |
| Edge test | Bootstrap 99.99% confidence interval (bias-corrected and accelerated, 10,000 resamples); an edge is kept if its lower bound > 0. No magnitude threshold |
| Direct edges | Partial correlation, conditioning **only** on nodes with a reliable positive full correlation to either endpoint, not on all 41 others. The authors cite Berkson's paradox, GSR-made negatives and degrees of freedom |
| Modules | Hierarchical clustering (average linkage) on full correlations. Node size in the graphs = betweenness centrality |

| Statistic | Full correlation | Partial correlation |
|---|---|---|
| Reliable edges, of all possible | 36.4% (mean `r = .27`, range .08–.64) | 12.1% (mean `r = .18`, range .08–.54) |
| Within- vs between-module strength | `.30` vs `.19`, `t(268) = 9.34` | `.19` vs `.14`, `t(61) = 3.74` |
| Highest betweenness | Bilateral **MFG BA 6** (control) | **msPFC** (control) |

The within > between contrast is not independent of the clustering, and the authors say so.

**Task-to-rest reassignment.** 5 of 43 nodes change network between the task components and resting clustering:

| Node | Task | Rest |
|---|---|---|
| Right SFG | Default | Control |
| Bilateral DLPFC (−38/32/30; 44/42/26), right dACC | Dorsal attention | Control |
| Bilateral TPJ, precuneus (−2/−60/50) | Control | Default |

**No node moves between default and dorsal attention.** Every reassigned node sits at a module border in the graph. The authors read reassignment as flexible allegiance.

**Three node types inside the control network.** The type is set by which outside network a node has reliable edges to. "Direct" means the edge survives partial correlation.

| Type | Full-correlation members | Direct (partial) members |
|---|---|---|
| **Dual-aligned** (edges to both default and dorsal attention) | Bilateral MFG BA 6, left MFG BA 9, left anterior insula, dACC, msPFC | Bilateral MFG BA 6, **msPFC** (direct to left FEF and left inferior precentral sulcus, and to left IFG and left SFG) |
| **Default-aligned** | Left aIPL, left RLPFC | Left aIPL |
| **Dorsal-attention-aligned** | Bilateral DLPFC; right MFG BA 9, right RLPFC, right anterior insula, right aIPL | Bilateral DLPFC, bilateral anterior insula, right aIPL |

Entry routes the authors propose:

- **Default side: left SFG.** It is default at task and at rest, and has direct edges to left MFG BA 6 and msPFC.
- **Dorsal-attention side: bilateral DLPFC.** It has no default edges, and the same DLPFC is the region most anticorrelated with the default network (Chai et al. 2012; Hampson et al. 2010). The authors read it as a lateral-prefrontal extension of dorsal attention.
- **Dual nodes.** MFG BA 6 is lateral premotor cortex (externally guided motor planning). msPFC overlaps pre-SMA (planning from working memory). So the authors place both at the step that translates goal-directed thought into action.

**For a model.**

- **The router has typed ports.** **The task test** shows the control network switching partner by content source. Here the resting wiring already holds partner-specific node subsets plus a small dual subset. So the per-trial read weight proposed there may select among sub-populations with fixed partner wiring, rather than re-weight one mixed population. This is `G122`'s mixed-vs-discrete fork at network scale: mostly `discrete`, with a dual-aligned bridge. `(brainstorm)` Probe for a model with a two-stream controller: lesion the dual units vs the single-partner units. A discrete arrangement predicts that cutting single-partner units removes one stream's planning only. Cutting the dual units should remove the switch.
- **The crossing point sits at the action end, not at the integrator.** The highest-betweenness nodes are premotor (MFG BA 6, pre-SMA-adjacent msPFC). The relational integrator (RLPFC) is single-aligned, and its alignment follows hemisphere. `(brainstorm)` For `G84`: the component that connects two competing streams is the one that commits to action. The reasoning component reads one stream at a time.
- **A hemisphere pattern the authors do not discuss.** Every right-hemisphere single-aligned control node they list aligns with dorsal attention (MFG BA 9, RLPFC, anterior insula, aIPL). The default-aligned nodes are all left (aIPL, RLPFC). Wendelken & Bunge 2010's on-screen transitive inference engages right RLPFC (below), and Hobeika et al. 2016's semantic analogy is left. `(brainstorm)` Left integrator wired to the retrieved stream, right to the perceived one. This would be a content-source account of `T336`'s laterality. It is untested and resting-state only, and it already has one counter-case: Hobeika's visuospatial analogy map is also left.
- **Weak edges are where the between-module traffic lives.** Raising an arbitrary threshold removes between-network edges disproportionately, because they are weaker (`.19` vs `.30`). The authors' reading is that weak, reliable edges let information enter or leave a module "without dominating the information processing". Bootstrap retention is their fix ([[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]).

**Limits.**

| Limit | Consequence |
|---|---|
| GSR, and negative edges discarded | Default↔dorsal-attention "little positive connectivity" is not a measurement of anticorrelation. Every edge here is positive by construction |
| Partial correlation is undirected | The authors call it "effective connectivity". It is direct-vs-indirect only, with no direction (`T257` untouched) |
| The conditioning set is chosen from the full-correlation graph | A direct edge between two nodes that share no reliable neighbour is never tested against a third path outside that set |
| Nodes are task-defined from one task triplet | Alignment types are conditional on the PLS components of autobiographical vs visuospatial planning; subcortical nodes were dropped |
| No task data in the edge analysis | That port types underlie the **task test** switch is the authors' proposal, not a measurement |

## The quantitative map: one domain-general node with domain-oriented flanks

Hobeika et al. 2016. Cluster-forming threshold `p < 0.001` uncorrected, cluster-level `p < 0.05`, 1000 permutations; contrast maps at `p < 0.05` uncorrected, ≥100 voxels, 10,000 permutations.

| Map | Peak clusters |
|---|---|
| **Global** (analogy + matrix, 27 experiments) | Left rostral inferior frontal sulcus/gyrus **BA 10** (5192 mm³, ALE 0.034, MNI −48/44/−8, **FWE-corrected**) extending to BA 47/45/46 and left insula; right posterior IFG/MFG BA 9/44 (FWE); right insula BA 13 (FWE); bilateral MFG BA 9; medial SFG/cingulate BA 6/32; bilateral superior parietal BA 7; left inferior parietal BA 40; bilateral posterior MFG/SFS BA 6 |
| **Semantic analogy** | A **left-lateral prefrontal** network only: rostrolateral IFG/MFG BA 10/47 and BA 46 (primary), posterior IFG BA 44, SFG BA 9 and BA 8, bilateral caudate head |
| **Visuospatial analogy** | Left rostrolateral IFS/IFG BA 10/47/46 (primary), right MFG BA 9, right anterior insula, cerebellum |
| **Matrix problems** | A large **bilateral** fronto-parietal network, 8 clusters: bilateral parietal BA 7/40 and precuneus BA 7/31 (largest), bilateral posterior IFS BA 6/44/9 with right predominance, posterior SFS BA 6/8, bilateral medial PFC, right anterior insula, left cingulate BA 32 |

**The left rostrolateral region of interest.** Semantic and visuospatial analogy maps overlap in one cluster — the rostral end of the left inferior frontal sulcus, BA 10 — and it is the cluster that survives voxel-level family-wise-error correction in the global map. The **right** rostrolateral cortex is not significant in either domain map. This upgrades the page's central claim from convergence-across-terms (Neurosynth, weak) to convergence-across-experiments with a coordinate and an effect: `n = 27` studies, and the same voxels appear whether the relation is *nose:smell :: mouth:taste* or a change of shape and size.

**Domain is carried by flanking regions on a dorsoventral axis, not by the integrator.** The subtraction maps place the two content feeds on opposite sides of the shared cluster:

| Contrast | Region | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic > visuospatial | Left anterior IFG **BA 47**, *ventral and posterior* to the shared cluster; plus left rostromedial BA 10 and left IFS BA 46 | Controlled semantic retrieval and selection — the VLPFC node above ([[wiki/concepts/controlled-semantic-cognition.md]]) |
| Visuospatial > semantic | Left anterior MFG **BA 10**, *dorsal* to the shared cluster; plus bilateral posterolateral PFC, left SPL/IPL/intraparietal sulcus, right fusiform BA 37 | Spatial-relation formation, spatial attention, and visual imagery of the schema |

**Why this is the architecturally interesting half.** The integrator is *not* re-instantiated per domain; what changes is which posterior/ventral store fans into it. (The within-subject primary for this axis, Wendelken et al. 2012 below, finds the same dorsoventral ordering *inside* left RLPFC and reads it as a non-uniform cell distribution rather than a fixed shared head — `T338`.) That is `G21`'s switchable fan-in composer, measured: one fixed second-order site, content-specific afferents, and the domain difference living entirely in the afferents. The authors' own conclusion is the same — "the connectivity of this region with domain-oriented regions has a crucial role." A machine reading `(brainstorm)`: a single shared relational head over per-modality encoders should reproduce the whole pattern, and the ablation that tests it is per-domain — remove the semantic encoder and visuospatial analogy should survive, remove the head and neither should.

**The three networks the global map decomposes into.** Fronto-parietal control (DLPFC + posterior parietal), **salience** (anterior insula + posterior IFG + anterior cingulate), and **dorsal attention** (posterior SFS/frontal eye fields, premotor, SPL). The insula is bilateral and FWE-significant on the right — an unexpected node for a reasoning network, and the one this page's three-node account has no term for at all ([[wiki/entities/salience-network.md]]). Under the switching account the salience network would be what *hands control* to the fronto-parietal system and suppresses the default-mode network for the duration of the analogy, which is the mechanism behind the negative default-mode/accuracy correlation reported below.

## The gradient *inside* the integrator, and the response-count control

> **Provenance (sixth ingest).** Wendelken, Chung & Bunge 2012, *Rostrolateral prefrontal cortex: domain-general or domain-sensitive?*, Human Brain Mapping 33(8):1952–1963, doi:10.1002/hbm.21336 (`raw/wendelken-2012-rlpfc-domain-general-or-domain-sensitive.md`). The primary for the two claims this page has been citing second-hand — the second-order > first-order contrast at identical displays, and the task-dependent RLPFC coupling that `G21`'s switchable fan-in rests on. `n = 23` healthy adults, event-related fMRI, relational matching in two content domains, plus a β-series functional-connectivity analysis seeded on the overlap cluster. It adds the within-subject version of Hobeika's dorsoventral axis and opens `T338`.

**The design fix, which is the reason this study is the primary and not one more activation report.** Earlier versions of the relational matching task asked for **one** response on first-order trials ("is there a match on the top *or* bottom pair?"), so a subject could answer without inspecting both pairs — and any second-order > first-order effect could be *number of relations processed* rather than joint consideration. Here first-order trials required **two** judgments and **two** button presses, second-order trials **one** of each:

| | First-order (SEM1 / VIS1) | Second-order (SEM2 / VIS2) |
|---|---|---|
| Relations inspected | 2 | 2 |
| Judgments / responses | **2** | **1** |
| Chance | 25% | 50% |
| Accuracy | no difference between orders (`F₁,₂₂ < 1`) | — |
| Response time | — | **slower** (`F₁,₂₂ = 12.2`, `P = 0.002`) |

So the contrast holds relation *count* fixed and runs the response count **against** the effect. It is the cleanest available test that RLPFC is driven by the need to consider two relations **jointly** rather than by considering more of them — and it explains the otherwise odd ROI result below, where inferior parietal and ventrolateral cortex go the *other* way.

**Whole brain.** Collapsed across domains, the only clusters surviving family-wise-error correction are **left RLPFC** (BA 10/11/47, MNI −42/54/−3, `T = 8.09`, 373 voxels) and **left IPS** (BA 40/7). Right RLPFC appears at 45/51/−12 but at 15 voxels. Bilateral DLPFC, dorsomedial prefrontal and bilateral SPL are present subthreshold. Anterior medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate — the default-mode nodes — **deactivate**, which is the same sign as the negative default-mode/accuracy relation reported below. Run separately, `SEM2 > SEM1` and `VIS2 > VIS1` each survive correction in left RLPFC and left IPS and nowhere else, with matching BOLD timecourses.

**The gradient.** Activation overlap between the two domains inside left RLPFC is large — semantic engagement extends well dorsal, visuospatial well ventral — but the **peaks are systematically offset**. Measured per subject, transverse to the orientation of the anterior middle frontal gyrus:

| Comparison | Effect | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Visuospatial vs semantic **integration** peak (`VIS2>VIS1` vs `SEM2>SEM1`) | `F₁,₂₁ = 9.1`, `P = 0.007`; visuospatial **dorsal** to semantic in almost every subject | left anterior MFG only |
| Same, second-order **vs baseline** | `F₁,₂₁ = 4.5`, `P < 0.05` | left anterior MFG only |
| Same, **first-order** vs baseline | `F < 1` — **no effect** | — |
| Right anterior MFG; left and right posterior MFG | no effect of domain on peak position, any contrast | — |
| Interaction `(SEM2−SEM1) > (VIS2−VIS1)` whole-brain | **no voxels anywhere** (and the equivalent-power main effect is robust, so not a power null) | — |

Three things are load-bearing there. The offset is **within-subject and within-region**, not a comparison of study coordinates as in Hobeika et al. 2016 — and it runs in the *same direction*, visuospatial dorsal, semantic ventral, which upgrades that axis from a between-study subtraction to a replicated topography. It is **specific to the second-order contrast**: first-order processing of the same stimuli in the same voxels shows no domain topography, so the gradient is a property of the integration operation, not of the stimuli entering it. And it is **specific to left anterior MFG** — the one region with the strongest integration effect is also the only one with the gradient.

**The region-of-interest ANOVA (hemisphere × integration × domain), and the two asymmetries in it.**

| Region | Integration (2nd > 1st) | Domain (VIS > SEM) | Domain × integration | Domain × hemisphere (L:SEM, R:VIS) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RLPFC** | `P < .001` | — | `P = 0.02` | **—** |
| VLPFC | **first > second** (`P = 0.02`) | — | — | `P = 0.006` |
| DLPFC | — | — | `P = 0.008` | `P = 0.03` |
| SPL | `P = 0.07` | `P = 0.002` | `P = 0.07` | — |
| IPL | **first > second** | VIS > SEM | — | — |

RLPFC is the **only** region with a main effect of integration, and the parietal ROIs are the only ones with a main effect of domain — the three-node division of labour at the top of this page, measured in one design. The first asymmetry: VLPFC and DLPFC carry the familiar **left-right** verbal/non-verbal split and RLPFC does **not** — so the integrator's domain organisation is dorsoventral where its inputs' is lateralised, and the two axes are independent. The second: IPL and VLPFC are higher for *first*-order trials, which is the response-count control showing up as a sign flip and is the strongest evidence on this page that the first-order store's load variable is the number of relations *judged*, not the difficulty of the trial.

**A third difficulty reversal, free from the design.** The semantic task was harder than the visuospatial task on both measures (accuracy `F₁,₂₂ = 47.0`, RT `F₁,₂₂ = 47.1`, both `P < 0.001`) — yet the integration effect in RLPFC and DLPFC is **larger for the easier visuospatial task** (`domain × integration`, `P = 0.02` and `P = 0.008`). Difficulty and integration demand are ordered oppositely across domains here, in the same subjects, without anyone designing for it. Added as a third arm to `I36` on [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]].

**The connectivity, which is the part `G21` uses.** β-series correlation, seed = the 177-voxel intersection of the `SEM2>SEM1` and `VIS2>VIS1` clusters:

| Contrast | Coupled region | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Across all trials | left IPS (strongest non-frontal), lateral PFC, medial frontal gyrus, middle temporal gyrus — i.e. the fronto-parietal control network (Vincent et al. 2008) | `P < 0.001` unc. |
| `SEM2 > VIS2` | **left MTG** (−60/−30/−3, 17 voxels) and midline SFG (6/57/30, 27 voxels) | `P < 0.001` unc. |
| `VIS2 > SEM2` | right DLPFC (54/36/21, 10 voxels) — **nothing at `P < 0.001`** | `P < 0.005` unc. |

One seed, one subject group, two content domains, and the seed's partners change with the content. That is the switchable fan-in stated as a measurement rather than inferred from a meta-analysis, and it is the mechanism `T289` Position B names. Two discounts: the visuospatial arm survives only at a relaxed threshold and at 10 voxels, and the authors' own explanation for the asymmetry is that *both* tasks are visuospatial while only one is semantic — so the clean result is the semantic one. Note also that the semantic partner is **left MTG**, which [[wiki/concepts/controlled-semantic-cognition.md]] identifies as the semantic-control adapter and which this page previously recorded as absent from the relational set: it is absent from the *co-activation* map and present in the *connectivity* map, which is exactly what an adapter recruited on demand should look like.

**What the authors conclude, and why it is not what this page previously said.** Their proposal is that RLPFC neurons are domain-general **in principle** and domain-sensitive **by the provenance of their inputs** — ventral cells with privileged access to first-order semantic content in VLPFC, dorsal cells with privileged access to visuospatial content in the superior frontal sulcus and nearby DLPFC. That is a claim about the *distribution of cells inside the integrator*, and this page's Hobeika section states the flatter version — "the integrator is *not* re-instantiated per domain; what changes is which posterior store fans into it." Both cannot be fully right about the machine: one says a shared head with routed inputs, the other says a tiled family of heads each with a fixed input set. Opened as **`T338`**.

## Matrix problems are not analogies, at least not to this network

The sharpest result on this page for anyone scoring a machine. Holding domain constant at *visuospatial*, four-term analogy and `3×3` matrix problems dissociate:

| Direction | What comes out |
|---|---|
| **Visuospatial analogy > matrix** | The left rostrolateral cluster (BA 10, extending 9/45/46), in the same location as the analogy-general region of interest — **and one cerebellar cluster. Nothing else.** |
| **Matrix > visuospatial analogy** | Bilateral posterior/dorsolateral PFC (BA 6/44/9, right-predominant), posterior SFS (BA 6/8), medial PFC (BA 9/32), left IPL/SPL (BA 7), right precuneus (BA 7/19/31), postcentral gyrus (BA 2/3), plus a *medial* left rostral BA 10 cluster distinct from the analogy region |

So the region that is present in **every** analogy contrast and causally required for analogy is the region matrix problems do not recruit; and what matrix problems add is the attention and executive control machinery. The proposed functional split inside the left frontal pole — a **ventral** site at the end of the inferior frontal sulcus for analogy, a **medial** site at the end of the superior frontal sulcus for matrix-format relational reasoning — is the authors' own and flagged by them as weak, since the medial cluster appears only in the subtraction and not in the matrix task map itself.

**This is opened as `T335`, at `L0-INSTR`,** because it puts the wiki's whole matrix-format benchmark stack ([[wiki/entities/raven.md]], [[wiki/entities/pgm.md]], [[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]]) on a construct that the only quantitative neural comparison says is not the analogy construct. The source names three confounds that could dissolve it, and the first is serious: **response mode is collinear with format**. All but two matrix studies used forced-choice completion, all but four analogy studies used evaluation — and Wendelken et al. 2008b already showed left rostrolateral cortex engaged by *evaluating* an analogy and not by *completing* one, with format held constant. *(Corrected from the primary: this reproduces only the **rostrolateral half** of the dissociation. Completion does not recruit medial prefrontal cortex — medial BA 10 is deactivated in every condition and merely less so under completion — and the control-network half goes the **other** way: evaluation, the analogy-typical mode, recruits right DLPFC, premotor and superior parietal cortex more than completion does, while in the meta-analysis the completion-typical matrix format is the one that recruits them. The material is verbal, not visuospatial. See **Compare vs complete** below.)* The other two: matrix items display more terms, so visuospatial load is higher; and more terms means more relations to integrate, which is the fronto-parietal control network's own load variable. **The discriminating experiment is cheap and unrun** — cross format with response mode on matched material.

> **The machine-side version is cheaper still `(brainstorm)`.** Train a solver on one format and test transfer to the other, both directions, against within-format transfer as the baseline. Under Position A the two formats are one construct and transfer should be near-free; under Position B a matrix-trained solver should be missing precisely the second-order comparison step. No wiki benchmark reports cross-format transfer, and [[wiki/entities/pgm.md]] — which declares its held-out abstractions — could run it without new items.

## The causal test: a 0.33 cc lesion site, and what it does *not* impair

> **Provenance (fourth ingest).** Urbanski, Bréchemier, Garcin, Bendetowicz, Thiebaut de Schotten, Foulon, Rosso, Clarençon, Dupont, Pradat-Diehl, Levy & Volle 2016, *Reasoning by analogy requires the left frontal pole: lesion-deficit mapping and clinical implications*, Brain, doi:10.1093/brain/aww072 (`raw/urbanski-2016-left-frontal-pole-analogy.md`). The paper the three ingests above cite second-hand for "causally required"; same lab as Hobeika et al. 2016. `n = 27` chronic (> 2 months) single focal frontal lesions of mixed aetiology (haemorrhage, tumour excision, epilepsy surgery) against 54 age- and education-matched controls; voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping (VLSM, Brunner–Munzel, permutation family-wise-error corrected, voxels with ≥ 3 lesions only), tract-disconnection analysis, and receiver-operating-characteristic analysis. The lesion set covers 74% of the frontal lobes and nothing outside them, so parietal and temporal *necessity* is untestable in this design.

**The task is the page's `I38` and `I36` designs run as a lesion study.** A source set on the left, two target sets on the right; choose the target that matches the source **on the relation among its elements** (Analogy) or **on a perceptual attribute** (Match) — same stimuli, same format, same response mode, 11.5 s deadline. Six geometric/mathematical schemas (proportion, subtraction, addition, mirroring, symmetry, progression) applied to identity, size, number, brightness or texture, with the schema-irrelevant features varied between source and target to block perceptual matching. Crossed with whether the schema is supplied:

| | Schema stated in the instruction | Schema must be found |
|---|---|---|
| **Relational** | AnalogyApply (48 trials) | AnalogyFind (48) |
| **Perceptual** | MatchApply (28) | MatchFind (28) |

| Reading | Result |
|---|---|
| VLSM on **Analogy** accuracy | One left rostral prefrontal cluster, MNI **−31/51/−3**, `z = 3.48`, **0.33 cc**, Brodmann area 47/10, at the rostral junction of superior and middle frontal gyri extending into *pars triangularis*; two smaller clusters at −34/41/3 and orbitofrontal −30/41/−10 |
| VLSM on the **Analogy index** (`(analogy − match)/mean`) | MNI **−35/48/9**, 1.57 cc, BA 10/47/45/46 — not fully overlapping the above but sharing BA 10/47 |
| VLSM on **Match** accuracy | **No significant voxel anywhere** |
| Find index (Find − Apply) in cluster patients vs the rest | **n.s.** |
| Cross index (cross- − intra-dimensional) | **n.s.** |
| Executive battery (Frontal Assessment Battery, Stroop, lexical and semantic fluency, Mini-Mental State Examination), age, education, lesion volume, lesion delay | **n.s.** between analogy-impaired and analogy-preserved groups; semantic knowledge preserved group-wide |
| Lesions of the analogy-**preserved** patients | Overlap in **right** prefrontal cortex |

**(1) The integrator is necessary, and the specificity is a double contrast.** Cluster patients are worse than the other patients on Analogy and not on Match (significant group difference on the index), and the whole-brain map for Match is empty. Region × task, not region alone — which is what distinguishes a required component from a region that a hard task happens to load. The coordinate sits ~2 cm dorsomedial to Hobeika et al. 2016's meta-analytic analogy-general peak (−48/44/−8); both are left BA 10/47, and no result here separates them.

**(2) The site is a convergence hub, and the *number* of severed afferents is the load-bearing variable.** Disconnection of the left inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus (occipital feed), uncinate fasciculus (temporal feed), fronto-marginal tract and anterior thalamic radiation each predicted an analogy deficit; **no right-hemisphere tract did**, in either task. The graded version is the stronger result: the number of severed tracts *that connect to the VLSM cluster* correlates with analogy accuracy (`rs = −0.553`, `P = 0.003`) and with the analogy index (`rs = −0.416`), while the number of severed tracts *not* connecting to it correlates with neither — although it does correlate with lesion volume (`rs = 0.693`), which is the control that makes the first correlation mean something. The authors' reading, and it is the architecturally useful one: this small site is critical because several converging tracts must be **conjointly** cut to produce the deficit — a Geschwind-style disconnection at a fan-in point, not a locally stored function. That is `T289` Position B's *afferent diversity* mechanism with a dose-response curve attached, and it is the first one in the wiki.

**(3) The deficit is invisible to every standard executive test.** Impaired and preserved groups are indistinguishable on the whole battery including the Stroop interference effect, which also removes the inhibition account of analogy failure (Morrison et al. 2004; Krawczyk et al. 2008) — response inhibition maps to right and medial frontal cortex, not here. Second reference measurement for `I37`, and a stronger one than the first because both arms are quantitative and matched.

**(4) It breaks the Find/Apply assignment this page carries, using the same task and the same lab.** The subprocess section above assigns schema induction to inferior frontal sulcus on a Find > Apply contrast (Aichelburg et al. 2016). In the lesion data the rostrolateral patients are impaired **equally** on Find and Apply, and equally on cross- and intra-dimensional trials. So the region is required even when the schema is handed to the subject *in words* and the only remaining demand is applying it across two sets: what is lost is **relational matching/integration**, not inference, not schema induction, not remote mapping. The null is underpowered (`n = 5` contributed to the cluster) and the cross-dimensional trend runs in the expected direction, so this bounds rather than refutes.

> **The null lands on a reconciliation the wiki records as untested.** [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontal-pole.md]] carries Chau et al. 2025's proposal that Mole et al. 2025's contrary *right*-frontal analogy result is explained by that task requiring analogy **identification** rather than **application**, with only the latter needing the frontopolar transform. Urbanski is the within-study version of that manipulation and it comes back flat. Identify/apply is not Find/Apply, so this is not a refutation — but the one region × manipulation interaction available to support the reconciliation is absent, and the same study finds right-frontal lesions in the analogy-*preserved* group and no right-hemisphere tract effect.

**The clinical short version is a difference score, and that is why it beats the accuracy score.** 28 AnalogyFind + 28 MatchFind trials, ~15 min, item reliability (Spearman–Brown) 0.761. Discriminating anatomically-defined left rostrolateral damage (`y > 44`, `x < −25`; 7 of 27 patients): AnalogyFind accuracy `AUC = 0.925`, **Analogy index `AUC = 0.954`**, MatchFind accuracy `AUC = 0.707` (n.s.). At index `< −33%` (control mean − 1.5 SD): sensitivity 85.7%, specificity 90–95%. Exported as `I39` on [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]] — the contrast against a matched non-relational control is a better localiser than the relational score itself, on the same trials.

## The 247-patient test: a right frontal network, and no parietal necessity

Mole et al. 2025. The design answers two of this page's own open problems — the frontal-only coverage of Urbanski et al. 2016, and the absence of any causal evidence for the IPL node — and returns the opposite hemisphere for the first question and a null for the second.

**Sample and method.** 247 unilateral focal lesions (tumour or stroke), of which 176 have ≥70% of the lesion inside frontal (`n = 102`; left 47 / right 55) or posterior (`n = 74`; left 32 / right 42) territory, against 81 controls matched on age, sex, education and National Adult Reading Test score. Imaging in 237. Lesion volume does not differ between frontal and posterior or between left and right frontal groups, and does not correlate with any score. Anatomy is inferred by **graph lesion-deficit mapping**: the brain is a graph whose nodes are anatomical loci and whose edges index shared damage, deficit and nuisance structure, and a *layered* non-parametric Bayesian stochastic block model separates communities shaped by the behaviour from communities shaped by the incidental spatial structure of lesions. Reported as entropy against a null model — ART 515,399 vs 776,413 nats, DRT 476,362 vs 725,748, RAPM 451,868 vs 699,072.

**The two tasks.**

| Test | Structure | Control |
|---|---|---|
| **Analogical Reasoning Test (ART)** — explicitly *inspired by Urbanski et al. 2016* | 24 items: a source set of coloured numbers on the left, two target sets on the right; pick the target following the same abstract rule. Rules **Progression** (numerical value, colour intensity or size increases/decreases) and **Odd-one-out**; half **intra-dimensional** (rule on the same feature in source and target), half **cross-dimensional** | 12-item Perceptual Matching Baseline: same stimuli, same layout, "looks most similar" |
| **Deductive Reasoning Test (DRT)** — entirely verbal | 24 three-sentence relational syllogisms, valid/invalid balanced; **18 determinate**, **6 indeterminate** (premises fix no ordering: *Chris > Helen, Chris > Dorothy, therefore Helen > Dorothy?*) | — |

| Reading | Result |
|---|---|
| Perceptual Matching Baseline | **No group difference anywhere** (all ≥96%) |
| ART overall | Frontal 67.79 < Posterior 77.38 and controls 79.40 (both `P < 0.001`); **Right frontal 63.19 < Left frontal 73.19** (`P < 0.05`) |
| ART by rule | **Odd-one-out: right-lateralised** (R 57.25 vs L 71.29, `P < 0.01`). **Progression: frontal but not lateralised** (R 69.14 vs L 75.22, n.s.) |
| ART by feature | Colour and size right-lateralised (both `P < 0.01`); numerical value frontal but not lateralised |
| ART by dimensionality | Cross-dimensional harder than intra-dimensional in *every* group, and right frontal < left frontal on **both** (`P < 0.05`) — so the laterality is not a difficulty effect |
| DRT overall | Frontal 71.67 < controls 82.79 (`P < 0.01`); left vs right frontal **n.s.** |
| DRT by determinacy | **Indeterminate: right-lateralised** (R 48.39 vs L 65.58, `P < 0.05`; frontal 55.89 < posterior 70.56 and controls 76.06). **Determinate: frontal but not lateralised** (frontal 76.69 < controls 84.79) |
| RAPM | Frontal < posterior and controls; **right frontal < left frontal** (`P < 0.05`), replicated in the 65 newly recruited patients (right `n = 14`, 5.64 ± 2.10; left `n = 10`, 7.30 ± 2.50) |
| Inferred networks | **ART**: right middle and inferior frontal gyri, hierarchy dominated by **caudal middle frontal gyrus**. **DRT**: right frontal, more **rostral** and more distributed, inferior + superior frontal gyri. **RAPM**: a still more extensive right frontal network dominated by **superior frontal gyrus**. Only remote node in any of the three: right dorsal postcentral gyrus (ART, RAPM) |
| Same-cohort positive control | **S fluency**: left dorsal superior and middle frontal gyri. **Stroop**: extensive left frontal centred on inferior frontal gyrus. Behaviourally, left frontal patients are worse than right on both |

**(1) The laterality is the result, and the same-cohort control is what makes it hard to dismiss.** A right-lateralised finding in a lesion series is normally attributable to lesion coverage: the left effects were there and the sample could not see them. Two tasks run in the *same* patients recover strongly left-lateralised networks in the expected places, which converts an argument about sampling into a measurement. This generalises past neuroanatomy and is exported as `I40` on [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]] — an absence claim needs a positive control *in the direction being denied*, measured on the same instrument and the same population.

**(2) It contradicts this page's central coordinate head-on, and `T336` is the row.** The ART is modelled on Urbanski's task; Urbanski's 0.33 cc necessary cluster is in the **left** frontal pole, no right-hemisphere tract disconnection predicted any deficit there, and the analogy-*preserved* patients' lesions overlapped in **right** prefrontal cortex. Hobeika's meta-analytic peak is left and the right rostrolateral homologue is not significant in either content domain. The three exculpations one reaches for first are all closed by the sources: not task format (same design family), not verbal lateralisation (the right network is recovered on the *entirely verbal* DRT), not difficulty (right < left on easy and hard trials alike). What is open is method — mass-univariate VLSM at `n = 27` against stochastic block modelling at `n = 247`, never run through one pipeline — and score definition: Urbanski's localiser is the **analogy − match difference index** (`I39`), and Mole reports raw accuracy with the matched control used only as a group-level null. See `T336`.

**(3) The parietal node has causal evidence at last, and it is negative.** Posterior patients (`n = 74`, including 32 left) are unimpaired on ART and on DRT overall, and the block model gives posterior cortex a minimal contribution to all three tasks. This page assigns IPL/IPS the **first-order relation store** — the representation the frontopolar integrator is supposed to compare — on activation evidence that scales with relation count. Necessity does not follow: on this test, the store is not required for the relational decision. Two readings, and the wiki cannot choose between them yet: either the first-order representation is reconstructible elsewhere (which would make the parietal signal a readout rather than the content, [[wiki/concepts/constitutive-vs-enabling.md]]), or these two tasks hold too few relations to load it — the ART compares two sets on one rule, and `I36`'s load arm is not run here. Note this null is also a **frontal** finding in disguise: it is the same null that refutes the multiple-demand and parieto-frontal-integration accounts, which are the only two theories on this page that predicted a posterior contribution at all.

**(4) Analogy, deduction and fluid intelligence come out of one network, at three scales.** ART, DRT and RAPM all map right frontal, with the extent ordered ART (caudal, focal) ⊂ DRT (rostral, distributed) ⊂ RAPM (most extensive, superior-frontal-dominated). At the resolution of *necessity* rather than activation, the Cattell–Horn–Carroll claim that reasoning abilities are the substance of `Gf` is supported and the analogy/matrix dissociation this page reports from activation data (`T335`) is not reproduced. The two results are not formally incompatible — a shared necessary network is compatible with a dissociating activation profile, and the sources use different tasks, different formats and different evidence types — but any account of `T335` now has to explain why lesions do not separate what activation does.

**(5) The authors' proposed common process is a capacity claim, not a binding claim, and it is the page's only rival to the integrator account.** Odd-one-out and indeterminate deduction are the two right-lateralised conditions; their shared property, per the authors, is that the rule is novel and **several representations must be actively maintained in parallel** — the Odd-one-out rule has to be applied to source and target set simultaneously, and an indeterminate premise pair yields two independent relations that cannot be collapsed onto one linear ordering. Progression items and determinate syllogisms both admit a single sequential structure and are the two non-lateralised conditions. This explicitly rejects Prado et al.'s visuospatial-continuum account, which predicts the *opposite* determinacy split; it partly agrees with Goel et al.'s right-frontal-for-indeterminate claim while denying its left-frontal-for-determinate half.

> **`(brainstorm)` — why a builder should take this seriously even if `T336` resolves against Position B.** The wiki's frontopolar story is *convergence*: a small site where many afferents meet, whose damage grades with fan-in. Mole's story is *parallel occupancy*: how many novel, mutually irreducible representations can be held live at once. These make opposite architectural predictions under scaling. A convergence bottleneck degrades with the number of **sources**; a parallel-occupancy limit degrades with the number of **simultaneous incomparable structures** at fixed source count. Both are cheap to sweep separately in a model and no wiki architecture reports either. The discriminating item already exists in the task set: an indeterminate premise pair holds exactly two relations from exactly one content feed.

## The dissociation that matters most: relational demand is not difficulty

Second-order problems are almost always harder than first-order ones, so any second-order > first-order contrast is confounded with effort. Two designs break the confound in **opposite directions**, and RLPFC follows relations both times:

| Design | Difficulty ordering | Relational ordering | RLPFC | DLPFC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Working memory: hold **4 items**, **7 items**, or **4 items + 3 relations** (arrows: "Q comes before Z") — Wendelken et al. 2008a | 7 items > 4 items + 3 relations > 4 items | 4+3 relations > both item-only loads | Higher for 4 items + 3 relations than for 7 items; **does not distinguish 7 from 4** | Scales monotonically with load |
| Propositional analogy, 2 × 2: relation cue (named term vs example pair) × task (**compare** two relations vs **complete** one) — Wendelken et al. 2008b, *corrected from the primary* | Set by the **cue** factor only: example 4.8 s vs term 4.0 s; compare = complete in response time; accuracy 82–84% in all four cells (`F < 1`) | Set by the **task** factor: compare requires a relation-between-relations, complete requires transferring one relation and generating a filler | Follows the **task** factor (compare > complete) and ignores the cue factor (`F < 1`) | No effect of either factor (the functional DLPFC ROI, `F < 1`) |

**Why this is the page's most exportable content.** It is a **crossed design**: relational demand high / difficulty low, and difficulty high / relational demand low, in the same subjects with the same stimuli. *(Corrected: the second row is an **orthogonal** design rather than a reversal — difficulty and relational demand sit on different factors of one 2 × 2, so each can be read with the other held fixed; and DLPFC does not scale with difficulty there. See the section below.)* It converts "does this system integrate relations?" from an unanswerable question about an internal state into a two-cell contrast on any effort-like read-out. Recorded as `I36` on [[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]].

A second consequence, for the wiki's load accounting: **items and relations are separate currencies.** Three relations over four items cost the RLPFC more than three extra items cost it, and cost the DLPFC less. [[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]'s capacity estimates are all in items; Halford's relational complexity ([[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]]) is in roles-to-be-integrated; this is the design that shows they dissociate in the substrate rather than only in theory.

## Compare vs complete: what the primary shows

Wendelken et al. 2008b. Cue on screen 1 s, then joined by the probe until response — so **maintenance demand is minimal**, unlike the delay design of Bunge et al. 2005. Activity modelled as a zero-duration event at the trial midpoint; a secondary model uses epochs of response-time length. ROIs are the left RLPFC peak of Bunge et al. 2005 (−45/45/6), its right mirror, and functional ROIs from all-correct-trials > baseline.

| Condition | Cue | Probe | Operation (authors' task analysis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Term Compare | "worn on" | boots : foot | Retrieve R(probe); compare with the named R |
| 2 Term Complete | "worn on" | glove | Apply the named R to the item; generate the filler |
| 3 Example Compare | shoe : foot | glove : hand | Retrieve R(A,B), retrieve R(C,D), **compare** |
| 4 Example Complete | shoe : foot | glove | Retrieve R(A,B), **transfer** it to C, **generate** D |

| Region | Task (compare vs complete) | Cue (term vs example) | Task × cue | vs fixation baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Left RLPFC (prior-study ROI) | **compare > complete**, `F₁,₁₉ = 17.5` | none, `F < 1` | none | left RLPFC (−45/42/−3) above baseline for both compare cells, neither complete cell (`p < .001` unc.) |
| Right RLPFC (mirror ROI) | **compare > complete**, `F₁,₁₉ = 47.3` | none | none | — |
| Left VLPFC (functional) | none | **term > example**, `p < .05` | none | active in all four |
| Left DLPFC (functional) | none (`F < 1`) | none | none | active in all four |
| Medial BA 10 (−6/63/9) | complete > compare, `p < .005` unc. only | — | — | **deactivated in all four** |
| Dorsal "Raven" RLPFC ROIs (41/45/6; −31/45/10) | same direction; significant only at 41/45/6 (`F₁,₁₉ = 8.5`) | — | — | parameter estimates almost uniformly **negative** |

Whole-brain compare > complete (`p < .001`): right RLPFC (45/45/−3) — left RLPFC only at `p < .005` — plus right DLPFC (BA 9), superior frontal gyrus (BA 8), supplementary motor area, bilateral premotor cortex, left superior parietal cortex. Response-time-epoch model keeps right RLPFC, supplementary motor area and right angular gyrus and drops DLPFC. Complete > compare: **nothing** at the standard threshold, in either model.

**Four second-hand claims this corrects.**

| Second-hand claim (source on this page) | Primary |
|---|---|
| Completion ≫ evaluation in difficulty; RLPFC higher for the *easier* problem (Vendetti & Bunge 2014 → `I36` arm 2) | Compare and complete do not differ in response time or accuracy. The only difficulty difference is the cue factor (example +0.8 s), and RLPFC ignores it. Orthogonality, not reversal |
| Bilateral RLPFC **and VLPFC** higher for comparison (Parsons & Davies 2022) | VLPFC has **no** task effect; its one effect is term > example. "BA 10/47" is the RLPFC peak label |
| Completion **engaged** left BA 8 and medial BA 10 (Parsons & Davies 2022; `T335`) | Complete > compare survives only at `p < .005` uncorrected, and medial BA 10 is *below* baseline in every condition — completion deactivates it less. The authors state the design is not suited to reading deactivations |
| Completion strictly adds inference to comparison, so adding a demand removed activation (Parsons & Davies 2022; `T330`) | Not under the authors' task analysis: each task has an operation the other lacks (compare has a second relation retrieval and a comparison; complete has transfer and filler generation). The contrast is between two operations, not a nested subtraction |

**What the primary adds for a model.**

- **The comparator reads a cue-invariant relation code.** No task × cue interaction anywhere: the RLPFC compare effect is the same whether the relation was handed over as a word or had to be extracted from an exemplar pair. The authors' reading: integration "operates on an internal representation of relations, and not directly on the external stimulus representations." Converges with Urbanski et al. 2016's Find = Apply lesion null (above) — the comparator does not care how the relation was obtained `(brainstorm: an L2 probe — a model's candidate comparator should be invariant to whether a relation arrives as a symbol or is inferred from a pair; one that is not is reading stimulus tokens, not relations)`.
- **Applying a relation does not need the comparator.** Relation transfer + filler generation (A : B :: C : ?) runs with RLPFC not above baseline. `(brainstorm)` The completion format is the one machine analogy benchmarks mostly use (embedding arithmetic, A : B :: C : ? cloze), so they may never exercise the component this page is about; the evaluation format (A : B :: C : D?) is the one that does.
- **RLPFC is not a buffer.** Engagement holds with the cue still on screen, so a working-memory delay is not a prerequisite.
- **Proposed signal flow** (authors, untested): items active in posterior cortex → VLPFC extracts the relations and holds links to them → RLPFC compares the VLPFC products. The anatomical premise — RLPFC "interacts primarily with other PFC subregions" (Ramnani & Owen 2004) — is macaque-derived and predates the human-only RLPFC↔IPL edge (Mars et al. 2011, below) and the posterior tracts terminating in Urbanski's cluster (above); the functional version (the comparator reads retrieved relation products, not raw content) survives either way.
- **VLPFC's cue effect runs opposite to the Find/Apply result below.** Here a *named* relation (≈ Apply) costs VLPFC more than an exemplar (≈ Find), read by the authors as name-cued retrieval of a relational concept being harder; Aichelburg et al. 2016 find inferior frontal sulcus (BA 45) higher for *Find*. Different ROIs, materials and baselines; neither is replicated (L3 localisation, no registry row).
- **Ventral verbal / dorsal visuospatial, at the between-study grade.** All four analogy peaks (this study + Bunge et al. 2005, z = −3 to −12) are ventral to all seven Raven peaks (z = 6 to 23), and the dorsal Raven ROIs are deactivated on verbal analogy. The authors flag that the two tasks differ in much more than material. This is the earliest instance of the axis Hobeika et al. 2016 and Wendelken et al. 2012 later measured (`T338`).
- **Anterior PFC is split by function, per the authors' coordinate comparison.** Subgoal processing (Braver & Bongiolatti 2002), which integrates a held item with a subtask outcome, peaks at nearly the RLPFC site; cognitive branching and prospective memory peak *anterior* to it (Gilbert et al. 2006: task coordination at the most rostral part). Mnemonic post-retrieval monitoring is recast as the same operation — compare a retrieved item with the current probe. Bears on whether [[wiki/entities/lateral-frontal-pole.md]]'s "concurrent streams" family and this page's integrator are one node.
- A computational reference, not a component: in SHRUTI (Shastri & Ajjanagadde 1993), comparison requires a dedicated circuit that brings together several relational instances (Wendelken 2003); the authors propose RLPFC as part of that comparator.

## Transitive inference with the premises on screen: frontal integrator, parietal and hippocampal encoders

Wendelken & Bunge 2010. Every earlier human transitive-inference imaging study used **memorised** premises (Nagode & Pardo 2002; Heckers et al. 2004; Greene et al. 2006), so their hippocampal effects mix relational memory with relational integration. Here four novel source relations between coloured balls stay on screen with one target relation, and the subject judges whether the target is valid. Each trial has a new array. Conditions are intermixed with no cue. Three of the four source relations share an item with the target, and the target never visually copies a source relation.

| Accuracy, response time | **Direct** (one source relation suffices) | **Inference** (two must be chained) |
|---|---|---|
| **General**: "basket", symmetric item–item association (equality only) | 98.5%, 3.0 s | 87.6%, 3.9 s |
| **Specific**: balance scale, ordered relation (heavier / equal), needs predicate–argument structure | 93.1%, 2.8 s | 80.1%, 4.7 s |

Swapping one ball's colour in the target turns a direct trial into an inference trial. Response-time interaction (`F = 36.5`): ordered relations cost more than symmetric ones **only on inference trials**.

| Contrast | Result |
|---|---|
| Inference > direct | **Right RLPFC** (BA 10, 33/54/6, `T = 5.37`, 28 voxels) is the top cluster; also right premotor BA 6 and medial frontal BA 8. Holds in a model with response-time-length epochs |
| Same, hippocampus | **No voxel**, even at `p < .005`. Four hippocampal ROIs (two literature spheres, anterior and posterior template halves) show no inference effect or interaction (all `p > .2`) |
| Specific > general | Bilateral **SPL** extending into IPL/precuneus (BA 7/40; largest cluster left −9/−51/60, 145 voxels), right premotor, bilateral middle occipital gyrus, thalamus, cerebellum–parahippocampal cluster. The response-time-epoch model adds left DLPFC BA 9 |
| Same, hippocampus | **Posterior** ROIs only (template `F = 4.1`, `p = .04`; sphere `F = 5.2`, `p = .02`); anterior null |
| β-series from right RLPFC, inference > baseline | RLPFC, DLPFC, VLPFC, medial frontal/anterior cingulate, SPL/IPL, occipital, thalamus |
| β-series from right RLPFC, **inference > direct** | **One cluster only: right hippocampus / parahippocampal gyrus** (27/−18/−15, relatively anterior, 11 voxels). Its own activation shows no inference or encoding effect |
| Inference > direct, split by relation type | General: bilateral RLPFC (right 30/54/6; left, more ventral, −30/48/−3). Specific: right RLPFC only, more **lateral** (45/57/9). Region × inference × relation `F = 17.6`, `p = .001` |

**What it settles or sharpens on this page.**

| Point | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Integration and encoding dissociate in one 2 × 2** | RLPFC tracks chaining two relations and ignores their format. SPL and posterior hippocampus track ordered-vs-symmetric format and ignore chaining. This is the page's store/integrator split measured in one design rather than across studies |
| **The integrator's read is selective; the store's activity is not** | Hippocampal activation is equal on inference and direct trials, but its coupling with RLPFC is higher on inference trials. The authors' reading: RLPFC draws on hippocampal relation codes while integrating. `(brainstorm)` A per-trial read gate on a store whose own activity does not change. This is `G21`'s switchable fan-in, with the switch visible only in coupling and not in the store's activation |
| **No known wire** | Macaque anterior PFC has no known direct hippocampal projection (Petrides & Pandya 2007). Indirect relays (e.g. via DLPFC) are not distinguished by the data |
| **Relation type moves the RLPFC peak, within one content domain** | The region × inference × relation interaction is a sub-region split inside RLPFC with the domain held visuospatial. Same shape as `T338` Position B, on a different axis (symmetric vs ordered relations, not semantic vs visuospatial). Both formats arrive from the same on-screen feed, so input provenance, B's proposed mechanism, does not explain it. The authors flag it as unreplicated |
| **Laterality** | Right RLPFC for a visuospatial task, attributed by the authors to hemisphere-local inputs. Hobeika et al. 2016's visuospatial analogy map is left. This is activation, not necessity, so it does not enter `T336`'s lesion comparison |
| **Memorised premises may not need the integrator** | Memorised-premise studies report DLPFC or left inferior frontal BA 47, not RLPFC (Acuna et al. 2002; Heckers et al. 2004). The authors' speculation: repeated exposure builds a mnemonic ordering that removes the need for online integration. Same direction as the offload reading in **Development** below |

**For a model.**

- **Two transitive-inference regimes, two solvers.** Premises in working memory: a frontal integrator chains two relations at query time and the hippocampus only encodes them. Premises learned over training: a hippocampus-dependent ordering, built slowly and improved by sleep (Ellenbogen et al. 2007, second-hand). Second-hand mouse lesion data (Van der Jeugd et al. 2009): hippocampal lesion **before** training impairs later transitive inference, and lesion **after** training does not. So the store builds the ordering and is not needed to read it. Bears on `T334` and [[wiki/entities/remerge.md]]. **The trained regime's one human lesion study proposes the same split independently.** It assigns explicit (in-view) inference to dorsolateral PFC and implicit (trained) inference to vmPFC. But it cannot test the split: both control groups are at chance on the transitive items. vmPFC lesions push interior pairs below chance, and the four dorsolateral-lesion cases sit at chance (0.50), like the controls (Koscik & Tranel 2012, [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]]).
- `(brainstorm)` A transitive-inference score identifies a component only if the premise regime is stated. On-screen premises test the reader's composition operator. Trained premises test the store's write. The ablated-twin test on [[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]] covers only the second regime.
- `(brainstorm)` Ordered relations cost extra only when chained. So a role-filler code pays its role cost at integration, not at encoding. Probe: one model, symmetric vs ordered relations crossed with direct vs chained queries; read the interaction, not the main effects.

**Limits.**

| Limit | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `n = 16`; voxel threshold `p < .001` uncorrected, 10-voxel extent | RLPFC cluster 28 voxels; the hippocampal coupling cluster 11 voxels |
| Inference is one-step chaining of two relations | Depth scaling (three or more premises) untested |
| The hippocampal null is fMRI | Signal drop-out hits anterior medial temporal lobe hardest ([[wiki/concepts/hippocampal-long-axis.md]]), so an anterior null is weaker than the posterior effect |
| Inference trials are harder (84% vs 96%, slower) | The response-time-epoch model keeps RLPFC. Accuracy is not equated, and there is no crossed difficulty arm as in `I36` |
| β-series correlation is undirected | "RLPFC draws on hippocampus" is a direction inferred from function, not measured |

## The focal-lesion test of verbal transitive inference: parietal necessary, rostrolateral not

Waechter et al. 2013. The first focal-lesion study of transitive inference to target RLPFC or parietal cortex. It answers the open problem below on the parietal node's causal evidence, and the answer is the opposite of Mole et al. 2025's.

**Sample.** All male Vietnam Head Injury Study (Phase 3) veterans: penetrating head injuries ~40 years before testing, so aetiology, injury date and recovery period are shared.

| Group | `n` | Lesion | Matching |
|---|---|---|---|
| RLPFC | 5 (2 L, 2 R, 1 bilateral) | Mostly BA 10 (10–31%), some BA 9/11/12/45/47; nothing outside frontal lobe | Age, education, pre-injury Armed Forces Qualification Test, total lesion volume, Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS) performance/full IQ, Wechsler Memory Scale (WMS) general and working memory all n.s. Verbal IQ is higher in RLPFC patients (110 vs 95, `p < .05`) and is used as a covariate |
| Parietal | 7 (2 L, 4 R, 1 bilateral) | Mostly BA 7/40 (7–44%), some BA 1–3/39; nothing outside parietal lobe | as above |
| Controls | 6 | none | as above |

**Task.** 48 verbal "four-term with three relevant terms" problems (e.g. *Mary taller than Fiona; Mary taller than Carol; Fiona taller than Rosemary; Mary taller than Rosemary?*). The fourth term is a distractor that raises the number of candidate models. Balance: 24 valid, 12 inconsistent, 12 indeterminate; 27 **chained** (Level 1: premises in order) and 21 **scrambled** (Level 2: the spatial array must be "flipped", or two relations held at once). The Level 1/2 definition and the stimuli follow Waltz et al. 1999 (citation stripped in the clipping; identified from the description). Premises stay on screen. Self-paced, 40 s limit, and **non-responses are scored as errors**.

|---|---|---|---|

| Scrambled, `t` vs .50 *(wiki-computed)* | `t(5) = 2.3`, `p = .07` | `t(4) = 2.8`, `p = .05` | `t(6) = −1.4`, `p = .21` |

|---|---|

| Parietal vs RLPFC | −.18, `p = .058` |

**What it settles or sharpens on this page.**

| Point | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **The parietal node's first positive necessity result** | BA 7/40 damage takes verbal transitive inference to chance. Working-memory scores are matched, so the authors read this as a deficit "over and above general working memory requirements". It contradicts Mole et al. 2025's posterior null on a verbal relational-syllogism task of the same family. Opened as `T412` |
| **RLPFC lesions spare the task** | Controls and RLPFC patients do not differ, on chained or scrambled items. Wendelken & Bunge 2010 put the top inference cluster in right RLPFC, also with premises on screen. That is activation and this is lesion, so the two are not in contradiction. But this is the only necessity test of the on-screen chaining operator, and it does not find it frontal |
| **Waltz et al. 1999's frontal × scrambled interaction is not reproduced** | Waltz's frontal-variant frontotemporal dementia patients failed scrambled items only. Here, with focal BA 10 lesions and similar stimuli, there is no group × difficulty interaction. The authors attribute Waltz's effect to diffuse dorsolateral and working-memory damage in dementia. *Floor caveat:* controls are only marginally above chance on scrambled items (`p = .07`). By the trained-premise rule on [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]], the intact system must pass an item class before its loss can be scored, so the Level 2 arm cannot carry a null interaction |
| **The authors' reconciliation is a format split, not a regime split** | Verbal premises need three steps: (1) map the proposition onto a representation that exemplifies the spatial relation; (2) hold and integrate; (3) resolve. Pictorial premises (Wendelken & Bunge 2010, and a premise-integration fMRI study whose citation is stripped from the clipping) arrive already exemplified, which skips step 1. The authors place step 1 in parietal cortex (mental-model theory: reasoning by building and manipulating a spatial model) and suggest that skipping it shifts processing to PFC. This is untested: neither format nor step was manipulated |

**For a model.**

- `(brainstorm)` **A third axis for the transitive-inference score.** The two-regime bullet above splits by where the premises live (working memory vs trained store). This adds how they are **encoded**. Propositional premises need a construction step before composition; exemplified ones do not. So "transitive inference" names at least three separately damageable operations: *construct* the ordered array (parietal, on this lesion evidence), *compose* it at query time (RLPFC, on activation evidence only), and *build* it over training (hippocampus, on second-hand rodent lesion evidence). Each needs its own ablation. The construct step is `G73`'s missing representation-constructor, observed as a necessary block in tissue.
- `(brainstorm)` **One parietal patient is far below chance, which a direction-flipped reader would produce.** Patient 2341 (left BA 7/39/40) scores .229, i.e. 11 of 48 (binomial `p = .0001` against .5). On this item mix, a reader that keeps the relation but **reverses its polarity** scores exactly .25: the 24 valid items become "inconsistent" and are rejected, the 12 inconsistent ones become "valid" and are accepted, and the 12 indeterminate ones stay indeterminate and are correctly rejected. A yes-bias, a no-bias or guessing all give .50. If this holds, the lesion destroyed the mapping from word order to array direction, not the array. It is the parietal counterpart of the vmPFC below-chance reversal (Koscik & Tranel 2012, [[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]]). Confound: timeouts are scored as errors and no per-patient timeout count is given, so a patient who times out a lot also lands below chance. Machine probe: score a reasoner on a valid/inconsistent/indeterminate 2:1:1 mix and read .25 as a polarity error, not a capacity error.
- `(brainstorm)` **On correct trials, chained items are slower than scrambled ones.** A single linear-array builder whose cost grows when premises arrive out of order predicts the reverse. The item-level cause cannot be checked, because the clipping's Appendix A scrambled table is garbled.

**Limits.**

| Limit | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `n = 5 / 7 / 6`; the key contrast is `p = .047` after Tukey correction | One study at the minimum sample size for a group effect; the parietal-vs-RLPFC contrast is a trend |
| Penetrating traumatic injury ~40 years earlier | Chronic reorganisation is unknown; diffuse or contre-coup damage is argued against from computed tomography scans but cannot be excluded |
| Lobar groups are Brodmann-area-weighted, not voxel maps | Lesions extend into BA 39 and BA 1–3 (parietal) and BA 9/11/12/45/47 (frontal). "BA 10 spared the task" means "these mostly-BA 10 lesions did" |
| No same-cohort positive control for the RLPFC null | The null is an absence claim with no task on which the same RLPFC group *is* impaired (`I40`) |
| Accuracy only; timeouts count as errors | A slowing deficit and a reasoning deficit are not separated. Parietal response-time SD is 2–3× the other groups' |
| Validity type (valid / inconsistent / indeterminate) not analysed | Mole et al. 2025's only verbal laterality effect is on indeterminate items, and that comparison cannot be made here |

## The four subprocesses, and the assignment that has not been earned

Parsons & Davies 2022 take the decomposition every structure-mapping architecture assumes — **access → mapping → inference → schema induction** ([[wiki/entities/sme.md]], [[wiki/entities/lisa.md]], [[wiki/entities/macfac.md]], Companion) — and ask which of the four each node implements.

| Subprocess | What the architecture claims | Best neural candidate | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Access** — spontaneous retrieval of a source from long-term memory | Optional; omitted entirely by four-term tasks where both analogs are on screen | VLPFC (controlled semantic retrieval) + hippocampus | **None direct.** Never investigated in fMRI. Inferred from *relational retrieval*: DLPFC, VLPFC and hippocampus all scale with the relational complexity of recognised word pairs (Giovanello & Schacter 2012), and hippocampus activates for subliminally-encoded relational inference (Reber et al. 2012, 2014) |
| **Mapping** — correspondence by relational integration | Obligatory; "mapping makes the analogy" | RLPFC | The only one with converging evidence: present in every analogy contrast (Hobeika et al. 2016), causally required (Urbanski et al. 2016 — a 0.33 cc left BA 10/47 lesion cluster, specific to Analogy over a matched perceptual Match control, see above), functionally identified as relational integration. Still only *activation*, never *representation* — no MVPA has asked whether RLPFC voxels encode a correspondence |
| **Inference** — transfer of unmapped source propositions | Optional; downstream of mapping | Left middle frontal gyrus (BA 8) + medial frontopolar (BA 10); hippocampus | One study (Wendelken et al. 2008b), with the sign reversed (below) — *corrected:* the BA 8 / medial BA 10 effect is `p < .005` uncorrected and the medial BA 10 one is a smaller deactivation; no region is above baseline for completion beyond those active for every condition |
| **Schema induction** — the analogy itself becomes a reusable structure | LISA yes, Companion has no such stage | VLPFC/inferior frontal sulcus (BA 45) for the induction; VMPFC↔hippocampus for the store | Weakest. VMPFC is a poor analogy correlate — the network is lateral, and VMPFC codes subjective **value**, which analogy tasks never manipulate |

**The Find/Apply manipulation is the one clean handle on schema induction — and the lesion data say RLPFC is not on its far side.** Urbanski et al. 2016's rostrolateral patients are impaired equally under Find and Apply (see **The causal test** above), so the region's necessity survives handing the subject the schema in words. Whatever RLPFC re-represents, the induction demand is not what damages it.

**The Find/Apply manipulation is the one clean handle on schema induction.** Give the source analog either as an instance the rule must be extracted from (*Find*) or as the stated rule (*Apply*). Inferior frontal sulcus (BA 45) is higher for *Find*, i.e. for **rule induction when no rule is supplied** (Aichelburg et al. 2016), with RLPFC proposed to re-represent the induced rule at a higher level of abstraction. Behaviourally the two conditions dissociate by modality: verbal analogies are *slower* under Find (Wendelken et al. 2008b: example cue 4.8 s vs term cue 4.0 s, with RLPFC unmoved by the cue, `F < 1`, and VLPFC higher for the *term* cue), visuospatial ones are not (Volle et al. 2010; Aichelburg et al. 2016) — attributed to subjects accumulating the relational schema across repeated Find trials, which is [[wiki/concepts/schema-assimilation.md]] happening inside the control condition.

### Three results that break the pipeline reading

**(a) The phase with no mapping in it produces more activation than the phase with mapping in it.** Two studies split a trial into encoding / mapping-inference / response phases and found RLPFC, DLPFC *and* VLPFC more active during **encoding** (Krawczyk et al. 2010; Volle et al. 2010). Neither study had an access demand, so there was nothing to retrieve. Parsons' reading: telling a subject an analogy is coming installs a **goal state**, and the encoding window is then filled with anticipatory guesses at the missing term — many small analogies rather than a pre-analogical stage. If that is right, the phase-locked design measures the *instruction*, not the subprocess, and every stage-decomposed fMRI result in this literature inherits the confound (`L0-INSTR`).

**(b) Adding a demand *removed* activation.** Comparison (all four terms given, judge the match) vs completion (three terms, infer the fourth) — completion strictly adds inference. Completion engaged left BA 8 and medial BA 10; **bilateral RLPFC and VLPFC (BA 10/47) were higher for the easier comparison condition** (Wendelken et al. 2008b). Attributed to the absence of a concrete baseline: Krawczyk et al. 2010, with a baseline, recovers RLPFC and VLPFC for the mapping/inference phase. *(Corrected from the primary: VLPFC shows no compare/complete effect; complete > compare is `p < .005` uncorrected with medial BA 10 deactivated throughout; and the authors' own task analysis does not nest the two tasks — completion swaps the second relation retrieval and the comparison for transfer and filler generation, so "adding a demand" presupposes a subtraction the design does not make. See **Compare vs complete** above.)*

> **This is a caveat on this page's own instrument.** The second reversal in `I36` — RLPFC higher for *evaluate* than for *complete* — is this same contrast, read there as relational demand dissociating from difficulty. Parsons reads the identical result as a subtraction artefact. *Updated from the primary:* neither reading survives as stated. Not a difficulty reversal — compare and complete are matched in response time and accuracy, and difficulty sits on the cue factor RLPFC ignores (an orthogonal design, which still separates relational demand from difficulty). Not a nested subtraction — the two tasks are not nested. And against the fixation baseline RLPFC is above baseline for compare and not for complete, though that per-condition null is threshold-dependent and fixation is not the matched task baseline Parsons asks for.

**(c) The subprocesses may not be separable in principle.** Hummel & Holyoak's own statement is that "analog retrieval, mapping, inference, and schema induction, as well as rule-based reasoning, are all special cases of the same sequential, directional algorithm for mapping"; ARCS and MAC/FAC require access and mapping to run **simultaneously**, since structural, semantic and pragmatic constraints have to shape the memory search in real time. Mapping completes in hundreds of milliseconds. The one temporal decomposition that exists is an ERP study (Qiu et al. 2008): a negativity in rostral prefrontal cortex (BA 10) at **900–1200 ms** post-target aligned with completing the analogy, and a second in dorsal prefrontal cortex (BA 9) at **2000–2500 ms** read as verification of the inference. That is the wiki's only latency budget for analogy, and it is two components inside one 2.5 s window with no spatial resolution behind it. See `T330`.

### At the network level, the default-mode network is a cost, not a contributor

MVPA over a large analogy sample recovers four coactivation networks — visuospatial, executive, default-mode, attention-to-salience — and **default-mode activation is negatively related to analogy accuracy while executive frontoparietal activation is positively related** (Hammer et al. 2019). The open question Parsons leaves: an analogy *with* an access demand should require internally-directed retrieval, so a task that forces spontaneous access is where default-mode and executive-control networks would have to cooperate rather than trade off — untested, and the cleanest available test of whether access is a real subprocess at all ([[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]). **The cooperation regime exists outside analogy** (Spreng et al. 2010, **The task test** above): autobiographical planning engages the default network and the control network together and couples them (`z' = 1.10`), while Tower-of-London planning couples the control network to dorsal attention instead. So the trade-off Hammer measured belongs to tasks whose content is on screen. An analogy with a forced access demand is still the untested case.

## Development: the integrator is freed, not grown

Longitudinal and cross-sectional data on ages 6–18 (Wendelken et al. 2011; Whitaker 2012; Ferrer et al. 2013), `n = 165` for the behavioural trajectory.

| Age band | Second-order > first-order contrast |
|---|---|
| 7–10 | Only a small left-DLPFC cluster — RLPFC and IPL are engaged *equally* by first- and second-order trials |
| 11–14 | Bilateral DLPFC + dorsomedial prefrontal cortex |
| 15–18 | Left RLPFC + bilateral IPL — the adult pattern (Bunge et al. 2009) |

**The direction of the developmental change is the finding.** Plotted against age, RLPFC activation on second-order trials is **flat**; what falls is RLPFC activation on **first-order** trials. Structural equation modelling attributes part of that fall to cortical **thinning in IPL** — the thinner the parietal cortex, the more functionally specific the RLPFC.

> **The architectural reading `(brainstorm)`.** The integrator's apparent capacity grows because the *cheap* store downstream of it gets good enough to stop calling it. This is a scheduling/caching claim, not a capacity claim: nothing about the second-order machinery changes across a decade of development, and the entire measured gain is first-order work migrating out of the expensive component. For a machine: an integrator whose call rate is not gated will look capacity-limited even when it is not, and the way to raise effective relational capacity is to make the first-order representation self-sufficient rather than to widen the binder. This is a third mechanism for the relational shift, alongside the two in [[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]] (`T193`): not knowledge accretion, not executive maturation, but **offload**.

**White matter.** Probabilistic tractography identifies left and right RLPFC–IPL tracts. Cross-sectionally, their fractional anisotropy predicts reasoning ability *no better than a whole-brain white-matter average* — consistent with a general fluid-intelligence/white-matter relation (Chiang et al. 2009; Tamnes et al. 2010). Longitudinally, change in **left** RLPFC–IPL fractional anisotropy over ~1.5 years predicts change in reasoning after partialling out age and initial values, where the right tract and the global measure do not; the relation is mediated by processing speed. Functional connectivity moves the same way and *selectively*: RLPFC↔IPL coupling rises across childhood and adolescence while RLPFC↔superior-parietal coupling **falls**, so this is not the general long-range-connectivity increase of Fair et al. 2008.

**A methodological rule worth carrying** (`L0-INSTR`, `(brainstorm)`): the same tract is non-specific cross-sectionally and specific longitudinally. A between-subject correlation with a global confound (age, overall myelination, overall scale) cannot localise anything; only the within-subject change can. Every wiki claim that reads a capability off a between-model correlation — parameter count, layer count, training compute — is at the cross-sectional grade.

## Evolution: a new edge, not a new node

| Change | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **RLPFC↔mid-IPL resting-state connectivity exists in humans and not in macaques** — no analogue even at a much relaxed threshold | Structural-connectivity-constrained resting-state fMRI, human vs macaque (Mars et al. 2011); DLPFC seeds by contrast give closely matching frontoparietal maps in both species | The single sharpest claim on this page, and a cross-species comparison of a fitted connectivity map |
| **RLPFC may have no macaque homologue**, and its function may map onto macaque DLPFC rather than macaque frontopolar cortex | Neubert et al. 2014 | Contested; parcellation-dependent |
| **The prefrontal hierarchy is inverted.** Human fMRI places RLPFC at the apex of a rostrocaudal hierarchy (Badre & D'Esposito 2009); in the macaque, frontopolar cortex ranks *below* both DLPFC and VLPFC in a structural-connectivity hierarchy recovered by simulated annealing over the acyclic-graph approximation | Goulas et al. 2014 | Structural tract-tracing, but the "hierarchy" is an inferred ordering |
| **Frontoparietal regions have higher node degree in humans than macaques** — more highly connected hubs distributed across the network | Miranda-Dominguez et al. 2014 | Graph statistic, sensitive to parcellation ([[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]) |
| **Cortical expansion is concentrated in association cortex**, greatest in Brodmann area 10; primary motor and sensory surface area is *smaller* in humans than chimpanzees relative to total. The regions most expanded across evolution overlap those most expanded across human development | Buckner & Krienen 2013; Fjell et al. 2013 | Volumetric |
| **More neuropil per neuron in the expanded regions.** Prefrontal/parietal layer III has *fewer* pyramidal neurons spaced further apart in humans than in chimpanzees and macaques (greater horizontal spacing distance); human prefrontal and parietal pyramidal cells carry greater dendritic length, density and spine count than human primary motor or superior temporal cells | Semendeferi et al. 2011; Spocter et al. 2012; Bianchi et al. 2013 | Cytoarchitecture; small `n` per species |

**"A little goes a long way" is a specific architectural claim, and it is not the same as either side of `T289`.** Position B there (Sherwood et al. 2008) says the human capacity needed no new component, only re-weighting and a shifted growth schedule. This page's proposal is narrower and more testable: one **long-range edge** between two existing regions, plus expansion of the neuropil at its endpoints, plus a **re-ordering** of an existing hierarchy so that the frontopolar node moves from below DLPFC to above it. That is not a new operator (Position A) and not a pure re-weighting either — it is a change in the *graph*, at fixed node inventory.

This is the first source in the wiki to propose an anatomical correlate for the behavioural discontinuity, which is one of the two disjuncts in `T289`'s closing condition. It does **not** settle the tension: the same three facts are read by these authors as continuity ("small changes") and could equally be read by Position A as exactly the novelty it predicts. See the note on `T289`.

## Comparative behaviour: the discontinuity may be a *rate*, not a boundary

The review concedes more to the animals than [[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]] does — great apes (Flemming et al. 2008; Haun & Call 2009) and Old World monkeys (Flemming et al. 2013) do solve relational match-to-sample — and relocates the difference:

| Quantity | Human | Nonhuman |
|---|---|---|
| Trials to 80% on `AAAA` matches `BBBB`, not `CDEF`, by trial and error | **35** | **~400** (baboons; Flemming et al. 2013) |
| Acquisition from verbal instruction | Immediate (Cole et al. 2011) | Not available |
| Accuracy vs set size on relational match-to-sample | ≥86% at every set size | Chance on *different* trials below set size 4 (Fagot et al. 2001) |

**Why a builder should care about the middle row rather than the top one.** The wiki's discontinuity evidence is almost entirely about *what can be represented*. A 10× sample-efficiency gap on a task both species eventually solve is a different axis, and it is the axis machine models are actually measured on. A model that reaches human final accuracy on a relational task after a large number of gradient steps has matched the human on the capability axis and sits on the baboon side of this one — and no wiki benchmark reports trials-to-criterion against a human trials-to-criterion. [[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]] is where the quantity belongs; this is the comparative anchor for it (`(brainstorm)`).

## What this does and does not decide for a model

- **It supplies a shape, not a mechanism.** Three components — relation store, integrator, general-purpose effort tier — with a privileged edge between the first two. It says nothing about *how* the integrator integrates; LISA's synchrony ([[wiki/entities/lisa.md]]) and the vector-symbolic binders remain the only mechanistic candidates, and neither is tested against this anatomy.
- **The integrator is a small, specialised, expensive component that most inputs should not reach.** Every wiki architecture that composes runs its composition on everything. The developmental result says the mature system is the one that composes *rarely*.
- **The relational store is parietal and the integrator is frontal, and they are separately damageable.** *Only the parietal half has a lesion dissociation, and it points the other way from the integrator story:* on verbal transitive inference, BA 7/40 lesions impair and BA 10 lesions do not (Waechter et al. 2013, `T412`). This matches the four-way decomposition already read off the analogy literature ([[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]]: temporal content store, hippocampal episode store, frontopolar integrator, inferior-frontal suppressor) and adds the parietal first-order term to it.
- **`G21`'s composer now has a candidate location.** The gap asks what composes the outputs of two encapsulated modules. This page's answer: a single region whose coupling to *whichever* posterior module supplies the current relational content is itself task-dependent — one 177-voxel left-RLPFC seed couples to left middle temporal gyrus on semantic second-order trials and to right DLPFC on visuospatial ones, in the same subjects (Wendelken et al. 2012, see above). That is a composer implemented as a **switchable fan-in** rather than as a fixed wiring, which is precisely the "afferent diversity" mechanism `T289` Position B names and nowhere instantiates.

### Three mechanism readings of one tissue, and the sweep that separates them

The five ingests on this page and its sibling leave three accounts of *what the integrator does*, each with a different load-bearing variable and a different scaling prediction. None has been tested against the others, and no wiki architecture reports any of the three sweeps.

| Reading | Source | What the site does | Load-bearing variable | Degrades with | Machine ablation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fan-in convergence** | Urbanski et al. 2016 (this page) | One small site where several content feeds meet; the comparison happens at the meeting point | Number of distinct afferents reaching the site | The number of **sources** cut — graded by count, indifferent to which (`rs = −0.553`) | Cut inputs into a candidate composition site one at a time; the deficit must grade with the count and not with the identity of the cut |
| **Parallel occupancy** | Mole et al. 2025 (this page, `T336`) | A wide network holding several novel, mutually irreducible structures live at once | Number of simultaneous incomparable structures at fixed source count | The number of **co-active structures** — flat in source count | Hold the source count fixed and raise the number of irreducible relations a decision needs; an indeterminate premise pair holds two from one feed |
| **Multi-filter decomposition** | Chau et al. 2025 ([[wiki/entities/lateral-frontal-pole.md]], `G21`) | Project high-dimensional input onto several low-dimensional features in parallel, filter weights set top-down, an older region recombines | Number of parallel filters (heads) | The number of **heads** cut toward one — monotonic; unaffected by source count if the basis is shared | Reduce head count at fixed input; representational similarity to the reference must fall monotonically |

The three are not exclusive, and that is the point of the table `(brainstorm)`: a fan-in bottleneck with `n` heads and an exclusive output slot instantiates all three at once, so one architecture can be scored on all three predictions in one sweep — and a system that has any of the three fixed by wiring (a fan-in set by type, an occupancy of one on an exclusive bus, a single frozen codebook) has that column's sweep closed and must say so.

## Open problems

| Problem | Why it is open |
|---|---|
| **RLPFC has no agreed function** | The review lists prospective memory, abstract thinking, counterfactual thinking, tracking alternative outcomes, and planning as separately proposed accounts and states plainly that no consensus exists. "Comparison and/or integration of several sets of mental representations" is these authors' own candidate, not a settled result |
| **The species comparison rests on fitted connectivity maps** | "No macaque analogue" is a null in a resting-state analysis across species with different scanner protocols, anaesthesia states, parcellations and brain sizes. The review calls for chimpanzee parcellation work that does not exist |
| **The developmental mediation is correlational** | Cortical thinning in IPL "accounts for" the RLPFC first-order decrease through structural equation modelling on observational data; no intervention, and cortical thinning is itself of contested cellular meaning (pruning vs. grey/white boundary shift under myelination) |
| **Nothing separates the integrator from a general effort signal at the region level** | The two crossed designs are the *only* evidence that RLPFC is not difficulty-driven, they come from one lab, and each has a single-cell-of-the-design reversal doing all the work |
| **No study has measured all four analogy subprocesses, and one of them has never been measured at all** | Access has no direct fMRI investigation in the literature (Parsons & Davies 2022); the proposed remedy is a train-then-probe design — pre-train relational categories on `A:B` pairs, then present `C:D` pairs in the scanner and ask whether the category is familiar, so access and mapping fire together against a comparison-only control |
| **Every region↔subprocess assignment here is univariate** | Activation says *how much* a region responds, not *what it represents*. MVPA and model-based fMRI (using LISA's or Companion's own predicted per-trial quantities as regressors) are both available and neither has been applied to the subprocesses — so "RLPFC is the mapping region" is currently an inference from co-occurrence |
| **The integrator's left lateralisation has no account** | Both semantic *and* visuospatial analogy maps are significant in left rostrolateral cortex and neither is in the right, so it is not a verbal-material effect; the meta-analysis rules out the only available explanation and offers none in its place. It also sits awkwardly with the primary sources for both the left-lateralised claim and the visuospatial half being partly the same lab, and with `T289`'s lesion evidence pointing at a right frontal network. The lesion data harden the asymmetry rather than explain it: no right-hemisphere tract disconnection predicts an analogy deficit, and the analogy-*preserved* patients' lesions overlap in right prefrontal cortex (Urbanski et al. 2016), while the authors' own candidate explanation — language — is excluded by preserved fluency in the impaired patients and by non-verbal analogies recruiting the same cluster. **Mole et al. 2025 removes the option of treating the right-frontal evidence as second-hand**: 247 patients, an analogy task built from Urbanski's, right frontal worse than left, and a same-cohort Stroop/fluency positive control showing the sample can see left effects. The asymmetry is now a contradiction with a registry row (`T336`) rather than an unexplained regularity |
| **Task-format and response-mode confounds are unresolved throughout** | `T335`: the analogy/matrix dissociation, the domain dissociation and `I36`'s second arm are all contrasts in which evaluate-vs-complete varies with the thing of interest. The meta-analysis inherits every design decision of its 27 inputs and can control none of them |
| **The parietal node's causal evidence is one positive and one null, and they disagree** | `T412`. Mole et al. 2025: 74 posterior patients, 32 of them left, unimpaired on analogy and on verbal deduction. But lesion assignment is a ≥70%-of-lesion lobar rule, no analysis targets IPL/IPS, and neither task manipulates relation *count*, the variable the parietal activation tracks. Waechter et al. 2013: 7 focal BA 7/40 patients at chance on verbal four-term transitive inference, with working memory matched. That study has a 5/7/6 design, `p = .047`, and timeouts scored as errors. Urbanski et al. 2016 tested no parietal lesions. Neither positive study separates the construction step (proposition → spatial array) from the comparison step |
| **Why a 0.33 cc site is critical at all** | Resting-state work argues that network disruption, not lesion location, explains high-level deficits; a deficit localised to a third of a cubic centimetre is the opposite result. The authors' fan-in account (several converging tracts must be cut together) predicts that any *other* site where the same tracts converge should show the same profile, and that a single-tract cut anywhere should not — neither has been tested |
| **Two lesion studies of the same task family disagree about the hemisphere, and no method controls for method** | `T336`. Voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping at `n = 27` and Bayesian stochastic block modelling at `n = 247` have never been applied to the same data. The cheapest resolution is not a new cohort: Mole collected a matched perceptual control and reports only raw accuracy, so Urbanski's difference index (`I39`) — the score that localises the left cluster best — is computable from data in hand |
| **The right-lateralised conditions are a subset, and nobody knows what defines the subset** | Odd-one-out but not Progression, indeterminate but not determinate deduction, colour and size but not numerical value. The authors' account — parallel maintenance of several novel representations — is a post-hoc reading of three splits and predicts nothing yet untested. A rule-novelty × parallel-load crossing on the same stimuli is the missing design, and it is the same shape as `I36` |
| **The claim is about second-order relations and stops there** | Third-order and deeper are never tested, so whether RLPFC is a general recursion site or a fixed-depth two-slot comparator is unaddressed — which is exactly the question `G104` and [[wiki/entities/lisa.md]]'s phase capacity raise |
| **The integrator's internal organisation is a peak-offset, and a peak is not a population** | `T338`. The dorsoventral gradient is a within-subject shift in the *location of the maximum* of a smoothed BOLD map, with large overlap on either side; it is consistent with two intermingled cell populations, with one population whose input weights vary smoothly, and with a vascular or registration gradient along the gyrus. No multivariate or cytoarchitectonic result separates them, and the offset has never been measured at a third domain to test whether the axis is *ordered* or merely two-valued |
| **Which IPL subregion carries the relational signal, and what it reads** | Resting connectivity splits the lobule into an anterior part (aIPL, coupled to the integrator) and a posterior part (pIPL, coupled to the hippocampus), and the two co-deactivate under task (Vincent et al. 2008). No relational IPL coordinate on this page has been placed against that boundary. Even inside aIPL, the authors' conflict reading predicts the same relation-count scaling as the store reading, since count and competing-mapping conflict covary in every design here |
| **Whether the control network's partner switch is interaction or shared timing** | The only task evidence is Spreng et al. 2010. Its coupling is the correlation of trial-averaged time courses (10 points per trial) plus one across-subject seed analysis, so two networks that both follow the evoked response are "coupled". A trial-by-trial (β-series) or residual-time-course estimate on the same autobiographical/visuospatial contrast would separate the two, and none is in the wiki. The resting node-level wiring now exists (Spreng et al. 2013: default-, dorsal-attention- and dual-aligned control nodes). It predicts that the default-coupled and dorsal-attention-coupled halves of the switch are carried by different node subsets, which is testable in the same task data and untested |
| **How the integrator reaches the hippocampus** | RLPFC↔hippocampus coupling rises on inference trials (Wendelken & Bunge 2010), but macaque anterior PFC has no known direct hippocampal projection. The authors allow a human-only direct route, given RLPFC's expansion. Whether the read goes through DLPFC, through parietal cortex, or through a human-specific tract is untested; a directed estimate (Granger or dynamic causal modelling) on the same contrast is the missing measurement |
| **The second-order contrast still confounds one switch** | Even with relation count and response count controlled, second-order trials require comparing *feature dimensions* and moving between externally given and internally held representations — the two other functions independently attributed to this tissue (Pollmann et al. 2000; Burgess et al. 2007), and the authors concede both. A second-order trial with the dimension held constant across pairs is the missing control and is trivial to author |

## Connections

- **[[wiki/concepts/relational-reinterpretation.md]]** — supplies the anatomy that page's supermodule argument lacked: the same three regions recur across memory, reasoning, decision-making and perception whenever a relation between relations is evaluated, which is the cross-domain convergence that page inferred from behaviour alone.
- **[[wiki/concepts/analogical-mapping.md]]** — the four-way functional decomposition on that page (content store, episode store, frontopolar integrator, inferior-frontal suppressor) with the missing fifth term supplied: parietal cortex holds the first-order relations that the frontopolar integrator compares, and the developmental data say the integrator's effective capacity is set by how much the parietal store can handle alone. The prefrontal relational-complexity row is weakened by a non-replication: Waltz et al. 1999's frontal deficit on scrambled transitive-inference items is absent with focal BA 10 lesions and similar stimuli (Waechter et al. 2013).
- **[[wiki/concepts/working-memory.md]]** — the design that shows items and relations are separate load currencies: three relations over four items engage RLPFC more than three extra items do, and engage DLPFC less, so a capacity measured in items does not bound relational integration.
- **[[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]** — separates two things that page's bias signal conflates: DLPFC activation tracks task difficulty across paradigms (the effort tier), while RLPFC tracks relational structure independently of difficulty, so "control" and "integration" are dissociable by a crossed design rather than being one graded resource.
- **[[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]** *(second link)* — home of `I38`, the domain-crossed relational battery abstracted from this page's semantic-vs-visuospatial subtraction maps: intersect the components two content domains recruit for the same relational operation to locate the domain-general operator, difference them to locate the content feeds.
- **[[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]** — home of `I36`, the relational-demand × difficulty crossing derived from this page's two reversal designs: the cheapest way to show a system's effort read-out is driven by structure rather than by cost.
- **[[wiki/entities/salience-network.md]]** — the same network under its other wiki description; that page's triple-network model treats "frontoparietal" as DLPFC + posterior parietal doing attention and working memory — the externally directed mode — and this page adds the rostrolateral node and the relational-integration function the switching account has no use for. Seeded from the rostral prefrontal integrator, resting connectivity puts that page's two anchors (anterior insula, anterior cingulate) *inside* this network (Vincent et al. 2008). So whether the switch is separate from the system it switches depends on the estimator: independent component analysis splits them (Seeley et al. 2007), and seed correlation does not. The two anchors also appear in the task-defined planning > counting component together with RLPFC and aIPL (Spreng et al. 2010), so the merge holds in task data too. At node grain the merge is typed: left anterior insula and dACC are dual-aligned control nodes, right anterior insula is dorsal-attention-aligned, and msPFC is the top direct-edge betweenness node (Spreng et al. 2013).
- **[[wiki/entities/lisa.md]]** — the mechanism candidate for the box this page localises: synchrony-based role-filler binding at a capacity of 2–3 propositions is what a frontopolar integrator with a two-slot second-order comparator would look like, and the anatomy neither confirms nor constrains it.
- **[[wiki/entities/default-mode-network.md]]** — shares the inferior parietal lobule as a node, which is a warning about both: a region-level function attribution built from one literature's contrasts collides with another literature's when the same coordinates carry two names ([[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]). Resting connectivity partly resolves the collision. The lobule holds two adjacent regions that task contrasts merge because they co-deactivate: an anterior one coupled to this network's frontal integrator and a posterior one coupled to that network's hippocampal subsystem. This network is also the system that page names as the interposed arbitrator candidate, positioned between that network and dorsal attention (Vincent et al. 2008). Under task, it couples to that network when plan content is retrieved from personal memory and to dorsal attention when it is on screen, with equal engagement in both cases (Spreng et al. 2010). So the interposed system routes between the two modes rather than suppressing one of them. At rest the routing has fixed entry points: left SFG on that network's side and DLPFC on dorsal attention's, with no node switching between the two outer networks from task to rest (Spreng et al. 2013).
- **[[wiki/concepts/developmental-heterochrony.md]]** — the same evolution/development coincidence measured rather than asserted: this page reports that the cortical regions most expanded across primate evolution overlap those most expanded across human development, which is the timing-shift hypothesis stated at the level of surface area.
- **[[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]]** — where this page's comparative anchor belongs: 35 vs ~400 trials to criterion on the same relational task relocates part of the human/nonhuman discontinuity onto a *rate* axis, and no wiki benchmark reports trials-to-criterion against a human.
- **[[wiki/concepts/node-definition-problem.md]]** — the standing discount on every claim here: node degree, hierarchy rank and "no analogue" are all parcellation-dependent statistics, and the cross-species comparison changes the parcellation by construction. The port-type result inherits two more of that page's free choices: 5 of 43 task-defined nodes change network at rest, and the alignment types exist only because a bootstrap test kept weak between-network edges that a magnitude threshold would have removed first (Spreng et al. 2013).
- **[[wiki/concepts/connectome-hubs-and-cores.md]]** — the same betweenness diagnostic on a functional graph of three networks: the highest-betweenness nodes are premotor control nodes (bilateral MFG BA 6 on full correlations, msPFC on partial), not the medial posterior core, so at this node set the crossing point between the internal and external streams sits at the action end of the controller (Spreng et al. 2013).
- **[[wiki/concepts/emergent-modularity.md]]** — a shared relational competence called by many functional modules, now with a location; the switchable RLPFC↔posterior coupling is what a learned rather than wired composer would look like in fMRI.
- **[[wiki/entities/macfac.md]]** — the two-stage retrieval architecture whose first stage this page's VLPFC node is the candidate substrate for, and the reason the assignment is unsafe: MAC/FAC's stage 1 is deliberately structure-blind, while VLPFC's measured sensitivity is to *associative strength*, which is the same surface quantity — so the anatomy is consistent with the model precisely where the model is known to be inadequate.
- **[[wiki/concepts/schema-assimilation.md]]** — supplies the induction half of that page's assimilation loop with a location and a manipulation: inferior frontal sulcus (BA 45) is engaged more when the rule must be extracted from an instance than when it is stated, and repeated extraction trials erase the behavioural cost, which is a schema being built inside a control condition.
- **[[wiki/entities/sme.md]]** — the architecture whose stage decomposition this page tests against brains and cannot confirm: access, mapping, inference and schema induction are separable in the code and, on the available imaging, are not separably measurable in the tissue (`T330`).
- **[[wiki/concepts/controlled-semantic-cognition.md]]** — the overlap and the non-overlap between two control networks: intraparietal sulcus and inferior frontal sulcus appear in both as the domain-general selection term, while the semantic-control nodes (posterior middle temporal gyrus, ventral prefrontal cortex) appear nowhere in this page's relational **co-activation** maps. They do appear in its **connectivity** map, and only when the content is semantic — left MTG couples to the RLPFC integrator on semantic second-order trials and not on visuospatial ones (Wendelken et al. 2012). So control is organised per representation-store, and the adapter is recruited by content rather than by task, which is why a co-activation map misses it.
- **[[wiki/entities/raven.md]]** *(second link)* — the causal anatomy of the parent instrument: RAPM performance in 247 focal patients depends on a right frontal network dominated by superior frontal gyrus, containing the ART and DRT networks as smaller subsets, so at the resolution of necessity the matrix format and the four-term format share a substrate that activation subtraction separates (`T335`, `T336`).
- **[[wiki/entities/raven.md]]** — the benchmark this page's task-format contrast puts a construct question under (`T335`): holding the visuospatial domain fixed, matrix problems recruit the attention and fronto-parietal control networks and *not* the left rostrolateral cluster that every four-term analogy contrast shares, so a matrix score indexes visuospatial load and multi-relation bookkeeping rather than the second-order integrator.
- **[[wiki/entities/pgm.md]]** — the one matrix benchmark that could run `T335`'s discriminating machine-side test without new items: its held-out abstractions are declared over the generator, so cross-format transfer (matrix-trained → four-term analogy) can be measured against its own within-format regimes as the baseline.
- **[[wiki/entities/arc-agi.md]]** — inherits the same exposure one step further out: grid-completion items are scored as evidence about analogy-grade abstraction, and the neural comparison behind `T335` says the format sits on the control-network side of the dissociation.
- **[[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]** *(fourth link)* — supplies `I40`, the same-cohort opposite-direction positive control: this page's right-lateralised reasoning networks are credible only because Stroop and phonemic fluency, run in the same 247 patients on the same pipeline, come back left, which is what turns "we saw no left effect" from a coverage complaint into a measurement.
- **[[wiki/concepts/constitutive-vs-enabling.md]]** — the partition this page's parietal null forces: IPL activation scales with relation count and IPL damage does not impair the relational decision, which is the signature of a readout or a background condition rather than the content, and is exactly the case that page says an activation correlate cannot settle alone. The re-sorting is now contested: focal BA 7/40 lesions take verbal transitive inference to chance with working memory matched (Waechter et al. 2013), so for that task the parietal signal behaves like content again (`T412`).
- **[[wiki/concepts/reasoning.md]]** — the deduction half this page acquired without a definition to attach it to: relational syllogisms and visuospatial analogies are damaged by the same right frontal network, and *within* deduction the determinate items behave like the non-lateralised analogy items while the indeterminate ones do not — so the truth-preserving step that page says the wiki never defines is not what the anatomy separates. The one focal-lesion study of verbal relational deduction disagrees on the posterior half: BA 7/40 damage impairs four-term transitive inference and BA 10 damage does not (Waechter et al. 2013, `T412`).
- **[[wiki/concepts/perturbation-elicitability.md]]** — the pairing that separates *steerable* from *necessary*: intracranial stimulation of frontopolar cortex changes reportable content at a rate of 0%, while a 0.33 cc lesion in the same tissue abolishes analogical reasoning specifically — so a null under focal excitation is evidence about the coding scheme and none at all about whether the component is load-bearing.
- **[[wiki/entities/lateral-frontal-pole.md]]** — the same tissue this page calls RLPFC, read from the decision-making literature instead of the analogy one, and the two function assignments are rivals rather than complements: *comparison of several sets of relational representations* here, *goal-conditioned multi-head decomposition of high-dimensional input into a low-dimensional feature basis* there, with a filter-count ablation behind the second and nothing yet relating it to the first. This page now holds the primary for **both** lesion results that page's discrepancy table names — Urbanski's left frontopolar cluster and Mole et al. 2025's right frontal network — and the identify-vs-apply reconciliation proposed there survives neither Urbanski's Find/Apply null nor the fact that the discrepancy's larger half is laterality, which that reconciliation does not address (`T336`). It also supplies this page's missing causal and connectivity detail — FPl vs FPm parcellation, TMS dissociating directed from random exploration, and the finding that the rostro-caudal apex claim reverses when the ordering criterion becomes effective connectivity (`T332`). Whether the two functions even share tissue is open: by coordinate comparison, cognitive branching and prospective memory peak anterior to the relational-comparison site while subgoal integration peaks at it (Wendelken et al. 2008b).
- **[[wiki/concepts/certification-instruments.md]]** *(fifth link)* — supplies `I36`'s third arm, the **response-count reversal**: make the low-structure condition require *more* responses than the high-structure one, so that any component tracking output load moves against the component tracking structure. Reference measurement: two button presses on first-order trials against one on second-order, with rostrolateral cortex up and inferior parietal and ventrolateral cortex down (Wendelken et al. 2012).
- **[[wiki/concepts/sparse-expert-routing.md]]** — the machine form of `T338`'s Position B: an integrator whose sub-populations are specialised by the provenance of their afferents is a mixture of experts whose gate is wired rather than learned, and the dorsoventral peak offset is what a fixed input-locality prior looks like in a smoothed read-out.
- **[[wiki/concepts/expected-value-of-control.md]]** — splits that page's single cost term by a crossed design: dorsolateral prefrontal activation tracks task *difficulty* while rostrolateral tracks *relational structure* independently of it, so `Cost(signal)` is a scalar placed over at least two dissociable resources, and an architecture charging one effort price cannot express the easy-relational versus hard-non-relational contrast.
- **[[wiki/concepts/abstraction-graph.md]]** — the requirements table this network answers, row by row: parietal cortex is row 1 (relations held as objects), rostrolateral cortex is row 2b (role-filler alignment, the binder for schemas as against the hippocampal binder for facts) and row 14 (width: several irreducible structures held at once, fan-in or parallel occupancy), dorsolateral cortex is the effort tier of row 3; and Mole's determinacy split is why that page now calls exact edges a formal kind the anatomy does not separate.
- **[[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]]** — the problem-side taxonomy this page's `T335` cannot be seen from: matrix problems and four-term analogies share a bit-vector there (edge label latent, from pairs) and dissociate on activation here, because the taxonomy has no coordinate for whether the label is a first-order relation or a relation between relations.
- **[[wiki/entities/medial-prefrontal-cortex.md]]** — the other prefrontal system in the same story, split by operation rather than by territory: the medial wall selects which stored structure is live and integrates conflicting items into one, this lateral network separates items and compares relations of relations; one conflict signal, two resolution policies, no arbitration rule ([[wiki/concepts/schema-assimilation.md]]). For transitive inference the same split is proposed as a premise-regime split: in-view premises go here, trained premises go to the ventral medial wall, whose lesion reverses interior-pair choices below chance (Koscik & Tranel 2012).
- **[[wiki/entities/remerge.md]]** — bounds that page's hippocampal transitive-inference solver: with the premises on screen, chaining two relations engages RLPFC and leaves hippocampal activation unchanged, so a retrieval-time settling loop in the store is at most the memorised-premise solver; and a second-hand post-training lesion that spares transitive inference says the store builds the ordering rather than computes it at test (Wendelken & Bunge 2010).
- **[[wiki/concepts/hippocampal-long-axis.md]]** — splits that page's "anterior = transitive inference" row: on-screen encoding of ordered relations loads posterior hippocampus, while the cluster more coupled to the frontal integrator on inference trials is relatively anterior and has no activation effect of its own (Wendelken & Bunge 2010).
- **[[wiki/concepts/effort-based-decision-making.md]]** — this page's crossed dissociation is the strongest objection to that page's single demand axis: cognitive effort discounting prices one resource by titrating `N`-back load, and dorsolateral-versus-rostrolateral separation says difficulty and relational structure are independently varying costs, so an elicited `SV(load)` curve leaves the relational price unmeasured and two agents with identical curves may differ entirely on what integration costs them.
- **[[wiki/concepts/problem-framing.md]]** — the authors' reading of this page's parietal lesion result is that page's first stage localised: verbal premises must be turned into an array that exemplifies the stated order before anything is composed, and that construction step, not the composition, is what BA 7/40 damage removes (Waechter et al. 2013, untested as a step manipulation).
