# SAYCam and baby-vision — self-supervised learning through the eyes of a child

**~500 hours of head-mounted video recorded from three children between 6 and 32 months, and a convolutional network trained on one child's stream at a time with no labels. The wiki's first source in which the *experience stream itself* is the designed object: the data is not a corpus assembled to be representative but a strict subset of one individual's actual sensory history, and every result is a claim about what that history makes learnable.**

> **Provenance.** Orhan, Gupta & Lake 2020, *Self-supervised learning through the eyes of a child*, NeurIPS 33 (`raw/orhan-2020-self-supervised-eyes-of-a-child.md`, ar5iv of arXiv:2007.16189). Dataset: SAYCam (Sullivan et al. 2020), hosted on Databrary under IRB-gated access. Code and pre-trained models: `github.com/eminorhan/baby-vision`.
>
> **What is not in the clip.** Figures 3, 4a, 5 and 7a carry the paper's main accuracy numbers and are images; the clipped text reports their *orderings* and not their values. Every ordering below is stated as an ordering. The only top-1 numbers available first-hand are the ImageNet ones (Appendix A5) and they are reported as such.

## The stream as an artefact

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Children | 3 — S 221 h, A 141 h, Y 137 h (~500 h total) |
| Age range | 6–32 months, ~1–2 h recorded per week over ~2 years |
| Capture | Head-mounted camera, 640×480, 30 fps (S, A), 25 fps (Y) |
| Pre-processing | Minor edge → 256 px bicubic, 224×224 centre crop shifted 16 px up to cut the burnt-in timestamp |
| Fraction of real experience | **~1 week** of a child's waking visual input, well-distributed over two years — the paper's own estimate, and it puts the stream **two orders of magnitude** short of a 2.5-year-old's |
| Evaluation set (`labeled S`) | ~58K frames, 26 classes, curated from human annotations of *what the child was looking at* on ~25% of S's videos; 414 raw labels → top 30 by frame count → drop `mom` and `book` (over-represented) → manual cleaning → drop classes <100 frames |
| Second evaluation set | **Toybox** (Wang et al. 2018) — 12 early-learned basic-level categories, 30 exemplars each, 10 transformation types (translation, rotation, …), ~84K frames at 1 fps |

**Intrinsic dimensionality (Appendix A1).** Embedding four corpora with a fixed ResNeXt WSL trunk and running PCA on equal-sized subsets: ImageNet > first-person video (child S, Charades-Ego first-person) > third-person video (Charades-Ego). Video pays for temporal correlation with information content; egocentric video buys some of it back through natural distortion and perturbation. **This is the clearest statement in the wiki that a developmentally realistic stream is a *worse* i.i.d. corpus and is nonetheless sufficient** — the learner's job is defined against the stream it actually gets.

## The objective: temporal classification

Partition the whole video into contiguous **temporal classes** (episodes) of equal duration and train an ordinary softmax classifier to predict which episode a frame came from.

```
label(frame at time t) = ⌊t / L⌋          L = segment length (seconds)
loss = cross-entropy over the episode index
```

| Design point | Setting in the best model |
|---|---|
| Trunk | MobileNetV2, trained from scratch, 1280-unit embedding layer |
| Sampling rate | 5 fps |
| Segment length `L` | **288 s (~5 min)** |
| Augmentation | Colour jitter (0.8, 0.8, 0.8, 0.2) at `p=0.8` + random grayscale at `p=0.2`, as in SimCLR |
| Optimiser | Adam, lr 5e-4, batch 732 (4 GPUs), 20 epochs at 1 fps / 6 epochs at 5 fps |
| Training accuracy on the pretext task | 80–85% top-1 throughout |
| Readout | Frozen trunk, **linear** probe on the penultimate embedding |

**Why this is not just another pretext task.** Slowness is the assumption (Földiák 1991; Wiskott & Sejnowski 2002): high-level variables change slowly, so a code that cannot tell adjacent frames apart but *can* tell episodes apart has kept the slow variables. The instruction signal is a **within-agent** one — the paper points at hippocampal sparse episode codes (Marr 1971) as the biological source of an episode index, i.e. the supervision is generated by a memory system rather than by a world. Algorithmic ancestor: time-contrastive learning for nonlinear ICA (Hyvärinen & Morioka 2016).

**Separate models per child.** TC-S, TC-A, TC-Y (temporal classification, trained on child S, A, Y — note the wiki's glossary already uses `TC` for Total Correlation; the expansion here is local to this page) are trained on one child's data only, so no result aggregates across individuals.

## What was measured

| Comparison | Result (orderings from Fig. 3 unless a number is given) |
|---|---|
| Temporal classification vs both contrastive learners | **TC > MoCo-Temp-S > MoCo-Img-S in every condition.** MoCo-v2 is near-SOTA on ImageNet and loses here; the authors' hypothesis is that long-range temporal correlation makes in-batch negatives unreliable |
| TC vs ImageNet-supervised trunk | Comparable, **sometimes better**, on `labeled S` and Toybox |
| TC vs shallow / untrained controls | HOG (histogram of oriented gradients) and an untrained MobileNetV2 are poor throughout ⇒ depth and learning both necessary |
| Cross-child transfer | TC-A and TC-Y generalise **well** to `labeled S` — another child's two years of experience supports the first child's categories |
| Temporal-correlation control | `labeled S` subsampled 10× (1 fps → 0.1 fps): TC still performs well ⇒ not a nearest-frame lookup |
| Novel-exemplar generalisation | Toybox **exemplar split** (27 train / 3 test exemplars per class): TC still performs well ⇒ categories, not instances |
| Hard binary pairs (car/road, door/window, foot/hand, chair/table) | TC-S ≈ ImageNet-trained, and class-activation maps land on the correct region in ambiguous images (red socks → `foot`, high-chair → `chair`) |
| **ImageNet linear probe (first-hand numbers)** | TC-S **20.9**, TC-A 18.1, TC-Y 17.6, MoCo-v2-S 16.4, all-three-children TC **~25**, untrained MobileNetV2 **1.2** |

### The three factor sweeps (Fig. 5), and the one that matters

| Factor | Direction | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Sampling rate | Higher is better | More variation per unit of stream; effectively augmentation |
| **Segment length `L`** | Longer is better, optimum ~**288 s** | A *scalar with an interior optimum* on the pair-construction lever — the timescale over which the scene stays semantically the same |
| Data augmentation | Better, but the effect is **small** | The paper's own explanation: egocentric natural video already carries the variability the augmentation list is there to inject |

### Single-feature selectivity

```
CSI(f) = (⟨f⟩_Cmax − ⟨f⟩_C−max) / (⟨f⟩_Cmax + ⟨f⟩_C−max)      0 = agnostic, 1 = perfectly class-selective
```

Individual units are generally **not** selective — class information is distributed — and CSI rises toward the output, matching the supervised-network finding (Leavitt & Morcos 2020). The class-activation maps are correspondingly impure: attention on a `cat` image extends well beyond the cat (contextual cues), and the map for a class is not silent on all non-class images.

## Limitations the paper states

| # | Limitation | Consequence for the wiki |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The training video is **~1 week** of real experience; closing the gap needs ~100× more data | Every result here is a *lower bound* on what the stream affords, never an upper one |
| 2 | Video only — **no embodiment**. The child selects its own views; the model receives them passively | The stream is designed *by someone else's body*; the sensorimotor contingency that would factorise the sources of variation is absent |
| 3 | **No multimodality** — SAYCam's audio is unused, so word learning cannot act on the visual features | The one channel developmental psychology says reshapes visual features is switched off |
| 4 | The best models still need **unrealistic augmentation** (colour distortion, grayscaling) | Small in effect (sweep above) but not zero — the declared-nuisance list is not fully eliminated |

## What this buys the wiki

- **`G32` gets its data-side artefact.** Every other instance on that row designs an *ordering* or a *sampler* over a corpus chosen for convenience. Here the corpus is a person's history, with a stated conversion rate to real experience (~1 week) and a stated shortfall (100×) — so "design the experience stream" acquires a unit and a scale.
- **`G95`'s wanted experiment is half-run.** The row asks for augmentation-defined pairing vs adjacency-defined pairing *on the same stream*. TC (adjacency, via episode identity) vs MoCo-Img-S (augmentation) on identical headcam video is exactly that contrast, and adjacency wins. What is missing is the row's scoring clause: the probes hold out samples and exemplars, not transformation conditions the stream's time structure never paired.
- **`T167` gets a fifth instance and its second scalar axis.** Loss, trunk, data and budget fixed; vary only the segment length — the same shape as CPC's prediction horizon, an interior optimum in a one-dimensional sweep, and the kind of object a rule could set.
- **(brainstorm) The ranking of self-supervised objectives is a function of the corpus.** MoCo-v2 is near-SOTA on ImageNet and loses to a plain softmax over episode indices on longitudinal egocentric video. The wiki's anti-collapse comparison table is an ImageNet table; nothing in it has been re-run on a stream with hour-scale autocorrelation. This is an `L3` observation and gets no registry row, but it is the cheapest challenge available to the family's published ordering.
- **(brainstorm) Augmentation and stream diversity are substitutes, and the exchange rate is measurable.** Augmentation buys little here and a lot on ImageNet (crop-only costs SimCLR 27.6 points, [[wiki/entities/byol.md]]). If the augmentation list is a synthetic stand-in for variability the stream failed to supply, then its measured value is an *inverse* index of stream quality — a statistic no source in the wiki computes and one that would make `G95`'s "declared vs discovered" split quantitative.
