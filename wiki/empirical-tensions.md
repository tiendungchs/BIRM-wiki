# Empirical Tensions

Places where sources in the wiki disagree, or where a claim is asserted more strongly than its evidence supports. One row per tension. Updated at every ingest that creates or resolves one.

**Status key:** `LIVE` — unresolved · `LEANING` — evidence favours one side · `RESOLVED` — settled, kept for the record.

| # | Tension | Position A | Position B | Where it bites | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Does the implementation level carry computational content?** | Only Marr levels 1–2 (computation, algorithm) need transfer; biophysics is substrate detail, deliberately excluded (Hassabis et al. 2017) | Spiking timing, energy constraints and one-shot synaptic change are computational, not incidental — the position of neuromorphic and biophysical modelling, which the same paper sets itself against | Decides whether a reasoning architecture may be specified purely functionally, or whether the substrate constrains what is expressible | `LIVE` |
| T2 | **Is the fast level a separate store or recurrent activity?** | Complementary learning systems: a second anatomical system with a sparse conjunctive code, coupled by replay | Meta-RL: the fast learner is the *activity dynamics* of a network whose weights are fixed — no second store required | Determines what fast **M** is built from, and whether one-shot binding and fast policy adaptation are the same mechanism | `LIVE` |
| T3 | **Is backpropagation biologically realizable?** | Not as stated: it needs weight transport and non-local error signals, neither of which cortex provides | Feedback alignment, predictive-coding and energy-based nets approximate it with fixed random feedback and purely local updates, with correspondences to spike-timing-dependent plasticity | Decides whether the slow-**W** meta-graph learner can exist in a neural substrate, and whether "biologically plausible" still rules anything out | `LEANING` — toward realizable approximations, unverified at depth/scale |
| T4 | **Do abstract structural codes exist in the brain?** | Grid-like periodic codes appear during abstract categorization in humans; periodic encoding may be a general hallmark of knowledge organization | The same review states we *lack direct evidence* for codes that are invariant to objects and code relational information, and flags the grid-like claim as requiring much further work | The wiki's `g` (structural code) has one contested biological anchor; if it fails, the factorization must be justified computationally alone | `LIVE` |
| T5 | **Was neuroscience causally responsible for the transferred mechanisms?** | Attention, replay, dropout, external memory and elastic weight consolidation are documented imports; TD learning and PDP have explicit historical lineage | Several were plausibly reachable by engineering alone and the inspiration was acknowledged only retrospectively — the paper itself says inspiration is often drawn "without explicit acknowledgment" | Sets how much predictive weight the biology deserves when choosing what to build next; if the record is retrofitted, the "look at the brain" prior is weaker than claimed | `LIVE` |
| T6 | **What is out-of-distribution failure a failure *of*?** | A flat learner fits the mixture `E_θ[p(obs\|θ)]`, a distribution no instance follows — o.o.d. failure is a missing level of hierarchy ([[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]]) | Networks generalise o.o.d. *prodigiously* — abstract patterns confidently classified as guitars, vast image sets mapped to object categories. The failure is directional: chosen features create insensitivity to all others, so the model extrapolates along the wrong axes (Geirhos et al. 2020) | Prescription differs: *add hierarchy* (fit the right object) vs. *add bias* (choose the right invariances). If B is right, a correctly hierarchical model with the wrong feature preference still fails | `LIVE` — likely complementary; A explains why the fitted object is wrong, B why the extrapolation is confident |
