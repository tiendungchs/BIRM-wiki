# Empirical Tensions

Places where sources in the wiki disagree, or where a claim is asserted more strongly than its evidence supports. One row per tension. Updated at every ingest that creates or resolves one.

**Status key:** `LIVE` — unresolved · `LEANING` — evidence favours one side · `RESOLVED` — settled, kept for the record.

| T1 | **Does the implementation level carry computational content?** | Only Marr levels 1–2 (computation, algorithm) need transfer; biophysics is substrate detail, deliberately excluded (Hassabis et al. 2017). Lake et al. 2017 push the same position further: understand the "software" before the "hardware", because what we know about the brain is not clear-cut enough to constrain a cognitive theory in either direction — and a hypothetical biological mechanism should be doubted if it is *cognitively* implausible | Spiking timing, energy constraints and one-shot synaptic change are computational, not incidental — the position of neuromorphic and biophysical modelling, which the same paper sets itself against. Schmidgall et al. 2023 argue it directly: spikes are theoretically shown to carry *more* information than rate codes despite being binary and sparse; energy is a first-order barrier at scale and on edge devices; the i.i.d. timeless data assumption "does not reflect physical reality" ([[wiki/entities/spiking-neural-networks.md]]) | Decides whether a reasoning architecture may be specified purely functionally, or whether the substrate constrains what is expressible | `LEANING B` — B now has a controlled test rather than an argument: Kawahara & Fujisawa 2025 rebuild an architecture the wiki already holds at the computational level ([[wiki/entities/tolman-eichenbaum-machine.md]]) in spiking neurons, and three mechanisms *inexpressible* in its functional specification turn out to be necessary for its central representation — grid cells fall from 59.6% to 0.00% without a neuromodulatory input gain, to 10.1% without STDP, to 25.6% without theta-phase inhibition — while the spiking version additionally produces phase precession and phase locking, which the rate model has no vocabulary for ([[wiki/entities/spiking-tem.md]]). B also has the sharpest *quantitative* case in the wiki, and it is older than the debate: an integrate-and-fire model of the barn-owl laminar nucleus fires with **20–25 µs** precision from 250 µs postsynaptic potentials, `τ_m ≈ 100 µs` and 40 µs input jitter, with the precision almost independent of `τ_m` — so temporal content is not merely present, it is *not bounded by* the speed of the components, and the variable carrying the answer (arrival-time difference) has no rate-level description (Gerstner et al. 1996; [[wiki/concepts/temporal-coding.md]]). The residual defence of A: the rate TEM obtains the same code *without* any of these, so they may be repairs for costs the substrate itself imposes rather than computational content it adds |
| T2 | **Is the fast level a separate store or recurrent activity?** | Complementary learning systems: a second anatomical system with a sparse conjunctive code, coupled by replay | Meta-RL: the fast learner is the *activity dynamics* of a network whose weights are fixed — no second store required. Reinforced by Schmidgall et al. 2023: in-context learning changes activations not weights, yet parameter-sharing in a meta-learner yields the *interpretation of activations as weights*, and self-attention's outer/inner products can be cast as learned weight updates that implement gradient descent | Determines what fast **M** is built from, and whether one-shot binding and fast policy adaptation are the same mechanism | `LIVE` — but possibly **dissolving**: if activations and weights are interconvertible under parameter sharing, the question is a change of basis, and the real variables are the fast level's degrees of freedom and decay timescale ([[wiki/concepts/meta-optimized-plasticity.md]]) |
| T3 | **Is backpropagation biologically realizable?** | Not as stated: it needs weight transport and non-local error signals, neither of which cortex provides | Feedback alignment, predictive-coding and energy-based nets approximate it with fixed random feedback and purely local updates, with correspondences to spike-timing-dependent plasticity | Decides whether the slow-**W** meta-graph learner can exist in a neural substrate, and whether "biologically plausible" still rules anything out | `LEANING` — toward realizable approximations, but **narrowed** by Schmidgall et al. 2023: plain feedback alignment is suboptimal at ImageNet scale, and only sign-symmetry — which still transports sign information — matches backpropagation on large data. The surviving approximation is the least biologically defensible one. **And the two objections must now be scored separately** (Whittington & Bogacz 2019): the four models that most convincingly answer *non-locality* — contrastive learning, continuous update, predictive coding, dendritic error, all reaching ~1.7–3% MNIST error with purely Hebbian updates — every one of them **assumes symmetric feedforward/feedback weights** and relies on its plasticity rule to maintain the symmetry. So the family that solves locality does not touch weight transport, and the family that solves weight transport (sign-symmetry) transports the load-bearing part. No model currently answers both. **A partial escape, asserted not measured:** in predictive coding the reciprocal prediction and error synapses obey the same pre×post rule with the roles swapped, so approximate symmetry is a fixed point the two synapses reach independently rather than an assumption — which, if it holds under nonlinearity at depth, converts the symmetry assumption into a *consequence* of the locality solution (talk-nd-brain-learning-algorithm, **(tentative)**) **And the question may be malformed** (Whittington & Bogacz 2017): the predictive-coding equivalence is not approximate-with-unknown-error but exact in the limit `Σ^(0) → ∞`, which is the limit that drives error-node activity to zero and so *removes* the spiking implementation. Realizability and equivalence trade off along a single parameter, so "is backpropagation realizable" has no fixed answer — see T119. |
| T4 | **Do abstract structural codes exist in the brain?** | Grid-like periodic codes appear during abstract categorization in humans; periodic encoding may be a general hallmark of knowledge organization Epstein et al. 2017 adds detail rather than a second report: the same morphed-stimulus result also shows the sixfold signal in ventromedial prefrontal cortex, with its strength tracking task performance | The same review states we *lack direct evidence* for codes that are invariant to objects and code relational information, and flags the grid-like claim as requiring much further work. And the nearest independent non-spatial map is *not* periodic — a social power × affiliation space is coded as hippocampal vector *angle* plus posterior-cingulate *magnitude* — so the general claim that survives is "maps outside space", not "grids outside space" ([[wiki/concepts/cognitive-map.md]]) | The wiki's `g` (structural code) has one contested biological anchor; if it fails, the factorization must be justified computationally alone | `LIVE` |
| T5 | **Was neuroscience causally responsible for the transferred mechanisms?** | Attention, replay, dropout, external memory and elastic weight consolidation are documented imports; TD learning and PDP have explicit historical lineage | Several were plausibly reachable by engineering alone and the inspiration was acknowledged only retrospectively — the paper itself says inspiration is often drawn "without explicit acknowledgment" | Sets how much predictive weight the biology deserves when choosing what to build next; if the record is retrofitted, the "look at the brain" prior is weaker than claimed | `LIVE` |
| T6 | **What is out-of-distribution failure a failure *of*?** | A flat learner fits the mixture `E_θ[p(obs\|θ)]`, a distribution no instance follows — o.o.d. failure is a missing level of hierarchy ([[wiki/concepts/latent-graph-discovery.md]]) | Networks generalise o.o.d. *prodigiously* — abstract patterns confidently classified as guitars, vast image sets mapped to object categories. The failure is directional: chosen features create insensitivity to all others, so the model extrapolates along the wrong axes (Geirhos et al. 2020) | Prescription differs: *add hierarchy* (fit the right object) vs. *add bias* (choose the right invariances). If B is right, a correctly hierarchical model with the wrong feature preference still fails | `LIVE` — likely complementary; A explains why the fitted object is wrong, B why the extrapolation is confident |
| T7 | **Are backpropagation-derived local rules worth pursuing at all?** | Yes — locality is what makes deployment-time learning, spiking substrates and neuromorphic hardware possible, and each new constraint has generated a new algorithm (feedback alignment → e-prop → neuromodulated credit assignment, each improving on the last) | No — measured against backpropagation through time they show **worse and more variable generalization** that does not close by scaling the step size, because the gradient approximation is poorly aligned with the true gradient. A local *approximation* of an optimization process is fundamentally sub-par, and Schmidgall et al. 2023 pose the abandonment question themselves. Bartunov et al. 2018 add the scaling half of the case: on ImageNet with locally-connected (weight-sharing-free) nets, **every** target-propagation variant sits at chance (98–99% top-1) and feedback alignment reaches 93.08% against backpropagation's 71.43% — the family's validation had all been done on a dataset (MNIST) that does not discriminate between the methods | Decides whether the wiki's slow-**W** learner should pursue local gradient approximations at all, or restrict local rules to the jobs backpropagation cannot do (deployment-time adaptation, delayed sparse reward, spiking substrates) | `LEANING` — toward B *for the approximation programme specifically*. Rules that never approximated a gradient ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]) are untouched by the result, and no experiment separates "intrinsic to approximation" from "artefact of the approximations tested" |
| T8 | **Is compositional machinery innate, or acquired from an external symbolic system?** | **Acquired.** Infants are endowed only with language-*learning* capacities (sensitivity to speech and prosody, an abstract content-word/function-word distinction); the syntax and compositional semantics of the acquired natural language are what break the modularity of core systems. An innate language of thought is rejected because it overgenerates — the infant could not know which concepts are useful, which apply, or which propositions are true (Spelke 2022) | **Innate.** Core-system outputs are already in a language-of-thought format and a general composer sits on top. The language route undergenerates in both directions: core concepts are taken for granted and so rarely uttered, while logical operators, tense/aspect and modals have no core-system counterpart. And the conceded escape hatch — that home-signing deaf children reinvent the needed features of language — makes those features indistinguishable from a pre-linguistic language of thought (Revencu & Csibra 2023) | Decides whether a reasoning architecture must *contain* a composer or may expect one to emerge from training on a symbolic corpus — i.e. whether a large language model is a candidate answer to gap G21 or only to G22 | `LIVE` — neither side has a criterion that separates productive composition from associative combination, so no evidence currently discriminates them |
| T9 | **Are installed priors unitary modules or bundles of independently violable constraints?** | **Unitary / all-or-none.** An entity that violates cohesion loses the continuity and persistence expectations too — infants stop expecting a non-cohesive entity to move continuously or survive occlusion (Huntley-Fenner et al. 2002). All-or-none operation is what distinguishes a core system from a graded composite (Spelke 2022) | **Graded.** Principles dissociate: adults accept teleportation (continuity without cohesion) and remote controls (objecthood without action-on-contact); infants explore *selectively by violation type*, banging solidity-violating objects on the table (Stahl & Feigenson 2015), which a unitary system cannot produce (Revencu & Csibra 2023) | Sets whether a prior is one gate or *n* gates. The graded form yields a **violation-typed prediction error** — an intrinsic-motivation signal pointing at the specific principle that broke — where the unitary form can only report "not an object" | `LEANING` — toward graded on the evidence cited, and the graded form is the better architecture either way |
| T10 | **Is an innate prior architecturally necessary, or just knowledge delivered early?** | **Necessary.** Core knowledge is a bank of domain-specific, entry-gated systems that guide learning top-down from the start; without them the learner has no `g`-side prior at all and the intended graph is unidentifiable ([[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]], gap G16) | **Not necessary.** For an ideal inductor, knowledge `z` presented as the *first observation* replaces `K(µ)` by `K(µ\|z)` in the convergence bounds; as long as the code interpreting `z` is `O(1)`, the agent absorbs it within a few cycles — so "the boundary between implementation and training is unsharp", and innateness is a claim about *timing*, not architecture (Hutter 2000) | Decides whether priors must be built into the architecture or can be supplied as data/context. If B holds at realistic scale, prompting and pretraining are substitutes for architectural priors; if A holds, they are not | `LIVE` — B's equivalence is asymptotic and assumes an `O(1)` interpreter and an agent that can already compress arbitrary input, which is precisely what a bounded learner lacks. The same source supplies A's strongest argument: if evolution encoded something `Ω`-like and incompressible into the genome, no `O(1)` program reaches human competence and the missing part is exactly the installed prior |
| T11 | **Is an installed prior a hard constraint or a strong default?** | **Hard.** Core principles operate all-or-none and *delete* candidate structures rather than reweighting them: an entity that violates cohesion is not admitted to the object system at all, and non-object substances (sand, liquids) never enter it ([[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]]) | **Defeasible.** The same principles are false at the smallest and largest scales — objects are neither cohesive nor continuous, space is not Euclidean or 3-D — and they are demonstrably overridden: by physics and non-Euclidean geometry in adults, by learning to count in preschoolers, by learning about eating and breathing in children's agent concepts (Spelke & Kinzler 2007) | Decides how a prior is implemented. A hard constraint has no path to a domain where it is wrong; a soft regularizer is washed out by data and buys no sample efficiency. The override channel in every documented case is an **external symbol system**, which is also T8's candidate for composition | `LEANING` — toward defeasible, with the open question being the *mechanism* of override, not its existence; note the two positions are the same source's, at the scale of action vs. outside it |
| T12 | **Are the conceptual primitives of infancy installed, or learned from sensorimotor prediction in the first months?** | **Installed.** Core knowledge is a bank of domain-specific systems present at birth, individuating entities by entry conditions and carrying principles (cohesion, continuity, contact) that no amount of sensorimotor correlation would supply ([[wiki/concepts/core-knowledge.md]]) | **Learned, but early.** Butz 2016 takes the *same* evidence — newborns anticipating thumb-to-mouth contact, the rooting reflex suppressed for self-touch, goal-directed reaching in the first months — as showing that a progressively accurate predictive model of one's *own body* is already in place, learned in utero. No such system exists |
| T13 | **Is long-term potentiation the mechanism behind behavioural learning?** | **Yes** — long-term potentiation and spike-timing-dependent plasticity are the standard biological warrant for the Hebbian rule family, cited as biologically supported (Bi & Poo 2001) and used as the substrate story for fast **M** ([[wiki/concepts/synaptic-plasticity.md]]) | **The timescales do not match.** LTP's critical interstimulus interval is orders of magnitude shorter than behaviourally relevant intervals, and manipulating interstimulus and intertrial intervals together shows **no critical interval exists**; behaviour persists for weeks or months while LTP decays to baseline in days (Power et al. 1997); learned behaviour is rapidly reacquired after extinction (Bouton 2004) with no matching facilitation in LTP (de Jonge & Racine 1985). Implementing structured model building with purely Hebbian mechanisms would be "especially challenging" (Gallistel & Matzel 2013, via Lake et al. 2017) | Decides whether "biologically supported" is an argument for a local coactivity rule at all. So the family is no longer restricted to a 20 ms kernel and the interval mismatch stops being decisive. | T14 | **Are structured representations real, or emergent descriptions of sub-symbolic processes?** | **Emergent.** Graphs, grammars, rules, objects, structural descriptions and programs are "useful yet misleading metaphors" — more epiphenomenal than real, emergent properties of sub-symbolic processes; the parallel-distributed-processing strategy is to train a generic network and add ingredients only when forced, and it has produced networks behaving *as if* they had past-tense rules, balance-beam rules, or a tree of living kinds (McClelland et al. 2010, reported in Lake et al. 2017) | **Explicit.** Human-level performance requires explicit representations of objects, identity, relations and causal structure; the caption failures (objects right, relations wrong) and the sample-efficiency gaps (924 h vs. minutes; 23% vs. 4% error at matched pre-training) are read as evidence that the emergent version is not the same representation despite matched behaviour on the trained task (Lake et al. 2017) | Decides whether the wiki should specify `g`, edges and node types as architecture, or train a generic model and look for them afterwards. | T15 | **Is training data a design lever or an anchor?** | **A lever, co-equal with the other three.** The control surface is architecture, training data, loss and optimizer; the data lever is the one that blocks a specific shortcut *by construction* (adversarial vulnerability, texture bias), and it is the only one whose effect on identifiability is provable — a single environment cannot distinguish a causal edge from a correlational one, an environment *family* can (Geirhos et al. 2020; [[wiki/concepts/shortcut-learning.md]]) | **An anchor, not a component.** The designed objects are objective function, learning rule and architecture; the environment is "a crucial consideration to anchor the core components, but not one of the components itself", because the components pass through the genome bottleneck while the environment supplies unbounded information that no compact design can contain (Richards et al. 2019; [[wiki/concepts/three-component-framework.md]]) | Whether curating environment families counts as *building the model* or as *choosing the benchmark* — i.e. whether gap G16 is closed by a data programme or must be paid for in architecture and objective | `LIVE` — the two are answering different questions (what a designer writes down vs. what determines the learned solution), and the disagreement is only substantive because the wiki uses the control surface for both |
| T16 | **Does the shortest program consistent with the data generalize?** | **Yes — compression is the provably sufficient bias.** The universal semimeasure dominates every computable hypothesis, convergence is bounded by `K(µ)` alone, and a domain is learnable exactly to the extent it is compressible ([[wiki/concepts/universal-induction.md]], Hutter 2000) | **No — policy compression is antagonistic to generalization.** `TrainSol^opt`, the shortest program optimal on the curriculum, by construction discards everything not needed for the *training* situations, including what evaluation needs. Worked case: on `(−0.75, F), (0.15, T)` the shortest fits (`λx: x > 0`) break on `(−0.1, T)` while a nearest-neighbour learner that stored the raw data survives; being ready for future uncertainty costs description length (Chollet 2019) | Decides whether a minimum-description-length objective is a route to a reasoning model or a trap. It also splits gap G26 in two: compression may fail to *expose* structure (Hutter's version) or fail to *contain* it (Chollet's). **(brainstorm)** Candidate reconciliation: compress the generator over environments (meta-graph), never the policy fitted to one curriculum (instance-graph) — which makes this an argument *for* the two-level factorization. Note the regimes differ — asymptotic passive mixture vs. single finite-horizon program — so both may hold as stated | `LIVE` — Delétang et al. 2023 supplies A's empirical arm without settling it: models trained on exactly this objective compress modalities they never saw better than the hand-built codecs for those modalities, which is generalization by any reading. It also supplies B's: those same models have no object or 2-D structure for the images they compress best, and once their parameters are charged to the code length they lose to gzip by two orders of magnitude ([[wiki/concepts/prediction-compression-equivalence.md]], gap G35) |
| T17 | **Is intelligence universal, or scoped?** | **Universal.** Intelligence is expected reward over a mixture across *all computable environments*; no task distribution is privileged, and the agent is optimal by construction relative to its prior ([[wiki/entities/aixi.md]], Hutter 2000). If so, human intelligence is an implementation of one algorithm, reverse-engineering the brain is the shortest path, and AI is close-ended | **Scoped.** Every measure of intelligence is relative to a stipulated scope of tasks and value weights; the human `g` factor is an evolutionary adaptation, general only in the limited sense that human morphology is (evolved for the savanna, sufficient for Everest and basketball, useless in interstellar space). No Free Lunch bites only under a uniform distribution over problems, which is not the distribution our universe supplies (Chollet 2019) | Sets the research strategy. Universal ⇒ pursue the algorithm and benchmark against optimality; scoped ⇒ AI is anthropocentric by necessity, must be benchmarked *against humans* on shared priors and a shared task scope, and "general intelligence" without a stated scope is not a claim. The wiki's scoring tables assume the scoped reading already (they compare against human performance), without having argued for it | `LIVE` |
| T18 | **Must a world model be generative?** | **Yes — the model's steps must resemble the generative process.** Causal model building defines a good model as one whose steps correspond to how the data were actually produced; the payoff is the richness criterion (classify, generate, parse, create, explain, re-goal), five of whose six queries require running the model *forward* to produce data (Lake et al. 2017; [[wiki/concepts/causal-model-building.md]]) | **No — generativity is what prevents abstraction.** A generative model must account for every detail of `y` and can only handle unpredictable detail by pushing it into a latent; it cannot *discard* it, because it has no abstract representation of `y` to discard it from. A joint-embedding architecture predicts the *representation*, so its encoder drops carpet texture and wind-blown leaves outright. "Generative latent-variable models are not capable of eliminating irrelevant details" — hence the explicit recommendation against them for world models (LeCun 2022; [[wiki/entities/h-jepa.md]]) | Decides what the wiki's environment model is for. If A, the model must be invertible into observations and the richness protocol (gap G17) is a valid instrument. If B, a world model is a compatibility function that "cannot easily be used to predict `y` from `x`", four of the six richness queries are unanswerable in principle, and the wiki loses one of its two shift-free evaluation protocols | `LIVE` — the two agree that predict-everything is the wrong objective and disagree on whether what survives must be runnable forward. **(brainstorm)** The disagreement may be about *level*: a hierarchy could be generative at the level where detail is causally relevant (strokes, contacts) and non-generative above it, which no source states |
| T19 | **Is scalar reward a sufficient training signal, or a marginal one?** | **Sufficient in principle** — "reward is enough" (Silver et al. 2021), and the wiki's own ceiling agrees at the ideal level: AIXI maximises expected reward over a universal mixture and needs nothing else ([[wiki/entities/aixi.md]]) | **Marginal in practice, and structurally so.** Scalar reward is low-information feedback, and in standard RL the cost function *is the environment* — an unknown, non-differentiable oracle whose gradient must be estimated by trials. Hutter's own accounting agrees quantitatively — demonstrations beat reward by `≈K(R)` bits ([[wiki/concepts/universal-induction.md]]) | Decides where the wiki's learning signal comes from. If B holds, `Π` and an epistemic bonus are not two variables read from one number but one variable — and a model that exposes uncertainty magnitude to downstream control is representing something the brain does not. It also bears on [[wiki/concepts/expected-free-energy.md]]'s open question of whether the epistemic term is a *state* of the planner or a *term in its loss* | `LIVE, weak` — B is a null under low power and the authors say so: the task never varied uncertainty magnitude parametrically, so degree had little variance to regress on. | T128 | **Are all precisions inferred, or are some structural constants?** | Precisions are a represented quantity optimised by the same free-energy descent as states and parameters, carried by synaptic gain — attention, salience and learning rate are all one inferred number (Friston 2009, [[wiki/concepts/precision-weighting.md]]) | At least one prior is **cognitively impenetrable**: in the hollow-mask illusion a correct, confidently held, reportable belief that the mask is concave never revises the percept, which is claimed to be evidence for a prior in older visual circuitry that higher levels cannot write to (`talk-nd-free-energy-principle`, **(tentative)** — an explainer, no experiment) | Decides whether an architecture may treat every prior's precision as a learnable parameter, or must partition the hierarchy into inferred and frozen gains — and whether "top-down wins" means "the agent's current best belief wins" or "a fixed prior wins" | `LIVE` — B's source is non-scientific and the illusion has scientific literature the wiki has not ingested; the tension is well-posed regardless, since A's formalism has no place to put a frozen gate |
| T129 | **Is the direct/indirect relation a scalar balance, or two concurrently active ensembles?** | **A balance.** The classical model reads movement as a shift in the net drive on a single output interface, and the wiki's model of it computes a literal difference, `net_j = [Go_j − NoGo_j]⁺` ([[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]). Dopamine depletion moving the two pathways in opposite directions, optogenetic activation of each pathway producing opposite motor effects (Kravitz et al. 2010; Hikida et al. 2010), and the opponent plasticity mechanism all fit it | **Two ensembles, both up.** Mink's elaboration predicts that during ongoing behaviour activity *increases* in ensembles of **both** pathways — select this program, suppress the competing ones — and primate limb-reaching recordings confirm it (Turner & Anderson 1997). On this reading the informative variable is *which* ensembles are co-active, and in Parkinson's models it is the **timing** of the two pathways' activity rather than the level that predicts movement choice and initiation (Bevan et al. 2002); Gerfen & Surmeier 2011 add that whether the two pathways even receive the same cortical information is unresolved | Decides whether an opponent gate can be collapsed to one scalar per channel. If B holds, a difference discards the identity of the co-active ensembles and the relative timing between them, and no architecture in the wiki represents either — PBWM's `kWTA` over `[Go − NoGo]⁺` cannot express "both channels active, different sets" at all | `LIVE` — the two are not strictly incompatible (a difference can be computed over co-active populations), but no wiki model computes one, and the timing result is outside both formulations |
| T130 | **In a gated working memory, is the basal ganglia a write-enable or the maintenance loop itself?** | **A write-enable that then lets go.** The module releases a reverberating loop and drops out — either a thalamo-cortical loop (Monchi et al. 2000; FROST, Ashby et al. 2005) or a cortico-cortical one whose switch it flips (Frank et al. 2001; Moustafa & Maida 2007; [[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]). Four of the five reviewed working-memory models take this position, and the cortico-cortical variant has an argument behind it: for thalamus to carry maintenance it would need as many dedicated cells as prefrontal cortex has, which is implausible (Frank et al. 2001) | **The loop runs through it.** In Schroll et al. 2012 the reverberation passes through the direct pathway, so the module *is* the maintenance mechanism, with the hyperdirect pathway supplying the reset that ends it. This is the most recent and most capable of the five — the only one that both learns what deserves maintenance and simulates delayed alternation and 1-2-AX — and it departs from the others without arguing for the departure (Helie et al. 2013) | Decides whether gating capacity is consumed for the whole retention interval. Under A, interference between maintenance and concurrent action selection scales with the *rate of writes*; under B, with the *number of items held*. No wiki architecture represents the second cost at all, and a transformer's key/value cache is position A taken to its limit | `LIVE` — no behavioural or lesion datum in the review separates them; the distinguishing dual-task simulation is cheap and has not been run |
| T131 | **When a behaviour becomes automatic, is the policy distilled into a new substrate or migrated to another loop?** | **Distilled and the module bypassed.** In SPEED (Ashby et al. 2007) the basal ganglia's role is to fire the correct premotor plan consistently enough for Hebbian learning to build a direct sensory-association → premotor cortico-cortical association; once that shortcut is strong the module is no longer in the path. This is the literal version of [[wiki/concepts/cognitive-control.md]]'s withdrawal step, and it predicts that practice *returns* controller capacity | **Migrated, and the module still required.** Control moves from an anterior visual-coordinate striatal loop to a posterior motor-coordinate one but never leaves the striatum (Nakahara et al. 2001), or the habitual action keeps being selected by the same direct/indirect competition (Chersi et al. 2013). Miyachi's muscimol dissociation — anterior caudate/putamen disrupts early production, middle-posterior putamen disrupts late production — is direct evidence that a striatal site is still load-bearing after overtraining | Decides the parallel capacity of automatic behaviour: distillation into cortical weights is unbounded, migration is capped by the number of loops. It also decides whether "handing a skill over" frees the controller, which is the assumption [[wiki/concepts/skill-acquisition-efficiency.md]] prices skills with | `LEANING A` — Desmurget & Turner 2010 inactivated the motor segment of the internal globus pallidus in trained monkeys, disconnecting the module from frontal cortex: movement kinematics were impaired, **sequence knowledge was not**. That is what A predicts and what both B models fail. Not decisive: the result is about an overlearned motor sequence, and B's late-phase striatal site is upstream of the inactivated output |
| T132 | **Is the indirect pathway a veto or an exploration channel?** | **A veto.** It carries `NoGo` — action-specific suppression of the alternatives, which must be individually addressable for probabilistic learning to work, and whose removal degrades performance (Frank 2005); the same reading drives the reaching and handwriting models (Bischoff 1998; Contreras-Vidal & Stelmach 1995) | **An explorer.** Chakravarthy's group inserts a third regime between `Go` and `NoGo` whose substrate is the indirect pathway: when the value gradient is absent or too weak to learn from, it sets the *stochasticity* of action selection. Dopamine-depleted simulations spend less time in `Go` and more in the other two, reproducing movement undershoot, bradykinesia and increased path variability in reaching, and increased search times in saccadic visual search (Magdoom et al. 2011; Krishnan et al. 2011) | Decides whether exploration is a hyperparameter or an anatomical channel with its own neuromodulatory control. Every wiki architecture bolts exploration on externally ([[wiki/entities/pbwm.md]]'s `Random Go` at fixed `p`); B makes annealing fall out of the same dopamine signal that sets the selection balance. The readings give opposite lesion predictions — A says a damaged veto makes an agent impulsive, B says it makes the agent deterministic and stuck | `LIVE` — the two are not formally exclusive (a stochastic veto is a veto), but they are fitted to disjoint data: A to probabilistic-learning and reversal behaviour, B to Parkinsonian motor kinematics, and no model in the 19-model census implements both |

It also decides which gap the anatomy answers: G24 (no principled planning horizon) or G61 (exploration is always an external schedule). | `LIVE` — neither is implemented, and the shared premise is weaker than either: the balance may not be a scalar at all, since both pathways are active during movement and *timing* rather than level predicts choice (T129) |
